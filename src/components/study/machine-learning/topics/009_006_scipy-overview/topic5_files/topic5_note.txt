1. WHAT IS A Z-SCORE?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- The standard score (Z-score) measures how many standard deviations ($\sigma$) an 
  individual data point ($x$) lies above or below the population mean ($\mu$).
- Formula:
        Z = (x - \mu) / \sigma
- In SciPy:
        from scipy import stats
        z_array = stats.zscore(data)

2. PROPERTIES OF Z-SCORE TRANSFORMED DATA:
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Mean of Z-scores is ALWAYS 0.
- Standard deviation and variance of Z-scores is ALWAYS 1.
- Unitless: Allows fair comparison of variables on completely different units
  (e.g., comparing annual salary in ₹ Lakhs with age in years).

3. THE EMPIRICAL RULE (68 - 95 - 99.7):
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- In any true Gaussian normal distribution:
  * 68.27% of observations fall within $\mu \pm 1\sigma$ ($|Z| \le 1$).
  * 95.45% of observations fall within $\mu \pm 2\sigma$ ($|Z| \le 2$).
  * 99.73% of observations fall within $\mu \pm 3\sigma$ ($|Z| \le 3$).
- Values with $|Z| > 3.0$ are statistically rare ($< 0.27\%$) and typically 
  classified as anomalies or data entry errors.

4. DIFFERENCE WITH SCIKIT-LEARN STANDARDSCALER:
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- `stats.zscore(X)` computes z-scores directly on the given dataset array.
- `sklearn.preprocessing.StandardScaler` is an estimator that stores `mean_` and `var_`
  to apply the exact same transformation later on unseen test/production data.
