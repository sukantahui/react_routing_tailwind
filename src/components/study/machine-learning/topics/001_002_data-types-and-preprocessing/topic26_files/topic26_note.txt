2. COMPREHENSIVE TAXONOMY & MATHEMATICAL FORMULARY
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------

2.1 Stevens' Four Levels of Measurement (Topics 0-4):
1. Nominal Scale: Categories without natural order. Admissible statistic: Mode.
   Encoding: One-Hot Encoding (drop-first for linear models), Bayesian Target Encoding.
2. Ordinal Scale: Ordered categories without uniform distance. Admissible: Median, Percentiles.
   Encoding: Monotonic integer rank encoding (e.g., [Junior: 1, Mid: 2, Senior: 3]).
3. Interval Scale: Known distances between values, arbitrary zero (e.g., Celsius).
   Admissible: Mean, Standard Deviation, Z-Score standardization.
4. Ratio Scale: Known distances, non-arbitrary true absolute zero (e.g., Income, Age, Kelvin).
   Admissible: Geometric Mean, Coefficient of Variation, Log1p transformations.

2.2 Rubin's Missing Data Taxonomy (Topics 5-9, 22):
Let Y = (Y_{\text{obs}}, Y_{\text{mis}}) and M \in \{0, 1\} be the missingness indicator.
- MCAR (Missing Completely at Random): P(M \mid Y_{\text{obs}}, Y_{\text{mis}}) = P(M).
  Missingness is independent of all variables. Sample mean/median remains unbiased.
- MAR (Missing at Random): P(M \mid Y_{\text{obs}}, Y_{\text{mis}}) = P(M \mid Y_{\text{obs}}).
  Missingness depends on observed covariates (e.g., job role). Unbiasedness is restored
  by stratified group imputation: \hat{\mu}_k = \arg\min_c \sum_{i \in \Omega_k} |y_i - c|.
- MNAR (Missing Not at Random): P(M \mid Y_{\text{obs}}, Y_{\text{mis}}) \neq P(M \mid Y_{\text{obs}}).
  Missingness depends on the unobserved value itself (e.g., high earners withholding CTC).
  Listwise deletion induces severe survivor truncation bias.

2.3 Categorical Encoding & Singularity Proof (Topics 10-14, 23):
When encoding K nominal categories with an intercept column \mathbf{1}:
    X = [\mathbf{1}, \mathbf{d}_1, \mathbf{d}_2, \dots, \mathbf{d}_K]
Because \sum_{k=1}^K \mathbf{d}_k = \mathbf{1}, there exists non-zero \mathbf{c} such that X\mathbf{c} = \mathbf{0}:
    1 \cdot \mathbf{1} - \sum_{k=1}^K 1 \cdot \mathbf{d}_k = \mathbf{0}
By the Rank-Nullity Theorem:
    \text{rank}(X) \le K < K + 1 \implies \det(X^T X) = 0.000000, \quad \kappa(X^T X) = \infty
OLS weights \hat{\mathbf{w}} = (X^T X)^{-1} X^T \mathbf{y} fail.
Resolution: Drop the reference dummy column (e.g. Barrackpore). Then \det(X_{\text{drop}}^T X_{\text{drop}}) > 0.

Bayesian Target Encoding with m-Estimate Smoothing:
    \hat{S}_c = \frac{n_c \cdot \bar{y}_c + m \cdot \mu_{\text{global}}}{n_c + m}

2.4 Feature Scaling, Breakdown Points & Distance Domination (Topics 15-18, 24):
Squared Euclidean distance in unscaled metric space:
    d^2(\mathbf{x}_i, \mathbf{x}_j) = (\Delta \text{Age})^2 + (\Delta \text{Income})^2
    \rho = \frac{(\Delta \text{Income})^2}{(\Delta \text{Age})^2} \approx \frac{(90 \times 10^5)^2}{(40)^2} \approx 5.06 \times 10^{10}
Unscaled distance is dominated by Income by a factor of 50 billion. Age is invisible.

Estimator Breakdown Points (\varepsilon^*):
- Arithmetic Mean & Variance: \varepsilon^* = 0\% (1 adversarial outlier destroys estimate).
- Sample Median: \varepsilon^* = 50\% (immune to up to 50% arbitrary corruption).
- Interquartile Range (IQR = Q_3 - Q_1): \varepsilon^* = 25\%.
- RobustScaler Formulation: x_{\text{rob}} = \frac{x - Q_2}{\text{IQR}}.

2.5 Fuzzy Entity Resolution (Jaro-Winkler) (Topics 19-21, 25):
Matching window: W = \lfloor \frac{\max(|s_1|, |s_2|)}{2} \rfloor - 1.
    d_j = \frac{1}{3} \left( \frac{m}{|s_1|} + \frac{m}{|s_2|} + \frac{m - t}{m} \right)
Winkler's prefix bonus for shared leading characters \ell \le 4 with scaling factor p = 0.1:
    d_w = d_j + \ell \cdot p \cdot (1 - d_j)

5. THE 10 DEADLY PREPROCESSING SINS (PRODUCTION ANTI-PATTERNS)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Sin 1: Fit-Before-Split Leakage. Fitting any transformer on the entire dataset
   before partitioning test data.
2. Sin 2: Sparse Matrix Densification. Applying StandardScaler(with_mean=True)
   on high-cardinality CSR matrices, causing fatal Out-Of-Memory crashes.
3. Sin 3: The Dummy Variable Trap. Encoding all K categories with an intercept
   column in unregularized linear regression, creating singular matrices.
4. Sin 4: Unhandled Cold-Start Tokens. Leaving handle_unknown='error' active on
   production microservices, causing unhandled 500 exceptions on unseen cities.
5. Sin 5: Unscaled Distance-Based Models. Feeding unscaled continuous features into
   KNN or K-Means, allowing high-magnitude features to dominate Euclidean distance by 99.9%.
6. Sin 6: Outlier Compression under MinMaxScaler. Compressing normal distribution
   tails into near-zero ranges due to extreme outliers, destroying feature variance.
7. Sin 7: Unnecessary Scaling on Tree Ensembles. Spending CPU cycles standardizing
   features before feeding into XGBoost (trees split strictly on rank order).
8. Sin 8: Listwise Deletion on MNAR Records. Systematically deleting rows with
   missing values when missingness depends on the value itself, inducing severe survivor bias.
9. Sin 9: Target Encoding Without Regularization. Computing raw class means on
   small sample categories, causing target leakage and severe overfitting.
10. Sin 10: Inverting Imputers and Scalers. Applying scaling before imputation,
    corrupting distance metrics and imputation donors.

7. 10 HIGH-YIELD MAKAUT MASTER VIVA QUESTIONS & ANALYTICAL ANSWERS
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------

Q1: Why is estimating the sample mean on an entire dataset before train-test splitting considered a fatal architectural flaw?
A1: Because it violates the Fundamental Pipeline Invariant (data leakage). Information about the test set distribution (its central tendency) bleeds into the training phase. The resulting offline model displays artificially optimistic validation scores that collapse upon production deployment.

Q2: Prove why ordinary least squares collapses when all K dummy variables are included alongside an intercept column.
A2: Because categories are mutually exclusive and exhaustive, the sum of all dummy columns identically equals the intercept column (sum d_k = 1). The design matrix columns are linearly dependent, so rank(X) <= K < K+1. The Gram matrix X^T X is singular (det(X^T X) = 0), and its condition number is infinite, making (X^T X)^(-1) undefined.

Q3: Under Rubin's taxonomy, how does MAR differ mathematically from MNAR, and what are the imputation consequences?
A3: Under MAR, missingness is conditionally independent of the missing value given observed features: P(M | Y_obs, Y_mis) = P(M | Y_obs). Stratified group median imputation yields asymptotically unbiased estimates. Under MNAR, P(M | Y_obs, Y_mis) depends on Y_mis itself. Listwise deletion truncates the distribution tail, introducing severe survivor bias.

Q4: What is the breakdown point of an estimator, and why is RobustScaler superior to StandardScaler in the presence of executive salary outliers?
A4: The breakdown point is the minimum fraction of arbitrary outlier contamination that can drive an estimator to infinity. The arithmetic mean and sample variance have a 0% breakdown point (a single ₹10 Crore salary skews the mean). The median has a 50% breakdown point and IQR has a 25% breakdown point. RobustScaler uses Median and IQR, ensuring normal applicant features are not crushed into near-zero ranges.

Q5: In K-Nearest Neighbors, why does an unscaled feature with range [0, 10^7] render a feature with range [0, 100] functionally invisible?
A5: Squared Euclidean distance is additive: d^2 = (Delta x_1)^2 + (Delta x_2)^2. The ratio of squared differences is rho = (10^7)^2 / (100)^2 = 10^14 / 10^4 = 10^10 (10 billion to 1). The high-magnitude feature contributes >99.999% of total distance, making the low-magnitude feature completely irrelevant during neighbor selection.

Q6: How does Winkler's modification improve standard Jaro distance for municipal string entity resolution?
A6: Human typists rarely misspell the first few characters of a word. Winkler adds a prefix bonus: d_w = d_j + l * p * (1 - d_j), where l is the common prefix length (up to 4) and p = 0.1. This significantly boosts similarity for pairs with identical initial characters, such as "barackpore" and "Barrackpore".

Q7: What is sparse matrix densification, and what parameter setting prevents it in scikit-learn?
A7: When One-Hot Encoding produces a sparse CSR matrix of mostly zeroes, applying StandardScaler with default with_mean=True subtracts the feature mean from every zero, turning structural zeroes into non-zero floats. This expands the matrix from megabytes to gigabytes, causing an Out-Of-Memory crash. Setting with_mean=False preserves matrix sparsity.

Q8: How does Bayesian target encoding with m-estimate smoothing prevent overfitting on rare categorical levels?
A8: Rare categories with very few samples (e.g., n_c = 1) can exhibit extreme target averages. The m-estimate shrinks the category mean toward the global target mean: S_c = (n_c * y_bar + m * mu) / (n_c + m). When n_c is small, the formula smoothly defaults to the prior global mean mu.

Q9: What happens if an incoming streaming customer provides a city that was not in the training vocabulary, and how should production encoders handle it?
A9: If the encoder has handle_unknown='error', it throws a runtime exception and crashes the API microservice. Enforcing handle_unknown='ignore' produces an all-zeros dummy vector [0, 0, 0, 0], which safely absorbs into the baseline intercept without crashing.

Q10: Why is feature scaling unnecessary before training decision tree ensembles like XGBoost or Random Forests?
A10: Decision tree algorithms evaluate split candidates using monotonic threshold inequalities (x_j <= threshold). They depend strictly on the rank ordering of feature values rather than metric distances or gradient step sizes. Scaling preserves rank order, providing zero performance benefit while adding unnecessary preprocessing latency.
