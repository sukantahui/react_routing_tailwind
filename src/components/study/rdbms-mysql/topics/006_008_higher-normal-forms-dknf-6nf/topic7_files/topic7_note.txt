2. THEORETICAL FOUNDATION & MATRICES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Core Concept: Relational normalization ensures that data dependencies are modeled
  strictly according to business invariants, preventing update, deletion, and insertion anomalies.
- Formal Rule: 1. Domain-Key Normal Form (DKNF): The Theoretical Pinnacle
- Decomposition Strategy: Separate tables for each temporal attribute ensure microsecond-level historical point-in-time auditability.

3. BEFORE-VS-AFTER SCHEMA TRANSFORMATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
UNNORMALIZED / NON-COMPLIANT STATE:
Every constraint on relation R must be a logical consequence of Domain Constraints and Key Constraints.
Root Cause: DKNF guarantees that NO modification anomalies of any kind can exist, but is non-algorithmic and hard to enforce in standard SQL.

NORMALIZED STATE:
Immunity to temporal nulls and historical overwrites in banking and medical record systems.

4. SENIOR ARCHITECTURAL PITFALLS & BEST PRACTICES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- PITFALL: Used extensively in Anchor Modeling and temporal time-series databases for valid-time and transaction-time intervals.
- BEST PRACTICE: Immunity to temporal nulls and historical overwrites in banking and medical record systems.
- ACID INVARIANT: Always declare explicit foreign keys with ON DELETE RESTRICT to protect transactional ledgers.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
