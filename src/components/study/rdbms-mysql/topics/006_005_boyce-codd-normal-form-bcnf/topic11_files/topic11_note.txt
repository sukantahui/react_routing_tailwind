2. THEORETICAL FOUNDATION & MATRICES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Core Concept: Relational normalization ensures that data dependencies are modeled
  strictly according to business invariants, preventing update, deletion, and insertion anomalies.
- Formal Rule: 1. Overlapping Candidate Keys Bypassing 3NF (Student-Subject-Advisor)
- Decomposition Strategy: Split into advisor_subjects(advisor PK, subject) and student_advisors(student, advisor PK).

3. BEFORE-VS-AFTER SCHEMA TRANSFORMATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
UNNORMALIZED / NON-COMPLIANT STATE:
In relation (student, subject, advisor), each advisor teaches only one subject, and a student has at most one advisor per subject.
Root Cause: Candidate keys are {student, subject} and {student, advisor}. FD advisor -> subject satisfies 3NF because subject is prime, yet redundancy remains!

NORMALIZED STATE:
All tables are in strict BCNF; advisor subject is stored in exactly 1 row.

4. SENIOR ARCHITECTURAL PITFALLS & BEST PRACTICES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- PITFALL: Enforcing BCNF removes update anomalies when an advisor switches subjects, but may sacrifice dependency preservation.
- BEST PRACTICE: All tables are in strict BCNF; advisor subject is stored in exactly 1 row.
- ACID INVARIANT: Always declare explicit foreign keys with ON DELETE RESTRICT to protect transactional ledgers.
