1. WHAT IS A MULTI-VALUED DEPENDENCY (MVD)?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A Multi-valued Dependency (denoted as $X \twoheadrightarrow Y$, read as "X multi-determines Y") 
was introduced by Ronald Fagin in 1977.

Formal Mathematical Definition:
Let R be a relation schema, and let X, Y, and Z = R - (X ∪ Y) be subsets of attributes in R.
A multi-valued dependency $X \twoheadrightarrow Y$ holds on relation r(R) if and only if:
Whenever two tuples $t_1$ and $t_2$ exist in r such that $t_1[X] = t_2[X]$, 
there must also exist tuples $t_3$ and $t_4$ in r such that:
• $t_3[X] = t_4[X] = t_1[X] = t_2[X]$
• $t_3[Y] = t_1[Y]$ and $t_3[Z] = t_2[Z]$
• $t_4[Y] = t_2[Y]$ and $t_4[Z] = t_1[Z]$

Plain English Meaning:
X multi-determines Y if the set of Y-values associated with a given X-value depends ONLY on X, 
and is COMPLETELY INDEPENDENT of the remaining attributes Z in the relation.

Crucial MVD Complementarity Property:
MVDs always occur in symmetric pairs: If $X \twoheadrightarrow Y$ holds in relation R, 
then $X \twoheadrightarrow (R - X - Y)$ AUTOMATICALLY holds as well ($X \twoheadrightarrow Y \mid Z$).


2. TRIVIAL VS NON-TRIVIAL MVDS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
• Trivial MVD: $X \twoheadrightarrow Y$ is trivial if $Y \subseteq X$ or $X \cup Y = R$.
  (A relation with only 2 attributes can ONLY contain trivial MVDs).
• Non-Trivial MVD: $X \twoheadrightarrow Y$ is non-trivial if $Y \not\subseteq X$ and $X \cup Y \neq R$.


3. THE BENCHMARK 4NF VIOLATION (INDEPENDENT MULTI-VALUED ATTRIBUTES)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Consider an academy instructor table tracking courses and hobbies:
`Instructor_Skills_Hobbies(instructor_name, course_taught, hobby)`

Domain Facts:
• Sukanta Hui teaches 'MySQL Master' and 'React Architect'.
• Sukanta Hui's hobbies are 'Chess' and 'Photography'.
• Teaching a course has ZERO relationship to what hobby the instructor enjoys.

If we store this in a single BCNF table with Composite PK `(instructor_name, course_taught, hobby)`, 
the cross-product of independent attributes forces redundant tuple multiplication:

Tuples in 1NF/BCNF:
1. ('Sukanta Hui', 'MySQL Master', 'Chess')
2. ('Sukanta Hui', 'MySQL Master', 'Photography')
3. ('Sukanta Hui', 'React Architect', 'Chess')
4. ('Sukanta Hui', 'React Architect', 'Photography')

Why this causes Cartesian Multiplicative Explosion:
• 2 courses × 2 hobbies = 4 rows.
• If Sukanta adds a 3rd course ('DevOps') and a 3rd hobby ('Cricket'), rows jump to 3 × 3 = 9 rows!
• Adding 1 course requires inserting 3 separate rows!


4. FOURTH NORMAL FORM (4NF) DEFINITION & DECOMPOSITION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Definition of Fourth Normal Form (4NF):
A relation schema R is in Fourth Normal Form (4NF) if and only if:
1. It is in Boyce-Codd Normal Form (BCNF).
2. For EVERY non-trivial multi-valued dependency $X \twoheadrightarrow Y$ in R:
   X MUST BE A SUPER KEY OF R.

4NF Decomposition Procedure:
To eliminate non-trivial MVD $X \twoheadrightarrow Y$ in R (where X is not a super key):
Decompose R into two independent binary relations:
1. $R_1 = X \cup Y$
2. $R_2 = R - Y$ (which is $X \cup Z$)

Decomposing `Instructor_Skills_Hobbies`:
1. `Instructor_Courses(instructor_name, course_taught)` ➔ 2 rows!
2. `Instructor_Hobbies(instructor_name, hobby)` ➔ 2 rows!
Total rows dropped from 4 down to 4, and from 9 down to 6! Zero cross-multiplication.


5. SUMMARY CHECKLIST FOR 4NF
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[x] Multi-valued Dependency ($X \twoheadrightarrow Y$): Y depends only on X, independent of Z.
[x] MVDs always occur in complementary pairs ($X \twoheadrightarrow Y \mid Z$).
[x] Independent multi-valued columns in one table cause Cartesian explosion ($M \times N$ rows).
[x] 4NF Rule: For every non-trivial $X \twoheadrightarrow Y$, X MUST be a Super Key.
[x] 4NF Decomposition: Split independent multi-valued facts into separate 2-column tables.
