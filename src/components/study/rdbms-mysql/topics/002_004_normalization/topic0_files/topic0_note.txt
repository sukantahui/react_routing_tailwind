1. WHAT IS DATABASE NORMALIZATION?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Database Normalization is the formal mathematical and structural process of 
organizing attributes and relations in a relational schema to minimize data 
redundancy, optimize storage efficiency, and completely eliminate data modification 
anomalies (Insertion, Update, and Deletion anomalies) without loss of information.

Invented by Edgar F. Codd (1970) as part of his seminal relational model, 
normalization uses functional dependencies as the rigorous mathematical basis 
for decomposing unnormalized, wide tables into smaller, well-structured relations.

Key Objectives of Normalization:
1. Eliminate Redundant Data: Avoid storing the exact same data value in multiple rows.
2. Eliminate Data Anomalies: Prevent insertion failures, update inconsistencies, and accidental deletions.
3. Guarantee Lossless Join: Ensure decomposed tables can be rejoined without generating spurious tuples ($R = R_1 \bowtie R_2$).
4. Preserve Functional Dependencies: Ensure all integrity constraints can be enforced locally within individual relations.
5. Provide Schema Flexibility: Allow database evolution without requiring widespread schema modifications.


2. THE THREE CARDINAL DATABASE MODIFICATION ANOMALIES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Consider an unnormalized table:
`Student_Enrollment_Universal(student_id, student_name, city, course_id, course_title, instructor, course_fee)`

ANOMALY 1: INSERTION ANOMALY
• Problem: You cannot insert a newly created course (e.g. 'Cloud DevOps' taught by 'Sukanta Hui' with fee ₹6,000) 
  unless at least one student has enrolled in it, because `student_id` is part of the primary key and cannot be NULL (Entity Integrity violation).
• Consequence: Business entities cannot exist independently.

ANOMALY 2: UPDATE / MODIFICATION ANOMALY
• Problem: If course 'MySQL Masterclass' fee changes from ₹4,500 to ₹5,000, and 500 students in Barrackpore are 
  enrolled in it, all 500 records must be updated simultaneously. If even one record is missed or updated during a 
  system crash, the database enters an inconsistent state where the same course has multiple conflicting fees.
• Consequence: Inconsistent, corrupted data and lack of a single source of truth.

ANOMALY 3: DELETION ANOMALY
• Problem: If 'Susmita Ghosh' is the ONLY student enrolled in 'React Architect', deleting Susmita's student 
  record accidentally deletes the entire course record, losing its title, fee, and instructor information forever.
• Consequence: Unintended, irreversible loss of critical business data.


3. THE NORMAL FORMS PROGRESSION LADDER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Normalization progresses through distinct stages called Normal Forms (NF):

1. Unnormalized Form (UNF): Tables containing repeating groups, multi-valued cells, or non-atomic data.
2. First Normal Form (1NF): Every cell contains exactly one atomic (indivisible) value. No repeating groups.
3. Second Normal Form (2NF): In 1NF + NO Partial Dependencies (every non-prime attribute must be fully functionally dependent on the ENTIRE primary key).
4. Third Normal Form (3NF): In 2NF + NO Transitive Dependencies (non-prime attributes cannot depend on other non-prime attributes).
5. Boyce-Codd Normal Form (BCNF): A stricter version of 3NF where EVERY determinant must be a super key ($X \rightarrow Y \implies X$ is a super key).
6. Fourth Normal Form (4NF): Eliminates Multi-Valued Dependencies (MVDs: $X \twoheadrightarrow Y$).
7. Fifth Normal Form (5NF / PJNF): Eliminates Project-Join Dependencies.
8. Domain-Key Normal Form (DKNF): Theoretical holy grail where all constraints are logical consequences of domain and key constraints.


4. THE DECOMPOSITION PARADIGM & TRADE-OFFS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Normalization decomposes 1 large table into multiple normalized tables connected via Foreign Keys:
• `Students(student_id, full_name, city)`
• `Courses(course_id, course_title, instructor, course_fee)`
• `Enrollments(student_id, course_id, enrollment_date, grade)`

Trade-Off Analysis:
• Normalized (3NF/BCNF):
  [+] Zero redundant storage.
  [+] Zero insertion, update, or deletion anomalies.
  [+] High write (INSERT/UPDATE/DELETE) throughput and data integrity.
  [-] Complex multi-table JOINs required for reporting queries.
• Denormalized (OLAP / Data Warehousing):
  [+] Ultra-fast read queries with zero joins.
  [-] Storage redundancy and potential update inconsistencies.


5. COMMON PITFALLS & DANGER ZONES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Over-normalizing high-read analytical reporting databases (causing join bottlenecks).
2. Decomposing relations into lossy joins (causing spurious rows when rejoined).
3. Ignoring functional dependency preservation during decomposition.
4. Assuming 1NF is sufficient for production OLTP applications.
