1. WHAT ARE DATABASE ANOMALIES?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A Database Anomaly is a structural flaw or operational failure in a relational 
database schema that causes data corruption, inconsistency, or unintended data loss 
during routine CRUD (Create, Read, Update, Delete) operations.

Anomalies occur exclusively in unnormalized or poorly designed tables where 
multiple distinct business entities (e.g. Students, Courses, Departments, Instructors) 
are mashed into a single wide relation containing redundant data and mismatched functional dependencies.

The 3 Cardinal Anomaly Classes:
1. Insertion Anomaly (Inability to add valid standalone data).
2. Update / Modification Anomaly (Inconsistent data due to partial updates of duplicate values).
3. Deletion Anomaly (Unintended, irreversible loss of independent information when removing child records).


2. BENCHMARK UNNORMALIZED RELATION FOR ANOMALY ANALYSIS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Consider the unnormalized flat relation:
`Student_Course_Emp(student_id, student_name, city, course_id, course_title, instructor_id, instructor_name, instructor_room, course_fee)`

Primary Key: `(student_id, course_id)`

Sample Tuples:
1. `(#101, 'Mamata Hui', 'Barrackpore', 'C101', 'MySQL Master', 'INS01', 'Sukanta Hui', 'Lab-A', 4500.00)`
2. `(#102, 'Mahima Sharma', 'Kolkata', 'C101', 'MySQL Master', 'INS01', 'Sukanta Hui', 'Lab-A', 4500.00)`
3. `(#103, 'Abhronila Das', 'Barrackpore', 'C102', 'React Architect', 'INS02', 'Pravin Roy', 'Lab-B', 5500.00)`
4. `(#104, 'Susmita Ghosh', 'Ichapur', 'C103', 'Cloud DevOps', 'INS03', 'Alok Sen', 'Lab-C', 6000.00)`


3. IN-DEPTH ANATOMY OF THE 3 ANOMALIES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

ANOMALY 1: INSERTION ANOMALY
• Case 1: You hire a new instructor 'Debangshu Roy' for 'Lab-D' who has not been assigned a course or students yet.
  - SQL: `INSERT INTO Student_Course_Emp (instructor_id, instructor_name, ...) VALUES ('INS04', 'Debangshu Roy', ...);`
  - Failure: `student_id` and `course_id` are primary keys and cannot be NULL (Entity Integrity violation).
• Case 2: You create a brand new course 'AI Masterclass' before any student registers.
  - Failure: Cannot insert because `student_id` is NULL.

ANOMALY 2: UPDATE ANOMALY
• Case 1: Instructor 'Sukanta Hui' moves from 'Lab-A' to 'Lab-X'.
  - Problem: 'Sukanta Hui' appears in 500 rows across Barrackpore.
  - Failure: If an update statement updates 499 rows and misses 1, or is aborted midway, the database holds two conflicting room numbers for the same instructor.
  - SQL: `UPDATE Student_Course_Emp SET instructor_room = 'Lab-X' WHERE instructor_id = 'INS01';` (High disk I/O, risk of desynchronization).

ANOMALY 3: DELETION ANOMALY
• Case: 'Susmita Ghosh' (#104) is the ONLY student enrolled in 'Cloud DevOps' (C103). She cancels her admission.
  - SQL: `DELETE FROM Student_Course_Emp WHERE student_id = '#104';`
  - Catastrophe: Deleting Susmita's row also permanently deletes all knowledge of the 'Cloud DevOps' course, its fee, instructor 'Alok Sen', and 'Lab-C'.


4. THE MATHEMATICAL CURE: 3NF RELATIONAL DECOMPOSITION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
By decomposing `Student_Course_Emp` into 4 independent, normalized relations:

1. `Students(student_id [PK], student_name, city)`
2. `Instructors(instructor_id [PK], instructor_name, instructor_room)`
3. `Courses(course_id [PK], course_title, course_fee, instructor_id [FK])`
4. `Enrollments(student_id [FK], course_id [FK], enrollment_date, grade)`
   - PK: `(student_id, course_id)`

Benefits of Decomposition:
• Insertion: New instructors and courses can be added to their respective tables anytime with 0 students.
• Update: Instructor room update modifies EXACTLY ONE row in `Instructors`.
• Deletion: Deleting an enrollment row in `Enrollments` leaves `Students`, `Courses`, and `Instructors` 100% intact.


5. SUMMARY CHECKLIST FOR DATABASE ANOMALIES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[x] Anomalies arise from mixing multiple entities with conflicting functional dependencies in 1 table.
[x] Insertion Anomaly: Cannot insert standalone parent data due to composite PK NOT NULL constraints.
[x] Update Anomaly: Multiple redundant copies lead to desynchronization and data inconsistency.
[x] Deletion Anomaly: Deleting child records accidentally destroys unrelated master catalog data.
[x] 3NF/BCNF Decomposition completely cures all 3 modification anomalies.
