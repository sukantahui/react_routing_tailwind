1. WHAT IS THIRD NORMAL FORM (3NF)?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Third Normal Form (3NF) was formulated by Dr. Edgar F. Codd in 1971 to eliminate 
transitive dependencies between non-prime attributes in relational schemas.

Formal Definition of 3NF:
A relation schema R is in Third Normal Form (3NF) if and only if:
1. It is in Second Normal Form (2NF).
2. NO non-prime attribute in R is transitively dependent on any candidate key of R.

Disjunctive Condition Definition (Mathematical Test):
A relation schema R is in 3NF if for EVERY non-trivial functional dependency $X \rightarrow A$ 
holding on R, AT LEAST ONE of the following conditions is satisfied:
• Condition 1: X is a Super Key of R (X is a candidate key or superset of a candidate key).
• Condition 2: A is a Prime Attribute of R (A is a member of at least one candidate key of R).

The Famous Normalization Mantra:
"Every non-key attribute must provide a fact about the key (1NF), the whole key (2NF), 
and nothing but the key (3NF), so help me Codd!"


2. THE CLASSIC 3NF VIOLATION (STUDENT DEPARTMENT HIERARCHY)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Consider a student admission table in 2NF:
`Students_Department(student_id, student_name, department_id, department_name, department_head, building_block)`

Primary Key: `student_id` (Single-column PK ➔ Automatically 2NF)
• Prime Attributes: `{student_id}`
• Non-Prime Attributes: `{student_name, department_id, department_name, department_head, building_block}`

Functional Dependencies:
1. `student_id → {student_name, department_id}` (Condition 1 satisfied: student_id is Super Key).
2. `department_id → {department_name, department_head, building_block}` ❌
   - Is `department_id` a Super Key? NO (Multiple students belong to department 'D01').
   - Is `department_head` a Prime Attribute? NO (It is not part of any candidate key).
   - Both 3NF conditions FAIL! This is a severe Transitive Dependency:
     `student_id → department_id ➔ department_head`

Production Modification Anomalies:
• Update Anomaly: If CS department head changes, all 500 student rows must be updated.
• Insertion Anomaly: Cannot insert a newly created department without an enrolled student.
• Deletion Anomaly: Deleting the last student in a department permanently erases department details.


3. THE 3NF DECOMPOSITION PROCEDURE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
To convert a 2NF relation into 3NF:
1. Identify each violating dependency $X \rightarrow Y$ where X is not a super key and Y is non-prime.
2. Create a dedicated relation $R_1(X, Y)$ with X as the Primary Key.
3. In the original relation, remove Y and retain X as a Foreign Key referencing $R_1$.

Resulting 3NF Decomposed Schema:
1. `Departments(department_id [PK], department_name, department_head, building_block)`
   - Super Key Determinant: `department_id` is PK (Condition 1 satisfied).
2. `Students(student_id [PK], student_name, department_id [FK])`
   - Super Key Determinant: `student_id` is PK (Condition 1 satisfied).
   - Foreign Key: `department_id` references `Departments(department_id)`.


4. GUARANTEES OF 3NF SYNTHESIS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lossless Join: Rejoining tables using `NATURAL JOIN` or `INNER JOIN` on `department_id` 
   reconstructs the exact original tuples without producing spurious data.
2. Dependency Preservation: All original functional dependencies can be enforced within 
   individual tables without requiring cross-table joins during `INSERT`/`UPDATE`.
