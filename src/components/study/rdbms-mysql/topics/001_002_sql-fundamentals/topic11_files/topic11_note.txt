1. WHAT ARE LOGICAL OPERATORS IN SQL?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Logical operators combine or negate multiple boolean search conditions in `WHERE`, `HAVING`,
and `ON` clauses to build compound filtering expressions.

THE 4 LOGICAL OPERATORS IN MYSQL:
1. `AND` (or `&&`): Returns TRUE only if BOTH conditions evaluate to TRUE.
2. `OR` (or `||`): Returns TRUE if EITHER condition (or both) evaluates to TRUE.
3. `NOT` (or `!`): Inverts the boolean outcome (TRUE becomes FALSE; FALSE becomes TRUE).
4. `XOR`: Exclusive OR; returns TRUE if exactly ONE operand is TRUE (and the other is FALSE).


2. OPERATOR PRECEDENCE & DE MORGAN'S LAWS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A. PRECEDENCE HIERARCHY:
   1. `NOT` (Highest logical precedence)
   2. `AND`
   3. `OR` / `XOR` (Lowest logical precedence)

B. WHY PARENTHESES `()` ARE MANDATORY:
   Because `AND` executes before `OR`, the query:
   `WHERE city = 'Barrackpore' OR city = 'Kolkata' AND fee >= 15000`
   is parsed by MySQL as:
   `WHERE city = 'Barrackpore' OR (city = 'Kolkata' AND fee >= 15000)`
   (Which accidentally returns ALL Barrackpore students regardless of their fee!).
   
   Correct grouping:
   `WHERE (city = 'Barrackpore' OR city = 'Kolkata') AND fee >= 15000;`

C. DE MORGAN'S LAWS FOR SQL LOGIC REFACTORING:
   • `NOT (A AND B)` is logically equivalent to `(NOT A) OR (NOT B)`
   • `NOT (A OR B)` is logically equivalent to `(NOT A) AND (NOT B)`


3. THREE-VALUED LOGIC TRUTH TABLES (WITH NULLs)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Expression           | Evaluates To
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
TRUE AND UNKNOWN     | UNKNOWN (Rejected in WHERE)
FALSE AND UNKNOWN    | FALSE (Rejected in WHERE)
TRUE OR UNKNOWN      | TRUE (Accepted in WHERE!)
FALSE OR UNKNOWN     | UNKNOWN (Rejected in WHERE)
NOT UNKNOWN          | UNKNOWN (Rejected in WHERE)


4. SHORT-CIRCUIT EVALUATION IN MYSQL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
MySQL evaluates conditions from left to right and short-circuits:
• In `A AND B`: If `A` is FALSE, MySQL skips evaluating `B` entirely.
• In `A OR B`: If `A` is TRUE, MySQL skips evaluating `B` entirely.
Placing cheaper, indexed, or highly selective conditions on the left improves performance.


5. REAL-WORLD CASE STUDIES (BARRACKPORE & KOLKATA)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CASE 1: Mamata Finding Eligible Scholarship Candidates in Barrackpore College
SELECT
    roll_no,
    CONCAT(first_name, ' ', last_name) AS student_name,
    admission_fee AS "Fee (₹)",
    city
FROM students
WHERE (city = 'Barrackpore' OR city = 'Ichapur')
  AND is_active = 1
  AND admission_fee <= 15000.00;

CASE 2: Debangshu Finding Flagged High-Risk Orders in Kolkata E-Commerce
SELECT
    order_number,
    customer_id,
    total_amount AS "Amount (₹)",
    order_status
FROM customer_orders
WHERE (order_status = 'pending' OR order_status = 'processing')
  AND total_amount >= 50000.00
  AND NOT (payment_method = 'COD');


6. COMMON PITFALLS & DANGER ZONES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Missing Parentheses when Mixing AND with OR:
   - The single most common logical bug in SQL application code.
2. Inverting Conditions Incorrectly:
   - Writing `WHERE NOT (city = 'Kolkata' AND fee > 10000)` excludes only Kolkata students with fee > 10000,
     while retaining Kolkata students with fee <= 10000 and all other cities!
3. Overlooking Short-Circuit Behavior in Stored Functions:
   - Stored functions with side effects may not execute if placed on the right side of a short-circuited AND/OR.


7. BEST PRACTICES & SUMMARY CHECKLIST
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[x] Always explicitly wrap `OR` groups in parentheses `( ... )` when combining with `AND`.
[x] Use `NOT IN` or `NOT EXISTS` rather than long chains of `AND col <> 'val'`.
[x] Apply De Morgan's laws to simplify convoluted negative conditions.
[x] Place indexed, highly selective conditions first to take advantage of short-circuiting.
[x] Test boolean expressions against truth tables when dealing with nullable columns.
