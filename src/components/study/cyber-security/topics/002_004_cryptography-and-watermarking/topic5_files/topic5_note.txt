1. EXECUTIVE OVERVIEW & DUAL-KEY ASYMMETRIC ARCHITECTURE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Asymmetric Key Cryptography (introduced by Diffie, Hellman, and Merkle in 1976) 
resolves the fundamental symmetric key distribution problem by deploying a mathematically 
linked pair of cryptographic keys:

  1. PUBLIC KEY (K_pub)   : Freely published and distributed over untrusted networks.
  2. PRIVATE KEY (K_priv) : Kept strictly secret and protected inside hardware crypto tokens.

* TWO COMPLEMENTARY OPERATIONAL MODES:
  A. CONFIDENTIALITY ENCRYPTION:
     - Sender encrypts data using Recipient's Public Key: C = E_{Bob_Pub}( P ).
     - ONLY Recipient's Private Key can decrypt: P = D_{Bob_Priv}( C ).
  B. AUTHENTICATION & NON-REPUDIATION (DIGITAL SIGNATURES):
     - Signer creates a signature using their Private Key: S = Sign_{Alice_Priv}( Hash(M) ).
     - Anyone in the world can verify using Alice's Public Key: Verify_{Alice_Pub}( Hash(M), S ).

2. ONE-WAY TRAPDOOR FUNCTIONS & THE 3 HARD MATHEMATICAL PROBLEMS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A One-Way Trapdoor Function y = f(x) is computationally easy to calculate in the 
forward direction, but computationally infeasible to invert (x = f^(-1)(y)) unless 
a secret mathematical "Trapdoor" (the Private Key) is known.

3. PUBLIC KEY INFRASTRUCTURE (PKI) & DIGITAL CERTIFICATES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

* THE MAN-IN-THE-MIDDLE (MitM) KEY REPLACEMENT THREAT:
  - Without authentication, an active attacker intercepts public keys and substitutes their own.

* X.509 CERTIFICATE AUTHORITIES (CAs):
  - A trusted Certificate Authority (e.g. DigiCert, Let's Encrypt) cryptographically signs the 
    user's public key, binding it to their verified domain name or legal identity.

* PERFECT FORWARD SECRECY (PFS - ECDHE):
  - Generates ephemeral key pairs dynamically per session. Compromising the server's long-term 
    private key in the future CANNOT decrypt recorded historical network sessions.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. POST-QUANTUM THREAT & NIST 2024 PQC STANDARDS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

* SHOR'S ALGORITHM (Peter Shor, 1994):
  - Solves Prime Factorization and Discrete Logarithms in polynomial time O((log N)^3) on quantum 
    computers with ~4,000 logical qubits, completely destroying RSA, DH, and ECC.

* NIST 2024 POST-QUANTUM STANDARDS (LATTICE MATHEMATICS):
  1. FIPS 203 (ML-KEM / CRYSTALS-Kyber)    : Lattice Key Encapsulation (Replaces RSA / ECDH).
  2. FIPS 204 (ML-DSA / CRYSTALS-Dilithium): Lattice Digital Signatures (Replaces RSA / ECDSA).
  3. FIPS 205 (SLH-DSA / SPHINCS+)         : Stateless Hash-Based Digital Signatures.

5. INDIAN STATUTORY COMPLIANCE & LEGAL ADMISSIBILITY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

* IT ACT 2000 SECTION 35 (Certifying Authorities & DSCs):
  - The Controller of Certifying Authorities (CCA India) licenses CAs (eMudhra, (n)Code) to issue 
    Class-3 Digital Signature Certificates (DSCs) using 2048-bit RSA keys on FIPS 140-2 crypto-tokens.

* IT ACT 2000 SECTION 5 (Legal Recognition of Digital Signatures):
  - Electronic records authenticated with an authentic DSC have identical legal validity and 
    non-repudiation to handwritten physical ink signatures in Indian courts of law.

7. PROFESSIONAL TIPS & COMMON PITFALLS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

* PROFESSIONAL ASYMMETRIC CRYPTOGRAPHY HABITS:
  1. Default to ECC (P-256 or Ed25519): Provides equal security to RSA-3072 with 10x smaller keys.
  2. Enforce Perfect Forward Secrecy (ECDHE): Prevents historical traffic decryption if server keys are stolen.
  3. Store Private Keys in Hardware Tokens: Class-3 DSCs must reside in FIPS 140-2 Level 2 cryptographic USBs.

* COMMON BEGINNER ASYMMETRIC MISTAKES:
  - Encrypting with your own Private Key (Private keys are for SIGNING, not encrypting confidential data).
  - Using "Textbook RSA" without OAEP padding (Vulnerable to multiplicative malleability attacks).
  - Neglecting certificate expiration and revocation checking (CRL / OCSP stapling).
  - Forgetting that Shor's algorithm will break classical RSA/ECC (Failing to plan for PQC migration).
