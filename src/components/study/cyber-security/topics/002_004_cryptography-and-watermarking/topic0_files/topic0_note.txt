1. EXECUTIVE OVERVIEW & THE SCIENCE OF CRYPTOLOGY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Cryptology (from Greek 'kryptos' hidden + 'logos' word/study) is the overarching mathematical 
and scientific discipline governing secure communications.

Cryptology is bifurcated into two mutually dependent branches:
  1. CRYPTOGRAPHY : The science and art of designing mathematical algorithms (ciphers) to secure 
     information, ensuring confidentiality, data integrity, authentication, and non-repudiation.
  2. CRYPTANALYSIS : The science and art of analyzing, breaking, and defeating cryptographic 
     algorithms, protocols, and implementations without possessing the legitimate key.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. KERCKHOFFS'S PRINCIPLE & SHANNON'S INFORMATION THEORY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

* KERCKHOFFS'S PRINCIPLE (1883):
  - "The security of a cryptosystem must depend solely on the secrecy of the cryptographic key, 
    NOT on the secrecy of the algorithm itself."
  - Rejection of "Security through Obscurity": Modern standards (AES, RSA, ECC) publish all 
    mathematical equations openly. True security relies 100% on key entropy and secrecy.

* CLAUDE SHANNON'S PROPERTIES OF SECURE CIPHERS (1949):
  1. CONFUSION : Obscures the mathematical relationship between the Key and the Ciphertext 
     (achieved via non-linear Substitution Boxes / S-Boxes).
  2. DIFFUSION : Spreads the statistical influence of each individual Plaintext bit across 
     the entire Ciphertext (achieved via Permutation / P-Boxes and bit shifting).
  3. AVALANCHE EFFECT : Flipping a single bit in the plaintext or key causes ~50% of the 
     output ciphertext bits to change pseudo-randomly.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. THE 4 PILLARS OF MODERN CRYPTOGRAPHY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  1. CONFIDENTIALITY : Ensures only authorized recipients can read plaintext (AES-GCM, RSA, ECC).
  2. DATA INTEGRITY  : Guarantees data was not altered or corrupted in transit (SHA-256, HMAC).
  3. AUTHENTICATION  : Verifies the authentic identity of communicating entities (Digital Certificates / PKI).
  4. NON-REPUDIATION : Proves undeniably that a specific sender authored a transaction (Digital Signatures).

5. THE ONE-TIME PAD (OTP) & PERFECT SECRECY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

* THE ONE-TIME PAD ($C = P \oplus K$):
  - Proved by Claude Shannon to be mathematically UNBREAKABLE (Information-Theoretically Secure).
  - Conditions: Key must be truly random, as long as the message, used ONLY ONCE, and kept secret.

* THE TWO-TIME PAD CATASTROPHE (Crib Dragging):
  - If Key $K$ is reused on two messages: $C_1 \oplus C_2 = (P_1 \oplus K) \oplus (P_2 \oplus K) = P_1 \oplus P_2$.
  - The secret key cancels out completely, allowing cryptanalysts to recover plaintexts via crib dragging.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. QUANTUM COMPUTING THREAT TO CRYPTOGRAPHY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. Shor's Algorithm:
   - Solves prime factorization in polynomial time, completely destroying RSA, Diffie-Hellman, and ECC.
   - Defense: Migration to Post-Quantum Cryptography (PQC - NIST ML-KEM / FIPS 203).

2. Grover's Algorithm:
   - Provides quadratic speedup on brute force ($\sqrt{2^n}$), halving symmetric key strength.
   - Defense: Upgrade from AES-128 to AES-256 (which provides 128-bit quantum security).

8. PROFESSIONAL TIPS & COMMON PITFALLS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

* PROFESSIONAL CRYPTOGRAPHIC HABITS:
  1. Never Implement Custom Crypto: Always use established, audited libraries (OpenSSL, Libsodium).
  2. Enforce Authenticated Encryption: Use AEAD modes (AES-GCM, ChaCha20-Poly1305) to prevent CCA attacks.
  3. Write Constant-Time Comparisons: Use `CRYPTO_memcmp()` to defeat side-channel timing leaks.

* COMMON BEGINNER CRYPTOGRAPHY MISTAKES:
  - Reusing nonces or keys in stream ciphers (enables catastrophic Two-Time Pad crib dragging).
  - Relying on "Security through Obscurity" by keeping proprietary algorithms secret.
  - Using unauthenticated encryption modes (AES-CBC without HMAC is vulnerable to padding oracles).
  - Failing to recognize that unauthorized decryption violates Section 66 of the IT Act 2000.
