3. THE NEXT TECHNOLOGICAL FRONTIER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  * NIST 2024 POST-QUANTUM CRYPTOGRAPHY (PQC) STANDARDS:
    - FIPS 203 (ML-KEM / Kyber-768) : High-dimensional lattice key encapsulation.
    - FIPS 204 (ML-DSA / Dilithium) : High-dimensional lattice digital signatures.
    - Hybrid TLS 1.3 Migration      : `X25519Kyber768Draft00` combines classical and quantum keys.

  * FULLY HOMOMORPHIC ENCRYPTION (FHE):
    - Allows cloud AI servers to compute additions and multiplications directly on encrypted 
      ciphertexts (E(P1) * E(P2) = E(P1 * P2)) with ZERO decryption in cloud RAM.

  * CRYPTO-SHREDDING UNDER DPDP ACT 2023:
    - Destroying a user's unique 256-bit AES Data Encryption Key (DEK) in the KMS instantly renders 
      all multi-petabyte historical backups unreadable, fulfilling the Right to be Forgotten.

4. INDIAN STATUTORY COMPLIANCE & LEGAL FRAMEWORK
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

* CERT-IN 6-HOUR INCIDENT REPORTING DIRECTIVE (IT ACT SECTION 70B):
  - Mandatory reporting of private key compromises, ransomware, and data leaks to CERT-In within 6 hours.

* DIGITAL PERSONAL DATA PROTECTION (DPDP) ACT 2023:
  - Enforces up to ₹250 Crores in statutory penalties for inadequate cryptographic protection of personal data.

* INDIAN COPYRIGHT ACT 1957 SECTION 65A & 65B:
  - Up to 2 years imprisonment for circumvention of DRM (Section 65A) or removal of digital watermarks (Section 65B).

6. PROFESSIONAL TIPS & COMMON PITFALLS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

* PROFESSIONAL ARCHITECTURAL HABITS:
  1. Default to Hybrid Cryptosystems: Combine Asymmetric ECDH/PQC key agreement with Symmetric AES-GCM bulk data.
  2. Implement Crypto-Shredding in Cloud DBs: Dedicated per-user DEKs satisfy DPDP Act Right to be Forgotten.
  3. Deploy Hardware Security Modules (HSMs): Root master keys (KEKs) must reside inside FIPS 140-3 silicon.
  4. Enforce CERT-In 6-Hour Reporting: Maintain automated incident response runbooks for key breach reporting.

* COMMON BEGINNER DEPLOYMENT PITFALLS:
  - Storing master private keys in unencrypted source code repositories or environment variables.
  - Using software-only cryptography for banking PIN blocks (Violates RBI HSM mandates).
  - Believing lossy watermarking is safe for medical scans (Permanent pixel noise impairs clinical diagnosis).
  - Failing to plan for Post-Quantum migration (Leaving infrastructure vulnerable to 'Harvest Now, Decrypt Later').
