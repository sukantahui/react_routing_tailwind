1. EXECUTIVE OVERVIEW & THE TRIPARTITE CONTROL ARCHITECTURE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Information security cannot rely solely on software firewalls or paper policies. A resilient 
Information Security Management System (ISMS) deploys a balanced Tripartite Control Architecture 
providing Defense-in-Depth (DiD) across three complementary pillars:

  * 1. TECHNICAL CONTROLS      : Automated software & hardware mechanisms (AES-256, MFA, DLP, WAF).
  * 2. ADMINISTRATIVE CONTROLS : Human governance, policies, training, and procedures (JML, SETA).
  * 3. PHYSICAL CONTROLS       : Tangible barriers and facilities monitoring (Mantraps, CCTV, Shredding).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. FUNCTIONAL CONTROL CLASSIFICATION MATRIX
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Controls are also categorized by their operational timing and purpose:
  * 1. PREVENTIVE   : Blocks an incident before occurrence (MFA, Encryption, Firewalls, Locked Doors).
  * 2. DETECTIVE    : Identifies an ongoing or past attack (SIEM Alerts, AI CCTV, Audit Logs).
  * 3. CORRECTIVE   : Restores systems and remediates damage (DR Failover, Automated Patch Scripts).
  * 4. COMPENSATING : Alternative safeguard when primary is impossible (SCADA Bastion jump hosts).
  * 5. DETERRENT    : Discourages adversaries from attempting attacks (IT Act penalty warning banners).

5. STATUTORY SAFE HARBOR UNDER INDIAN CYBER LAW
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  * IT ACT 2000 SECTION 43A (SPDI RULES 2011):
    - Operating all three control pillars constitutes conclusive judicial proof of "Reasonable 
      Security Practices", providing complete Safe Harbor immunity in civil liability suits.
  * DPDP ACT 2023 SECTION 8 & SECTION 33:
    - Combines Administrative policies (DPO, notices) with Technical safeguards (Masking A.8.11, 
      DLP A.8.12) to shield the enterprise from ₹250 CRORE statutory penalties!
  * CERT-In DIRECTIONS 2022:
    - Enforces Administrative 6-hour escalation playbooks + Technical 180-day SIEM log retention.

7. PROFESSIONAL TIPS & COMMON PITFALLS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

* PROFESSIONAL TRIPARTITE CONTROL HABITS:
  1. Layer All Three Pillars: Never rely on software alone; balance with human policies and physical locks.
  2. Enforce Maker-Checker (A.5.3): Require dual authorization for all production changes and wire transfers.
  3. Deploy Compensating Controls for Legacy OT: Air-gap and record Bastion sessions for older SCADA devices.
  4. Perform NIST SP 800-88 Media Sanitization: Physically cross-cut decommissioned drives to < 2mm.

* COMMON BEGINNER CONTROL PITFALLS:
  - "Software-Only" Fallacy (Spending millions on firewalls while leaving physical server rooms unlocked).
  - "Paper Policy" Syndrome (Writing elaborate security binders that employees never read or execute).
  - Single Point of Failure (Assuming a single security layer can stop an advanced persistent threat).
  - Failing to sanitize decommissioned drives (Allowing data recovery from discarded magnetic media).
