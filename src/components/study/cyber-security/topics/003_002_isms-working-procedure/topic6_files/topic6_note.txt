1. EXECUTIVE OVERVIEW & THE INCIDENT RESPONSE LIFECYCLE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Security incidents are an inevitable reality of modern computing. An Information Security 
Management System (ISMS) must maintain rapid, repeatable, and legally compliant incident response 
capabilities under ISO/IEC 27001 (Controls A.5.24 to A.5.28) and NIST SP 800-61:

  * EVENT vs INCIDENT : An 'Event' is any observable occurrence (e.g. firewall ping); an 'Incident' 
                        is an unwanted event that threatens the Confidentiality, Integrity, or Availability.
  * THE LIFECYCLE     : Preparation ➔ Detection & Analysis ➔ Containment, Eradication & Recovery 
                        ➔ Post-Incident Activity & Lessons Learned.

3. INCIDENT SEVERITY MATRIX & ESCALATION SLA
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  * P1 CRITICAL (Enterprise Outage / Data Breach) :
    - Escalation: War room in 15m ➔ CISO & CEO alerted ➔ CERT-In reported in < 6 hours.
  * P2 HIGH (Single Server / Database Compromised) :
    - Escalation: Tier 3 Forensic Lead assigned ➔ Containment within 1 hour.
  * P3 MEDIUM (Isolated Phishing / Malware Blocked) :
    - Escalation: Tier 2 SOC Analyst assigned ➔ Triage within 4 hours.
  * P4 LOW (Minor Policy Violation / Port Scan) :
    - Escalation: Automated SOAR playbook closes ticket within 24 hours.

4. STATUTORY MANDATES UNDER INDIAN LAW
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  * IT ACT 2000 SECTION 70B (CERT-In DIRECTIONS 2022):
    - Mandatory duty to report 20 specified categories of cyber incidents (ransomware, leaks, 
      unauthorized access) to 'incident@cert-in.org.in' WITHIN 6 HOURS of noticing!
  * DPDP ACT 2023 SECTION 8(6) & SECTION 33:
    - Mandatory duty to notify the Data Protection Board of India (DPBI) and affected citizens 
      in the event of a personal data breach, shielding the firm from ₹250 Crore fines!
  * SECTION 65B OF INDIAN EVIDENCE ACT / BSA 2023:
    - Meticulous Chain of Custody + SHA-256 hashes + signed Section 65B Certificate ensures 
      all digital logs and memory dumps are 100% legally admissible in court.

5. FORENSIC EVIDENCE PRESERVATION PROTOCOL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  CRITICAL RULE: NEVER POWER DOWN OR REBOOT AN INFECTED SERVER IMMEDIATELY!
  1. Capture Volatile Memory (RAM) using LiME / DumpIt before rebooting.
  2. Disconnect Network Cable (Soft Isolation) to halt lateral spread.
  3. Capture Bit-by-Bit Disk Image (dd / FTK Imager) and record SHA-256 hash.
  4. Fill Chain of Custody Manifest and store image in encrypted offline vault.

7. PROFESSIONAL TIPS & COMMON PITFALLS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

* PROFESSIONAL INCIDENT RESPONSE HABITS:
  1. Use Out-of-Band Communications: Coordinate P1 war rooms on Signal, never corporate email.
  2. Dump Live RAM Before Shutdown: Preserve encryption keys and active malware sockets.
  3. Comply with the 6-Hour CERT-In SLA: Submit reports to incident@cert-in.org.in on time.
  4. Conduct Blameless Post-Mortems: Focus on architectural 5-Whys root causes rather than blaming staff.

* COMMON BEGINNER IR PITFALLS:
  - Rebooting infected machines immediately (Destroys all volatile forensic RAM evidence).
  - Discussing active breaches on corporate email (Allows the attacker to read your containment plans).
  - Concealing data breaches (Violates DPDP Act Section 8(6) -> triggers ₹250 Cr statutory fines).
  - Failing to maintain Chain of Custody (Renders digital evidence inadmissible in court).
