--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. DEFINITION & LAYER 2 HARDWARE IDENTIFICATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A Media Access Control (MAC) Address—also known as a Physical Address, Hardware
Address, or Burned-In Address (BIA)—is a globally unique 48-bit (6-byte) permanent
identifier etched into the non-volatile EEPROM/ROM of every Network Interface Card
(NIC) by the hardware manufacturer.

Key Operational Principles:
• Layer 2 Flat Addressing: Operates strictly at OSI Layer 2 (Data Link Layer),
  delivering Ethernet frames locally within the same broadcast domain/VLAN.
• Non-Hierarchical: Unlike IP addresses, MAC addresses do not specify network locations;
  they identify physical hardware endpoints.
• Hop-by-Hop Rewriting: While source/destination IP addresses remain constant end-to-end,
  MAC addresses are stripped and rewritten at every intermediate router hop.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. ANATOMY & 48-BIT STRUCTURE OF A MAC ADDRESS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A 48-bit MAC address consists of 12 hexadecimal characters (e.g., 00:1A:2B:3C:4D:5E).

1. First 24 Bits (3 Octets) - Organizationally Unique Identifier (OUI):
   - Assigned by the IEEE Registration Authority to hardware vendors.
   - Examples: Cisco (00:00:0C), Intel (00:1A:2B), Apple (00:03:93), VMware (00:50:56).

2. Last 24 Bits (3 Octets) - Vendor-Assigned Device Serial:
   - Unique device serial number assigned by the manufacturer to that specific NIC.

3. First Octet Bit Flags:
   - Bit 0 (I/G Bit): 0 = Unicast (Individual Node), 1 = Multicast / Broadcast (Group).
   - Bit 1 (U/L Bit): 0 = Globally Unique (IEEE OUI), 1 = Locally Administered (LAA/Randomized).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. SWITCH CAM TABLE & FRAME FORWARDING LOGIC
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Switches maintain a Content Addressable Memory (CAM) table mapping MAC addresses to
physical switch ports:
1. Learning: Switch inspects the Source MAC of incoming frames and records [MAC -> Ingress Port].
2. Forwarding: If the Destination MAC exists in the CAM table, the frame is forwarded
   directly to the learned port.
3. Unknown Unicast Flooding: If the Destination MAC is unlearned, the frame is flooded
   to all active ports on the VLAN (except the ingress port) until a reply is received.
4. Aging Timer: Inactive entries are purged after 300 seconds (5 minutes).

4. FOUR REAL-WORLD BENGAL CASE STUDIES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

CASE STUDY 1: Precision Foundry Switch Port Security Lockdown (Debangshu - Barrackpore)
Debangshu configured `switchport port-security maximum 1` and `mac-address sticky` across
48 managed switch ports in Barrackpore for ₹42,000. When an unauthorized laptop was attached,
the port immediately shut down, preventing rogue device access to factory telemetry.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CASE STUDY 2: Diagnostic Clinic Medical Cart Dynamic MAC VLANs (Mahima - Ichapur)
Mahima configured 802.1X MAC Authentication Bypass (MAB) on diagnostic carts in Ichapur.
The RADIUS server identifies medical device MAC OUIs, automatically assigning them to an
isolated HIPAA-compliant VLAN without manual configuration.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CASE STUDY 3: University Campus Public Wi-Fi MAC Randomization (Mamata - Kolkata)
Mamata configured the campus captive portal in Kolkata to support iOS/Android private
MAC randomization. The system authenticates students via 802.1X user credentials rather
than hardware MACs, protecting student privacy across university grounds.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CASE STUDY 4: High-Tech Cyber Lab MAC Flooding & DAI Defense (Abhronila - Jadavpur)
Abhronila simulated a MAC flooding attack (100,000 fake MACs) in Jadavpur using `macof`.
Managed core switches equipped with Dynamic ARP Inspection (DAI) and Port Security
blocked the overflow in 12ms, preventing CAM exhaustion and packet sniffing.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. COMMON PITFALLS & PRACTICAL ADVICE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Never rely on Wi-Fi MAC filtering alone for security; wireless MACs are easily sniffed and spoofed.
- Enable Switch Port Security (`maximum 1` / `violation shutdown`) to stop MAC flooding attacks.
- Recognize that MAC addresses change at every router hop; only IP addresses remain constant end-to-end.
- State all managed switch hardware and 802.1X licensing budgets in Indian Rupees (₹).
