1. DEFINITIONS & ARCHITECTURAL COMPARISON
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
• The Internet: A global, public, decentralized collection of interconnected networks
  and Autonomous Systems (ASNs) operating over public BGP, public DNS, and public IPv4/IPv6
  addressing. Untrusted security zone with open accessibility.
• The Intranet: A private, restricted, organizational network reserved exclusively for
  authenticated internal employees. Utilizes RFC 1918 private addressing, internal DNS,
  and perimeter firewalls. Trusted security zone.

Detailed Architectural Matrix:
Dimension            | Public Internet                  | Private Intranet
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Accessibility        | Public (Anyone worldwide)        | Private (Authorized Staff Only)
Addressing           | Public Routable IPv4 / IPv6      | RFC 1918 Private (10.0, 172.16, 192.168)
Routing Protocols    | BGP (Border Gateway Protocol)    | OSPF, EIGRP, Static Routing (IGP)
Trust Level          | Untrusted (Zero-Trust Required)  | Controlled / Microsegmented Trust
Typical Speed        | 10 Mbps – 1 Gbps (Variable)      | 1 Gbps – 100 Gbps (Local LAN Fabric)
Latency & Jitter     | 15 ms – 300 ms (Multi-hop WAN)   | < 1 ms (Deterministic Switch Fabric)
Ownership & Cost     | Distributed / ISP Transit Fees   | Direct Organizational Asset Ownership

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. 3-TIER PERIMETER DEMARCATION & DMZ ARCHITECTURE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Security Zones:
1. Untrusted Zone: The Public Internet.
2. Semi-Trusted Zone (DMZ / Screened Subnet): Hosts Internet-facing services
   (Web Servers, Reverse Proxies, Mail Relays, Public DNS, Bastion Hosts).
3. Trusted Zone: The Internal Intranet (Core Databases, ERP, HR Payroll, File Shares).

Perimeter Defense Mechanisms:
• Dual-Firewall Setup: External Firewall screens Internet traffic into the DMZ; Internal
  Firewall screens DMZ traffic into the trusted Intranet.
• Network Address Translation (NAT/PAT): Translates internal RFC 1918 IPs into public IPs.
• Split-Horizon DNS: Returns public IPs to Internet clients and private IPs to internal staff.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. MODERN INTRANET SECURITY: FROM CASTLE-AND-MOAT TO ZERO TRUST (ZTNA)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Legacy "Castle-and-Moat" models trusted everything inside the internal network once authenticated.
Modern Intranets enforce Zero Trust Network Access (ZTNA):
• Least-Privilege Access: Users are granted access only to specific applications, not the full network.
• Microsegmentation: Internal firewalls and VLANs block unauthorized East-West lateral movement.
• 802.1X Port Security: Physical switch wall ports require RADIUS authentication.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. FOUR REAL-WORLD BENGAL CASE STUDIES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

CASE STUDY 1: Precision Foundry DMZ Reverse Proxy Deployment (Debangshu - Barrackpore)
Debangshu isolated ERP database clusters from the Internet in Barrackpore by deploying an NGINX
Reverse Proxy in the DMZ with dual FortiGate firewalls for ₹1,85,000. Customer portal requests
are terminated in the DMZ, completely blocking direct external access to internal database ports.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CASE STUDY 2: Diagnostic Clinic Healthcare Intranet Microsegmentation (Mahima - Ichapur)
Mahima implemented VLAN microsegmentation on hospital intranet switches in Ichapur for ₹65,000.
Radiology MRI machines, patient billing workstations, and doctor consultation tablets are isolated
into dedicated VLANs with strict firewall rules, stopping malware propagation across departments.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CASE STUDY 3: University Campus Split-Horizon DNS & ZTNA Rollout (Mamata - Kolkata)
Mamata configured BIND Split-Horizon DNS and Zero Trust application access in Kolkata for ₹85,000.
Faculty accessing `payroll.univ.edu` receive internal private IPs on campus and must pass MFA
device compliance checks when connecting remotely, eliminating vulnerable legacy VPN gateways.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CASE STUDY 4: Cyber Security Lab Air-Gap USB Defense & Honeypots (Abhronila - Jadavpur)
Abhronila deployed internal honeypot sensors and automated USB isolation kiosks in Jadavpur
for ₹45,000. When a student plugged in an infected USB drive, the honeypot detected port scan
activity within 80 milliseconds, triggering an immediate switch port shutdown before lateral spread.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. COMMON PITFALLS & PRACTICAL ADVICE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Never place internal database or active directory servers directly in the DMZ.
- Do not rely on flat, unsegmented internal networks; enforce microsegmentation and ZTNA.
- Configure Split-Horizon DNS to prevent leaking internal server hostnames to the public.
- Budget perimeter Next-Gen Firewalls and UTM licenses in Indian Rupees (₹).
