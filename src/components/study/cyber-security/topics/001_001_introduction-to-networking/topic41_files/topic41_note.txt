1. HOSPITAL INFORMATION SYSTEMS & CLINICAL DATA PROTOCOLS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
• DICOM (Digital Imaging and Communications in Medicine):
  Standard radiological imaging protocol (MRI, X-ray, 3D CT scans) operating over TCP Port 104.
• PACS (Picture Archiving and Communication System):
  Centralized medical imaging archive communicating over 10G/40G SAN networks using
  lossless Jumbo Frames (MTU 9000) to deliver 2GB image series in under 4 seconds.
• HL7 & FHIR (Fast Healthcare Interoperability Resources):
  Modern RESTful JSON/XML APIs operating over HTTPS (Port 443) with Mutual TLS (mTLS),
  exchanging electronic health records (EHR), lab orders, and patient vital telemetry.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. INTERNET OF MEDICAL THINGS (IOMT) & BIOMEDICAL ISOLATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
• Biomedical Network Segmentation:
  Life-support hardware (infusion pumps, ICU ventilators, dialysis units, anesthesia consoles)
  must be isolated into dedicated, firewall-restricted VLANs (e.g. VLAN 50) with strict
  access control lists (ACLs) to prevent lateral malware propagation from office PCs.
• Mobile Medical Cart Fast-Roaming (802.11r / 802.11k):
  Enables mobile nursing workstations to roam across hospital corridor Wi-Fi APs in < 50ms
  without dropping active patient electronic charting sessions.
• 5G Smart Ambulance Telemetry:
  Streams 12-lead ECG, ultrasound video, and patient vital signs directly to the hospital
  Emergency Room trauma team over encrypted IPsec tunnels before arrival.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. HEALTHCARE CYBER SECURITY THREATS & COMPLIANCE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
• Hospital Ransomware Defense (AIIMS Delhi & WannaCry lessons):
  Requires strict network microsegmentation, immutable air-gapped WORM offline backups,
  and Endpoint Detection and Response (EDR) agents on all clinical terminals.
• Medjack (Medical Device Hijacking):
  Mitigated by Deep Packet Inspection (DPI) and virtual firewall patching on medical subnets.
• HIPAA & DISHA Compliance:
  Mandates AES-256 GCM encryption at rest and in transit for all Protected Health Information (PHI).

4. FOUR REAL-WORLD BENGAL CASE STUDIES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

CASE STUDY 1: Multi-Speciality Hospital 10G PACS & Jumbo Frame Rollout (Debangshu - Barrackpore)
Debangshu upgraded hospital radiology networks in Barrackpore with dual 10G optical switches and
9000-byte Jumbo Frames for ₹6,50,000. 1.8GB MRI multi-slice image series load on doctor diagnostic
workstations in 3.6 seconds, accelerating critical stroke triage decisions.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CASE STUDY 2: Diagnostic Clinic IoMT Infusion Pump Microsegmentation (Mahima - Ichapur)
Mahima implemented strict VLAN microsegmentation and Next-Gen Firewall policies for 80 smart
infusion pumps and patient monitors in Ichapur for ₹2,10,000. Isolating medical devices from the
general hospital office subnet blocked lateral scanning and unauthorized firmware tampering.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CASE STUDY 3: State Health Tele-ICU & 5G Ambulance Telemetry (Mamata - Kolkata)
Mamata engineered a dedicated 5G cellular IPsec and WebRTC tele-ICU network in Kolkata for ₹4,80,000.
Senior intensivist doctors in Kolkata monitor rural district ICU ventilators and review live ECG
waveforms from transit ambulances in real time with sub-15ms latency.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CASE STUDY 4: Cyber Security Lab Medical Device DPI & Ransomware Audit (Abhronila - Jadavpur)
Abhronila audited medical IoT security in Jadavpur for ₹1,15,000 using Deep Packet Inspection (DPI).
DPI detected and neutralized an unpatched buffer overflow vulnerability in legacy blood analyzer
firmware, establishing an air-gapped immutable backup vault for patient records.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. COMMON PITFALLS & PRACTICAL ADVICE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Never place unpatchable medical devices (ventilators, infusion pumps) on open office subnets.
- Always configure 9000-byte Jumbo Frames on dedicated PACS imaging storage switch ports.
- Maintain immutable air-gapped offline backups to guarantee rapid recovery from ransomware.
- Formulate all hospital PACS, IoMT firewalls, and medical Wi-Fi budgets in Indian Rupees (₹).
