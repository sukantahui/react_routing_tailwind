1. CORE TENETS OF ZERO TRUST (NIST SP 800-207)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Zero Trust Architecture (ZTA) replaces the obsolete "Castle-and-Moat" perimeter model with a continuous, 
session-based, dynamic authorization doctrine: "Never Trust, Always Verify; Assume Breach."

The 7 Foundational Tenets (NIST SP 800-207 Section 2.1):
1. All data sources and computing services are considered resources.
2. All communication is secured regardless of network location (internal corporate LAN is as untrusted as public internet).
3. Access to individual enterprise resources is granted on a per-session basis.
4. Access to resources is determined by dynamic policy (including client identity, application, device posture, and environmental attributes).
5. The enterprise monitors and measures the integrity and security posture of all owned and associated assets continuously.
6. All resource authentication and authorization are dynamic and strictly enforced before access is allowed.
7. The enterprise collects as much information as possible about the current state of assets, network infrastructure, 
   and communications and uses it to improve its security posture.


2. ARCHITECTURAL COMPONENTS: PDP & PEP
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Policy Decision Point (PDP):
   - Policy Engine (PE): The mathematical brain responsible for deciding whether to grant, deny, or revoke access to a resource based on enterprise policies and continuous risk scoring.
   - Policy Administrator (PA): Responsible for establishing or shutting down the communication channel between Subject and Resource by commanding the PEP (e.g., issuing ephemeral mTLS credentials).

2. Policy Enforcement Point (PEP):
   - A distributed proxy/gateway (e.g., Envoy / Cloudflare Tunnel / Zscaler ZPA) that intercepts, inspects, and terminates live connections, strictly enforcing the PA's instructions.


3. MICRO-SEGMENTATION & SOFTWARE-DEFINED PERIMETER (SDP)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Micro-Segmentation: Dividing network workloads into granular, cryptographically isolated zones down to individual microservices and containers, preventing lateral movement.
- Software-Defined Perimeter (SDP): Hiding internal servers behind an invisible default-deny boundary; servers have no public listening ports ($0.0.0.0:0$) and become accessible ONLY via dynamic mutual TLS tunnels established after successful PDP evaluation.


4. CONTINUOUS ADAPTIVE RISK EVALUATION
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
$$\text{Trust Score} = w_1 \cdot \text{Identity} + w_2 \cdot \text{DeviceHealth} + w_3 \cdot \text{BehaviorRisk} + w_4 \cdot \text{Context}$$
- If a user's device disables EDR or initiates abnormal file downloads mid-session, the PDP immediately terminates the connection without waiting for session expiration.


5. REGIONAL SOC CASE STUDIES (WEST BENGAL DEPLOYMENTS)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Case 1: Barrackpore Municipal Treasury Micro-Segmentation
- Architecture: Replaced internal flat VLANs with Zero Trust micro-segmentation across 40 department subnets.
- Result: Contained a localized ransomware outbreak to a single testing workstation; 0.0% lateral spread to treasury databases.

Case 2: Salt Lake Sector V FinTech Continuous PDP Authorization
- Architecture: Integrated Microsoft Entra Continuous Access Evaluation (CAE) with CrowdStrike EDR signals.
- Result: Sub-second session revocation upon impossible travel or malware execution; zero credential reuse.
