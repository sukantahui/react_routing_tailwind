1. THE QUANTUM COMPUTING THREAT LANDSCAPE
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Classical computers use binary bits ($0$ or $1$). Quantum computers leverage Quantum Mechanics principles:
- Superposition: A qubit state $|\psi\rangle = \alpha|0\rangle + \beta|1\rangle$ exists in a continuum of states.
- Entanglement: Non-local correlation between qubits ($|\Phi^+\rangle = \frac{|00\rangle + |11\rangle}{\sqrt{2}}$).
- Quantum Interference: Constructive interference amplifies the correct mathematical solution while destructive 
  interference cancels out wrong answers.

Peter Shor proved that quantum computers solve the Hidden Subgroup Problem in polynomial time:

1. Targeted Mathematical Problems:

3. GROVER'S ALGORITHM (1996): IMPACT ON SYMMETRIC CIPHERS & HASHES
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Lov Grover demonstrated a quantum search algorithm providing a quadratic speedup for unstructured databases:
- Search complexity reduced from $O(N)$ to $O(\sqrt{N})$.
- Effective Key Security: An $n$-bit symmetric key provides only $\frac{n}{2}$ bits of quantum security.
- Impact on AES:
  • AES-128 → 64-bit quantum security (Vulnerable to brute-force 🚨).
  • AES-256 → 128-bit quantum security (QUANTUM SAFE ✔).
- Impact on Hash Functions:
  • SHA-256 (Collision Resistance via BHT algorithm) $→ 2^{256/3} \approx 2^{85}$ (Use SHA-384 or SHA-512).
