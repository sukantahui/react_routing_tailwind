1. PHYSICAL FOUNDATIONS OF QUANTUM KEY DISTRIBUTION (QKD)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Unlike Post-Quantum Cryptography (PQC), which relies on unproven mathematical computational complexity 
(e.g., Lattice problems), QKD security is guaranteed by the fundamental Laws of Quantum Physics:

1. Heisenberg Uncertainty Principle:
   - Measuring one observable of a quantum state unavoidably perturbs conjugate observables.
   - Any eavesdropper (Eve) attempting to measure a photon in flight irreversibly disturbs its quantum state.

2. No-Cloning Theorem:
   - An unknown quantum state cannot be cloned or copied without measuring (and disturbing) it.
   - Eve cannot intercept a photon, copy it, and forward the original to Bob.


2. THE BB84 PROTOCOL STEP-BY-STEP (BENNETT & BRASSARD 1984)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Preparation (Alice):
   - Alice generates a random bit string (0 or 1) and selects a random measurement basis:
     • Rectilinear Basis ($+$): $|0^\circ\rangle = 0$, $|90^\circ\rangle = 1$.
     • Diagonal Basis ($\times$): $|45^\circ\rangle = 0$, $|135^\circ\rangle = 1$.
   - Alice emits single polarized photons across the quantum optical fiber channel.

2. Measurement (Bob):
   - Bob independently and randomly chooses a basis ($+$ or $\times$) to measure each incoming photon.
   - If Bob chooses the same basis as Alice, he gets the exact bit Alice sent.
   - If Bob chooses the wrong basis, quantum collapse causes him to measure 0 or 1 with 50% random probability.

3. Sifting Phase (Public Authenticated Classical Channel):
   - Alice and Bob announce their chosen bases over a classical authenticated channel (NOT the bit values!).
   - They discard all bits where their bases did not match, retaining the Sifted Key ($\approx 50\%$ of transmitted bits).

4. Parameter Estimation & Quantum Bit Error Rate (QBER):
   - Alice and Bob compare a random sample subset of their sifted bits to calculate the error rate:
     $$\text{QBER} = \frac{\text{Number of Erroneous Bits}}{\text{Total Sample Bits}}$$
   - Normal Optical Fiber Noise: $\text{QBER} \approx 1\% - 3\%$.
   - Eavesdropper (Intercept-Resend Attack): Eve chooses wrong bases 50% of the time, inducing an average 
     $\text{QBER} = 25\%$.
   - Security Threshold (Shor-Preskill Bound): If $\text{QBER} > 11.0\%$, they ABORT the exchange!

5. Error Correction (Cascade / LDPC) & Privacy Amplification (Toeplitz Universal Hashing):
   - Reconciles minor optical noise and compresses the sifted key into a final symmetric One-Time Pad (OTP) 
     or AES-256 key, completely eliminating any partial information Eve might have intercepted.


3. QKD HARDWARE ARCHITECTURE & PHYSICAL CHALLENGES
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Distance Limits: Single-photon optical fiber attenuation limits point-to-point QKD to $\sim 100 - 150\text{ km}$ 
  without Quantum Repeaters.
- Trusted Node Networks: Long-distance QKD relies on secure physical bunkers (Trusted Relays) or Satellite QKD 
  (e.g., Chinese Micius satellite / ISRO quantum payloads).
- Single-Photon Light Source Vulnerability (Photon Number Splitting - PNS):
  • Weak coherent laser pulses occasionally emit 2 or more identical photons in a single pulse.
  • Eve splits off 1 photon and lets the second pass undisturbed (PNS Attack).
  • Mitigation: Decoy State Protocol (interspersing pulses of varying photon intensities).
