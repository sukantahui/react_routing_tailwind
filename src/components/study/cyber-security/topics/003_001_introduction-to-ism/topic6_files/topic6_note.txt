1. EXECUTIVE OVERVIEW & STATUTORY COMPLIANCE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Information Security Management is no longer an optional IT best practice—it is a legally binding 
statutory mandate governed by strict national and global regulatory frameworks:

  * THE REALITY   : Non-compliance exposes an enterprise to catastrophic financial penalties 
                    (up to ₹250 CRORES under the Indian DPDP Act 2023), mandatory 6-hour incident 
                    reporting under CERT-In, and personal criminal liability for corporate directors.
  * THE PARADIGM  : Maintaining continuous, verifiable technical and organizational compliance across 
                    statutory frameworks (DPDP, IT Act, CERT-In, RBI, ISO 27001).

3. GLOBAL REGULATORY & INDUSTRY STANDARDS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  * GDPR (EU 2016/679) : Articles 32 (Technical Safeguards), 33 (72h Breach Notice), 
                         83 (Fines up to €20 Million or 4% of Global Annual Turnover).
  * PCI-DSS v4.0       : 12 Mandatory Requirements for protecting Cardholder Data (AES-256, MFA, 
                         no CVV storage post-authorization, quarterly ASV scans).
  * ISO/IEC 27001:2022 : International ISMS specification requiring a formal Statement of 
                         Applicability (SoA) mapping 93 Annex A security controls.
  * HIPAA (US)         : Security & Privacy Rules governing Protected Health Information (PHI).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. THE STATEMENT OF APPLICABILITY (SoA / ISO 27001)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The Statement of Applicability (SoA) is the non-negotiable master artifact of an ISO 27001 audit:
  - Details all 93 controls from Annex A (Organizational, People, Physical, Technological).
  - Documents whether each control is APPLICABLE (Yes/No).
  - Provides the formal business/legal justification for inclusion.
  - Documents current implementation status and justifies any excluded controls.

6. DIGITAL EVIDENCE ADMISSIBILITY (SECTION 65B CERTIFICATES)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Under Section 65B of the Indian Evidence Act (Section 63 Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam):
  - Computer printouts, audit logs, and SIEM records are admissible ONLY when accompanied by a 
    signed Section 65B Certificate by the lawful system custodian.
  - Must state the computer operated properly and verify tamper-proof SHA-256 cryptographic hashes.

8. PROFESSIONAL TIPS & COMMON PITFALLS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

* PROFESSIONAL COMPLIANCE HABITS:
  1. Automate CERT-In 6-Hour Escalation: Conduct unannounced drills to ensure incident reporting in < 6 hours.
  2. Maintain an Updated SoA: Review the Statement of Applicability before every change cycle.
  3. Generate Section 65B Evidence Certificates: Hash and archive SIEM logs with SHA-256 for court readiness.
  4. Enforce DPDP Section 8 Safeguards: Deploy automated data minimization and storage limitation purges.

* COMMON BEGINNER COMPLIANCE PITFALLS:
  - Treating compliance as a once-a-year paper audit (Compliance is continuous!).
  - Failing to synchronize server clocks with Indian Standard Time NTP servers (Violates CERT-In rules).
  - Storing CVVs post-authorization (Direct violation of PCI-DSS Requirement 3).
  - Assuming the corporate veil protects Directors from IT Act Section 85 criminal liability.
