1. EXECUTIVE OVERVIEW & THE SUPPLY CHAIN THREAT VECTOR
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
No modern enterprise is an isolated island. Organizations connect to hundreds of external cloud vendors, 
APIs, contractors, and thousands of open-source software dependencies. Under ISO/IEC 27001:2022 
Controls A.5.19–A.5.22 and ISO 27036, Third-Party and Supply Chain Risk Management (TPRM) establishes 
the defensive perimeter around the external ecosystem:

  * THE VULNERABILITY  : Hackers bypass fortified perimeters by compromising trusted third-party suppliers.
  * THE METHODOLOGY    : 3-Tier Vendor Risk Classification + Automated Software Bill of Materials (SBOM).
  * THE STATUTORY RULE : DPDP Act Sec 8(2) holds the Data Fiduciary 100% liable for vendor breaches (₹250 Cr)!

3. SOFTWARE SUPPLY CHAIN SECURITY & SBOM STANDARDS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  * SOFTWARE BILL OF MATERIALS (SBOM) :
    - Standardized, machine-readable inventory of all direct and transitive dependencies 
      formatted in CycloneDX (OWASP) or SPDX (Linux Foundation).
  * SOFTWARE COMPOSITION ANALYSIS (SCA) :
    - Automated CI/CD tools (Snyk, Dependency-Check) that block builds containing Critical CVEs.
  * CRYPTOGRAPHIC PROVENANCE :
    - Code signing of container images and binaries using Cosign / Sigstore to prevent build tampering.

4. STATUTORY SAFE HARBOR UNDER INDIAN CYBER LAW
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  * DPDP ACT 2023 SECTION 8(2) & SECTION 33 :
    - Data Fiduciaries remain strictly vicariously liable for personal data breaches caused by 
      third-party Data Processors; vendor negligence triggers ₹250 CRORE fines on the Fiduciary!
  * RESERVE BANK OF INDIA (RBI) MASTER DIRECTIONS ON OUTSOURCING :
    - 100% Indian Data Residency for payment data; RBI inspectors maintain direct audit rights over vendors.
  * CERT-In DIRECTIONS 2022 (IT ACT SEC 70B) :
    - Mandatory statutory reporting of third-party supply chain compromises within 6 HOURS.

6. PROFESSIONAL TIPS & COMMON PITFALLS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

* PROFESSIONAL TPRM HABITS:
  1. Enforce 24-Hour Breach Notification: Require third-party vendors to notify you in < 24 hours.
  2. Mandate Machine-Readable SBOMs: Require CycloneDX or SPDX SBOMs for all third-party software.
  3. Automate Vendor Offboarding: Revoke OAuth tokens, API keys, and VPN logins within 1 hour of contract end.
  4. Block Vulnerable Pull Requests: Integrate SCA into CI/CD to prevent Log4j-style library merges.

* COMMON BEGINNER TPRM PITFALLS:
  - The "Trusted Vendor" Assumption (Assuming big-name vendors are automatically immune to cyber attacks).
  - Ignoring Transitive Dependencies (Focusing on direct libraries while ignoring 400 nested packages).
  - Spreadsheet Vendor Surveys (Relying on self-attested Word docs without verified SOC 2 Type II audits).
  - Orphaned Vendor Logins (Leaving third-party contractor accounts active months after contract expiry).
