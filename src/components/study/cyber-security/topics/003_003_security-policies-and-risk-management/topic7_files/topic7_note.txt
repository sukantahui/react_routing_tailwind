1. EXECUTIVE OVERVIEW & THE ANALYSIS METHODOLOGY CROSSROADS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Risk analysis is the scientific process of evaluating the likelihood and consequences of identified 
cyber threats. Organizations must choose between, or strategically combine, two primary methodologies:

  * QUALITATIVE ANALYSIS : Descriptive severity scales (Low, Medium, High) ➔ Fast & Intuitive.
  * QUANTITATIVE ANALYSIS: Monetary values in Rupees (₹) & Percentages (%) ➔ Financially Defensible.
  * HYBRID PARADIGM      : Use 5x5 qualitative heatmaps for broad triage; conduct FAIR quantitative 
                           modeling for top-tier critical assets requiring multi-lakh budgets!

3. THE 5X5 QUALITATIVE RISK HEATMAP MATRIX
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  * FORMULA : Risk Score = Likelihood (1 to 5) x Impact (1 to 5)  [Scores 1 to 25].
  * SEVERITY BANDS :
    - Scores 1 - 4   : LOW RISK      ➔ Acceptable / Monitor periodically.
    - Scores 5 - 9   : MEDIUM RISK   ➔ Standard operational patch SLA (< 30 days).
    - Scores 10 - 16 : HIGH RISK     ➔ Priority remediation SLA (< 14 days).
    - Scores 20 - 25 : CRITICAL RISK ➔ Immediate escalation to CISO (< 24 hours).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. QUANTITATIVE FAIR & ALE FINANCIAL EQUATIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  * Single Loss Expectancy (SLE)   : $$SLE = Asset Value (AV) \times Exposure Factor (EF)$$
  * Annual Loss Expectancy (ALE)   : $$ALE = SLE \times Annual Rate of Occurrence (ARO)$$
  * Return on Security Investment  : $$ROSI = \frac{(ALE_{before} - ALE_{after}) - Control Cost}{Control Cost} \times 100$$

7. PROFESSIONAL TIPS & COMMON PITFALLS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

* PROFESSIONAL RISK ANALYSIS HABITS:
  1. Use Qualitative for Fast Triage: Screen 1,000 assets quickly using a 5x5 heatmap.
  2. Use Quantitative for CFO Pitches: Present Rupee ALE and ROSI when requesting security budgets.
  3. Use Delphi for Consensus: Eliminate dominant personality bias in qualitative scoring workshops.
  4. Model Tail Risk with Monte Carlo: Run probability distributions for rare catastrophic cyber events.

* COMMON BEGINNER ANALYSIS PITFALLS:
  - Pitching Colors to the CFO (Expecting executives to fund projects based on a 'red box' without numbers).
  - The "Infinite Accuracy" Trap (Spending 6 months collecting data for a ₹10,000 internal asset).
  - Subjective Inconsistency (Allowing two different teams to define 'High Risk' in conflicting ways).
  - Ignoring Control Costs in ROSI (Calculating savings without subtracting ongoing software license fees).
