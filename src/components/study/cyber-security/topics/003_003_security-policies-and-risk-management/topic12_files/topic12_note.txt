1. EXECUTIVE OVERVIEW & THE SHIFT-LEFT PHILOSOPHY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Threat Modeling is the proactive engineering discipline of analyzing software architecture and data flows 
to identify and neutralize security vulnerabilities BEFORE writing code. Under ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Control 
A.8.25 (Secure Development Lifecycle), threat modeling answers four fundamental architectural questions:

  * QUESTION 1 : What are we building?      ➔ Data Flow Diagrams (DFDs) & Trust Boundaries.
  * QUESTION 2 : What can go wrong?         ➔ STRIDE Threat Categorization.
  * QUESTION 3 : What are we going to do?   ➔ Engineering Countermeasures & Cryptographic Controls.
  * QUESTION 4 : Did we do a good job?      ➔ Automated Unit Verification & Penetration Testing.

3. THE DREAD QUANTITATIVE THREAT RATING MODEL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  * MATHEMATICAL FORMULA :
    $$DREAD Score = \frac{Damage + Reproducibility + Exploitability + Affected Users + Discoverability}{5}$$
  * SEVERITY BANDS (Scores 1.0 to 10.0) :
    - 8.0 - 10.0 : CRITICAL RISK ➔ Emergency architectural fix; blocks production build.
    - 6.0 - 7.9  : HIGH RISK     ➔ Priority sprint remediation ticket (< 14 days).
    - 4.0 - 5.9  : MEDIUM RISK   ➔ Standard backlog remediation item (< 30 days).
    - 1.0 - 3.9  : LOW RISK      ➔ Monitored during routine maintenance cycles.

6. PROFESSIONAL TIPS & COMMON PITFALLS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

* PROFESSIONAL THREAT MODELING HABITS:
  1. Model Trust Boundaries: Focus STRIDE analysis on perimeters where untrusted data enters trusted zones.
  2. Never Trust Client Roles: Always verify RBAC and authorization parameters on the server side.
  3. Assume Discoverability is 10: In the modern era of automated mass scanners, every vulnerability is findable.
  4. Integrate Threat Models in CI/CD: Re-evaluate threat models whenever architectural DFDs change.

* COMMON BEGINNER THREAT MODELING PITFALLS:
  - "Security by Obscurity" (Assuming hackers won't find an unauthenticated internal endpoint).
  - Post-Production Modeling (Threat modeling after the software is already coded and deployed).
  - Focusing Only on Software (Ignoring network trust boundaries and cloud infrastructure flows).
  - Unprioritized STRIDE Lists (Creating a list of 500 threats without using DREAD to prioritize fixes).
