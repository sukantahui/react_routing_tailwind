1. EXECUTIVE OVERVIEW & THE 4 RISK TREATMENT PILLARS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Evaluating risk is only half the battle; the ultimate goal of Information Security Management is 
treating risk. Under ISO/IEC 27005:2022 Clause 9 and ISO 31000, organizations must select and execute 
one of four structured risk treatment options to align residual risk with Board Risk Appetite:

  * 1. RISK MITIGATION (Reduction)   ➔ Apply technical/administrative/physical security controls.
  * 2. RISK TRANSFER   (Sharing)     ➔ Shift financial loss to cyber insurance or vendor SLAs.
  * 3. RISK AVOIDANCE  (Termination) ➔ Eliminate the risk by discontinuing the risky activity.
  * 4. RISK ACCEPTANCE (Retention)   ➔ Formally retain residual risk with signed executive approval.

3. RISK TREATMENT PLAN (RTP) & STATEMENT OF APPLICABILITY (SOA)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Under ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Clause 6.1.3, every risk exceeding appetite must be documented in an RTP:
  * Risk Treatment Plan (RTP) :
    - Specifies Risk ID, Selected Treatment Option, Assigned Owner, Implementation SLA, and Budget.
  * Statement of Applicability (SoA) :
    - Links all 93 Annex A controls to the RTP, providing formal audit justification for inclusions.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. STATUTORY SAFE HARBOR UNDER INDIAN CYBER LAW
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  * DPDP ACT 2023 SECTION 8 & SECTION 33 :
    - Data Fiduciaries must implement mandatory mitigation safeguards for personal data; 
      failing to treat known personal data risks triggers up to ₹250 CRORE statutory fines!
  * IRDAI CYBER INSURANCE GUIDELINES :
    - Regulates enterprise cyber insurance policies in India, governing first-party response 
      reimbursement and third-party legal liability coverage.
  * IT ACT 2000 SECTION 43A :
    - Documented RTP and SoA prove "Reasonable Security Practices" in civil breach litigation.

6. PROFESSIONAL TIPS & COMMON PITFALLS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

* PROFESSIONAL TREATMENT HABITS:
  1. Link RTP to SoA: Ensure every Annex A control in the SoA cites a specific risk in the RTP.
  2. Remember Accountability is Non-Transferable: Insurance reimburses money, not regulatory blame.
  3. Enforce 12-Month Expiry on Acceptance: Re-evaluate accepted risks annually during Management Review.
  4. Use Compensatory Controls: Always deploy isolation safeguards when accepting legacy system risks.

* COMMON BEGINNER TREATMENT PITFALLS:
  - "Risk Ignoring" (Confusing doing nothing with formal, documented Risk Acceptance).
  - The "Insurance Silver Bullet" (Believing cyber insurance excuses an enterprise from patching CVEs).
  - Verbal Risk Acceptance (Accepting risks without signed executive justification from the CISO).
  - Infinite Mitigation (Spending ₹50 Lakhs to protect an asset with a ₹10,000 annual loss expectancy).

7. STUDENT MINI CHECKLIST (POINTS TO REMEMBER FOR EXAMS & INTERVIEWS)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[ ] 4 Treatment Options: Mitigate (Reduce), Transfer (Share), Avoid (Stop), Accept (Retain).
[ ] Mitigation deploys Technical, Administrative, and Physical controls under ISO 27001 Annex A.
[ ] Risk Transfer shifts financial loss (Insurance/SLAs), but legal accountability is NEVER transferred.
[ ] Risk Avoidance terminates the risky activity (e.g. payment tokenization avoids raw card storage).
[ ] Risk Acceptance requires written justification, CISO sign-off, and a mandatory 12-month review timer.
[ ] Risk Treatment Plan (RTP - Clause 6.1.3) defines who fixes which risk by what deadline.
[ ] Statement of Applicability (SoA) documents justifications for all 93 Annex A controls.
[ ] DPDP Act Section 8 mandates technical mitigation to prevent ₹250 Crore Section 33 penalties.
