1. EXECUTIVE OVERVIEW & THE 5 PHASES METHODOLOGY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Hacking is not an act of random chaos; it is a systematic, structured engineering discipline. 
In ethical cybersecurity (CEH / EC-Council standards), an intrusion lifecycle is structured 
into five sequential phases:

  PHASE 1 : RECONNAISSANCE (Information Gathering & Footprinting - Passive vs Active)
  PHASE 2 : SCANNING & ENUMERATION (Discovering live hosts, open ports, and service banners)
  PHASE 3 : GAINING ACCESS (Exploiting vulnerabilities to achieve initial compromise)
  PHASE 4 : MAINTAINING ACCESS (Establishing backdoors, persistence, and C2 beacons)
  PHASE 5 : CLEARING TRACKS (Anti-forensics by adversaries vs artifact cleanup by ethical testers)

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

4. THE CARDINAL ETHICAL CLEANUP MANDATE (PHASE 5)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

* BLACK HAT ADVERSARY:
  - Wipes Windows Event Logs (`wevtutil cl Security`).
  - Deletes Linux audit trails (`/var/log/auth.log`).
  - Timestomps malware binaries to blend with authentic Windows DLLs.

* ETHICAL PENETRATION TESTER:
  - NEVER deletes or tampers with client audit logs (Preserves compliance evidence).
  - Systematically removes all dropped webshells, test accounts, and C2 beacons.
  - Restores all modified configuration files to their original baseline.
  - Documents all cleanup actions in the final executive penetration testing deliverable.

5. INDIAN CYBER LAW (IT ACT 2000) ENFORCEMENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. Section 43 & Section 66 (Unauthorized Probing & Hacking):
   - Probing open ports without authorization (Phase 2) or gaining access (Phase 3) 
     carries up to 3 years imprisonment and ₹5,00,000 fine.

2. Section 65 (Tampering with Source & Log Documents):
   - Wiping or manipulating computer logs (Phase 5) carries up to 3 years imprisonment.

3. Section 66F (Cyber Terrorism):
   - Unauthorized access targeting critical power grids or defense carries LIFE IMPRISONMENT.

7. PROFESSIONAL TIPS & COMMON PITFALLS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

* PROFESSIONAL METHODOLOGICAL HABITS:
  1. Spend 70% of Time on Recon & Scanning: Thorough enumeration makes exploitation effortless.
  2. Maintain a Detailed Handoff Log: Record every payload dropped for complete Phase 5 cleanup.
  3. Stream Logs to Immutable WORM SIEM: Defeat adversary anti-forensics by archiving logs off-site.

* COMMON BEGINNER MISTAKES:
  - Rushing into Phase 3 (Exploitation) without adequate Phase 2 enumeration (leads to missed bugs).
  - Deleting server logs during an ethical audit (Destroys client legal compliance).
  - Leaving persistent webshells or admin accounts on client servers after the test concludes.
  - Firing noisy automated scanners against live production SCADA devices (Causes system crashes).
