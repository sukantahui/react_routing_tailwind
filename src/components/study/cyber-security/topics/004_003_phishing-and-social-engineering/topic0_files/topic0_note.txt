1. EXECUTIVE OVERVIEW & THE PSYCHOLOGY OF HUMAN VULNERABILITY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The human factor is universally described as the most critical vulnerability in cybersecurity: 
attackers do not need to break AES-256 encryption or find zero-day software exploits if they 
can manipulate an authorized human into voluntarily handing over credentials or clicking links:

  * KEVIN MITNICK'S AXIOM :
    "You can have the best technology, firewalls, and encryption, but if an attacker can 
    manipulate a human into clicking a link or revealing a password, all technical controls 
    are bypassed."

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. THE 6 CORE COGNITIVE VULNERABILITY VECTORS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A. TRUTH-DEFAULT THEORY & DEFAULT-TO-TRUST :
   - Humans presume honesty in social communications unless blatant anomalies trigger suspicion.

B. COGNITIVE OVERLOAD & DECISION FATIGUE :
   - Mental exhaustion depletes System 2 analytical scrutiny, forcing fast, intuitive System 1 clicks.

C. AUTHORITY BIAS & MILGRAM OBEDIENCE :
   - Unquestioning compliance when an email impersonates the CEO, Managing Director, or Police.

D. URGENCY BIAS & FEAR (AMYGDALA HIJACKING) :
   - Artificial time limits ("15 minutes to account deactivation") trigger panic overriding logic.

E. RECIPROCITY & THE HELPFULNESS TRAP :
   - Innate human desire to assist a polite colleague or vendor bypasses verification rules.

F. SCARCITY & SOCIAL PROOF :
   - Fear of missing out (FOMO) on bonuses or conforming to peer actions ("everyone updated").

4. STATUTORY & REGULATORY COMPLIANCE IN INDIA
================================================================================

  * INFORMATION TECHNOLOGY ACT 2000 :
    - Section 66D   : Cheating by Personation using computer resources (Up to 3 Years Prison).
    - Section 43(a) : Civil damages up to ₹1 CRORE for unauthorized access via phishing.
    - Section 66    : Criminal hacking & fraudulent deception (Up to 3 Years Prison + ₹5 Lakh Fine).

  * INDIAN PENAL CODE (IPC) :
    - Section 420   : Cheating & Dishonestly Inducing Delivery of Property (Up to 7 Years Prison).

  * CERT-In MANDATORY INCIDENT REPORTING DIRECTIVE :
    - All credential phishing compromises & unauthorized access must be reported within 6 HOURS.

  * DIGITAL PERSONAL DATA PROTECTION (DPDP) ACT 2023 :
    - Section 8(5) & 33: Mandatory employee security training; failure triggers fines up to ₹250 CRORES.
