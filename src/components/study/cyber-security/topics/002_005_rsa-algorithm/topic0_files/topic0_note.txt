1. EXECUTIVE OVERVIEW & THE ASYMMETRIC PARADIGM SHIFT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The RSA Cryptosystem (invented in 1977 at MIT by Ron Rivest, Adi Shamir, and Leonard Adleman; 
independently discovered in 1973 by Clifford Cocks at GCHQ) is the foundational public-key 
algorithm that enabled trust between strangers on the global internet.

* THE CORE ASYMMETRIC DUALITY:
  - PUBLIC KEY  (e, N) : Published openly; used by anyone to ENCRYPT messages or VERIFY signatures.
  - PRIVATE KEY (d, N) : Kept strictly secret; used by owner to DECRYPT messages or SIGN documents.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. THE ONE-WAY TRAPDOOR MATHEMATICS OF RSA
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The security of RSA rests upon the integer prime factorization problem:

  1. FORWARD COMPUTATION (Trivial):
     - Multiplying two large prime numbers: N = p * q (Takes <0.001 ms).

  2. INVERSE COMPUTATION (Computationally Infeasible):
     - Given only modulus N, finding p and q requires exponential time using the 
       General Number Field Sieve (GNFS) on classical computers.

  3. THE MATHEMATICAL TRAPDOOR (Private Key d):
     - Compute Euler's Totient: phi(N) = (p - 1) * (q - 1).
     - Select public exponent e coprime to phi(N) (Standard: e = 65537 = 2^16 + 1).
     - Compute private exponent d: e * d ≡ 1 (mod phi(N)).

* ENCRYPTION & DECRYPTION FORMULAS:
  - Encryption : Ciphertext C ≡ M^e (mod N)  (where Plaintext M < N)
  - Decryption : Plaintext  M ≡ C^d (mod N)
  - Proof of Correctness : (M^e)^d ≡ M^(e*d) ≡ M^(1 + k*phi(N)) ≡ M * (M^phi(N))^k ≡ M (mod N).

4. CRITICAL VULNERABILITIES & HARDENING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  * TEXTBOOK RSA MALLEABILITY & RSA-OAEP:
    - Unpadded Textbook RSA (C = M^e mod N) is homomorphic: C1 * C2 = (M1 * M2)^e mod N.
    - Attackers can double wire transfers without knowing the private key!
    - Mandatory Remedy: RSA-OAEP (Optimal Asymmetric Encryption Padding / PKCS#1 v2.2).

  * WIENER'S LOW PRIVATE EXPONENT ATTACK:
    - If d < (1/3) * N^(1/4), continued fraction expansion of (e / N) yields private key d in seconds.
    - Rule: Always generate private key d with full 2048-bit entropy.

  * BLEICHENBACHER'S PADDING ORACLE ATTACK:
    - Adaptive chosen-ciphertext attack exploiting PKCS#1 v1.5 error responses to decrypt data.
    - Remedy: Standardize on RSA-OAEP (PKCS#1 v2.2) and RSA-PSS for digital signatures.

  * SHOR'S QUANTUM THREAT:
    - Quantum computers running Shor's algorithm factor 2048-bit and 4096-bit N in polynomial time 
      O((log N)^3). Organizations must plan migration to NIST FIPS 203 (ML-KEM / Kyber).

5. INDIAN STATUTORY COMPLIANCE & LEGAL ADMISSIBILITY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

* IT ACT 2000 SECTION 35 (Certifying Authorities & DSCs):
  - The Controller of Certifying Authorities (CCA India) mandates at least 2048-bit RSA keys with 
    SHA-256 for all Class-3 Digital Signature Certificates (DSCs) stored on FIPS 140-2 crypto-tokens.

* IT ACT 2000 SECTION 5:
  - RSA-based electronic digital signatures have full evidentiary admissibility and non-repudiation 
    in Indian courts, equal to physical ink signatures.

7. PROFESSIONAL TIPS & COMMON PITFALLS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

* PROFESSIONAL RSA HABITS:
  1. Always Use e = 65537: Provides blazingly fast encryption with 17 operations and immunity to low-exponent attacks.
  2. Mandate RSA-OAEP for Encryption & RSA-PSS for Signatures: Eliminates padding oracle and malleability vulnerabilities.
  3. Deploy Chinese Remainder Theorem (RSA-CRT): Delivers a 4x speedup for private key decryption operations.
  4. Enforce 2048-bit Minimum: Strictly ban 1024-bit RSA in all production environments.

* COMMON BEGINNER RSA PITFALLS:
  - Using unpadded Textbook RSA (Vulnerable to multiplicative malleability).
  - Choosing a small private exponent d to speed up decryption (Vulnerable to Wiener's continued fraction attack).
  - Sharing modulus N between multiple users with different exponents (Vulnerable to common modulus attacks).
  - Storing RSA private keys in plain text files without hardware HSM or token protection.
