1. DEFINITION & NATURE OF CYBERSPACE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Cyberspace is a global, human-created domain within the information environment consisting of the
interdependent network of information technology infrastructures, including the Internet,
telecommunications networks, computer systems, and embedded processors and controllers.

Distinct Characteristics:
• Artificial & Malleable: Purely man-made and constantly reshaped by software code and protocols.
• Borderless & Transnational: Digital packet flows ignore physical geography and sovereign boundaries.
• Speed-of-Light Propagation: Cyber weapons and data streams traverse global fiber optic links in milliseconds.
• Asymmetry of Warfare: A small, low-cost hacking team can threaten massive corporations or nation-states.
• Attribution Problem: Obfuscation techniques (Tor, VPN proxies, false flags) make proving guilt difficult.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. THE THREE STRUCTURAL LAYERS OF CYBERSPACE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Physical Layer:
   • Hardware components: Submarine undersea fiber optic cables (99% of global traffic), satellite links,
     terrestrial microwave towers, server data centers, core routers, and physical switch silicon.
2. Logical Layer:
   • Software & protocols: TCP/IP packet structures, BGP routing tables, DNS resolution, operating systems,
     web services, APIs, and cryptographic communication protocols (TLS 1.3, IPsec).
3. Cyber-Persona & Cognitive Layer:
   • Digital identities and human minds: User accounts, email addresses, biometric profiles, social personas,
     and the human cognitive decision-making processes targeted by phishing and disinformation.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. CYBERSPACE GOVERNANCE & INDIAN LEGAL FRAMEWORK
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
• Information Technology Act 2000:
  - Section 43/66: Unauthorized access, hacking, and computer system damage penalties.
  - Section 66C: Identity theft and fraudulent impersonation.
  - Section 69A: Sovereign authority to issue website and domain takedowns for national security.
• National Regulatory Bodies:
  - CERT-In: National nodal agency mandating 6-hour cybersecurity incident reporting.
  - NCIIPC: Designated agency protecting Critical Information Infrastructure (CII) under Section 70A.

4. FOUR REAL-WORLD BENGAL CASE STUDIES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

CASE STUDY 1: Precision Foundry External Attack Surface Management (Debangshu - Barrackpore)
Debangshu deployed an External Attack Surface Management (EASM) platform in Barrackpore for ₹3,80,000.
The system discovered an unpatched legacy VPN gateway exposed to public cyberspace, closing the vector
before automated foreign ransomware scanners could exploit it.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CASE STUDY 2: Diagnostic Clinic DNSSEC Cryptographic Zone Protection (Mahima - Ichapur)
Mahima implemented DNSSEC across hospital domain registrars in Ichapur for ₹1,20,000. Cryptographically
signing all DNS resource records eliminated DNS cache poisoning risks, ensuring 85,000 patients are never
diverted to rogue phishing portals.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CASE STUDY 3: State Research University BGP RPKI Route Hijack Defense (Mamata - Kolkata)
Mamata deployed Resource Public Key Infrastructure (RPKI) and Route Origin Authorizations (ROAs)
for university BGP routers in Kolkata for ₹4,50,000. When a rogue foreign autonomous system attempted
to hijack the university's IP prefix, invalid BGP routes were autonomously dropped.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CASE STUDY 4: Cyber Security Lab LEO Satellite & Cyber Range Sandbox (Abhronila - Jadavpur)
Abhronila established a simulated cyberspace warfare cyber range in Jadavpur for ₹2,80,000.
Students simulate multi-layer cyberspace attacks, defending simulated physical satellite downlinks,
logical BGP routers, and cognitive social engineering vectors against live red-team adversaries.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. COMMON PITFALLS & PRACTICAL ADVICE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Never view cyberspace as purely abstract software; protect its physical cables and data centers.
- Deploy DNSSEC and BGP RPKI to defend against logical layer cyberspace redirection and hijacking.
- Remember that human cognitive biases are an integral layer of cyberspace attack surfaces.
- Formulate all enterprise EASM, DNSSEC, and cyber range budgets in Indian Rupees (₹).
