1. DEFINITION OF TOPOLOGY:
   - Network topology refers to the geometric arrangement or physical layout in which computers, cables, and peripheral devices are interconnected.
   - Types: Physical Topology (actual physical cable layout) vs Logical Topology (path data travels through the medium).

2. BUS TOPOLOGY ARCHITECTURE:
   - Also known as Linear Bus Topology.
   - Single Central Backbone: All nodes (workstations, printers, servers) connect directly to a single, continuous central transmission cable called the Backbone (usually coaxial cable, e.g., 10BASE2 or 10BASE5).
   - T-Connectors / Vampire Taps: Nodes tap into the backbone cable at intervals.
   - Terminators (50 Ohms): Mandatory resistors placed at BOTH ends of the backbone to absorb electrical signals and eliminate signal reflections (ghosts).

3. HOW DATA TRAVELS IN A BUS TOPOLOGY:
   - Broadcast Propagation: A transmitting computer sends data frames out onto the backbone in both directions.
   - All connected nodes receive the signal simultaneously and inspect the destination MAC address.
   - Only the node whose address matches processes the frame; all other nodes discard it.
   - CSMA/CD: If two nodes transmit simultaneously, an electrical collision occurs, corrupting the signal. Nodes back off for a random interval.

4. ADVANTAGES & DISADVANTAGES (CBSE EXAM COMPARISON):
   - Advantages:
     * Minimum Cable Length: Requires significantly less cable than Star topology because nodes connect to a single central line.
     * Inexpensive: Low material cost, no expensive central switches required.
     * Easy Setup for Small LANs: Simple to install for 3 to 10 computers in a straight line.
   - Disadvantages:
     * Single Point of Failure: If the central backbone cable breaks, is cut, or if a terminator is removed, the ENTIRE network collapses immediately!
     * Difficult Fault Isolation: Pinpointing the exact location of a cable fault or bad connector requires testing the whole line.
     * Heavy Collision Congestion: As more computers are added, collisions multiply exponentially, severely degrading speed.
     * Limited Scalability: Maximum segment length (185m - 500m) and limited maximum node count (30 nodes in 10BASE2).
