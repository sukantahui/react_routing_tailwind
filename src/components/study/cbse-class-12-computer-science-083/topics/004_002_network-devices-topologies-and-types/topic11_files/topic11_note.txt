1. ARCHITECTURE OF TREE TOPOLOGY:
   - Also known as Hierarchical Topology.
   - Definition: A hybrid network topology that combines the characteristics of Bus topology and Star topology.
   - Structural Anatomy:
     * Consists of groups of star-configured workstations connected to a central linear backbone cable (or core switch).
     * Root Node: Topmost core switch/server at Level 0.
     * Intermediate (Distribution) Nodes: Departmental switches at Level 1.
     * Leaf (Access) Nodes: Individual client workstations at Level 2.

2. HOW DATA FLOWS IN A TREE TOPOLOGY:
   - Data flows down the hierarchy from parent to child, or up from child to parent.
   - Intra-Cluster Traffic: Workstations in the same star group communicate locally through their departmental switch without burdening the central root backbone.
   - Inter-Cluster Traffic: Workstations communicating across departments travel up through the departmental switch, across the backbone/core switch, and down into the destination branch.

3. ADVANTAGES & DISADVANTAGES (CBSE COMPARISON):
   - Major Advantages:
     * Extreme Scalability: Networks can be expanded easily by attaching new star clusters (branches) to the backbone without re-cabling existing departments.
     * Hierarchical Organization: Matches real-world corporate and academic hierarchies (e.g. University Core -> School of Engineering / School of Medicine -> Individual Labs).
     * Branch Fault Isolation: If an entire departmental switch or branch cable fails, ONLY that specific branch goes down. Other departmental branches continue operating normally.
     * Dedicated High-Speed Links: Different branches can use different media (e.g. Optical fiber backbone connecting copper star clusters).
   - Major Disadvantages:
     * Root / Backbone Dependency: If the central root switch or main backbone cable fails, communication between different branches is completely severed!
     * Complex Configuration & High Cost: Requires extensive cabling and multi-tiered managed switches, making design and maintenance more expensive than simple star or bus.

4. CBSE 5-MARK CASE STUDY APPLICATION:
   - In campus-wide case studies connecting multiple buildings (e.g. Admin Block, Science Block, Library, Hostels to a Server Block), the campus backbone connecting individual building star networks constitutes a classic TREE TOPOLOGY.
