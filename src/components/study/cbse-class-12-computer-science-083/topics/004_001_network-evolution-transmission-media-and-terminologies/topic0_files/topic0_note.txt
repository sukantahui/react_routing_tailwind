TOPIC 0: EVOLUTION OF NETWORKING (ARPANET, NSFNET, INTERSPACE, INTERNET)

1. NEED FOR NETWORKING:
- Resource Sharing: Sharing high-cost hardware (laser printers, scanners, storage servers) and software across multiple nodes.
- High Reliability: Alternative paths for data delivery; if one node or link fails, traffic reroutes.
- Cost Reduction: Shared software licenses and centralized server management reduce IT expenditure.
- Rapid Communication: Instantaneous transfer of messages, emails, sensor data, and multimedia globally.

2. HISTORICAL MILESTONES:
A. ARPANET (1969):
   - Advanced Research Projects Agency Network, funded by US Department of Defense (DoD).
   - Connected 4 university research nodes: UCLA, Stanford Research Institute (SRI), UC Santa Barbara (UCSB), and University of Utah.
   - Pioneered packet switching and TCP/IP protocol suite.
B. NSFNET (Mid-1980s):
   - National Science Foundation Network created a high-capacity national backbone network.
   - Connected supercomputing centers across US universities and research facilities.
   - Far more capable and higher bandwidth than early ARPANET.
C. INTERSPACE:
   - Visionary multi-media software project for interconnected distributed virtual spaces.
   - Allowed real-time cross-disciplinary communication and information retrieval.
D. THE MODERN INTERNET (1990s - Present):
   - Result of interconnecting ARPANET, NSFNET, and commercial private networks.
   - In 1989-1991, Tim Berners-Lee invented the World Wide Web (WWW), HTML, and HTTP at CERN.
   - A global network of interconnected computer networks adhering to the TCP/IP standard suite.
