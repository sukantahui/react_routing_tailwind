TOPIC 3: TRANSMISSION MODES (SIMPLEX, HALF-DUPLEX, FULL-DUPLEX)

1. WHAT IS A TRANSMISSION MODE?
- The direction of signal flow between two interconnected communication devices.
- Categorized into three fundamental modes: Simplex, Half-Duplex, and Full-Duplex.

2. SIMPLEX MODE (UNIDIRECTIONAL):
- Data transmission occurs in only ONE direction (Sender -> Receiver).
- The sender can only send; the receiver can only receive and CANNOT reply.
- The entire channel capacity is dedicated to one-way transmission.
- Examples:
  * Traditional Television & FM Radio broadcasting.
  * Keyboard sending keystrokes to CPU.
  * CPU outputting display signals to a computer monitor.

3. HALF-DUPLEX MODE (BIDIRECTIONAL, ALTERNATE):
- Data transmission can occur in BOTH directions, but only ONE direction at a time.
- When station A is transmitting, station B can only receive. Station B must wait until station A finishes before replying.
- The full channel capacity is shared between both directions alternately.
- Examples:
  * Walkie-Talkies (users say "Over" to release the channel).
  * Push-to-talk marine and aviation radios.

4. FULL-DUPLEX MODE (BIDIRECTIONAL, SIMULTANEOUS):
- Data transmission occurs in BOTH directions SIMULTANEOUSLY at the exact same moment.
- Both stations can transmit and receive concurrently without waiting.
- Channel capacity is divided between the two directions, or two separate physical transmission paths exist.
- Examples:
  * Modern telephone and mobile phone conversations (both callers can talk at once).
  * Video conferencing (Zoom, Google Meet).
  * Full-duplex switched Gigabit Ethernet connections.
