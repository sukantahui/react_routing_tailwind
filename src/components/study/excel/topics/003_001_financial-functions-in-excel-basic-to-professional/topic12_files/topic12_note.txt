Topic: Working Capital and Free Cash Flow to Firm (FCFF) financial engineering
Module: 003_001_financial-functions-in-excel-basic-to-professional
Course: EXCEL-PRO-901: Microsoft Excel From Zero to Ultra Expert
Instructor: Sukanta Hui (Coder & AccoTax)

KEY FORMULA / SYNTAX:
FCFF = EBIT*(1-t) + D&A - Capex - ΔNWC | NWC = (Current Assets - Cash) - (Current Liabilities - ShortTermDebt)

PRIMARY CONCEPT:
Engineering Free Cash Flow to Firm (FCFF) from audited financial statements, integrating Non-Cash D&A, Capex reinvestment, and Net Working Capital changes

TEACHER'S ADVICE:
Profit is an opinion; Cash is a fact! A company can show massive accounting profits on paper while bleeding cash due to working capital traps. Always bridge Net Income to FCFF.

COMMON TRAPS TO AVOID:
- Including Cash in Working Capital Calculation: Including cash balances in Current Assets when calculating Net Working Capital creates double-counting in cash flow models. → Fix: Use Non-Cash Working Capital: `NWC = (Current Assets - Cash) - (Current Liabilities - Short-term Debt)`.
- Inverting Change in NWC Sign: Treating an increase in Accounts Receivable as a positive cash flow (it actually represents uncollected cash). → Fix: Subtract ΔNWC: `Increase in NWC = Cash Outflow (-)`; `Decrease in NWC = Cash Inflow (+)`.
- Confusing Net Income with FCFF: Using Net Income instead of NOPAT includes interest expense, distorting the firm-level capital-structure-neutral valuation. → Fix: Always start FCFF from EBIT*(1-t), not Net Income.
