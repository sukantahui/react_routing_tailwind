KEY FORMULA / SYNTAX:
Investment Valuation: =XNPV(DiscountRate, CashFlows, Dates) | =XIRR(CashFlows, Dates) | Loan Amortization: =PMT(r, n, pv) | =PPMT(...) | =IPMT(...)

PRIMARY CONCEPT:
Testing advanced corporate financial modeling: irregular cash flow valuation (XNPV, XIRR vs NPV/IRR), multi-tier loan amortization schedules (PMT, PPMT, IPMT), and cost of capital discounting

TEACHER'S ADVICE:
In corporate finance, NEVER use standard NPV or IRR—always use XNPV and XIRR! They use exact dates so your calculations are 100% accurate. And remember: your initial investment MUST be a negative number!

COMMON TRAPS TO AVOID:
- Using Standard NPV with Irregular Dates: Standard `=NPV()` assumes all cash flows occur at strictly equal 1-year intervals, producing invalid valuations. -> Fix: Always use `=XNPV(rate, cash_flows, dates)` for real-world projects.
- Omitting Negative Sign on Initial Investment Outflow: Passing positive number for Year 0 investment in XIRR causes `#NUM!` error. -> Fix: Initial capital outflow MUST be negative: `-50000000`.
- Annual vs Monthly Interest Rate Mismatch in PMT: Passing 12% annual rate into monthly loan formula without dividing by 12. -> Fix: Divide annual rate by 12 and multiply years by 12: `=PMT(Rate/12, Years*12, -LoanAmount)`.
