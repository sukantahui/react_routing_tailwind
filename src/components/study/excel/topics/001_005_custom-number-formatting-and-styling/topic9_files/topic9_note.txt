Topic: Phone numbers, Tax IDs (PAN/GSTIN/SSN), and credit card masking patterns
Module: 001_005_custom-number-formatting-and-styling
Course: EXCEL-PRO-901: Microsoft Excel From Zero to Ultra Expert
Instructor: Sukanta Hui (Coder & AccoTax)

KEY FORMULA / SYNTAX:
+91 ##### ##### | @@@@@0000@ (PAN) | 0000-0000-0000 (Aadhaar) | 0000-****-****-0000

PRIMARY CONCEPT:
Specialized masks for phone numbers (+91), Tax IDs (PAN, GSTIN), and sensitive credit card masking

TEACHER'S ADVICE:
Formatting identity numbers cleanly prevents costly data-entry mistakes. For regulatory compliance, ensure sensitive credentials are both formatted and access-controlled.

COMMON TRAPS TO AVOID:
- Dropping Leading Country Zeros: Entering phone numbers as raw numbers drops leading zeros or country codes. → Fix: Use custom format '+91 ##### #####' or enter as text.
- Alphanumeric Text Token Errors: Using numeric 0 placeholders on alphanumeric PAN cards fails because PAN contains letters. → Fix: Use @ text placeholders or UPPER() functions for mixed alphanumeric codes.
- Formatting vs True Security: Formatting hides digits on screen, but the raw number remains in cell memory and formula bar. → Fix: For true security, hash or truncate values using formulas (=RIGHT(A1,4)).
