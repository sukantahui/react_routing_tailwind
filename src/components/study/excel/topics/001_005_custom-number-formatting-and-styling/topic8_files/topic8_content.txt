1. EXECUTIVE OVERVIEW
Elapsed duration tracking exceeding 24 hours with [h]:mm:ss for timesheets is an essential discipline in modern spreadsheet data engineering. 
It establishes the separation between raw data storage and visual UI representation.

2. SYNTAX & TOKEN STRUCTURE
Format Code: [h]:mm:ss | [m]:ss | [s]

3. CORE TECHNICAL MECHANICS
1. The 24-Hour Modulo Reset Issue:
   Standard time formatting `hh:mm` operates modulo 24, resetting to 00:00 every 24 hours (e.g. 26.5 hours displays as 02:30).

2. Square Bracket Duration Token [h]:
   Enclosing 'h' in brackets `[h]:mm` instructs Excel to display total cumulative elapsed hours without rolling over at 24 hours.

3. Converting Time Serials to Pay Rates:
   Because 1 hour equals 1/24 in serial math, multiply time cells by 24 before multiplying by hourly wage rates.

5. TROUBLESHOOTING & COMMON ERRORS
• 24-Hour Clock Rollover:
  Root Cause: Using regular 'hh:mm' causes 25 hours to display as '01:00' because regular time loops modulo 24.
  Diagnostic Fix: Enclose the hour token in square brackets: [h]:mm to display cumulative elapsed hours.
• Time as Text Addition Failure:
  Root Cause: Typing '25:30' as text causes =SUM() to return 0.
  Diagnostic Fix: Enter time as true fraction serial numbers and format as [h]:mm.
• Negative Time Display (#VALUE!):
  Root Cause: Subtracting later time from earlier time in 1900 date system displays ######.
  Diagnostic Fix: Use IF() checks or absolute formulas to prevent negative time serial numbers.

6. PRO TIPS & SHORTCUTS
• Elapsed Hours ([h]:mm): Displays cumulative hours without 24-hour rollover cap.
• Elapsed Minutes ([m]:ss): Displays cumulative minutes for call center and race tracking.
• Fraction Math (=Time * 24): Multiply time serial by 24 to convert to standard decimal hours for billing.
