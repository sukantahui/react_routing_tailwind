Topic: Moving averages and rolling totals: DATESINPERIOD and DATESBETWEEN
Module: 005_004_dax-formulas-mastery-and-time-intelligence
Course: EXCEL-PRO-901: Microsoft Excel From Zero to Ultra Expert
Instructor: Sukanta Hui (Coder & AccoTax)

KEY FORMULA / SYNTAX:
Rolling Window: CALCULATE([Measure], DATESINPERIOD(Dim_Calendar[Date], MAX(Dim_Calendar[Date]), -3, MONTH)) | DATESBETWEEN

PRIMARY CONCEPT:
Constructing rolling time-window analytics using DATESINPERIOD and DATESBETWEEN: 3-month moving averages, rolling 12-month (R12) revenue, and smoothing seasonal volatility

TEACHER'S ADVICE:
Rolling 12-Month (TTM) is the gold standard of corporate finance. To build it: `CALCULATE([TotalSales], DATESINPERIOD(Dim_Calendar[Date], MAX(Dim_Calendar[Date]), -12, MONTH))`. It removes all seasonal dips and shows true underlying growth.

COMMON TRAPS TO AVOID:
- Omitting MAX(Date) Anchor in DATESINPERIOD: Passing a static date instead of `MAX(Dim_Calendar[Date])` freezes the rolling window to a fixed historical date. -> Fix: Always anchor with `MAX(Dim_Calendar[Date])` or `LASTDATE(Dim_Calendar[Date])`.
- Distortion in Initial Historical Months (Ramp-Up Glitch): Calculating a 12-month rolling average in Month 2 averages only 2 months of data, distorting trend charts. -> Fix: Check if 12 full months of historical data exist before calculating.
- Using DATESBETWEEN with Hardcoded Dates: Hardcoding start/end dates prevents the rolling window from moving dynamically with the calendar. -> Fix: Use `DATESINPERIOD` for dynamic relative rolling windows.
