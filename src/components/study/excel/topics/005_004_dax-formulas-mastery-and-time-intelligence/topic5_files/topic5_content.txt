Topic 5: Iterator functions (X-functions): SUMX, AVERAGEX, MINX, MAXX, and COUNTX
================================================================================

1. EXECUTIVE OVERVIEW
Iterator functions (X-functions): SUMX, AVERAGEX, MINX, MAXX, and COUNTX is an essential discipline in enterprise business intelligence, quantitative finance, and advanced data modeling.
It provides the core protocols required to construct complex multi-dimensional financial measures, calculate period-over-period growth, and evaluate dynamic rankings.

2. SYNTAX & TOKEN STRUCTURE
Operational Architecture: Iterator Functions: SUMX(Table, Expression) | AVERAGEX(Table, Expression) | MINX | MAXX | COUNTX

3. CORE TECHNICAL MECHANICS
1. Row-by-Row Iteration Engine:
   Iterators accept a Table as their 1st argument and an Expression as their 2nd argument. They loop through the table row-by-row, evaluate the expression, and aggregate the results.

2. Eliminating Calculated Columns:
   `SUMX(Fact_Sales, Fact_Sales[Qty] * Fact_Sales[UnitPrice] * (1 - Fact_Sales[Discount]))` calculates total revenue dynamically without adding stored columns in RAM.

3. Iterating Filtered Virtual Tables:
   Iterators can iterate dynamically filtered tables: `SUMX(FILTER(Sales, Sales[Qty] > 100), Sales[Amount])`.

5. TROUBLESHOOTING & COMMON ERRORS
• Using SUMX When Standard SUM Suffices:
  Root Cause: Writing `SUMX(Sales, Sales[Amount])` when `SUM(Sales[Amount])` accomplishes the exact same thing faster.
  Diagnostic Fix: Use `SUM()` for single-column additions; reserve `SUMX()` for multi-column math or expressions.
• Iterating Massive Unfiltered Fact Tables with Complex Measures:
  Root Cause: Calling a complex measure inside SUMX over 10 million rows causes high CPU latency.
  Diagnostic Fix: Iterate small Dimension tables (`SUMX(Dim_Customer, ...)`), or push row math to raw columns.
• Confusing SUM of Averages with Average of Sums:
  Root Cause: Writing `AVERAGEX(Sales, [Margin%])` averages transactions equally rather than weighting by dollar volume.
  Diagnostic Fix: Use the ratio of sums: `DIVIDE([TotalMargin], [TotalSales])`.

6. PRO TIPS & SHORTCUTS
• Row Math Without Columns (SUMX(Sales, Qty * Price)): Calculates line-item revenue on the fly with zero RAM storage overhead.
• Iterate Dimension Tables (SUMX(Dim_Customer, [CustomerRevenue])): High-performance pattern to compute customer-level distributions.
• MAXX / MINX for Peak Analysis (MAXX(Dim_Calendar, [DailySales])): Finds highest single-day sales volume across historical calendar.
