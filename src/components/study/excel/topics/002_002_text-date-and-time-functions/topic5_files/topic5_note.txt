TOPIC 5 QUICK SUMMARY & INSTRUCTOR REFERENCE
================================================================================
Topic: Working day calculations: WORKDAY, WORKDAY.INTL, NETWORKDAYS, and NETWORKDAYS.INTL
Module: 002_002_text-date-and-time-functions
Course: EXCEL-PRO-901: Microsoft Excel From Zero to Ultra Expert
Instructor: Sukanta Hui (Coder & AccoTax)

KEY FORMULA / SYNTAX:
=WORKDAY(start_date, days, [holidays]) | =WORKDAY.INTL(start_date, days, [weekend], [holidays]) | =NETWORKDAYS(start_date, end_date, [holidays]) | =NETWORKDAYS.INTL(start_date, end_date, [weekend], [holidays])

PRIMARY CONCEPT:
Calculating commercial project milestones and net working days excluding weekends and statutory holiday schedules

TEACHER'S ADVICE:
Never use simple addition (Date + 30) for contractual deadlines! Statutory contracts require =WORKDAY.INTL with explicit holiday schedules to avoid costly legal disputes.

COMMON TRAPS TO AVOID:
- Default Weekend Misalignment in India/Middle East: Standard NETWORKDAYS assumes Sat/Sun off, miscalculating factories operating on 6-day (Sunday-only) workweeks. → Fix: Use NETWORKDAYS.INTL with weekend code 11 (Sunday only) or custom 7-character string mask.
- Holiday Falls on Weekend Double-Counting: Worrying that a holiday falling on a Sunday subtracts 2 days (Excel handles this correctly). → Fix: Provide complete holiday lists; Excel automatically avoids double-deducting weekend holidays.
- Start Date Exceeds End Date Negative Result: Passing a start_date later than end_date to NETWORKDAYS returns negative day counts. → Fix: Ensure chronological order or wrap in ABS() if unsigned duration is needed.
