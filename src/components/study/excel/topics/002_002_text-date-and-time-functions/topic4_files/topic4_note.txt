KEY FORMULA / SYNTAX:
=DATE(year, month, day) | =DAY(serial_date) | =MONTH(serial_date) | =YEAR(serial_date) | =TODAY()

PRIMARY CONCEPT:
Understanding Excel's integer serial date system (Day 1 = Jan 1, 1900) and constructing dates with DATE, DAY, MONTH, YEAR, and TODAY

TEACHER'S ADVICE:
Never type dates as text! When building financial models, always construct dates with =DATE(year, month, day) to eliminate regional format bugs between US and UK systems.

COMMON TRAPS TO AVOID:
- Text Date Entry Trap: Entering dates as text strings ('27.08.2026') causes date math and sorting to fail. → Fix: Always construct dates using =DATE(year, month, day) or enter valid system date formats.
- TODAY() Volatility Lag: Overusing TODAY() in 100,000 cells forces continuous worksheet recalculation on every keystroke. → Fix: Use static timestamps (Ctrl + ;) where fixed transaction dates are required.
- The 1900 Leap Year Bug: Excel intentionally treats 1900 as a leap year for legacy Lotus 1-2-3 compatibility. → Fix: Avoid calculating historical dates prior to March 1, 1900.
