KEY FORMULA / SYNTAX:
What-If Decision Framework: Assumptions (Variables) → Financial Logic Engine → Sensitivity Outputs (KPIs)

PRIMARY CONCEPT:
Understanding the What-If Analysis decision science framework: separating deterministic base cases from uncertainty distributions, stress testing, and scenario planning

TEACHER'S ADVICE:
What-If Analysis is what separates junior spreadsheet typists from strategic C-suite advisors. You are not just presenting numbers; you are quantifying risk and optimizing decisions.

COMMON TRAPS TO AVOID:
- Single-Point Estimate Bias: Presenting a single 'expected' revenue figure to executive leadership without modeling downside risk boundaries. → Fix: Always present 3-scenario bounds (Best, Base, Worst) and 2D sensitivity matrices.
- Unlinked Assumption Cells: Typing hardcoded numbers into formulas prevents What-If tools from recalculating model outputs dynamically. → Fix: Enforce strict dynamic cell links to central Assumption Blocks.
- Ignoring Multi-Variable Correlations: Assuming price can increase by 50% without any reduction in customer sales volume. → Fix: Incorporate realistic price elasticity demand curves in sensitivity assumptions.
