1. WHAT IS AN ADJECTIVE? (FUNCTIONAL DEFINITION)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
An adjective modifies, describes, qualifies, or restricts the meaning of a 
noun or pronoun by providing information regarding its quality, size, age, color, 
origin, material, quantity, or specific identity.

Adjectives operate primarily in TWO syntactic positions:
  1. ATTRIBUTIVE POSITION: Directly before the noun it qualifies.
     - "She bought an ELEGANT dress."
     - "The COURAGEOUS firefighter entered the building."
  2. PREDICATIVE POSITION: Following a linking/copular verb (be, seem, look, feel), 
     acting as a Subject Complement.
     - "The dress was ELEGANT."
     - "The firefighter seemed COURAGEOUS."


2. TAXONOMY OF ADJECTIVES (8 CORE CLASSES)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
+-------------------+----------------------------+-----------------------------+
| CLASS             | DEFINING QUESTION/FUNCTION | CANONICAL EXAMPLES          |
+-------------------+----------------------------+-----------------------------+
| 1. Quality        | What kind?                 | brave, honest, brilliant,   |
|    (Descriptive)  | (Inherent attributes)      | heavy, magnificent          |
+-------------------+----------------------------+-----------------------------+
| 2. Quantity       | How much?                  | much, little, some, enough, |
|                   | (Uncountable mass)         | any, whole, half, all       |
+-------------------+----------------------------+-----------------------------+
| 3. Number         | How many? / In what order? |                             |
|    - Cardinal     | Exact countable tally      | one, two, ten, fifty        |
|    - Ordinal      | Exact sequential rank      | first, second, next, last   |
|    - Indefinite   | Non-specific count         | many, several, few, sundry  |
|    - Distributive | Individualized members     | each, every, either, neither|
+-------------------+----------------------------+-----------------------------+
| 4. Demonstrative  | Which one?                 | this, that, these, those,   |
|                   | (Pointing in space/time)   | such, yonder                |
+-------------------+----------------------------+-----------------------------+
| 5. Interrogative  | Questioning nominal identity| which, what, whose (before N)|
+-------------------+----------------------------+-----------------------------+
| 6. Possessive     | Whose?                     | my, your, his, her, its,    |
|                   | (Determiner form)          | our, their                  |
+-------------------+----------------------------+-----------------------------+
| 7. Proper         | Derived from Proper Noun   | Indian, Shakespearean,      |
|                   | (Always capitalized)       | Victorian, Parisian         |
+-------------------+----------------------------+-----------------------------+
| 8. Emphasizing    | Rhetorical intensifier     | own ("my own eyes"),        |
|                   |                            | very ("the very man")       |
+-------------------+----------------------------+-----------------------------+


3. STRICTLY PREDICATIVE ADJECTIVES ("A-" PREFIX INVARIANTS)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Certain adjectives can NEVER precede a noun in attributive position. They can 
only follow linking verbs:
  - AFRAID, ASLEEP, ALIVE, ALIKE, ASHAMED, AWARE, ALONE, AFLAME, AFLOAT.

  * INCORRECT: The asleep boy looked peaceful.
  * CORRECT:   The sleeping boy looked peaceful. (Attributive requires participle)
  * CORRECT:   The boy was asleep. (Predicative is valid)

  * INCORRECT: An afraid soldier surrendered.
  * CORRECT:   A frightened soldier surrendered.
  * CORRECT:   The soldier was afraid.


4. PARTICIPIAL ADJECTIVES: -ING VS -ED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
+-------------------+-----------------------------+-----------------------------+
| ASPECT            | PRESENT PARTICIPLE (-ING)   | PAST PARTICIPLE (-ED)       |
+-------------------+-----------------------------+-----------------------------+
| Semantic Role     | Active / Source of Feeling  | Passive / Receiver of Feeling|
| Meaning           | "Causing the emotion"       | "Experiencing the emotion"  |
| Examples          | - a boring lecture          | - a bored student           |
|                   | - an exciting football match| - an excited crowd          |
|                   | - a terrifying nightmare    | - a terrified child         |
|                   | - an exhausting journey     | - an exhausted traveler     |
+-------------------+-----------------------------+-----------------------------+


5. THE ROYAL ORDER OF MULTIPLE ADJECTIVES (OSASCOMP)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
When stacking two or more descriptive adjectives before a single noun, follow 
the rigid cross-linguistic hierarchy:

  [1] OPINION      -> lovely, beautiful, terrible, delicious, elegant
  [2] SIZE         -> large, tiny, gigantic, huge, small
  [3] AGE          -> old, ancient, young, antique, modern
  [4] SHAPE        -> round, square, oval, cylindrical, rectangular
  [5] COLOR        -> red, jet-black, emerald-green, azure
  [6] ORIGIN       -> Indian, Italian, French, Victorian, lunar
  [7] MATERIAL     -> wooden, silk, porcelain, metallic, leather
  [8] PURPOSE      -> dining (table), sleeping (bag), sports (car)
  [ + HEAD NOUN ]

MASTER EXAMPLES:
  - "A lovely (1) small (2) antique (3) round (4) brown (5) Italian (6) wooden (7) dining (8) table."
  - "An expensive (1) modern (3) German (6) sports (8) car."
  - "A charming (1) old (3) square (4) stone (7) cottage."


6. COMPOUND ADJECTIVES & HYPHENATION MECHANICS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
When two or more words combine to modify a succeeding noun, they must be 
hyphenated, and measurement units MUST remain singular.

  * Formula: NUMBER + SINGULAR NOUN + NOUN
    - "a ten-year-old boy"     (NOT: a ten-years-old boy)
    - "a five-star hotel"      (NOT: a five-stars hotel)
    - "a two-hour exam"        (NOT: a two-hours exam)
    - "a twenty-mile journey"  (NOT: a twenty-miles journey)

  * Position Rule:
    - Preceding Noun (Hyphenated): "He is a well-known author."
    - Following Verb (No Hyphen):   "The author is well known."


7. SENSORY LINKING VERBS + ADJECTIVES (NOT ADVERBS!)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Verbs of perception (look, sound, smell, taste, feel) act as copulas and require 
predicate ADJECTIVES to describe the subject:
  * CORRECT:   "The freshly baked cake smells DELICIOUS." (NOT: deliciously)
  * CORRECT:   "The velvet fabric feels SMOOTH." (NOT: smoothly)
  * CORRECT:   "Her explanation sounds LOGICAL." (NOT: logically)


8. HIGH-FREQUENCY COMPETITIVE EXAM TRAPS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRAP 1: Misplaced Predicative-only Adjectives
    - INCORRECT: We observed the alive fish in the pond.
    - CORRECT:   We observed the living fish in the pond. / The fish was alive.

  TRAP 2: Pluralizing Adjectival Measurement Units
    - INCORRECT: He presented a five-hundred-rupees note.
    - CORRECT:   He presented a five-hundred-rupee note.

  TRAP 3: Violating OSASCOMP Hierarchy
    - INCORRECT: She wore a leather black stylish coat.
    - CORRECT:   She wore a stylish black leather coat. (Opinion -> Color -> Material)
