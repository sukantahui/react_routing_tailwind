1. THE CARDINAL LAW OF TRANSFORMATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Transformation is the process of altering the SYNTACTIC FORM of a sentence 
without changing its MEANING (semantic content) in any way.

Formula:
  Form A (Simple / Affirmative / Assertive) 
    <== 100% SEMANTIC EQUIVALENCE ==> 
  Form B (Complex / Negative / Interrogative)

3. REMOVING & INSERTING "TOO...TO"
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Formula:
  • [Subject] + [Verb] + TOO + [Adj/Adv] + TO + [V1]
    ==>
  • [Subject] + [Verb] + SO + [Adj/Adv] + THAT + [Subject] + [CANNOT/COULD NOT] + [V1]

Examples:
  - Present: "He is TOO frail TO walk." ==> "He is SO frail THAT he CANNOT walk."
  - Past:    "He was TOO late TO catch the bus." ==> "He was SO late THAT he COULD NOT catch the bus."

================================================================================
4. TIME TRIGGER SHIFTS: AS SOON AS <--> NO SOONER <--> HARDLY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Affirmative:
   "AS SOON AS the teacher entered the classroom, the students stood up."
2. Negative (Inversion with Did):
   "NO SOONER DID the teacher ENTER the classroom THAN the students stood up."
3. Negative (Inversion with Had):
   "NO SOONER HAD the teacher ENTERED the classroom THAN the students stood up."
4. Inversion with Hardly / Scarcely:
   "HARDLY HAD the teacher ENTERED the classroom WHEN the students stood up."

Key Rules:
  • "No sooner" strictly takes "THAN".
  • "Hardly / Scarcely" strictly takes "WHEN".

5. INTERCHANGE OF SENTENCE PURPOSES & POLARITIES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A. AFFIRMATIVE <--> NEGATIVE:
   - "Man is mortal." ==> "No man is immortal." (Antonym method)
   - "Only the brave deserve the prize." ==> "None but the brave deserve the prize."
   - "As soon as he saw me, he smiled." ==> "No sooner did he see me than he smiled."

B. ASSERTIVE <--> INTERROGATIVE (Rhetorical Questions):
   - "Everyone knows Rabindranath." ==> "Who does not know Rabindranath?"
   - "Their sacrifice will never be forgotten." ==> "Will their sacrifice ever be forgotten?"

C. EXCLAMATORY <--> ASSERTIVE:
   - "How lovely the rose is!" ==> "The rose is very lovely."
   - "What a tragic disaster it was!" ==> "It was a truly tragic disaster."
   - "O that I were a bird!" ==> "I wish that I were a bird."

6. INTERCHANGE OF PARTS OF SPEECH
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Transforming sentences by morphing the grammatical category of a key root word:

1. Noun to Verb:
   - "He gave an AGREEMENT to the terms." ==> "He AGREED to the terms."
2. Adjective to Adverb:
   - "She gave a CAREFUL response." ==> "She responded CAREFULLY."
3. Verb to Noun:
   - "He SUCCEEDED in the venture." ==> "He achieved SUCCESS in the venture."
4. Adjective to Noun:
   - "He is HONEST in all dealings." ==> "He conducts all dealings with HONESTY."
