1. THE LEXICAL SPECTRUM OF CONFUSABLE WORDS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Understanding the precise boundary definitions across English vocabulary:

  A. HOMOPHONES (Same Sound, Different Spelling & Meaning):
     - Sound identical when pronounced phonetically.
     - Spelled differently and have completely independent etymologies.
     - Examples: Affect / Effect, Principal / Principle, Site / Sight / Cite.

  B. HOMOGRAPHS / HETERONYMS (Same Spelling, Different Sound & Meaning):
     - Spelled identically.
     - Pronunciation (and syllable stress) differs depending on Word Class.
     - Examples: OB-ject (Noun) vs ob-JECT (Verb); PRE-sent (Noun) vs pre-SENT (Verb).

  C. PARONYMS (Shared Etymological Root, Distinct Semantic/Syntactic Application):
     - Derived from the same Latin or Greek root word.
     - Diverged into distinct words with specific semantic domains.
     - Examples: Historic (Famous) vs Historical (Pertaining to history); 
       Continual (Repeated with pauses) vs Continuous (Unbroken without pauses).

2. HIGH-FREQUENCY HOMOPHONE PAIRS (THE GRAND MATRIX)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. AFFECT vs EFFECT:
   • Affect (Verb): To influence or make an impact.
     -> "The bad weather will AFFECT the harvest."
   • Effect (Noun): The outcome, result, or impact.
     -> "The medication had a rapid therapeutic EFFECT."
   • Effect (Verb - Rare/Formal): To bring about or implement.
     -> "The manager EFFECTED sweeping organizational changes."

2. PRINCIPAL vs PRINCIPLE:
   • Principal (Noun/Adj): Chief, head of an institution, capital sum.
     -> "The school PRINCIPAL addressed the morning assembly."
     -> "The PRINCIPAL reason for our success is consistency."
   • Principle (Noun): Fundamental moral doctrine or scientific law.
     -> "He is a man of uncompromised moral PRINCIPLES."

3. COMPLIMENT vs COMPLEMENT:
   • Compliment (Noun/Verb - with 'i'): Praise, admiration, flattery.
     -> "He paid her a charming COMPLIMENT on her painting."
   • Complement (Noun/Verb - with 'e'): Complete, balance, enhance together.
     -> "The white sauce COMPLEMENTS the grilled asparagus perfectly."

4. STATIONARY vs STATIONERY:
   • Stationary (Adj - with 'ar'): Fixed, motionless, standing still.
     -> "The car collided with a STATIONARY truck."
   • Stationery (Noun - with 'er'): Writing materials, pens, paper, envelopes.
     -> "Buy writing pads from the college STATIONERY shop."
   (Mnemonic: StationERy has 'e' for Envelopes!)

5. SITE vs SIGHT vs CITE:
   • Site (Noun): Physical location or web domain. ("Construction site")
   • Sight (Noun): Vision or a spectacle. ("A magnificent sight")
   • Cite (Verb): To quote, mention, or reference. ("Cite three research papers")

6. DESERT vs DESSERT:
   • Desert (Noun - stress on 1st syllable): Arid, sandy wilderness.
   • Desert (Verb - stress on 2nd syllable): To abandon or leave stranded.
   • Dessert (Noun - double 's'): Sweet culinary dish after a meal.

4. THE HETERONYM SYLLABLE-STRESS LAW
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
When a two-syllable word functions both as a NOUN and a VERB:
  • NOUN  -> Primary stress on the FIRST syllable.
  • VERB  -> Primary stress on the SECOND syllable.

Examples:
  - PRE-sent (Noun: a gift)         vs   pre-SENT (Verb: to introduce/show)
  - OB-ject (Noun: a physical item) vs   ob-JECT (Verb: to oppose)
  - RE-cord (Noun: data archive)    vs   re-CORD (Verb: to capture audio/data)
  - PRO-ject (Noun: an enterprise)  vs   pro-JECT (Verb: to cast forward/plan)
  - CON-duct (Noun: behavior)       vs   con-DUCT (Verb: to lead/direct)
