1. WHAT IS A PREPOSITION? (SYNTACTIC ARCHITECTURE)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A Preposition is a closed-class functional word positioned before a Noun, Pronoun, 
or Noun Phrase (the "Object of the Preposition") to establish relational links:
  - Temporal Relations (When an event occurs)
  - Spatial Relations (Where an entity resides)
  - Directional / Motion Relations (Whither or whence movement flows)
  - Logical & Agentive Relations (Who performs, what tool is used, what material)

Formula:
  [Head Element: Verb / Noun / Adjective] + [Preposition] + [Object of Preposition]
  Example: "She walked [Verb] INTO [Preposition] the auditorium [Noun Object]."

2. THE TIME PYRAMID: AT -> ON -> IN
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
                 /\
                /  \        AT: Precise points & clock times
               / AT \       • at 5:30 PM, at noon, at midnight, at dawn, at dusk
              /------\
             /   ON   \     ON: Days and specific calendar dates
            /          \    • on Monday, on 15th August, on New Year's Day
           /------------\
          /      IN      \  IN: Long periods, months, years, seasons, centuries
         /                \ • in July, in 2026, in summer, in the 21st century
        --------------------

Special Time Collocation Matrix:
+-------------------+--------------------+--------------------+
| AT                | ON                 | IN                 |
+-------------------+--------------------+--------------------+
| at 10 o'clock     | on Sunday          | in April           |
| at midnight       | on Friday evening  | in 1947            |
| at dawn / dusk    | on Christmas Day   | in the morning     |
| at lunch time     | on my birthday     | in the afternoon   |
| at the weekend*   | on July 4th        | in the evening     |
| at present        | on that day        | in the Middle Ages |
+-------------------+--------------------+--------------------+
*Note: British English favours "at the weekend"; American English favours "on the weekend".

3. TIME DISTINCTIONS: HIGH-YIELD EXAM TRAPS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A. FOR vs SINCE vs DURING:
   - FOR + Duration / Period of time (for 3 hours, for 5 years, for centuries)
   - SINCE + Specific Starting Point (since Monday, since 2010, since 8 AM)
   - DURING + Known Event / Period Noun (during the concert, during World War II)

B. BY vs UNTIL (TILL):
   - BY = "No later than a deadline" (Single completion point).
     Example: "Submit the assignment BY Friday 5 PM."
   - UNTIL = "Continuing non-stop up to a boundary point" (Durative state).
     Example: "We will stay in the library UNTIL 8 PM."

C. IN vs WITHIN:
   - IN + Period = "At the end / after the lapse of that duration".
     Example: "The train arrives IN 20 minutes" (= after 20 minutes).
   - WITHIN + Period = "Before the expiration of that duration".
     Example: "You must solve the paper WITHIN 3 hours" (= before 3 hours elapse).

4. PREPOSITIONS OF PLACE & SPATIAL HIERARCHY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. AT (Point / Specific Location / Address):
   - at the bus stop, at the entrance, at 221B Baker Street, at the corner.
2. ON (2D Surface / Line / Floor):
   - on the table, on the wall, on the ceiling, on the 3rd floor, on MG Road.
3. IN (3D Enclosed Space / City / Region / Country):
   - in the box, in the room, in Kolkata, in India, in the forest.

Hierarchical Rule:
  "Small Locality AT, Large City/State IN"
  Correct: "He resides AT Barrackpore IN North 24 Parganas."

Vertical & Contact Matrix:
+----------------+--------------------------+---------------------------------+
| Preposition    | Physical Contact         | Orientation / Semantics         |
+----------------+--------------------------+---------------------------------+
| ON             | YES                      | Resting on top of a surface     |
| OVER           | NO (usually) / Direct    | Directly above or spanning over |
| ABOVE          | NO                       | Higher altitude / level         |
| UNDER          | NO / Direct              | Directly below                  |
| BELOW          | NO                       | Lower level / grade / altitude  |
+----------------+--------------------------+---------------------------------+

5. DYNAMIC DIRECTION & MOTION PREPOSITIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A. IN vs INTO:
   - IN: Static presence inside. ("He is swimming in the pool.")
   - INTO: Dynamic entrance from outside to inside. ("He dived into the pool.")

B. ON vs ONTO:
   - ON: Static presence on a surface. ("The cat sat on the rug.")
   - ONTO: Dynamic leaping/landing on a surface. ("The cat jumped onto the roof.")

C. THROUGH vs ACROSS:
   - THROUGH: Traversing a 3D enclosed medium (forest, tunnel, crowd, pipe).
   - ACROSS: Crossing a 2D surface or flat plane (road, field, bridge, river).

D. ALONG vs PAST:
   - ALONG: Parallel to the linear path of a river, road, or wall.
   - PAST: Moving beyond a specific point of interest.

6. DISTRIBUTION, AGENCY & MATERIAL TRANSFORMATIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A. BETWEEN vs AMONG:
   - BETWEEN: Used for TWO entities, OR for MORE THAN TWO when distinct, 
     individual, named reciprocal entities are specified (e.g. Treaties).
     Example: "Negotiations BETWEEN India, Bhutan, and Nepal."
   - AMONG: Used for indefinite, uncounted groups or crowds (3 or more).
     Example: "Distribute the sweets AMONG the villagers."

B. AGENCY (BY) vs INSTRUMENTALITY (WITH):
   - BY + Living Agent / Doer: "The portrait was painted BY Sukanta Sir."
   - WITH + Instrument / Tool / Weapon: "He cut the ribbon WITH a pair of scissors."

C. MADE OF vs MADE FROM:
   - MADE OF: Physical substance remains unchanged in chemical nature.
     Example: "The ring is MADE OF 24-carat gold."
   - MADE FROM: Chemical transformation converts the raw material into a new product.
     Example: "Cheese is MADE FROM milk." / "Paper is MADE FROM wood pulp."
