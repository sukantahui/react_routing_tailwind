MODULE 003.002: DEGREES OF COMPARISON & SYNTACTIC TRANSFORMATION
================================================================================

1. THE THREE DEGREES OF COMPARISON
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Adjectives change form (or take periphrastic auxiliaries) to show different 
levels of intensity and comparison:

  1. POSITIVE DEGREE: Denotes simple quality without direct comparison.
     - "Rahul is a TALL boy."
     - Formula: as + Positive + as / so + Positive + as
  2. COMPARATIVE DEGREE: Compares the quality between TWO entities.
     - "Rahul is TALLER than Rohan."
     - Formula: Comparative + than
  3. SUPERLATIVE DEGREE: Denotes the highest degree among THREE OR MORE entities.
     - "Rahul is the TALLEST boy in the class."
     - Formula: the + Superlative


2. MORPHOLOGICAL FORMATION & IRREGULAR SETS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  A. Regular Formation:
     * Monosyllabic words add '-er' / '-est':
       - tall -> taller -> tallest
       - brave -> braver -> bravest
       - hot -> hotter -> hottest (consonant doubling)
     * Polysyllabic words (2+ syllables) take 'more' / 'most':
       - beautiful -> more beautiful -> most beautiful
       - intelligent -> more intelligent -> most intelligent

  B. Irregular Comparison Sets (CRITICAL):
+--------------------+-------------------------+-------------------------------+
| POSITIVE           | COMPARATIVE             | SUPERLATIVE                   |
+--------------------+-------------------------+-------------------------------+
| Good / Well        | Better                  | Best                          |
| Bad / Ill / Evil   | Worse                   | Worst                         |
| Little             | Less / Lesser           | Least                         |
| Much / Many        | More                    | Most                          |
| Late (Time)        | Later                   | Latest                        |
| Late (Position)    | Latter                  | Last                          |
| Old (General)      | Older                   | Oldest                        |
| Old (Kinship)      | Elder                   | Eldest                        |
| Far (Physical dist)| Farther                 | Farthest                      |
| Far (Degree/Depth) | Further                 | Furthest                      |
| In                 | Inner                   | Innermost / Inmost            |
| Up                 | Upper                   | Uppermost / Upmost            |
| Out                | Outer / Utter           | Outermost / Utmost            |
+--------------------+-------------------------+-------------------------------+


3. LATIN COMPARATIVES (FOLLOWED BY 'TO', NEVER 'THAN')
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Certain adjectives borrowed from Latin ending in '-ior' (plus 'preferable') are 
inherently comparative and take 'TO':
  - SENIOR, JUNIOR, SUPERIOR, INFERIOR, PRIOR, ANTERIOR, POSTERIOR, PREFERABLE.

  * INCORRECT: He is senior than me in the organization.
  * CORRECT:   He is senior TO me in the organization.

  * INCORRECT: Coffee is more preferable than tea.
  * CORRECT:   Coffee is preferable TO tea. (No 'more', no 'than')


4. ABSOLUTE / NON-GRADABLE ADJECTIVES (RESIST COMPARISON)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Qualities that denote absolute states cannot logically take degrees:
  - UNIQUE, PERFECT, DEAD, COMPLETE, UNIVERSAL, CHIEF, SQUARE, ROUND,
    ETERNAL, SUPREME, ENTIRE, FATAL, EMPTY.

  * INCORRECT: This is the most unique ancient artifact.
  * CORRECT:   This is a unique ancient artifact.


5. THE TWO MASTER TRANSFORMATION FORMULAE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
================================================================================
TYPE 1: ABSOLUTE / ALL-ENCOMPASSING COMPARISON
================================================================================
  [SUPERLATIVE]:  Subject + Verb + THE + Superlative + Singular Noun...
                  -> "Shakespeare is THE GREATEST dramatist in English."

  [COMPARATIVE]:  Subject + Verb + Comparative + THAN ANY OTHER + Singular Noun...
                  -> "Shakespeare is GREATER THAN ANY OTHER dramatist in English."

  [POSITIVE]:     NO OTHER + Singular Noun + Verb + AS/SO + Positive + AS + Subject
                  -> "NO OTHER dramatist in English is AS GREAT AS Shakespeare."

================================================================================
TYPE 2: "ONE OF THE..." / GROUP COMPARISON
================================================================================
  [SUPERLATIVE]:  Subject + Verb + ONE OF THE + Superlative + Plural Noun...
                  -> "Kolkata is ONE OF THE LARGEST cities in India."

  [COMPARATIVE]:  Subject + Verb + Comparative + THAN MOST OTHER + Plural Noun...
                  -> "Kolkata is LARGER THAN MOST OTHER cities in India."

  [POSITIVE]:     VERY FEW + Plural Noun + Verb (Plural) + AS + Positive + AS + Subj
                  -> "VERY FEW cities in India ARE AS LARGE AS Kolkata."


6. CRITICAL SPECIAL COMPARATIVE RULES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  RULE 1: ILLOGICAL COMPARISONS (That of / Those of)
    * INCORRECT: The streets of London are wider than Paris.
    * CORRECT:   The streets of London are wider than THOSE OF Paris.
    * INCORRECT: The climate of Chennai is hotter than Kolkata.
    * CORRECT:   The climate of Chennai is hotter than THAT OF Kolkata.

  RULE 2: COMPARING TWO QUALITIES OF THE SAME PERSON
    * INCORRECT: He is braver than wise.
    * CORRECT:   He is MORE BRAVE than wise. (Use 'more + positive' for dual traits)

  RULE 3: SELECTION OF TWO ('OF THE TWO')
    * When choosing between exactly two, the comparative takes 'THE':
      - "Of the two sisters, Priya is THE PRETTIER." (NOT: prettiest)

  RULE 4: PARALLEL / PROPORTIONAL COMPARISON
    * "THE + Comparative ..., THE + Comparative ..."
      - "THE MORE you study, THE BETTER your results will be."

  RULE 5: SELF-EXCLUSION WITH 'ANY OTHER'
    * In-class: "Iron is more useful than ANY OTHER metal." (Iron is a metal)
    * Out-of-class: "Diamond is harder than ANY metal." (Diamond is not a metal)
