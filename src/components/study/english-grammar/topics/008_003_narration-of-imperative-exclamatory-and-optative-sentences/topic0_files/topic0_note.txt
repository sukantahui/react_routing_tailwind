1. NARRATION OF IMPERATIVE SENTENCES (COMMANDS, REQUESTS, ADVICE)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Imperative sentences express orders, advice, requests, warnings, or prohibitions.

Conversion Formula:
  [Speaker] + [NUANCED REPORTING VERB] + [Personal Object] + [TO + V1...]
  - For Negative Imperatives: [NOT TO + V1...]

Reporting Verbs Portfolio:
  • Command / Order:   Ordered, Commanded, Directed
  • Polite Request:    Requested, Begged, Implored, Entreated, Pleaded
  • Counsel / Caution: Advised, Urged, Warned, Instructed
  • Prohibition:       FORBADE (Never use 'not' with forbade!)

Canonical Examples:
  • Direct:   The officer said to the soldier, "Halt!"
  • Indirect: The officer COMMANDED the soldier TO HALT.
  • Direct:   Mother said, "Do not touch the fire."
  • Indirect: Mother FORBADE me TO TOUCH the fire. (OR: Advised me NOT TO touch)

2. THE DUAL-BRANCH MECHANICS OF "LET" SENTENCES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BRANCH 1: "LET US..." (Mutual Proposal or Suggestion)
  • Meaning: An invitation to collaborative action.
  • Formula: [Subject] + PROPOSED / SUGGESTED TO [Object] + THAT [We/They] + SHOULD + [V1...]
  • Direct:   Rohan said, "Let us begin the discussion."
  • Indirect: Rohan PROPOSED THAT THEY SHOULD BEGIN the discussion.

BRANCH 2: "LET ME / LET HIM..." (Request for Permission)
  • Meaning: Seeking authorization or freedom to act.
  • Formula: [Subject] + REQUESTED + THAT [He/She] + MIGHT BE ALLOWED TO + [V1...]
  • Direct:   The boy said to the teacher, "Let me go home early today."
  • Indirect: The boy REQUESTED the teacher THAT HE MIGHT BE ALLOWED TO GO home early that day.
              (Alternative: "The boy requested the teacher to let him go home early that day.")

3. NARRATION OF EXCLAMATORY SENTENCES (EMOTIONS & SURPRISE)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Exclamatory sentences express sudden bursts of emotional intensity.

Conversion Formula:
  [Subject] + EXCLAIMED WITH [EMOTION] + THAT + [Declarative Clause (Subj + Verb...)]

Interjection to Emotion Translation Matrix:
+--------------------+---------------------------------------------------------+
| Direct Interjection| Indirect Reporting Phrase                               |
+--------------------+---------------------------------------------------------+
| Hurrah! / Ha!      | Exclaimed with joy / delight that...                    |
| Alas! / Oh no!     | Exclaimed with sorrow / grief that...                   |
| What a...! / How..!| Exclaimed with wonder / astonishment that (it was very..|
| Bravo! / Well done!| Applauded [someone] saying that...                      |
| Fie! / Pooh! / Ugh!| Exclaimed with contempt / disgust that...               |
+--------------------+---------------------------------------------------------+

Exclamatory Phrase Expansion:
  • Direct:   "What a glorious sunset!"
  • Indirect: He exclaimed with wonder THAT IT WAS A VERY GLORIOUS SUNSET.

================================================================================
4. NARRATION OF OPTATIVE SENTENCES (WISHES, PRAYERS, BLESSINGS, CURSES)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Optative sentences express desires, blessings, invocations, or curses.

Conversion Formula:
  [Subject] + PRAYED / WISHED / BLESSED / CURSED + THAT + [Subject] + MIGHT + [V1...]

Canonical Examples:
  • Direct:   The hermit said, "May God forgive your transgressions!"
  • Indirect: The hermit PRAYED THAT GOD MIGHT FORGIVE his transgressions.
  • Direct:   The grandmother said to me, "May you achieve great success!"
  • Indirect: The grandmother WISHED / BLESSED THAT I MIGHT ACHIEVE great success.
  • Direct:   The crowd shouted, "Long live the King!"
  • Indirect: The crowd PRAYED THAT THE KING MIGHT LIVE LONG.

================================================================================
5. MASTER SUMMARY OF REPORTED DISCOURSE PUNCTUATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  1. ALL indirect sentences (Statements, Questions, Commands, Exclamations, Prayers) 
     MUST terminate with a single FULL STOP (.).
  2. Question marks (?), Exclamation marks (!), and Quotation marks (" ") are 
     PERMANENTLY REMOVED in indirect speech.
