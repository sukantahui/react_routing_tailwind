1. WHAT IS A CLAUSE? (PRINCIPAL vs SUBORDINATE)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A Clause is a syntactic unit containing a Subject and a Finite Verb Predicate.

  • Principal (Independent) Clause: Can stand alone as a complete sentence.
  • Subordinate (Dependent) Clause: Cannot stand alone; depends on a Principal 
    clause for complete meaning.

Three Major Functional Classes of Subordinate Clauses:
  1. NOUN CLAUSE      (Performs the job of a Noun)
  2. ADJECTIVE CLAUSE (Modifies an Antecedent Noun / Pronoun)
  3. ADVERBIAL CLAUSE (Modifies a Verb, Adjective, or another Adverb)

================================================================================
2. NOUN CLAUSES: THE 5 SYNTACTIC ROLES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A Noun Clause answers "WHAT?" or "WHO?" in relation to the main sentence elements.

1. SUBJECT OF A VERB:
   - "THAT he is innocent is known to all."
   - "WHAT you just said makes complete sense."

2. OBJECT OF A TRANSITIVE VERB:
   - "I know THAT honesty is the best policy."
   - "She explained WHY she was absent."

3. OBJECT OF A PREPOSITION:
   - "Pay strict attention TO [what the professor demonstrates]."
   - "There is no meaning IN [what he claims]."

4. SUBJECT COMPLEMENT (After Linking Verbs like 'is', 'was', 'seems'):
   - "The reality is THAT we have run out of funds."
   - "My fear was THAT the ship might sink."

5. IN APPOSITION TO A NOUN (Explains the internal content of a Noun):
   - "The news THAT the king died shocked the nation."
   - "The rumour THAT the bank failed is completely baseless."

3. ADJECTIVE (RELATIVE) CLAUSES: DEFINING vs NON-DEFINING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
An Adjective Clause modifies a preceding Noun/Pronoun (called the "Antecedent").

+------------------------+--------------------------+--------------------------+
| Feature                | Defining (Restrictive)   | Non-Defining (Parenthetical)
+------------------------+--------------------------+--------------------------+
| Function               | Essential to identify    | Extra, non-essential     |
|                        | WHICH specific item      | descriptive information  |
| Punctuation            | NO COMMAS                | ENCLOSED IN COMMAS ( , ) |
| Pronouns for Things    | THAT or WHICH            | WHICH ONLY (Never That!) |
| Pronoun Omission       | YES (if object of clause)| NEVER                    |
| Canonical Example      | "The car THAT I bought is| "My Ferrari, WHICH is red|
|                        | exceptionally fast."     | , is parked outside."    |
+------------------------+--------------------------+--------------------------+

Relative Pronoun Omission (Contact Clauses):
  - "The book WHICH I read yesterday." -> "The book I read yesterday." (Object pronoun omitted).
  - Note: You can NEVER omit a Subject relative pronoun: "The man WHO called me" (Wrong to say: "The man called me").

================================================================================
4. ADVERBIAL CLAUSES: THE 9 FUNCTIONAL CLASSES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Adverbial clauses modify verbs, adjectives, or adverbs in the principal clause.

1. TIME (When?): when, while, before, after, as soon as, since, until.
   - "As soon as the train arrived, passengers rushed."
2. PLACE (Where?): where, wherever, whence, whither.
   - "Fools rush in WHERE angels fear to tread."
3. CAUSE / REASON (Why?): because, since, as, for.
   - "He was promoted BECAUSE he solved the crisis."
4. PURPOSE (With what aim?): so that, in order that, lest (should).
   - "Study diligently SO THAT you may achieve mastery."
5. RESULT / CONSEQUENCE (With what effect?): so... that, such... that.
   - "He was SO exhausted THAT he collapsed onto the couch."
6. CONDITION (On what terms?): if, unless, provided that, in case.
   - "UNLESS you work systematically, success is impossible."
7. CONCESSION / CONTRAST (In spite of what?): although, though, even though.
   - "ALTHOUGH he was fatigued, he continued the march."
8. COMPARISON / DEGREE (To what extent?): than, as... as, so... as.
   - "She is more insightful THAN her contemporaries are."
9. MANNER (In what way?): as, as if, as though.
   - "He spoke AS IF he owned the corporation."

5. STEP-BY-STEP CLAUSE PARSING ALGORITHM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Step 1: Identify all Finite Verbs in the sentence. (N verbs = N clauses).
Step 2: Identify the Subordinating Connectives (that, who, which, although, because).
Step 3: Isolate the Principal Clause (the clause that makes complete sense alone).
Step 4: Analyze each Subordinate Clause:
        - If it answers "What?" -> Noun Clause (Check 5 roles).
        - If it modifies a preceding Noun -> Adjective Clause (Check defining vs non-defining).
        - If it explains time, reason, condition, manner -> Adverbial Clause (Check 9 types).
