1. WHAT IS AN ADVERB? (SYNTACTIC SCOPE)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
An adverb is a modifier that adds descriptive precision to:
  1. A VERB:                 "She spoke ELOQUENTLY."
  2. AN ADJECTIVE:           "The tea is EXTREMELY hot."
  3. ANOTHER ADVERB:         "He ran VERY quickly."
  4. A PREPOSITIONAL PHRASE: "The bird flew RIGHT over the roof."
  5. A COMPLETE CLAUSE:      "FORTUNATELY, nobody was injured."


2. THE 7 FUNCTIONAL CLASSES OF ADVERBS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
+-------------------+----------------------------+-----------------------------+
| CLASS             | DEFINING QUESTION          | CANONICAL EXAMPLES          |
+-------------------+----------------------------+-----------------------------+
| 1. Manner         | How? In what way?          | quickly, softly, bravely,   |
|                   |                            | beautifully, well, fast     |
+-------------------+----------------------------+-----------------------------+
| 2. Place          | Where? In what direction?  | here, there, everywhere,    |
|                   |                            | forward, outside, upstairs  |
+-------------------+----------------------------+-----------------------------+
| 3. Time           | When? At what point?       | now, yesterday, soon, late, |
|                   |                            | already, recently, today    |
+-------------------+----------------------------+-----------------------------+
| 4. Frequency      | How often?                 | always, never, often, rarely|
|                   |                            | seldom, usually, frequently |
+-------------------+----------------------------+-----------------------------+
| 5. Degree/Quantity| How much? To what extent?  | very, extremely, quite, too,|
|                   |                            | enough, almost, nearly      |
+-------------------+----------------------------+-----------------------------+
| 6. Reason/Purpose | Why? On what grounds?      | therefore, hence, thus, why |
+-------------------+----------------------------+-----------------------------+
| 7. Sentence/Disjunct| Speaker's perspective on | fortunately, obviously,     |
|                   | the entire situation       | certainly, honestly         |
+-------------------+----------------------------+-----------------------------+


3. FLAT ADVERBS VS DIVERGENT '-LY' PAIRS (EXAM TRAPS!)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Flat adverbs maintain the identical form as their adjectives. Adding '-ly' 
creates an entirely different word with a divergent meaning:

+-------------+-------------------------------+--------------------------------+
| BASE / FLAT | MEANING & EXAMPLE             | DIVERGENT '-LY' FORM & MEANING |
+-------------+-------------------------------+--------------------------------+
| HARD        | With great effort / energy    | HARDLY: Scarcely / Almost not  |
|             | "He works HARD."              | "He HARDLY works." (Lazy)      |
+-------------+-------------------------------+--------------------------------+
| LATE        | Behind schedule / after time  | LATELY: Recently / in recent   |
|             | "He arrived LATE."            | "I haven't seen him LATELY."   |
+-------------+-------------------------------+--------------------------------+
| NEAR        | Close in physical space       | NEARLY: Almost / closely       |
|             | "Come NEAR."                  | "He NEARLY crashed the car."   |
+-------------+-------------------------------+--------------------------------+
| DIRECT      | Straight without stopping     | DIRECTLY: Immediately / at once|
|             | "Fly DIRECT to London."       | "I will call you DIRECTLY."    |
+-------------+-------------------------------+--------------------------------+
| HIGH        | Great physical altitude       | HIGHLY: Deeply / very much     |
|             | "The kite flew HIGH."         | "A HIGHLY praised scholar."    |
+-------------+-------------------------------+--------------------------------+
| FAST        | High speed (Flat Adverb only) | [NO SUCH WORD AS 'FASTLY']     |
|             | "He drives FAST."             |                                |
+-------------+-------------------------------+--------------------------------+


4. THE THREE SENTENCE POSITIONS OF ADVERBS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  A. FRONT POSITION (Opening sentence):
     - Connecting adverbs & Sentence adverbs:
       "SUDDENLY, the lights went out."
       "FORTUNATELY, the backup generator started."

  B. MID POSITION (Central predicate):
     - Frequency, degree, and focus adverbs:
       Rule 1: BEFORE single lexical main verbs:
               "She ALWAYS arrives on time."
       Rule 2: AFTER the verb 'to be' (am/is/are/was/were):
               "He is NEVER late."
       Rule 3: AFTER the FIRST auxiliary verb in multi-word verb phrases:
               "She has ALWAYS been praised for her honesty."

  C. END POSITION (Post-verbal):
     - Manner, place, and definite time adverbs:
       "He spoke softly."
       "We met in Delhi yesterday."


5. THE ROYAL ORDER OF ADVERBS: MPT (MANNER -> PLACE -> TIME)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
When multiple adverbial modifiers appear together at the end of a clause, 
they follow the MPT sequence:

  [1] MANNER (How)  ->  [2] PLACE (Where)  ->  [3] TIME (When)

  CANONICAL MASTER EXAMPLES:
  * "She sang BEAUTIFULLY (Manner) IN THE AUDITORIUM (Place) YESTERDAY (Time)."
  * "The troops marched VALIANTLY (Manner) INTO THE CITY (Place) AT DAWN (Time)."


6. THE DELICATE PLACEMENT OF 'ONLY' (7 MEANINGS)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
'Only' modifies the exact constituent immediately following it:
  1. ONLY he told me that he loved her.       (Nobody else told me)
  2. He ONLY told me that he loved her.       (He merely told me, didn't write)
  3. He told ONLY me that he loved her.       (He told no other person)
  4. He told me ONLY that he loved her.       (He stated nothing else)
  5. He told me that ONLY he loved her.       (Nobody else loved her)
  6. He told me that he ONLY loved her.       (He merely loved, didn't marry)
  7. He told me that he loved ONLY her.       (He loved no other woman)


7. CRITICAL SYNTACTIC PROHIBITIONS & IDIOMS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  RULE 1: NEVER Separate Transitive Verb from Direct Object
    * INCORRECT: He closed quietly the door.
    * CORRECT:   He closed the door quietly. / He quietly closed the door.

  RULE 2: Special Position of 'ENOUGH'
    * Adverb of Degree: Follows adjective/adverb -> "tall ENOUGH", "fast ENOUGH"
    * Determiner/Adjective: Precedes noun        -> "ENOUGH money", "ENOUGH time"

  RULE 3: Standard Negative Correlative Pairs
    * Standard: "He seldom OR never makes a mistake." (Absolute negation)
    * Standard: "He seldom IF ever makes a mistake." (Conditional rarity)
    * INCORRECT: He seldom or ever makes a mistake. (Fatal exam error!)
