1. WHAT IS A PHRASE?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A Phrase is a group of words acting as a single conceptual unit or part of speech 
WITHOUT containing a Subject + Finite Verb combination.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. THE 5 MAJOR FUNCTIONAL PHRASE CATEGORIES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
+---+----------------------+--------------------+-------------------------------+
| # | Phrase Category      | Head / Core Unit   | Structural Exemplar           |
+---+----------------------+--------------------+-------------------------------+
| 1 | Noun Phrase (NP)     | Head Noun          | "The young scholar from BT Rd"|
| 2 | Verb Phrase (VP)     | Auxiliaries + Verb | "has been coding diligently"  |
| 3 | Prepositional (PP)   | Preposition + Obj  | "in front of the classroom"   |
| 4 | Adjective Phrase(AdjP)| Head Adjective    | "extremely passionate about AI"|
| 5 | Adverb Phrase (AdvP) | Head Adverb        | "with incredible speed"       |
+---+----------------------+--------------------+-------------------------------+

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. SPECIALIZED PHRASE ARCHITECTURES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A. APPOSITIVE PHRASE:
   Renames or defines a preceding noun:
   - "Sukanta Sir, OUR MASTER GRAMMAR MENTOR, explained the syntax tree."

B. PARTICIPIAL PHRASE:
   Present (-ing) or Past (-ed/en) participle acting as an adjective modifier:
   - "WALKING ALONG THE RIVERBANK, Swadeep watched the sunset."

C. INFINITIVE PHRASE:
   'To + Base Verb' functioning nominally, adjectivally, or adverbially:
   - "TO MASTER FULL-STACK ARCHITECTURE requires immense dedication."

D. GERUND PHRASE:
   '-ing' verb form acting strictly as a noun (Subject, Object, or Prep Object):
   - "Debangshu enjoys BUILDING MODERN REACT INTERFACES."

E. ABSOLUTE PHRASE:
   Noun + Participle modifying the entire sentence independently:
   - "THE WEATHER BEING INCLEMENT, the outdoor tournament was cancelled."

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. BENGALI LEARNER BRIDGES & EXAM TRAPS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. PHRASE VS CLAUSE DISTINCTION:
   * Phrase: "In the morning" (No subject, no finite verb).
   * Clause: "When the morning arrived" (Subject 'morning' + Finite Verb 'arrived').

2. CONDENSATION DRILL:
   * Adjective Phrase -> Adjective: "A man OF GREAT COURAGE" -> "A COURAGEOUS man".
   * Adverb Phrase -> Adverb: "In a BRAVE MANNER" -> "BRAVELY".
