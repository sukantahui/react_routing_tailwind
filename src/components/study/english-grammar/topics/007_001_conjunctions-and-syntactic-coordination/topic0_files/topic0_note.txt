1. WHAT IS A CONJUNCTION? (SYNTACTIC BRIDGES)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A Conjunction is a functional part of speech that connects words, phrases, or 
clauses, establishing logical, temporal, causal, or coordinate relationships.

Three Major Classes of Conjunctions:
  1. COORDINATING CONJUNCTIONS (FANBOYS)
  2. SUBORDINATING CONJUNCTIONS
  3. CORRELATIVE CONJUNCTIONS (Paired Connectors)

================================================================================
2. COORDINATING CONJUNCTIONS: THE "FANBOYS" FAMILY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Join grammatically equal elements (Noun with Noun, Phrase with Phrase, Independent Clause with Independent Clause).

  • F - For      (Cause / Reason: "He stayed calm, FOR he knew the truth.")
  • A - And      (Addition: "She sang, AND he played the piano.")
  • N - Nor      (Negative Addition: "He doesn't smoke, NOR does he drink.")
  • B - But      (Contrast: "She worked hard, BUT she failed.")
  • O - Or       (Alternative / Choice: "Study diligently, OR you will fail.")
  • Y - Yet      (Unexpected Concession: "He is wealthy, YET he is humble.")
  • S - So       (Result / Consequence: "It rained heavily, SO we stayed home.")

Punctuation Law for FANBOYS:
  [Independent Clause 1] + [ , ] + [FANBOYS Conjunction] + [Independent Clause 2]
  - Example: "The storm arrived, and the lights failed." (Comma MANDATORY)
  - If joining single verbs or compound predicates (no new subject), DO NOT USE COMMA:
    "The storm arrived and destroyed the trees." (No comma!)

================================================================================
3. SUBORDINATING CONJUNCTIONS (CLAUSE ANCHORS)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Connect an Independent (Principal) Clause with a Dependent (Subordinate) Clause.

+----------------+------------------------------+------------------------------+
| Category       | Common Connectives           | Example                      |
+----------------+------------------------------+------------------------------+
| Time           | when, while, before, after,  | As soon as the bell rang,    |
|                | as soon as, until, since     | they left the hall.          |
| Cause / Reason | because, as, since           | Since you are tired, rest.   |
| Purpose        | so that, in order that, LEST | Walk fast lest you miss it.  |
| Condition      | if, unless, provided that    | Unless you work, no reward.  |
| Concession     | although, though, whereas    | Although poor, he is honest. |
| Manner / Comp  | as if, as though, as         | He acts as if he were king.  |
+----------------+------------------------------+------------------------------+

THE LAW OF "LEST":
  • "Lest" inherently means "so that... not" (পাছে ঘটে / যাতে না ঘটে).
  • Formula: [Main Clause] + LEST + [Subject] + SHOULD + [V1]
  • Rule 1: Never use 'not' with lest. (Double negative error)
  • Rule 2: Always use 'should' or bare subjunctive, never 'will/may'.

4. CORRELATIVE CONJUNCTIONS & THE LAW OF PARALLELISM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Paired conjunctions that work as a team to link balanced grammatical units.

Standard Pairs:
  • Not only ... but also
  • Either ... or
  • Neither ... nor
  • Both ... and (NEVER 'both... as well as')
  • Whether ... or
  • Scarcely / Hardly ... when (NEVER 'than')
  • No sooner ... than (NEVER 'when' or 'then')

THE PARALLELISM BALANCE SCALE LAW:
Whatever syntactic unit follows Connector 1 MUST be the exact same syntactic unit 
following Connector 2!

  WRONG: "He [NOT ONLY] lost his wallet [Noun Phrase] [BUT ALSO] his passport."
         (Here 'lost' is placed before 'not only', leaving unbalanced verbs).
  RIGHT: "He lost [NOT ONLY] his wallet [Noun Phrase] [BUT ALSO] his passport [Noun Phrase]."

  WRONG: "She [EITHER] can choose French or German."
  RIGHT: "She can choose [EITHER] French [Noun] [OR] German [Noun]."

================================================================================
5. CONJUNCTIVE ADVERBS & SEMICOLON PUNCTUATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Conjunctive Adverbs (However, Therefore, Moreover, Furthermore, Nevertheless, Consequently) 
are NOT conjunctions; they are transition adverbs.

When linking two independent clauses, use a SEMICOLON before and a COMMA after:
  Formula: [Independent Clause 1] ; [Conjunctive Adverb] , [Independent Clause 2]
  - Example: "He studied diligently; however, he found the paper challenging."
