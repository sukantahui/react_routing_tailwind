1. THE PRIMARY NOMINAL & VERBAL TRIAD
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Every standard English clause requires a Subject entity (Noun or Pronoun) and
an Action/State engine (Verb).

2. NOUNS (THE NAMING ANCHORS)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- 5 Major Families:
  1. Proper Noun: Specific unique names (Kolkata, Barrackpore, Sukanta Hui).
  2. Common Noun: General class names (city, student, teacher, laptop).
  3. Collective Noun: Unitary groups (jury, committee, team, flock).
  4. Material Noun: Physical matter/substances (gold, water, iron, cotton).
  5. Abstract Noun: Qualities, states, feelings (wisdom, honesty, courage).
- Syntactic Roles: Subject, Direct Object, Indirect Object, Subject Complement,
  Object Complement, Object of Preposition.

3. PRONOUNS (THE DEICTIC SUBSTITUTES)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Personal: Nominative (I, he, she), Objective (me, him, her), Possessive (mine, hers).
- Demonstrative: this, that, these, those.
- Relative: who, whom, whose, which, that (links subordinate clauses).
- Indefinite: someone, everyone, nobody, none, all, few.
- Reflexive & Emphatic: myself, himself, themselves.
- Distributive & Reciprocal: each, neither, either, each other, one another.

4. VERBS (THE DYNAMIC PREDICATE ENGINE)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Finite vs Non-Finite:
  * Finite: Tensed, inflects for Person/Number ("She writes code").
  * Non-Finite: Untensed, invariant (to write [Infinitive], writing [Gerund/Participle]).
- Transitive, Intransitive & Linking:
  * Transitive: Takes Direct Object ("Swadeep built an app").
  * Intransitive: No Direct Object ("The train arrived").
  * Linking / Copula: Connects to Subject Complement ("The soup tastes delicious").
- Primary & Modal Auxiliaries:
  * Primary: be, do, have (constructs aspects, negatives, passives).
  * Modals: can, could, may, might, must, should, will, would (expresses mood/modality).
