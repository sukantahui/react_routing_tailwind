1. WHAT IS GRAMMAR?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Grammar is the architectural rule system of human language that dictates how
individual words combine into phrases, clauses, and meaningful sentences.
Words do not possess static, permanent labels; rather, their "Part of Speech"
is determined dynamically by their syntactic work (function) in a sentence.

2. OPEN CLASSES VS CLOSED CLASSES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
+--------------------+------------------------------------+--------------------+
| Word Class Type    | Members (Parts of Speech)          | Core Property      |
+--------------------+------------------------------------+--------------------+
| OPEN CLASSES       | Nouns, Verbs, Adjectives, Adverbs  | Readily accept new |
|                    | (Content / Lexical Words)          | coinages & slang.  |
+--------------------+------------------------------------+--------------------+
| CLOSED CLASSES     | Pronouns, Prepositions,            | Fixed inventory;   |
|                    | Conjunctions, Determiners          | rarely admit new   |
|                    | (Grammatical / Function Words)     | additions.         |
+--------------------+------------------------------------+--------------------+

3. THE EIGHT PARTS OF SPEECH (TAXONOMY & FUNCTION)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. NOUN (Naming Word):
   - Names a person, place, thing, substance, state, quality, or concept.
   - Example: "Kolkata is a historic city." | "Honesty is the best policy."

2. PRONOUN (Substitute Word):
   - Replaces a noun phrase to prevent awkward repetition.
   - Example: "Swadeep studied hard; he scored the top grade."

3. VERB (Action / State / Relation Core):
   - The indispensable engine of every English clause; indicates action or state.
   - Example: "Tuhina solved the equation." (Action) | "She is brilliant." (State)

4. ADJECTIVE (Qualifying / Modifying Word):
   - Describes, quantifies, or limits a noun or pronoun.
   - Example: "Abhronila solved a challenging puzzle."

5. ADVERB (Modifier of Verbs, Adjectives & other Adverbs):
   - Answers: How? When? Where? Why? To what degree?
   - Example: "Debangshu answered remarkably well."

6. PREPOSITION (Relational Word):
   - Placed before a noun/pronoun to show relation to another word in space/time.
   - Example: "The book is on the table." | "They met at Barrackpore station."

7. CONJUNCTION (Syntactic Linker):
   - Connects words, phrases, or clauses (FANBOYS: For, And, Nor, But, Or, Yet, So).
   - Example: "He was tired, yet he continued reading."

8. INTERJECTION (Emotive Outburst):
   - Expresses sudden spontaneous emotion; grammatically independent.
   - Example: "Bravo! You have mastered the first module."

5. BENGALI LEARNER PEDAGOGICAL BRIDGES (বাংলা মাধ্যম শিক্ষার্থীদের জন্য)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
* Trap 1: Zero Copula Absence
  - Bengali: "সে একজন ডাক্তার" (প্রকাশ্য ক্রিয়া নেই)
  - English: "He IS a doctor" (Mandatory Finite Linking Verb)
  - Error to avoid: Never write "He a doctor".

* Trap 2: Word Order Disparity
  - Bengali: Subject + Object + Verb (SOV) -> "আমি চা খাই"
  - English: Subject + Verb + Object (SVO) -> "I drink tea"

* Trap 3: The '-ly' Suffix Misconception
  - Noun + '-ly' = ADJECTIVE (Friendly, Lovely, Brotherly, Costly, Deadly)
  - Adjective + '-ly' = ADVERB (Quickly, Slowly, Quietly, Beautifully)
  - "He behaved in a friendly manner" (NOT "He behaved friendly").
