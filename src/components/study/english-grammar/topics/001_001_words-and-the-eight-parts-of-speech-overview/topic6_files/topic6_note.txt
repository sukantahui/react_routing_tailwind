1. BARRACKPORE SEMINAR HIGHLIGHTS (শ্রেণিকক্ষের প্রশ্নোত্তর নির্যাস)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STUDENTS: Swadeep, Tuhina, Debangshu, Abhronila
EDUCATOR & MASTER MENTOR: Sukanta Hui

KEY DIALOGUE BREAKDOWN & PEDAGOGICAL RESOLUTIONS:

Q1 (Swadeep): "Sir, why is 'fastly' incorrect if 'quickly' is correct?"
-> RESOLUTION: 'Fast' is an ancient Germanic flat adverb. It functions as both 
   Adjective ("fast train") and Adverb ("ran fast"). 'Fastly' does not exist!

Q2 (Tuhina): "Why is 'friendly' an Adjective even though it ends in '-ly'?"
-> RESOLUTION: Morphological Rule:
   • Noun + '-ly' = ADJECTIVE (Friend + ly = Friendly; Love + ly = Lovely).
   • Adjective + '-ly' = ADVERB (Quick + ly = Quickly; Brave + ly = Bravely).
   • To use 'friendly' as an adverb: "He spoke in a friendly manner."

Q3 (Debangshu): "Why do you say 'The rose smells sweet' and not 'sweetly'?"
-> RESOLUTION: Sensory verbs (smell, taste, sound, feel, look) act as LINKING 
   (Copular) verbs describing the state of the subject. They require ADJECTIVE 
   subject complements, not adverbs of manner.

Q4 (Abhronila): "How do I know if 'before' is a Preposition or a Conjunction?"
-> RESOLUTION:
   • Preposition: Followed only by a nominal object ("He arrived before 5 PM").
   • Conjunction: Followed by a complete clause with subject and finite verb 
     ("Look before you leap").

2. HIGH-FREQUENCY BENGALI TRANSLATION TRAPS DECONSTRUCTED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[X] TRAP 1: Zero Copula (বাংলায় ক্রিয়া উহ্য থাকা)
    - Bengali: "সে অসুস্থ" -> Bengali learners write: *"He ill"* [WRONG!].
    - Rule: English sentences require an explicit Finite Verb: "He IS ill."

[X] TRAP 2: Stative Verbs in Progressive Aspect
    - Bengali: "আমি উত্তরটি বুঝতে পারছি" -> *"I am understanding the answer"* [WRONG!].
    - Rule: Verbs of cognition resist -ing: "I understand the answer."

[X] TRAP 3: 'Between you and I' (Hypercorrection)
    - Rule: Prepositions govern Objective Case: "Between you and ME."
