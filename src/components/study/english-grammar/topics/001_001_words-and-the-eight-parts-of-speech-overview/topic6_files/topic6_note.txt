1. THE BARRACKPORE MENTORING WORKSHOP TRANSCRIPT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Key questions posed by students and resolved by Sukanta Sir:

Q1 (Swadeep): "Why is 'He ran fastly' rejected in grammar checks?"
- Mentor: 'Fast' is an invariant flat adverb. English morphology never generated 
  'fastly'. 'Fast' acts as both Adjective ("a fast train") and Adverb ("he ran fast").

Q2 (Tuhina): "If words ending in '-ly' are adverbs, why is 'lovely' an adjective?"
- Mentor: Check the root!
  * Noun + '-ly' = ADJECTIVE (Friend + ly = Friendly, Love + ly = Lovely).
  * Adjective + '-ly' = ADVERB (Quick + ly = Quickly, Clear + ly = Clearly).

Q3 (Debangshu): "How do I know whether 'before' is a preposition or conjunction?"
- Mentor: Inspect the following structure!
  * Followed by Noun Phrase: Preposition ("He arrived before [sunset]").
  * Followed by Clause (S+V): Conjunction ("He arrived before [the train left]").

2. HIGH-FREQUENCY EXAM CONFUSABLES SUMMARY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Hard (with intense effort) vs Hardly (almost not at all - Negative).
2. Late (not on time) vs Lately (recently).
3. Near (close in distance) vs Nearly (almost).
4. Direct (straight, without stopping) vs Directly (immediately / without delay).
