1. PUNCTUATION: THE ARCHITECTURAL ROAD SIGNS OF THOUGHT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Punctuation marks govern the syntactic grouping, rhythm, and legal clarity of 
English prose. Misplaced punctuation has caused multi-million dollar legal disputes 
and profound semantic absurdities.

The Core Toolkit:
  • Period / Full Stop (.)
  • Comma (,) & Oxford Comma
  • Semicolon (;)
  • Colon (:)
  • Em Dash (—) & En Dash (–) & Hyphen (-)
  • Parentheses ( ) & Brackets [ ]
  • Apostrophe (')
  • Quotation Marks (" " / ' ')

================================================================================
2. THE COMMA SPLICE & THE 4 STANDARD REPAIR MECHANISMS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A Comma Splice occurs when two independent clauses are joined with only a comma:
  - FAULTY (COMMA SPLICE): "The weather was stormy, the ship did not sail."

THE 4 WAYS TO REPAIR A COMMA SPLICE:
  1. THE PERIOD REPAIR (Two separate sentences):
     "The weather was stormy. The ship did not sail."
  2. THE SEMICOLON REPAIR (Closely linked thoughts):
     "The weather was stormy; the ship did not sail."
  3. THE COMMA + FANBOYS REPAIR (Coordinating conjunction):
     "The weather was stormy, SO the ship did not sail."
  4. THE SUBORDINATION REPAIR (Complex sentence):
     "BECAUSE the weather was stormy, the ship did not sail."

================================================================================
3. THE 6 CARDINAL COMMA RULES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. SERIAL / OXFORD COMMA:
   - Before 'and/or' in a list of 3+ items: "apples, bananas, and oranges."
2. INTRODUCTORY CLAUSE / PHRASE:
   - "When the bell rang, the students departed." / "In the morning, we left."
3. FANBOYS JOINING INDEPENDENT CLAUSES:
   - "She wrote the code, and he designed the database."
4. NON-ESSENTIAL PARENTHETICAL APPOSITIVES:
   - "Dr. Sen, my biology professor, published a new paper."
5. DIRECT ADDRESS (VOCATIVE COMMA):
   - "Let's eat, grandma!" (Saves grandma from being eaten!)
6. DIRECT DIALOGUE QUOTATIONS:
   - He said, "Punctuation matters."

4. SEMICOLON (;) vs COLON (:) vs DASH (—)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A. THE SEMICOLON (;):
   • Connects two independent clauses that are closely related in thought.
     - "I love reading; it broadens my horizons."
   • Used with Conjunctive Adverbs:
     - "The experiment failed; however, we learned valuable data."
   • Used in complex lists with internal commas:
     - "Delegates came from Kolkata, India; London, UK; and Paris, France."

B. THE COLON (:):
   • Must follow an INDEPENDENT CLAUSE to introduce a list, explanation, or quote.
     - CORRECT: "She bought three essential items: pens, paper, and ink."
     - WRONG:   "She bought: pens, paper, and ink." (Never put colon after a verb!)

C. DASHES vs HYPHENS:
   • Em Dash (—): Dramatic interruption or strong aside. ("He had one goal—victory.")
   • En Dash (–): Spans or ranges of numbers/dates. ("May 15–May 20", "pages 45–50")
   • Hyphen (-): Compound adjectives before nouns. ("a well-known author")

================================================================================
5. THE APOSTROPHE: POSSESSION vs CONTRACTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
• Possession:
  - Singular Noun: "The teacher's desk"
  - Plural ending in -s: "The teachers' lounge"
  - Plural NOT ending in -s: "The children's playground", "Men's department"
  - Joint Ownership: "Rohan and Soham's car" (One car owned together)
  - Separate Ownership: "Rohan's and Soham's cars" (Two distinct cars)

• The Grocer's Apostrophe Fallacy:
  - WRONG: "Fresh Mango's for Sale"
  - RIGHT: "Fresh Mangoes for Sale" (Never use apostrophe for simple plural!)

• It's vs Its:
  - IT'S = It is / It has (Contraction).
  - ITS  = Possessive Pronoun (like his / her). Never takes an apostrophe!
