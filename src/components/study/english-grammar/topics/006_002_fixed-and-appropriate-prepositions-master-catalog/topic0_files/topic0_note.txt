1. THE NATURE OF FIXED / DEPENDENT PREPOSITIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A Dependent (Fixed/Appropriate) Preposition is an idiomatic binding between a 
Head Word (Verb, Adjective, or Noun) and a specific Preposition governed not by 
spatial physics, but by historic lexicogrammatical convention.

Core Formula:
  [Head Word] + [MANDATORY PREPOSITION] + [Object / Gerund]
  - Example: "He insists [Verb] ON [Fixed Preposition] punctuality."

2. CONTEXT-DEPENDENT PREPOSITION SHIFTS (CRITICAL EXAM TRAPS)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A. AGREE:
   - Agree WITH a person           -> "I agree with Sukanta Sir."
   - Agree TO a proposal / plan    -> "The committee agreed to the new roadmap."
   - Agree ON a matter / topic     -> "We finally agreed on a common date."

B. DIE:
   - Die OF a disease / hunger     -> "He died of malaria / cancer / thirst."
   - Die FROM an external cause    -> "He died from overwork / loss of blood / wound."
   - Die BY violence / poison      -> "He died by suicide / poison."
   - Die FOR a noble cause         -> "Martyrs die for their country."

C. DEAL:
   - Deal IN a commodity / trade   -> "He deals in handloom textiles."
   - Deal WITH a person / subject  -> "How will you deal with this customer?"

D. DIFFER:
   - Differ WITH a person          -> "I differ with you on political ideology."
   - Differ FROM something         -> "Bengali grammar differs from English syntax."

E. BLIND:
   - Blind IN (or OF) an eye       -> "He is blind in his left eye." (Physical)
   - Blind TO faults / reality     -> "Parents should not be blind to their children's faults." (Metaphorical)

F. PART:
   - Part WITH a possession        -> "He refuses to part with his old books."
   - Part FROM a person            -> "Tears flowed when she parted from her mother."

G. CHARGED vs ACCUSED:
   - Charged WITH a crime          -> "Charged with grand theft."
   - Accused OF a crime            -> "Accused of financial fraud."

6. LATIN COMPARATIVES RULE: NEVER USE "THAN"
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
All 7 Latin comparative adjectives ending in "-ior" take "TO" instead of "than":
  • Senior TO
  • Junior TO
  • Superior TO
  • Inferior TO
  • Prior TO
  • Anterior TO
  • Posterior TO

  WRONG: "He is senior than me."
  RIGHT: "He is senior to me."
