1. WHAT IS PASSAGE NARRATION?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Passage Narration is the advanced skill of converting an entire multi-speaker 
dramatic or conversational passage into a single, cohesive, continuous reported 
narrative paragraph.

Key Objectives:
  1. Eliminate disjointed speech tags ("he said", "she said").
  2. Deploy high-register Nuanced Reporting Verbs (admitted, refused, warned, apologized).
  3. Expand elliptical (incomplete) dialogue replies into full grammatical clauses.
  4. Integrate vocatives, greetings, and farewells naturally into narrative flow.

================================================================================
2. HANDLING SPECIAL CONVERSATIONAL ELEMENTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A. VOCATIVES (TERMS OF ADDRESS):
   • "Sir / Madam"         ==> Respectfully / Addressing him as Sir
   • "Mother / Father"     ==> Affectionately addressing her mother / Told his mother
   • "Friends / Comrades"  ==> Addressing them as friends

B. INCOMPLETE / ELLIPTICAL ANSWERS:
   • Direct:   "Are you hungry?" "Yes."
   • Indirect: He asked if I was hungry, to which I replied in the affirmative (that I was).
   • Direct:   "Did you steal the money?" "No."
   • Indirect: He inquired if I had stolen the money, and I replied in the negative.

C. GREETINGS & FAREWELLS:
   • "Good morning / evening" ==> Wished [him] a good morning / evening
   • "Good bye / Farewell"    ==> Bade [him] goodbye / farewell
   • "Thank you"              ==> Thanked [him] for...
   • "Congratulations"        ==> Congratulated [him] on...
   • "Happy Birthday / Puja"  ==> Wished [him] a Happy Birthday / Puja

3. THE NUANCED REPORTING VERBS ARSENAL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Replace repetitive "said / told" with precise semantic reporting verbs:

+--------------------+-------------------------+-------------------------------+
| Reporting Verb     | Semantic Domain         | Canonical Example             |
+--------------------+-------------------------+-------------------------------+
| Acknowledged       | Recognizing a truth     | Acknowledged his limitations. |
| Admitted           | Conceding a mistake     | Admitted that he was wrong.   |
| Apologized         | Expressing regret       | Apologized for the delay.     |
| Confessed          | Admitting a crime/sin   | Confessed that he stole it.   |
| Congratulated      | Commending success      | Congratulated on the trophy.  |
| Denied             | Repudiating accusation  | Denied having broken the vase.|
| Insisted           | Emphatic demand         | Insisted on paying the fee.   |
| Objected           | Opposing a proposal     | Objected to the unfair rule.  |
| Promised           | Binding commitment      | Promised to return by noon.   |
| Refused            | Declining to act        | Refused to sign the document. |
| Reminded           | Prompting memory        | Reminded him to take his keys.|
| Threatened         | Menacing ultimatum      | Threatened to report the theft|
| Urged              | Strong exhortation      | Urged them to remain united.  |
| Warned             | Cautioning of danger    | Warned not to enter the woods.|
| Welcomed           | Greeting hospitably     | Welcomed the delegates.       |
+--------------------+-------------------------+-------------------------------+

4. PASSAGE NARRATION WORKED BLUEPRINT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ORIGINAL DIALOGUE:
  "Where are you going, my boy?" said the old hermit.
  "I am going to the capital to seek employment, Sir," replied the youth.
  "Do not go there. The city is full of wicked temptations," warned the hermit.
  "Thank you for your advice, but I must try my fortune," said the youth.

REPORTED CONTINUOUS NARRATIVE:
  The old hermit affectionately asked the youth where he was going. The youth 
  respectfully replied that he was going to the capital to seek employment. The 
  hermit then warned him not to go there, explaining that the city was full of 
  wicked temptations. The youth thanked him for his advice, but politely added 
  that he had to try his fortune.

5. REVERSE NARRATION (INDIRECT TO DIRECT SPEECH)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Step 1: Identify the Reporting Verb and recreate the speaker tag (e.g. 'He said to me,').
Step 2: Add opening quotation marks (").
Step 3: Reverse the Tense Backshift (Past Perfect -> Simple Past or Present Perfect; 
        Simple Past -> Simple Present; Would -> Will).
Step 4: Restore 1st & 2nd Person Pronouns (I, you, we, my, your).
Step 5: Restore Immediate Deictic Time/Place Words (that day -> today; there -> here).
Step 6: If an indirect question, restore Question Inversion and terminate with '?'.
Step 7: Close quotation marks (").
