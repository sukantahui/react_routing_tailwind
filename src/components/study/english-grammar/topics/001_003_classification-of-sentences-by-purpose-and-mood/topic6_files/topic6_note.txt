1. BARRACKPORE MENTORING LAB CASE STUDIES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
This lab analyzes real student errors and establishes immutable syntactic invariants.

CASE 1: THE INVISIBLE NEGATIVE TRAP
- Student Attempt: "He has little money, hasn't he?"
- Sir's Correction: "He has little money, HAS HE / DOES HE?"
- Invariant: 'Little' and 'Few' are semantically negative -> Require POSITIVE tags.
  Contrast with: "He has A little money, HASN'T HE?" (Positive quantifier -> Negative tag).

CASE 2: THE 1ST PERSON OPINION MATRIX
- Student Attempt: "I think he is honest, don't I?"
- Sir's Correction: "I think he is honest, ISN'T HE?"
- Invariant: When introduced by 'I think / I believe / I suppose', the tag attaches 
  to the SUBORDINATE clause proposition.
  Contrast: "YOU think he is honest, DON'T YOU?" (2nd/3rd person matrices attach to main clause).

CASE 3: THE DITRANSITIVE / PERMISSION 'LET US' TRAP
- Student Attempt: "Let us go to the riverfront, will you?"
- Sir's Correction: 
  * Proposal/Joint Action ('Let's'): "Let's go, SHALL WE?"
  * Seeking Permission ('Allow us'): "Let us enter the lab, WILL YOU?"

2. SPOKEN INTONATION & DISCOURSE PRAGMATICS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
+-------------------+----------------------------+-----------------------------+
| Intonation Type   | Pitch Contour              | Speaker's Intent            |
+-------------------+----------------------------+-----------------------------+
| Falling (\)       | Downward pitch at the end  | Expects agreement / Certain |
| Rising (/)        | Upward pitch at the end    | Genuinely asking / Uncertain|
+-------------------+----------------------------+-----------------------------+

Examples:
* "You are ready, aren't you? \" -> "I know you're ready; just confirming."
* "You haven't seen my bag, have you? /" -> "I genuinely don't know if you saw it."

| # | Subject Entity in Statement      | Tag Pronoun      | Tag Verb Number    |
