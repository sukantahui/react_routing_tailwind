1. THE GOLDEN RULE OF TRANSFORMATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
"Change the outward syntactic form, but preserve the underlying semantic 
 meaning with 100% fidelity."

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. MASTER TRANSFORMATION CATALOG (ACROSS ALL 5 FUNCTIONAL TYPES)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A. AFFIRMATIVE <===> NEGATIVE (MEANING PRESERVED)
1. Using Antonyms + Not:
   * "Man is mortal." <===> "Man is NOT immortal."
   * "I will always remember." <===> "I will NEVER forget."
2. 'Only / Alone' Rules:
   * Persons: "Only Swadeep came." <===> "NONE BUT Swadeep came."
   * Things:  "He bought only books." <===> "He bought NOTHING BUT books."
   * Numbers: "He is only ten." <===> "He is NOT MORE THAN ten."
3. 'As soon as' Rule:
   * "As soon as he saw the mentor, he stood up."
     <===> "NO SOONER DID he see the mentor THAN he stood up."
4. 'Too...to' Rule:
   * "He is too weak to walk." <===> "He is SO weak THAT he CANNOT walk."
5. 'Every' Rule:
   * "Every mother loves her child." <===> "There is NO mother BUT loves her child."

B. ASSERTIVE <===> INTERROGATIVE (RHETORICAL INVERSION)
1. Affirmative Statement ===> Negative Question:
   * "Everyone knows Netaji." <===> "WHO DOES NOT know Netaji?"
   * "Prevention is better than cure." <===> "ISN'T prevention better than cure?"
2. Negative Statement ===> Positive Question:
   * "Nobody can solve this." <===> "WHO CAN solve this?"
   * "Their glory can never fade." <===> "WHEN CAN their glory fade?"
3. 'There is no use' Rule:
   * "There is no use in worrying." <===> "WHAT IS THE USE OF worrying?"

C. EXCLAMATORY <===> ASSERTIVE
1. 'What a...' Rule:
   * "What a terrible storm it was!" <===> "It was a VERY terrible storm."
2. 'How...' Rule:
   * "How sweet the melody is!" <===> "The melody is VERY sweet."
3. 'O that / If only / Would that' Rule:
   * "If only I were young again!" <===> "I EARNESTLY WISH I were young again."

D. IMPERATIVE <===> ASSERTIVE
1. "Please help me." <===> "You are REQUESTED to help me."
2. "Shut the door."  <===> "You are ORDERED to shut the door."
3. "Work hard."      <===> "You SHOULD work hard / You are ADVISED to work hard."

E. OPTATIVE <===> ASSERTIVE
1. "May you succeed!" <===> "I PRAY / WISH THAT you may succeed."
2. "Long live the Republic!" <===> "We EARNESTLY PRAY THAT the Republic may live long."
