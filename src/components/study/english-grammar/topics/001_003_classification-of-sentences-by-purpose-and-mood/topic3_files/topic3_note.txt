1. CORE DEFINITION & SYNTACTIC ARCHITECTURE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
An Imperative sentence expresses a command, request, piece of advice, invitation, 
prohibition, or instructional directive.

* SYNTACTIC RULE 1: The grammatical subject is almost always the implied 
  second-person pronoun '(You)', omitted from surface structure.
  - Surface: "[0] Open the compiler terminal."
  - Underlying: "(You) open the compiler terminal."

* SYNTACTIC RULE 2: The verb is ALWAYS in the BARE INFINITIVE / BASE FORM (V1).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

3. SPECIAL IMPERATIVE CONSTRUCTIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A. EMPHATIC IMPERATIVE (With 'Do'):
   Prefixing 'Do' to the base verb adds emotional warmth, urgency, or insistence:
   - "Do write to us whenever you face difficulties!"

B. CONDITIONAL IMPERATIVE (Imperative + and/or):
   - "Work with dedication, AND you will achieve mastery." (= If you work...)
   - "Save your changes now, OR you will lose data." (= If you do not save...)

C. PASSIVE TRANSFORMATIONS:
   - Command: "Shut the gate." ===> "Let the gate be shut."
   - Advice:  "Help the needy." ===> "The needy should be helped."
   - Request: "Please come." ===> "You are requested to come."

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. BENGALI LEARNER BRIDGES & EXAM TRAPS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. INFINITIVE CONFUSION TRAP:
   * INCORRECT: *Please to help me.
   * CORRECT:    Please help me. ('Please' takes bare infinitive, no 'to').

2. LET + PRONOUN CASE TRAP:
   * INCORRECT: *Let he do the work.
   * CORRECT:    Let HIM do the work. ('Let' is a transitive verb taking OBJECT pronouns).

3. OVERT 'YOU' USAGE:
   * Standard: "Sit down."
   * Emphatic/Reproachful: "YOU sit down and keep quiet!" (Explicit 'You' expresses anger/contrast).
