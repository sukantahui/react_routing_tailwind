1. THE FUNDAMENTAL POLARITY PRINCIPLE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A question tag is a mini-question tagged onto the end of a statement to seek 
confirmation or agreement.

* RULE 1: POSITIVE Statement  ===>  NEGATIVE Tag (Contracted)
  "Swadeep is preparing well," ===> "isn't he?"
  "You passed the test,"       ===> "didn't you?"

* RULE 2: NEGATIVE Statement  ===>  POSITIVE Tag
  "Debangshu cannot attend,"   ===> "can he?"
  "They didn't arrive,"        ===> "did they?"

2. MASTER INVENTORY OF SPECIAL TAG EXCEPTIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
+---+-----------------------------+-------------------+-------------------------+
| # | Statement Structure         | Tag Rule          | Exemplar Sentence       |
+---+-----------------------------+-------------------+-------------------------+
| 1 | I am (Positive)             | aren't I?         | "I am late, aren't I?"  |
| 2 | I am not (Negative)         | am I?             | "I am not late, am I?"  |
| 3 | Let's (Suggestion)          | shall we?         | "Let's begin, shall we?"|
| 4 | Let us (Request/Permission) | will you?         | "Let us leave, will you?"|
| 5 | Imperative (Command/Req)    | will you?/won't you?| "Help me, will you?" |
| 6 | Negative Imperative (Don't) | will you?         | "Don't shout, will you?"|
| 7 | Existential 'There'         | ... there?        | "There is hope, isn't there?"|
| 8 | Persons (everyone/nobody)   | ... they?         | "Everyone came, didn't they?"|
| 9 | Things (everything/nothing) | ... it?           | "Nothing broke, was it?"|
|10 | Used to                     | didn't / usedn't  | "You used to swim, didn't you?"|
|11 | Had better                  | hadn't ...?       | "You'd better go, hadn't you?"|
|12 | Would rather                | wouldn't ...?     | "He'd rather read, wouldn't he?"|

3. SEMI-NEGATIVE WORDS (THE INVISIBLE NEGATIVES)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Words carrying negative semantic weight render the statement NEGATIVE, 
mandating a POSITIVE TAG:
- hardly, scarcely, barely, seldom, rarely, neither, none, nobody, nothing, few, little

Examples:
* "He seldom speaks in public, DOES HE?"
* "They have scarcely enough memory, HAVE THEY?"
* "Few students failed the test, DID THEY?" (vs "A few failed, DIDN'T THEY?")
* "Little milk was left, WAS IT?" (vs "A little milk was left, WASN'T IT?")

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. SPOKEN INTONATION DYNAMICS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. FALLING INTONATION (\): Speaker is confident, expecting agreement.
   "It's a hot day, isn't it? \" (Not a genuine question, just making conversation).
2. RISING INTONATION (/): Speaker is genuinely uncertain and seeking information.
   "You haven't seen my keys, have you? /" (Real question).
