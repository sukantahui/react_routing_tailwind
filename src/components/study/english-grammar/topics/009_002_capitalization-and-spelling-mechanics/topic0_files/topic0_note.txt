1. CAPITALIZATION ARCHITECTURE (UNIVERSAL RULES & EXCEPTIONS)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Capitalization (বড় হাতের অক্ষর ব্যবহারের নিয়ম) signals semantic hierarchy,
proper identification, and syntactic boundaries.

A. Universal Rules:
   1. Sentence Openers: Always capitalize the first word of every sentence.
      - "Punctuation creates cadence; capitalization anchors identity."
   2. The Pronoun 'I' and Interjection 'O':
      - Always capitalized regardless of position: "May I help you, O King?"
   3. Proper Nouns and Proper Adjectives:
      - Specific individuals: Rabindranath Tagore, William Shakespeare.
      - Nationalities and Languages: Bengali, English, French, Japanese.
      - Days, Months, and Holidays: Monday, August, Durga Puja, Christmas.
        *EXCEPTION*: Seasons are LOWERCASE unless personified!
        - "We love autumn." / "I met winter's icy grasp."

B. Tricky Capitalization Traps:
   1. Geographic Regions vs. Compass Directions:
      - Capitalize compass terms when referring to a SPECIFIC REGION or CULTURAL ENTITY:
        - "The tensions between the North and the South."
        - "She moved to the Middle East."
        - "Western philosophy vs. Eastern spirituality."
      - Lowercase compass terms when indicating pure DIRECTION or GENERAL LOCATION:
        - "Drive north for two miles."
        - "The wind is blowing from the east."
        - "southern Bengal" (general area) vs. "South Bengal State Transport" (proper entity).

   2. Kinship / Family Titles:
      - CAPITALIZE when used as a direct proper name or address, or directly preceding a name:
        - "Did Mother call you?" (Substitute 'Mother' with a proper name like 'Sita': "Did Sita call you?").
        - "I went shopping with Aunt Reeta."
      - LOWERCASE when preceded by a possessive pronoun (my, your, his, her, their, our) or article:
        - "My mother called yesterday."
        - "She is a loving aunt."

   3. Academic Subjects vs. Languages:
      - Capitalize only languages or specific numbered courses:
        - "I am studying English, French, and biology." (English/French = languages; biology = general subject).
        - "He enrolled in History 101 and Chemistry II."

   4. Titles of Offices and Dignitaries:
      - Capitalize when immediately preceding the person's name:
        - "Prime Minister Narendra Modi", "President Droupadi Murmu", "Professor Sen".
      - Lowercase when used alone or descriptively:
        - "The prime minister addressed the nation."
        - "The president signed the decree."
        *Exception*: Stylistic reverence in legal/formal state charters ("the President").

2. TITLE CAPITALIZATION STANDARDS (HEADLINE STYLE)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Standard Rules (Chicago Manual of Style / APA / MLA):
   1. Always capitalize the FIRST and LAST words of the title/subtitle.
   2. Capitalize all MAJOR words:
      - Nouns, Pronouns, Verbs (including 'Is', 'Be', 'Was'), Adjectives, Adverbs, Subordinating Conjunctions.
   3. Lowercase MINOR words (unless they start/end the title):
      - Articles: a, an, the
      - Coordinating Conjunctions: and, but, or, nor, for, so, yet (FANBOYS)
      - Prepositions under 5 letters: in, on, at, to, for, with, from, of, by, into
      - The word 'to' in infinitives: "How to Master English"
   
   Examples:
   - "The Old Man and the Sea" (not 'And The Sea')
   - "War and Peace"
   - "Much Ado About Nothing"
   - "Waiting for Godot"
   - "How to Win Friends and Influence People"

3. THE 6 MASTER ORTHOGRAPHIC SPELLING RULES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

RULE 1: THE 1-1-1 DOUBLING RULE
-------------------------------
When adding a vowel suffix (-ed, -ing, -er, -est, -able):
- A monosyllabic word (1 syllable) with 1 single vowel followed by 1 single consonant doubles the final consonant!
  * run + ing   -> running (1 syllable, 1 vowel 'u', 1 consonant 'n')
  * hop + ed    -> hopped   (vs. hope + ed -> hoped)
  * fat + est   -> fattest
  * plan + ed   -> planned

Multi-syllable Extension (Stress Rule):
- If the word has 2+ syllables, double the final consonant ONLY if the stress is on the LAST syllable:
  * pre-FER + ing  -> preferring (stress on 'FER')
  * oc-CUR + ed    -> occurred   (stress on 'CUR')
  * be-GIN + er    -> beginner   (stress on 'GIN')
- If the stress is on the FIRST syllable, DO NOT double (in American English):
  * VI-sit + ed    -> visited    (stress on 'VI')
  * BEN-e-fit + ed -> benefited  (stress on 'BEN')
  * HAP-pen + ing  -> happening  (stress on 'HAP')

RULE 2: SILENT 'E' (DROP OR KEEP?)
-----------------------------------
- DROP the silent 'e' when adding a suffix beginning with a VOWEL (-ing, -ed, -able, -ous, -ary):
  * write + ing -> writing (NOT writeing)
  * hope + ing  -> hoping
  * fame + ous  -> famous
  * value + able -> valuable

- KEEP the silent 'e' when adding a suffix beginning with a CONSONANT (-ment, -ful, -ly, -ness):
  * hope + ful   -> hopeful
  * manage + ment -> management
  * safe + ty    -> safety
  * late + ly    -> lately
  * EXCEPTIONS: truly (true+ly), argument (argue+ment), judgment (in US: judgement/judgment), ninth (nine+th).

- CRITICAL SOFT 'C' & 'G' EXCEPTION:
  Keep the 'e' before -able or -ous to preserve the soft sound (/s/ or /dʒ/):
  * courage + ous -> courageous (NOT couragous!)
  * notice + able -> noticeable (NOT noticable!)
  * change + able -> changeable (NOT changable!)
  * peace + able  -> peaceable

RULE 3: 'Y' TO 'I' MUTATION RULE
---------------------------------
- When a consonant precedes 'y', change 'y' to 'i' before any suffix (EXCEPT -ing):
  * study + es  -> studies
  * happy + ly  -> happily
  * beauty + ful -> beautiful
  * carry + ed  -> carried
  * BUT: carry + ing -> carrying (avoid double 'i': 'carriing' is illegal).

- When a vowel precedes 'y', KEEP the 'y':
  * play + ed   -> played (vowel 'a' + 'y')
  * boy + s     -> boys   (vowel 'o' + 'y')
  * delay + ed  -> delayed
  * EXCEPTIONS: say -> said, pay -> paid, lay -> laid.

RULE 4: "I BEFORE E EXCEPT AFTER C" (AND ITS TRUE RULES)
---------------------------------------------------------
The Classic Rhyme:
"I before E, except after C, or when sounding like 'A' as in neighbor and weigh."

- Normal 'ie' (sound of long /ee/):
  * achieve, believe, chief, piece, relief, yield, thief.

- After 'c' (produces /see/ sound):
  * receive, ceiling, deceive, receipt, conceive, perceive.

- Sound of 'ay' /eɪ/:
  * neighbor, weigh, freight, veil, beige, reign.

- THE CRITICAL 8 EXAM EXCEPTIONS (Must Memorize!):
  1. weird      (NOT wierd)
  2. seize      (NOT sieze)
  3. leisure    (NOT liesure)
  4. forfeit    (NOT forfiet)
  5. sovereign  (NOT soveriegn)
  6. species    (NOT speceis)
  7. caffeine   (NOT caffiene)
  8. foreign    (NOT foriegn)

RULE 5: SUFFIX SELECTIONS (-ABLE vs -IBLE, -ANCE vs -ENCE)
----------------------------------------------------------
A. -ABLE vs -IBLE:
   - -able is attached to complete base words:
     * depend -> dependable, accept -> acceptable, read -> readable.
   - -ible is attached to root stems that cannot stand alone as words:
     * aud + ible -> audible, cred + ible -> credible, vis + ible -> visible.
     * Common full-word exceptions: digestible, flexible, accessible, irresistible.

B. -CEED vs -CEDE vs -SEDE:
   - ONLY ONE word ends in -SEDE: supersede.
   - ONLY THREE words end in -CEED: exceed, proceed, succeed.
   - ALL OTHER words end in -CEDE: precede, concede, accede, recede, intercede.

RULE 6: PLURALIZATION OF BORROWED & LATIN/GREEK WORDS
------------------------------------------------------
- Words ending in -sis become -ses:
  * crisis -> crises, analysis -> analyses, hypothesis -> hypotheses, thesis -> theses.
- Words ending in -on/-um become -a:
  * criterion -> criteria, phenomenon -> phenomena, datum -> data, stratum -> strata.
- Words ending in -us become -i:
  * stimulus -> stimuli, cactus -> cacti, radius -> radii, alumnus -> alumni.
- Words ending in -o (Italian/Spanish origin):
  * Living things/Vegetables often add -es: tomato -> tomatoes, potato -> potatoes, hero -> heroes.
  * Music/Technical/Shortened terms add -s: piano -> pianos, photo -> photos, kilo -> kilos, solo -> solos.

4. BRITISH (UK) VS. AMERICAN (US) SPELLING MATRIX
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Understanding dialect divergence is mandatory for competitive and professional writing.

Pattern                 British English (UK)            American English (US)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
-our vs -or             colour, flavour, honour,        color, flavor, honor,
                        labour, neighbour, rumour       labor, neighbor, rumor

-re vs -er              centre, theatre, metre,         center, theater, meter,
                        fibre, calibre, litre           fiber, caliber, liter

-ise / -yse vs -ize     realise, organise, analyse,     realize, organize, analyze,
                        paralyse, recognise             paralyze, recognize

-ce vs -se (Noun/Verb)  Licence (N) / License (V)       License (both N and V)
                        Practice (N) / Practise (V)     Practice (both N and V)
                        Defence, offence, pretence      Defense, offense, pretense

Double 'l' in unaccented travelled, travelling,          traveled, traveling,
syllables               cancelled, quarrelling,         canceled, quarreling,
                        jeweller, marvellous            jeweler, marvelous

-ogue vs -og            catalogue, dialogue, analogue   catalog, dialog, analog

-ae- / -oe- vs -e-      paediatric, anaesthetic,        pediatric, anesthetic,
(Greek roots)           foetus, amoeba                  fetus, ameba

5. TOP 20 HIGH-FREQUENCY SPELLING MISTAKES BY BENGALI STUDENTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Bengali phonetic transliteration often drops double letters or confuses vowels:

1.  EMBARRASS     -> Two 'r's, two 's's (em-bar-rass). NOT: embarass.
2.  ACCOMMODATE   -> Two 'c's, two 'm's (ac-com-mo-date). NOT: accomodate.
3.  DEFINITELY    -> Contains 'finite' (de-finite-ly). NOT: definately.
4.  SEPARATE      -> Has 'a rat' in the middle (sep-a-rate). NOT: seperate.
5.  RECEIVE       -> 'e' before 'i' after 'c'. NOT: recieve.
6.  PRIVILEGE     -> No 'd' in privilege (priv-i-lege). NOT: priviledge.
7.  OCCURRENCE    -> Two 'c's, two 'r's, ends in -ence. NOT: occurance.
8.  MAINTENANCE   -> Root 'maintain' turns to 'main-ten-ance'. NOT: maintainance.
9.  BUREAUCRACY   -> French root 'bureau' + 'cracy' (bu-reau-cra-cy). NOT: beurocracy.
10. MILLENNIUM    -> Two 'l's, two 'n's (mil-len-ni-um). NOT: milennium.
11. NECESSARY     -> 1 collar ('c'), 2 socks ('s's). NOT: neccessary.
12. CONSCIENTIOUS -> Contains 'science' root (con-sci-en-tious). NOT: consiencious.
13. HARASS        -> One 'r', two 's's (ha-rass). NOT: harrass.
14. DISAPPOINT    -> One 's', two 'p's (dis-ap-point). NOT: dissappoint.
15. GAUGE         -> 'a' before 'u' (g-a-u-g-e). NOT: guage.
16. MISCHIEVOUS   -> Three syllables only (mis-chie-vous). NOT: mischievious.
17. HIERARCHY     -> 'i' before 'e' (hier-ar-chy). NOT: heirarchy.
18. VACUUM        -> One 'c', two 'u's (vac-u-um). NOT: vaccum.
19. RYTHM -> RHYTHM -> R-H-Y-T-H-M (Rhythm Helps Your Two Hips Move). NOT: rythm.
20. PRONUNCIATION -> No 'o' in the middle (pro-nun-ci-a-tion). NOT: pronounciation.

6. BENGALI EXPLANATORY SUMMARY (বাংলা ব্যাকরণিক বিশ্লেষণ)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Capitalization এর ক্ষেত্রে সবচেয়ে বড় ভুল হয় Compass Directions (দিশা) বনাম Regions (অঞ্চল)। 
  সাধারণ দিক নির্দেশ করলে ছোট হাতের (e.g., travel east), কিন্তু নির্দিষ্ট অঞ্চল বোঝালে বড় হাতের (e.g., the Middle East, the Western world)।
- আত্মীয়দের সম্পর্কের ক্ষেত্রে (Mother, Father, Uncle): যদি তার আগে my, your, his, her ইত্যাদি possessive pronoun থাকে, তবে ছোট হাতের (e.g., my mother)। কিন্তু নাম হিসেবে সম্বোধন বা ব্যবহার করলে বড় হাতের (e.g., I asked Mother for help)।
- Spelling এর ক্ষেত্রে 1-1-1 Doubling Rule খুব গুরুত্বপূর্ণ: ১টি syllable + ১টি vowel + ১টি consonant থাকলে suffix যোগের সময় শেষ consonant ডাবল হয় (run -> running, hop -> hopped)। কিন্তু ২য় syllable এ stress না থাকলে ডাবল হয় না (visit -> visited)।
- "I before E except after C" নিয়মের ৮টি ব্যতিক্রম (weird, seize, leisure, forfeit, sovereign, species, caffeine, foreign) প্রতিটি সরকারি ও প্রতিযোগিতামূলক পরীক্ষায় বারবার আসে।
- British ও American বানানের পার্থক্য লক্ষ্য করুন: -our/-or (colour/color), -re/-er (theatre/theater), -ise/-ize (realise/realize), এবং দ্বৈত 'l' (travelling/traveling)। পরীক্ষায় যেকোনো একটি শৈলী সুনির্দিষ্টভাবে মেনে চলা উচিত।
