MODULE 005.003: NON-FINITE VERBS: THE INFINITIVE (TO- & BARE INFINITIVES)

1. FINITE VS NON-FINITE VERBS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  * FINITE VERBS: Bound by TENSE, NUMBER, and PERSON. They form the core predicate.
    - "He GOES to school." / "They WENT to school."

  * NON-FINITE VERBS: Infinite, unbounded by tense, number, or person.
    They function as Nouns, Adjectives, or Adverbs across 3 families:
      1. INFINITIVES: To-Infinitive ('to speak') & Bare Infinitive ('speak')
      2. GERUNDS:     Verbal Nouns ending in '-ing' ('Speaking well is an art')
      3. PARTICIPLES: Verbal Adjectives ('a speaking doll', 'spoken words')


2. THE MULTI-FUNCTIONAL TO-INFINITIVE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
+-------------------+----------------------------+-----------------------------+
| SYNTACTIC ROLE    | EXPLANATION                | CANONICAL EXAMPLES          |
+-------------------+----------------------------+-----------------------------+
| 1. Noun Subject   | Acts as sentence subject   | - "TO ERR is human."        |
|                   |                            | - "TO FORGIVE is divine."   |
+-------------------+----------------------------+-----------------------------+
| 2. Noun Object    | Direct object of verb      | - "He decided TO RESIGN."   |
|                   |                            | - "She hopes TO WIN."       |
+-------------------+----------------------------+-----------------------------+
| 3. Subject Compl. | Explains the subject       | - "His ambition is TO LEAD."|
+-------------------+----------------------------+-----------------------------+
| 4. Adjective Mod. | Modifies a preceding noun  | - "I have no time TO WASTE."|
|                   |                            | - "Water TO DRINK."         |
+-------------------+----------------------------+-----------------------------+
| 5. Adverb Modifier| Explains Purpose / Reason  | - "He came TO LEARN."       |
|                   | Modifies an Adjective      | - "The water is fit TO DRINK|
+-------------------+----------------------------+-----------------------------+


3. THE BARE INFINITIVE INVARIANT CATALOG (MEMORIZE!)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The Bare Infinitive is the base verb form WITHOUT 'to'. It is triggered by:

  [A] MODAL AUXILIARIES (except 'ought to' and 'used to'):
      - "He can SWIM." / "You must GO." / "She will RETURN."

  [B] CAUSATIVE VERBS IN ACTIVE VOICE ('MAKE', 'LET', 'BID', 'HAVE'):
      - "She made the boy WEEP." (NOT: to weep)
      - "Let him GO." (NOT: to go)
      - "I had my assistant TYPE the report."

  [C] SENSORY VERBS ('SEE', 'HEAR', 'WATCH', 'FEEL', 'NOTICE'):
      - "I saw him CROSS the road." (Witnessed entire action)
      - "I heard her SING."

  [D] IDIOMATIC CONNECTIVES:
      - HAD BETTER:   "You had better LEAVE now." (NOT: to leave)
      - WOULD RATHER: "I would rather STARVE than beg."
      - DO NOTHING BUT: "He did nothing but CRY all afternoon."


4. THE CAUSATIVE ACTIVE VS PASSIVE TRAP (CRITICAL EXAM INVARIANT!)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
+------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| ACTIVE CAUSATIVE (Bare Infinitive):                                         |
|   "The magistrate made the prisoner CONFESS." (No 'to'!)                     |
|                                                                              |
| PASSIVE CAUSATIVE (Full To-Infinitive):                                      |
|   "The prisoner was made TO CONFESS by the magistrate." (Must have 'TO'!)    |
+------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
*(EXCEPTION: 'LET' retains bare infinitive even in passive: "He was let go")*


5. COMPLEX INFINITIVE PARADIGMS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
+--------------------------+-----------------------+---------------------------+
| INFINITIVE FORM          | MORPHOLOGY            | CANONICAL EXAMPLE         |
+--------------------------+-----------------------+---------------------------+
| 1. Simple Active         | to + V1               | to write                  |
| 2. Continuous Active     | to be + V-ing         | "He seems TO BE WRITING." |
| 3. Perfect Active        | to have + V3          | "He claims TO HAVE SEEN." |
| 4. Simple Passive        | to be + V3            | "There is work TO BE DONE"|
| 5. Perfect Passive       | to have been + V3     | "Glad TO HAVE BEEN HELPED"|
+--------------------------+-----------------------+---------------------------+


6. SYNTHESIS WITH 'TOO... TO' AND 'ENOUGH TO'
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  * 'TOO... TO' (Negative Implication = So... that cannot):
    - "He is VERY weak. He CANNOT walk."
    -> "He is TOO weak TO walk."

  * 'ENOUGH TO' (Positive Capability = So... that can):
    - "She is VERY intelligent. She CAN solve this."
    -> "She is intelligent ENOUGH TO solve this." (Adjective + enough)


7. HIGH-FREQUENCY COMPETITIVE EXAM INFINITIVE TRAPS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRAP 1: Using 'To' with Causative 'Make' in Active Voice
    - INCORRECT: She made me to laugh.
    - CORRECT:   She made me LAUGH.

  TRAP 2: Omitting 'To' with Causative 'Make' in Passive Voice
    - INCORRECT: He was made write the test.
    - CORRECT:   He was made TO WRITE the test.

  TRAP 3: Adding 'To' after 'Had better'
    - INCORRECT: You had better to start early.
    - CORRECT:   You had better START early.
