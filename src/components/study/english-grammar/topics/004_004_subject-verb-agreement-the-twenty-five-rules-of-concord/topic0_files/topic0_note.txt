MODULE 004.002: SUBJECT-VERB AGREEMENT (THE 25 INVARIANT RULES OF CONCORD)

1. THE FUNDAMENTAL LAW OF SYNTACTIC CONCORD
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A finite verb must agree with its true grammatical subject in NUMBER and PERSON:
  - Singular Subject -> SINGULAR VERB (takes '-s/-es' in present: writes, is, has)
  - Plural Subject   -> PLURAL VERB   (base form in present: write, are, have)

*(CRITICAL PRINCIPLE: Nouns add '-s' to become PLURAL; Verbs add '-s' to become SINGULAR!)*


2. THE 25 INVARIANT RULES OF CONCORD (MASTER CATALOG)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

[RULE 1: COMPOUND SUBJECTS WITH 'AND']
  Two or more distinct subjects joined by 'and' take a PLURAL verb:
  - "Rahul and Rohan ARE playing chess."

[RULE 2: SINGLE UNIFIED IDEA WITH 'AND']
  When two nouns joined by 'and' represent a single item, meal, or concept:
  - "Bread and butter IS his staple food."
  - "Slow and steady WINS the race."
  - "The horse and carriage IS at the gate."

[RULE 3: 'EACH' / 'EVERY' BEFORE COMPOUND SUBJECTS]
  When singular nouns joined by 'and' are preceded by 'each' or 'every':
  - "Each boy and each girl WAS awarded a medal."
  - "Every man, woman, and child WAS vaccinated."

[RULE 4: INTERVENING PARENTHETICAL PHRASES (FIRST SUBJECT LAW)]
  When subjects are joined by: 'along with', 'together with', 'as well as', 
  'in addition to', 'accompanied by', 'like', 'unlike', 'besides', 'with':
  -> The verb agrees strictly with the FIRST SUBJECT!
  - "The captain, along with his sailors, WAS rescued." (Captain = Singular)
  - "The teachers, as well as the principal, WERE present." (Teachers = Plural)

[RULE 5: INTERVENING PREPOSITIONAL PHRASE TRAP]
  Prepositional phrases between subject and verb NEVER alter concord:
  - "The quality of these mangoes IS superior." (Subject = quality)
  - "The doors of this ancient temple WERE carved by artisans." (Subject = doors)

[RULE 6: THE RULE OF PROXIMITY (NEAREST SUBJECT LAW)]
  When subjects are joined by: 'Either... or', 'Neither... nor', 'Or', 'Nor', 
  'Not only... but also':
  -> The verb agrees with the NEAREST SUBJECT!
  - "Neither the teacher nor the students WERE present."
  - "Neither the students nor the teacher WAS present."

[RULE 7: COLLECTIVE NOUNS - UNIFIED ENTITY]
  When the collective noun acts unanimously as a single body -> SINGULAR VERB:
  - "The jury HAS reached a unanimous decision."
  - "The committee MEETS every Wednesday."

[RULE 8: COLLECTIVE NOUNS - NOUN OF MULTITUDE]
  When the members act individually or have conflicting opinions -> PLURAL VERB:
  - "The jury WERE divided in their opinions."
  - "The committee ARE arguing among themselves."

[RULE 9: INDEFINITE PRONOUNS (ALWAYS SINGULAR)]
  Everyone, somebody, nobody, anyone, everything, something, nothing, anything, 
  each, either, neither, one:
  - "Everyone HAS submitted the assignment."
  - "Neither of the two books IS available."

[RULE 10: INDEFINITE PRONOUNS (ALWAYS PLURAL)]
  Both, few, a few, several, many:
  - "Both of the candidates ARE qualified."
  - "Several issues WERE discussed."

[RULE 11: SANAM VARIABLE PRONOUNS (SOME, ANY, NONE, ALL, MORE/MOST)]
  The verb agrees with the noun inside the prepositional 'of' phrase:
  - "All of the MILK IS spoiled." (Milk = Uncountable -> Singular)
  - "All of the STUDENTS ARE present." (Students = Countable Plural -> Plural)
  - "Some of the CAKE WAS eaten."
  - "Some of the COOKIES WERE eaten."

[RULE 12: PLURAL IN FORM, SINGULAR IN MEANING]
  Names of academic disciplines, diseases, sports, and 'news':
  Mathematics, Physics, Economics, Politics, Ethics, Measles, Rickets, News, Innings:
  - "Mathematics IS a challenging subject."
  - "No news IS good news."

[RULE 13: DISCIPLINE AS PRACTICAL CALCULATIONS (POSSESSIVE PRECEDING)]
  When preceded by 'his/her/their/my' and meaning calculation data:
  - "His mathematics ARE remarkably weak." (Calculations = Plural)

[RULE 14: NOUNS ALWAYS PLURAL IN FORM AND MEANING]
  Scissors, trousers, binoculars, spectacles, cattle, police, gentry, people:
  - "The police ARE investigating the crime."
  - "The scissors ARE sharp."

[RULE 15: 'A PAIR OF' MODIFIER]
  When tools/clothing are preceded by 'a pair of', the subject is 'pair':
  - "A pair of scissors IS lying on the table."

[RULE 16: LUMP-SUM UNITS OF MONEY, TIME, DISTANCE, WEIGHT]
  Treated as a single whole quantity -> SINGULAR VERB:
  - "Ten kilometers IS a long distance."
  - "Fifty thousand rupees IS a generous grant."
  - "Five years IS a long duration."

[RULE 17: DISBURSED / FRACTIONAL MONEY UNITS]
  When units are distributed separately to different recipients -> PLURAL VERB:
  - "Fifty thousand rupees WERE distributed among the five victims."

[RULE 18: 'A NUMBER OF' VS 'THE NUMBER OF']
  - "A number of students ARE participating." ('A number of' = Many -> Plural)
  - "The number of students IS fifty." ('The number' = Specific total -> Singular)

[RULE 19: 'ONE OF THE + PLURAL NOUN + RELATIVE PRONOUN']
  The relative pronoun refers to the plural antecedent -> PLURAL VERB:
  - "Swadeep is one of the students who WERE selected."

[RULE 20: 'THE ONLY ONE OF THE + PLURAL NOUN + RELATIVE PRONOUN']
  'The only one' shifts the grammatical focus to the singular -> SINGULAR VERB:
  - "Swadeep is the ONLY one of the students who WAS selected."

[RULE 21: 'MORE THAN ONE' VS 'MORE THAN TWO']
  - "More than one student WAS absent." (Syntactic singular agreement)
  - "More than two students WERE absent." (Plural)

[RULE 22: 'MANY A' + SINGULAR NOUN]
  - "Many a man HAS attempted and failed." (Takes singular noun and verb)

[RULE 23: FRACTIONS AND PERCENTAGES]
  Verb agrees with the noun following 'of':
  - "Two-thirds of the CITY WAS submerged."
  - "Two-thirds of the BUILDINGS WERE submerged."

[RULE 24: POSTPONED SUBJECTS WITH 'THERE' AND 'HERE']
  - "There IS a book on the desk." (Subject = a book)
  - "There ARE five books on the desk." (Subject = five books)

[RULE 25: GERUND AND INFINITIVE SUBJECTS]
  A singular gerund or infinitive phrase takes a SINGULAR verb:
  - "Swimming in the morning IS invigorating."
  - "To master 25 concord rules REQUIRES dedication."


3. HIGH-FREQUENCY COMPETITIVE EXAM CONCORD TRAPS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRAP 1: False Agreement with Intervening Plural Noun
    - INCORRECT: The bouquet of roses smell sweet.
    - CORRECT:   The bouquet of roses SMELLS sweet. (Subject = bouquet)

  TRAP 2: 'A number of' taking singular
    - INCORRECT: A number of students is absent today.
    - CORRECT:   A number of students ARE absent today.

  TRAP 3: 'One of those who' taking singular
    - INCORRECT: He is one of those men who is always ready to help.
    - CORRECT:   He is one of those men who ARE always ready to help.
