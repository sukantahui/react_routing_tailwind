2. THE 10 INVARIANT COMMANDMENTS OF ENGLISH GRAMMAR FOR BENGALI LEARNERS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.  NEVER drop the linking 'be' verb in zero-copula Bengali thoughts ("He IS honest", NOT *He honest).
2.  NEVER use continuous (-ing) tenses with Stative Verbs of feeling, thinking, or possession (*I am knowing -> I know).
3.  NEVER put a preposition after direct transitive verbs (*discuss about -> discuss, *reach to -> reach).
4.  NEVER omit 'other' when comparing a subject to members of its own class (*taller than any boy -> taller than any other boy).
5.  ALWAYS balance correlative conjunctions (not only...but also) before identical parts of speech.
6.  ALWAYS place the true human doer immediately after an introductory participial modifier (curing dangling modifiers).
7.  ALWAYS use the possessive pronoun before a gerund ("insist on MY going", NOT *me going).
8.  ALWAYS keep the base bare infinitive after causative 'make' ("made him cry", NOT *made him to cry).
9.  NEVER use 'would have' inside the conditional If-clause ("If he had worked hard...", NOT *If he would have...).
10. ALWAYS prioritize active, vigorous verbs over sluggish zombie nominalizations ("investigate", NOT *conduct an investigation).

3. MASTER VIVA VOCE ORAL DEFENSE PROTOCOL (মৌখিক পরীক্ষার প্রশ্নোত্তর)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Q1: Why is "Between you and I" ungrammatical?
A1: Prepositions govern the objective case; therefore, the preposition 'between' requires the objective pronoun 'me' ("Between you and me").

Q2: What is the difference between "He has gone to London" and "He has been to London"?
A2: "Has gone" means he is currently in London or on his way there (completed outward journey). "Has been" means he visited London and has already returned (completed round-trip).

Q3: When do collective nouns take a plural verb?
A3: When the individual members of the collective group act separately, discordantly, or in disagreement ("The jury WERE divided in their opinions").

Q4: Why does "Scarcely had I entered" require inversion?
A4: Fronting a negative or restrictive adverbial puts rhetorical focus on the negation, requiring the auxiliary verb to precede the subject (Subject-Auxiliary Inversion).

Q5: What is the MEAL paragraph framework?
A5: It is an academic paragraph blueprint comprising Main Idea (Topic Sentence), Evidence (Data/Quotes), Analysis (Critical Interpretation), and Link (Synthesis/Bridge).
