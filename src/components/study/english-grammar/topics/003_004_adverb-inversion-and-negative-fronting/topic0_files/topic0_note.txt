1. WHAT IS GRAMMATICAL INVERSION?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Standard declarative English follows the SUBJECT + VERB order.
INVERSION reverses this order so that an AUXILIARY VERB precedes the SUBJECT:

  Standard Order:   Subject + Auxiliary + Main Verb
                    "I have NEVER seen such courage."

  Inverted Order:   Fronted Negative Word + AUXILIARY + SUBJECT + MAIN VERB
                    "NEVER HAVE I seen such courage."

Why use Inversion?
  1. Dramatic rhetorical emphasis in formal or literary English.
  2. Syntactic necessity after negative, restrictive, or locative fronting.


2. THE THREE MASTER TEMPLATES OF TIME INVERSION (MEMORIZE!)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
+------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| TEMPLATE 1: 'NO SOONER ... THAN' (PAST PERFECT OR SIMPLE PAST)              |
+------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
  Formula A (Past Perfect):
    NO SOONER + HAD + Subject + V3 (Past Participle) + THAN + Subject + V2
    - "No sooner HAD THE BELL RUNG THAN the students rushed out."

  Formula B (Simple Past with Did):
    NO SOONER + DID + Subject + V1 (Base Form) + THAN + Subject + V2
    - "No sooner DID THE BELL RING THAN the students rushed out."

  *(CRITICAL TRAP: 'No sooner' MUST pair with 'THAN', never 'when' or 'then'!)*

+------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| TEMPLATE 2: 'HARDLY / SCARCELY / BARELY ... WHEN'                            |
+------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
  Formula:
    HARDLY / SCARCELY / BARELY + HAD + Subject + V3 + WHEN / BEFORE + S + V2
    - "Hardly HAD WE REACHED the platform WHEN the train departed."
    - "Scarcely HAD HE CLOSED his eyes WHEN the alarm rang."

  *(CRITICAL TRAP: 'Hardly'/'Scarcely' pairs strictly with 'WHEN', never 'than'!)*

+------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| TEMPLATE 3: 'NOT ONLY ... BUT ALSO' (FIRST CLAUSE INVERSION)                |
+------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
  Formula:
    NOT ONLY + AUXILIARY + Subject + Main Verb..., BUT (Subject) ALSO...
    - "Not only DID HE WIN the gold medal, BUT HE ALSO broke the record."


3. FRONTED NEGATIVE & RESTRICTIVE TRIGGERS CATALOG
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
When any of the following expressions open a sentence, Subject-Auxiliary 
Inversion is 100% MANDATORY:

  * NEVER:                  "Never have I witnessed such injustice."
  * SELDOM:                 "Seldom do we see such extraordinary talent."
  * RARELY:                 "Rarely does he make a careless mistake."
  * LITTLE:                 "Little did they realize the impending danger."
  * NOWHERE:                "Nowhere will you find a more dedicated team."
  * AT NO TIME:             "At no time did the suspect admit his guilt."
  * UNDER NO CIRCUMSTANCES: "Under no circumstances should you leave."
  * ON NO ACCOUNT:          "On no account must this file be deleted."
  * IN NO WAY:              "In no way can his behavior be justified."


4. INVERSION WITH 'ONLY + ADVERBIAL / CLAUSE'
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
When 'Only' modifies a time or condition clause at the beginning of a sentence, 
inversion occurs in the MAIN CLAUSE, NOT the subordinate clause:

  * "Only after the doctor arrived DID THE PATIENT RELAX."
    (Subordinate: 'the doctor arrived' [Normal] | Main: 'did the patient relax' [Inverted])

  * "Only when the sun set DID WE BEGIN the journey."
  * "Only by practicing daily WILL YOU MASTER advanced syntax."


5. CONDITIONAL INVERSION (DROPPING 'IF')
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
In formal, legal, and academic writing, 'if' can be eliminated by fronting the auxiliary:

  * First Conditional (Should):
    "If you should need help..." -> "SHOULD YOU NEED help, please call me."

  * Second Conditional (Were):
    "If I were in your position..." -> "WERE I in your position, I would accept."

  * Third Conditional (Had):
    "If he had practiced more..." -> "HAD HE PRACTICED more, he would have won."


6. LOCATIVE / DIRECTIONAL FULL VERB INVERSION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
When spatial/directional prepositions are fronted, the ENTIRE main lexical verb 
moves before the subject (no dummy auxiliary 'do/did' is needed):

  * "Down CAME THE RAIN."
  * "Into the classroom WALKED THE PRINCIPAL."
  * "Round the corner STOOD A POLICEMAN."

  EXCEPTION FOR PRONOUNS:
  If the subject is a personal pronoun, normal word order is retained:
  * "Down IT CAME." (NOT: Down came it)
  * "Away THEY RAN." (NOT: Away ran they)


7. HIGH-FREQUENCY COMPETITIVE EXAM ERROR TRAPS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRAP 1: Mispairing Correlatives
    - INCORRECT: No sooner had he arrived when the lights went off.
    - CORRECT:   No sooner had he arrived THAN the lights went off.

  TRAP 2: Using 'Then' instead of 'Than'
    - INCORRECT: No sooner did the bell ring then we left.
    - CORRECT:   No sooner did the bell ring THAN we left.

  TRAP 3: Forgetting Inversion after Fronted Negatives
    - INCORRECT: Seldom I have seen such enthusiasm.
    - CORRECT:   Seldom HAVE I seen such enthusiasm.

  TRAP 4: Using Past Form (V2) after 'Did'
    - INCORRECT: No sooner did he arrived than...
    - CORRECT:   No sooner did he ARRIVE than...
