1. WHAT ARE MODAL AUXILIARIES? (THE 4 INVARIANTS)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Modals express the speaker's ATTITUDE toward an action (ability, certainty, 
permission, obligation, advice, possibility).

  RULE 1: NO 3rd Person Singular '-s':
          - "He MUST go." (NOT: He musts go)
  RULE 2: Followed by BARE INFINITIVE (without 'to'):
          - "She CAN speak French." (NOT: She can to speak)
          *(Exception: 'Ought to' and 'Used to' inherently contain 'to')*
  RULE 3: NO 'Do-Support' for Negatives & Questions:
          - "CAN you swim?" / "She CANNOT swim." (NOT: Do you can swim?)
  RULE 4: NO Infinitives (-to) or Participles (-ing/-ed):
          - Modals have no 'to can' or 'canning'.


2. THE MASTER MODALITY MATRIX (DEGREES OF CERTAINTY & DUTY)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

+------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| 1. CERTAINTY & LOGICAL DEDUCTION (SPECTRUM)                                  |
+------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
  * 100% Positive Deduction:  MUST       -> "The lights are on; he MUST be home."
  * 50% Realistic Possibility: MAY       -> "It MAY rain this evening."
  * 30% Tentative/Remote:      MIGHT     -> "He MIGHT pass if he studies hard."
  * 0% Logical Impossibility:  CAN'T     -> "He only left at 2 PM; he CAN'T be in Delhi!"
  *(CRITICAL TRAP: The negative of deduction 'must be' is 'CAN'T BE', not 'must not'!)*

+------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| 2. ABILITY: GENERAL VS SPECIFIC ACHIEVEMENT                                  |
+------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
  * General Present Ability:  CAN        -> "She CAN swim across the river."
  * General Past Ability:     COULD      -> "He COULD run fast when he was young."
  * Specific Past Achievement:WAS/WERE ABLE TO / MANAGED TO:
    - "Although the sea was stormy, the swimmer WAS ABLE TO reach the shore."
    *(Do NOT use 'could' for a single successful achievement in a crisis!)*

+------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| 3. OBLIGATION: INTERNAL VS EXTERNAL VS PROHIBITION                          |
+------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
  * Internal (Speaker's conviction): MUST       -> "I MUST call my mother today."
  * External (Law / Institutional):  HAVE TO    -> "You HAVE TO wear a helmet."
  * Prohibition (Strictly Forbidden):MUST NOT   -> "You MUST NOT park here."
  * No Obligation (Optional / Free): DON'T HAVE TO / NEEDN'T:
    - "Tomorrow is a holiday; you DON'T HAVE TO wake up early."

+------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| 4. ADVICE, DUTY & WARNING                                                    |
+------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
  * General Advice / Recommendation: SHOULD     -> "You SHOULD exercise daily."
  * Moral / Civic Duty:              OUGHT TO   -> "We OUGHT TO respect nature."
  * Urgent Warning (+ Negative Risk):HAD BETTER (+ Bare Infinitive):
    - "You HAD BETTER leave now, or you will miss the train." (No 'to'!)


3. PAST MODALS: DEDUCTION, REGRET & UNREALIZED ACTIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
+-------------------+----------------------------+-----------------------------+
| PAST MODAL FORM   | PRECISE MEANING            | CANONICAL EXAMPLES          |
+-------------------+----------------------------+-----------------------------+
| MUST HAVE + V3    | Logical positive past      | "The ground is soaked; it   |
|                   | deduction from evidence    |  MUST HAVE RAINED heavily." |
+-------------------+----------------------------+-----------------------------+
| CAN'T/COULDN'T    | Logical negative past      | "He was with me in Kolkata; |
| HAVE + V3         | impossibility              |  he COULDN'T HAVE committed |
|                   |                            |  the crime in Mumbai."      |
+-------------------+----------------------------+-----------------------------+
| SHOULD HAVE + V3  | Unfulfilled moral duty /   | "You SHOULD HAVE TOLD the   |
|                   | Past regret & criticism    |  truth to the police."      |
+-------------------+----------------------------+-----------------------------+
| COULD HAVE + V3   | Unutilized past capability | "You COULD HAVE HELPED him  |
|                   | (had ability, didn't do)   |  (but you chose not to)."   |
+-------------------+----------------------------+-----------------------------+
| WOULD HAVE + V3   | Past conditional intention | "I WOULD HAVE VISITED you,  |
|                   | blocked by circumstances   |  but my flight was delayed."|
+-------------------+----------------------------+-----------------------------+
| NEEDN'T HAVE + V3 | Action WAS done, but in    | "I NEEDN'T HAVE BROUGHT an  |
|                   | hindsight was unnecessary  |  umbrella; it didn't rain." |
+-------------------+----------------------------+-----------------------------+
| DIDN'T NEED TO +V1| Action was NOT done        | "I DIDN'T NEED TO BRING an  |
|                   | because known unnecessary  |  umbrella as it was sunny." |
+-------------------+----------------------------+-----------------------------+


4. SEMI-MODALS / MARGINAL AUXILIARIES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  [A] DARE:
      - As Modal (Negative/Question, Bare Inf): "I DARE NOT speak." / "DARE he ask?"
      - As Lexical Verb (with 'to'):            "He DID NOT DARE TO speak."

  [B] NEED:
      - As Modal (Negative/Question, Bare Inf): "You NEED NOT go." / "NEED I stay?"
      - As Lexical Verb (takes -s and 'to'):    "He NEEDS TO consult a doctor."

  [C] USED TO:
      - Discontinued past habit / state:        "He USED TO smoke (no longer does)."
      - Be / Get Used to + Gerund (-ing):       "I AM USED TO living in a big city."


5. HIGH-FREQUENCY COMPETITIVE EXAM MODAL TRAPS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRAP 1: Double Modal Redundancy
    - INCORRECT: He can be able to solve it.
    - CORRECT:   He CAN solve it. / He IS ABLE TO solve it.

  TRAP 2: Inserting 'To' after 'Had better'
    - INCORRECT: You had better to consult a physician.
    - CORRECT:   You had better CONSULT a physician.

  TRAP 3: Conflating 'Needn't have' and 'Didn't need to'
    - Context: Rahul bought milk, but his mother had already bought two bottles.
    - CORRECT: Rahul NEEDN'T HAVE BOUGHT milk. (He bought it unnecessarily)
