1. ANATOMY OF A PHRASAL VERB (MULTI-WORD VERBS)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A Phrasal Verb is a multi-word lexical unit composed of a Base Verb combined with 
an Adverb Particle and/or a Preposition, functioning as a single semantic predicate.

Structural Formulas:
  1. Verb + Adverb Particle             -> "The car BROKE DOWN."
  2. Verb + Preposition                 -> "She LOOKS AFTER her parents."
  3. Verb + Adverb Particle + Prep      -> "I cannot PUT UP WITH this noise."

Compositional vs Non-Compositional Meaning:
  • Compositional (Literal): Meaning derived from individual parts.
    - "He LOOKED UP at the ceiling." (Looked in an upward direction)
  • Non-Compositional (Idiomatic): Meaning is figurative and fused.
    - "He LOOKED UP the word in the lexicon." (Searched and found)

2. SYNTACTIC CLASSIFICATION: SEPARABILITY & TRANSITIVITY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A. INTRANSITIVE PHRASAL VERBS (No Direct Object):
   - "The plane TOOK OFF smoothly."
   - "The fire BROKE OUT at midnight."
   - "His health BROKE DOWN under pressure."

B. TRANSITIVE SEPARABLE PHRASAL VERBS:
   The direct object can be positioned AFTER the particle OR IN BETWEEN:
   - "Turn off the light"  OR  "Turn the light off" (Both are 100% correct).

   *** THE PRONOUN SANDWICH LAW (CRITICAL EXAM TRAP) ***
   If the Object is a PRONOUN (it, them, him, her, us, me), it MUST sit 
   in the middle between the Verb and the Particle!
   - CORRECT: "Turn IT off." / "Give THEM up." / "Pick HIM up."
   - WRONG:   "Turn off it." / "Give up them." / "Pick up him."

C. TRANSITIVE INSEPARABLE PHRASAL VERBS:
   The verb and preposition CANNOT be separated under any circumstance:
   - "He came across an old coin." (NOT: "He came an old coin across")
   - "She looked after the patient." (NOT: "She looked the patient after")

D. THREE-PART PHRASAL-PREPOSITIONAL VERBS (Always Inseparable):
   - Put up with (Tolerate)          -> "Put up with the inconvenience."
   - Look down on (Despise)          -> "Never look down on the poor."
   - Look forward to (Anticipate)    -> "Looking forward to meeting you."
   - Run out of (Exhaust supply)     -> "We have run out of printer toner."
   - Get away with (Escape penalty)  -> "He won't get away with this fraud."
   - Cut down on (Reduce consumption)-> "Cut down on sugar intake."

3. THE GRAND 8 PHRASAL VERB FAMILIES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. BREAK:
   • Break down: Fail mechanically (engine) or collapse emotionally.
   • Break out:  Erupt suddenly (war, fire, epidemic, riot).
   • Break into: Force entry into a building (burglary).
   • Break up:   Disperse a crowd / terminate a relationship / school recess.
   • Break off:  Stop abruptly during speech or engagement.

2. BRING:
   • Bring about: Cause to happen ("Reforms brought about growth").
   • Bring up:    Rear/educate a child OR introduce a discussion topic.
   • Bring out:   Publish a book or reveal a hidden quality.
   • Bring round: Restore to consciousness or convince someone.

3. CALL:
   • Call off: Cancel an event, strike, or wedding.
   • Call on:  Visit someone formally ("I called on the Minister").
   • Call for: Demand or necessitate ("This crisis calls for swift action").
   • Call in:  Summon an expert ("Call in a physician immediately").
   • Call up:  Recollect/recall a memory OR draft into military service.

4. COME:
   • Come across: Meet or find by chance ("Came across an old manuscript").
   • Come by:     Acquire or obtain something rare or difficult.
   • Come of:     Originate from or result from ("He comes of a noble family").
   • Come round:  Recover consciousness or agree after disagreement.
   • Come off:    Succeed or take place ("The deal came off smoothly").

5. GET:
   • Get over:       Recover from illness, shock, or sorrow.
   • Get along with: Have a harmonious relationship with someone.
   • Get through:    Succeed in an exam / establish a phone connection.
   • Get by:         Manage to survive with minimal resources.
   • Get away with:  Escape punishment for a misdeed.

6. LOOK:
   • Look after: Take care of / tend to someone.
   • Look into:  Investigate a crime or issue.
   • Look for:   Search for a missing person/item.
   • Look up to: Admire and respect a mentor or role model.
   • Look down on: Treat with contempt / despise.
   • Look up:    Search for information in a reference book.

7. PUT:
   • Put off:     Postpone or delay ("Never put off your studies").
   • Put out:     Extinguish a flame, fire, or light.
   • Put up with: Tolerate or endure bad behavior.
   • Put on:      Wear clothing or pretend an attitude.
   • Put away:    Store in its proper place or save money.

8. TURN:
   • Turn down: Reject a request, offer, or application.
   • Turn up:   Arrive or appear unexpectedly.
   • Turn off:  Switch off an appliance / cause disinterest.
   • Turn out:  Prove to be in the end / produce a result.
