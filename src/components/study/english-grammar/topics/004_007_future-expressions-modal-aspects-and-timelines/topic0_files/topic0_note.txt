1. THE MULTI-FACETED FUTURE IN ENGLISH
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Unlike Latin or French, English has no single inflectional future verb tense. 
Instead, future time is expressed through 5 nuanced syntactic structures depending 
on intentionality, evidence, official scheduling, and immediacy.

+------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| 1. THE 5 CORE WAYS TO EXPRESS FUTURE TIME                                    |
+------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
  [A] SIMPLE FUTURE (will / shall + V1):
      - Spontaneous decisions made AT the moment of speaking:
        "The phone is ringing. I WILL answer it."
      - Unplanned offers, promises, and threats:
        "I will help you with your React project tomorrow."
      - Predictions based on personal opinion, belief, or hope:
        "I think India WILL win the championship."

  [B] 'BE GOING TO + V1':
      - Pre-meditated intentions/plans formed BEFORE the moment of speaking:
        "Swadeep is GOING TO purchase a new laptop next Saturday."
      - Predictions based on VISIBLE PRESENT EVIDENCE (sensory certainty):
        "Look at those heavy black clouds! It IS GOING TO rain."
        "Watch out! That ladder IS GOING TO collapse."

  [C] PRESENT CONTINUOUS (am / is / are + V-ing):
      - Definite personal arrangements where time, date, and other parties are fixed:
        "We ARE MEETING the director tomorrow at 11:30 AM."
        "She IS FLYING to London next Tuesday (tickets already booked)."

  [D] SIMPLE PRESENT (V1 / V5 -s/-es):
      - Fixed official timetables, public transportation, and institutional schedules:
        "The Rajdhani Express LEAVES at 4:45 PM tomorrow."
        "The semester examination COMMENCES on 15th November."

  [E] 'BE ABOUT TO + V1' & 'BE TO + V1':
      - Immediate impending future:
        "Hurry up! The train IS ABOUT TO depart."
      - Formal duties, official arrangements, and commands:
        "The Prime Minister IS TO inaugurate the bridge tomorrow."


+------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| 2. FUTURE CONTINUOUS TENSE (will / shall be + V-ing)                         |
+------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
  * FORMULA: Subject + will be + Present Participle (V-ing)
  * USAGE:
      [A] Action in progress at a specific designated future time:
          - "At 8:00 PM tonight, I will be attending a live webinar."
      [B] Routine events expected in the ordinary course of things:
          - "I will be seeing Mr. Bose at the office tomorrow anyway."
      [C] Polite inquiries about someone's future plans:
          - "Will you be using your vehicle this evening?"


+------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| 3. FUTURE PERFECT TENSE (will / shall have + V3)                             |
+------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
  * THE CONCEPT OF FUTURE COMPLETION BEFORE A DEADLINE:
    Used to indicate an action that will be fully finished BEFORE a specific 
    point of time or another event in the future.

  * MANDATORY CANONICAL FORMULAS:
      Formula 1 (with 'BY + Future Time'):
        BY + [Specific Future Time], Subject + will have + V3
        - "BY next Friday, Swadeep WILL HAVE COMPLETED his dissertation."
        - "BY 5:00 PM, they WILL HAVE PACKED all the luggage."

      Formula 2 (with 'BY THE TIME + Present Simple'):
        BY THE TIME + [Subordinate Clause: Present Simple (V1/V5)], [Main Clause: will have + V3]
        - "BY THE TIME you REACH home, mother WILL HAVE PREPARED dinner."
        - "BY THE TIME the police ARRIVE, the thief WILL HAVE ESCAPED."
      
      * FATAL TRAP: Never use 'will' after 'By the time'!
        - INCORRECT: By the time you will reach home...
        - CORRECT:   By the time you REACH home...


+------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| 4. FUTURE PERFECT CONTINUOUS (will have been + V-ing)                        |
+------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
  * FORMULA: Subject + will have been + V-ing + [for + duration] + [by + future marker]
  * USAGE:
      Measures the duration of an ongoing activity up to a specific milestone in the future:
      - "By next August, Debangshu WILL HAVE BEEN CODING in JavaScript FOR five years."


2. THE SUBORDINATE TIME & CONDITION PROHIBITION RULE (COMPETITIVE EXAM CRUCIAL)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
In English syntax, when a sentence refers to future time, the SUBORDINATE clause 
introduced by conjunctions of TIME (when, as soon as, before, after, until, while) 
or CONDITION (if, unless, provided that, in case) MUST use SIMPLE PRESENT, NOT Future:

  * RULE: [If / When / As soon as + SIMPLE PRESENT] + [Main Clause: WILL + V1]
  * EXAMPLES:
      - INCORRECT: If it WILL rain tomorrow, we will cancel the picnic.
      - CORRECT:   If it RAINS tomorrow, we will cancel the picnic.

      - INCORRECT: As soon as he WILL arrive, we will start the meeting.
      - CORRECT:   As soon as he ARRIVES, we will start the meeting.

      - INCORRECT: Unless you WILL work hard, you will not pass.
      - CORRECT:   Unless you WORK hard, you will not pass.


3. BENGALI GUIDANCE & COMPARATIVE ANALYSIS (বাংলা বিশ্লেষণ)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  * বাংলা ভাষায় ভবিষ্যতের সব ক্ষেত্রেই 'ব / বে / বেন' (যাব, খাবে) ব্যবহৃত হয়, কিন্তু
    ইংরেজিতে পরিস্থিতি অনুযায়ী ৫টি রূপ রয়েছে:
    - পূর্ব পরিকল্পনা ছাড়া তাত্ক্ষণিক সিদ্ধান্ত: 'I will help you.'
    - পূর্ব পরিকল্পিত ইচ্ছা / দৃশ্যমান প্রমাণ: 'I am going to start / It is going to rain.'
    - নির্ধারিত ব্যবস্থা (Arrangement): 'We are having an exam tomorrow.'
    - অফিসিয়াল সময়সূচি (Timetable): 'The train leaves at 9 AM.'

  * শর্তযুক্ত ও সময়মূলক ক্লজে 'will' নিষিদ্ধ:
    - ভুল: If you will come, I will go.
    - সঠিক: If you COME, I will go.
