1. THE NATURE & TAXONOMY OF THE VERB
================================================================================

an ACTION, an OCCURRENCE / EVENT, or a STATE OF BEING.

2. THE MASTER 26 AUXILIARY VERBS CATALOG (26 ANOMALOUS FINITES & HELPING VERBS)
================================================================================
Auxiliary verbs help lexical verbs form tenses, continuous/perfect aspects, passive 
voice, negations, interrogative inversions, emphatic statements, and modalities.

Traditional and modern grammars catalog exactly 26 standard auxiliary forms:
- 14 Primary Auxiliaries (Forms of BE, DO, HAVE)
- 9 Pure Modal Auxiliaries (Shall, Should, Will, Would, Can, Could, May, Might, Must)
- 3 Semi-Modals / Marginal Auxiliaries (Ought to, Used to, Need/Dare)

---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
#   Auxiliary  Category       Negative Form   Primary Function               Exemplar Sentence
---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1   be         Primary (Be)   (be not)        Base/Imperative/Subjunctive    "Be quiet." / "She will be ready."
2   am         Primary (Be)   am not/aren't I? 1st Person Present Continuous  "I am preparing for competitive exams."
3   is         Primary (Be)   isn't           3rd Person Sing Present        "He is lecturing on English syntax."
4   are        Primary (Be)   aren't          Plural/2nd Person Present      "We are analyzing the 26 auxiliaries."
5   was        Primary (Be)   wasn't          Singular Past Continuous/Pass. "The manuscript was transcribed by him."
6   were       Primary (Be)   weren't         Plural Past Continuous/Subj.   "If I were you, I would accept."
7   been       Primary (Be)   (hasn't been)   V3 Auxiliary (Perfect/Passive) "She has been teaching since 2012."
8   being      Primary (Be)   (isn't being)   V4 Continuous Passive Aux.     "The new bridge is being constructed."

9   have       Primary (Have) haven't         Present Perfect / Causative    "I have solved every diagnostic drill."
10  has        Primary (Have) hasn't          3rd Person Sing Pres. Perfect  "She has mastered irregular verbs."
11  had        Primary (Have) hadn't          Past Perfect / Conditional     "The train had departed before we arrived."

12  do         Primary (Do)   don't           Dummy Aux / Negation / Emphasis"I do understand your perspective."
13  does       Primary (Do)   doesn't         3rd Person Sing Neg / Question "Does this formula apply here?"
14  did        Primary (Do)   didn't          Past Negation & Question       "Did they complete the laboratory work?"

15  shall      Pure Modal     shan't          Future Determination / Offer   "Shall we commence the proceedings?"
16  should     Pure Modal     shouldn't       Moral Duty / Advisability      "You should practice error spotting daily."
17  will       Pure Modal     won't           Future Certainty / Willingness "I will achieve distinction in English."
18  would      Pure Modal     wouldn't        Past Habit / Polite Request    "Would you kindly explain this rule?"
19  can        Pure Modal     can't           Present Ability / Permission   "She can speak five languages fluently."
20  could      Pure Modal     couldn't        Past Ability / Polite Request  "Could you lend me your reference book?"
21  may        Pure Modal     mayn't          Formal Permission / Possibility"May you achieve glorious success!"
22  might      Pure Modal     mightn't        Remote / Tentative Possibility "It might rain in the evening."
23  must       Pure Modal     mustn't         Strong Necessity / Deduction   "Candidates must carry valid hall tickets."

24  ought (to) Semi-Modal     oughtn't (to)   Moral Obligation / Social Duty "We ought to respect our elders."
25  used (to)  Semi-Modal     usedn't / didn't use to Discontinued Past Habit"He used to bathe in the river daily."
26  need/dare  Semi-Modal     needn't/daren't Semi-modal negation without 'do'"You needn't worry." / "How dare you!"

BENGALI SUMMARY (বাংলা অর্থ ও তাৎপর্য):
- Primary Auxiliaries (Be, Have, Do): কাল (Tense), ঘটমানতা (Continuous), পুরাঘটিত অবস্থা (Perfect) ও কর্মবাচ্য (Passive) গঠনে প্রধান ভূমিকা পালন করে।
- Modal Auxiliaries (Shall, Will, Can, May, Must ইত্যাদি): বক্তার মনোভাব, বাধ্যবাধকতা, সামর্থ্য, সম্ভাবনা ও অনুমতি প্রকাশ করে।
- Semi-Modals (Ought to, Used to, Need, Dare): আংশিক Modal ও আংশিক Main Verb-এর মতো আচরণ করে।

3. TRANSITIVITY CLASSIFICATIONS
================================================================================
  [A] MONOTRANSITIVE (Subject + Verb + Direct Object):
      - "She wrote A NOVEL." (Direct Object answers 'What?' or 'Whom?')

  [B] DITRANSITIVE (Subject + Verb + Indirect Object + Direct Object):
      - "The teacher gave US (IO) A DIFFICULT TASK (DO)."
      - Alternate Prepositional Pattern: "The teacher gave a difficult task TO us."

  [C] COMPLEX-TRANSITIVE / FACTITIVE (Subject + Verb + Object + Object Complement):
      - "The citizens elected HIM (Obj) MAYOR (Obj Complement)."
      - "They painted the fence (Obj) WHITE (Obj Complement)."

  [D] INTRANSITIVE OF COMPLETE PREDICATION (No Object Needed):
      - "The birds sing." / "The baby slept peacefully."

  [E] LINKING / COPULAR VERBS (Subject + Copula + Subject Complement):
      - Be, seem, appear, become, remain, look, sound, smell, taste, feel.
      - "The soup smells DELICIOUS (Adjective Complement)."

  [F] ERGATIVE / INCHOATIVE VERBS (Transitive <-> Intransitive Dual Action):
      - Transitive:   "The boy BROKE the window." (External agent)
      - Intransitive: "The window BROKE." (Subject undergoes the change)
      - Examples: open, close, melt, boil, freeze, start, drop, sink.

4. THE STATIVE VS DYNAMIC DOMAIN (CRITICAL EXAM LAW!)
================================================================================
  * DYNAMIC VERBS describe voluntary physical or mental activities that have a 
    beginning, duration, and end. They freely accept Progressive (-ing) tenses.
    - "He is running." / "She is coding." / "They are discussing."

  * STATIVE VERBS describe static continuous conditions, states of mind, 
    emotions, relationships, or sensory impressions. In standard English, they 
    RESIST progressive (-ing) continuous aspects!

+------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| THE 4 BUCKETS OF PURE STATIVE VERBS (DO NOT USE IN CONTINUOUS TENSES!)       |
+------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
  1. COGNITION & THOUGHT:
     - Know, believe, understand, recognize, remember, forget, doubt, mean, realize.
     * INCORRECT: I am knowing the answer.
     * CORRECT:   I KNOW the answer.

  2. EMOTION & FEELING:
     - Love, hate, prefer, adore, detest, despise, wish, want, care.
     * INCORRECT: She is loving chocolate.
     * CORRECT:   She LOVES chocolate.

  3. POSSESSION & RELATION:
     - Have (ownership), belong to, own, possess, comprise, contain, resemble, cost.
     * INCORRECT: This property is belonging to Mr. Sharma.
     * CORRECT:   This property BELONGS TO Mr. Sharma.
     * INCORRECT: I am having a bike.
     * CORRECT:   I HAVE a bike.

  4. SENSATION & PERCEPTION:
     - See (involuntary), hear, smell, taste, feel, notice, sound.
     * INCORRECT: The medicine is tasting bitter.
     * CORRECT:   The medicine TASTES bitter.

6. THE 5 PRINCIPAL VERB FORMS
================================================================================
Every English verb operates across 5 primary morphological paradigms:
  - V1 (Base / Infinitive):           write, play, go
  - V2 (Simple Past):                 wrote, played, went
  - V3 (Past Participle):             written, played, gone
  - V4 (Present Participle / Gerund): writing, playing, going
  - V5 (3rd Person Singular Present): writes, plays, goes

7. HIGH-FREQUENCY COMPETITIVE EXAM TRAPS
================================================================================
  TRAP 1: Stative Progressive Violation
    - INCORRECT: I am understanding what you mean.
    - CORRECT:   I understand what you mean.

  TRAP 2: Inappropriate Sensory Adverbs
    - INCORRECT: The velvet blanket feels smoothly.
    - CORRECT:   The velvet blanket feels SMOOTH. (Linking verb + Adjective)

  TRAP 3: Conflating Lay and Lie
    - INCORRECT: She lay the baby on the cot. (Past of 'lay' is 'laid')
    - CORRECT:   She LAID the baby on the cot.

  TRAP 4: Passivizing Unpassivizable Intransitives
    - INCORRECT: The accident was occurred at noon.
    - CORRECT:   The accident occurred at noon.
