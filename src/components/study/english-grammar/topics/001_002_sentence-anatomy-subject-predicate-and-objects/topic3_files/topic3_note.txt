1. CORE TAXONOMY: THE PREDICATE ENGINE (বিধেয় ও ক্রিয়া কাঠামো)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. SIMPLE PREDICATE (The Pure Verb Engine):
   • Consists strictly of the Finite Verb Phrase (Lexical verb + Auxiliaries/Modals).
   • Excludes all adverbs, negative particles ('not'), objects, and prepositional phrases.
   • Examples:
     - "Swadeep [codes]." -> Simple Predicate = "codes".
     - "Swadeep [has been coding]." -> Simple Predicate = "has been coding".
     - "Swadeep [might have been studying]." -> Simple Predicate = "might have been studying".

2. COMPLETE PREDICATE (The Full Predication):
   • Encompasses the Simple Predicate + all Direct/Indirect Objects + Complements + Adverbials.
   • Example: "[has been diligently coding the web application all evening]".

2. SPECIAL PREDICATE VARIATIONS & DISCONTINUOUS STRUCTURES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. DISCONTINUOUS / SPLIT PREDICATES:
   • Interrupted by Adverbs: "He has [always] arrived on time." -> Simple Pred = "has arrived".
   • Interrupted by Negation: "She does [not] understand." -> Simple Pred = "does understand".
   • In Questions: "[Did] Tuhina [complete] the task?" -> Simple Pred = "Did complete".
   • In Negative Inversion: "Rarely [have] I [seen] such dedication." -> Simple Pred = "have seen".

2. COMPOUND PREDICATES (Multiple Actions for One Subject):
   • Single Subject + Two or more coordinate verbs:
     - "Swadeep [entered the lab] and [started coding]."
   • Rule: Maintain strict Tense Harmony across coordinate verbs (*"entered and start" is WRONG!).
   • Punctuation: Do NOT use a comma between two verbs of a simple compound predicate.

3. PHRASAL VERB PREDICATES:
   • Verb + Particle functions as a single semantic unit:
     - "The police [looked into] the incident." -> Simple Pred = "looked into".

3. BENGALI LEARNER BRIDGES & TRAPS (বাংলা মাধ্যমের সতর্কতা)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[X] TRAP 1: Treating 'not' or adverbs as part of the verb phrase
    - 'not' is a Negative Adverb; it is part of the Complete Predicate, but not the Simple Predicate.

[X] TRAP 2: Missing the helping verb in passive structures
    - In "The report was submitted yesterday", the Simple Predicate is "was submitted" (Aux + V3), 
      not just "submitted".

[X] TRAP 3: Tense discord in compound predicates
    - "Debangshu came home and eat lunch" [WRONG: Past + Present].
    - "Debangshu came home and ATE lunch" [CORRECT: Past + Past].

4. ESSENTIAL REVISION CHECKLIST
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

[✓] Recognize split/discontinuous verb phrases in questions and negative clauses.
[✓] Maintain tense parallelism across all verbs in a Compound Predicate.
[✓] Master the engine analogy: Simple Predicate = Verb; Complete Predicate = Verb + Cargo.
