1. THE DUAL BIPARTITE ARCHITECTURE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Every standard English clause divides into exactly TWO structural halves:
  [ COMPLETE SUBJECT ] + [ COMPLETE PREDICATE ]

2. THE COMPLETE SUBJECT (THE THEME)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Definition: The entire nominal phrase representing who or what performs the 
  action or is being described.
- Components:
  * Simple Subject: The core noun/pronoun head alone.
  * Modifiers: Articles, Attributive Adjectives, Prepositional Modifiers, 
    Relative Clauses.
- Example: "[The dedicated young software engineering student from Barrackpore]"

3. THE COMPLETE PREDICATE (THE RHEME)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Definition: Everything asserted, questioned, or commanded about the subject.
- Mandatory Core: At least ONE FINITE VERB GROUP.
- Components:
  * Simple Predicate: Finite verb group (e.g., 'has been developing').
  * Direct & Indirect Objects: (e.g., 'an AI model').
  * Complements & Adverbials: (e.g., 'meticulously in the laboratory').
- Example: "[has been developing an AI model meticulously in the laboratory]."

4. SPECIAL SYNTACTIC STRUCTURES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Inverted Sentences:
   - "Into the room rushed [the anxious students]."
   - Verb precedes subject for dramatic or stylistic emphasis.
2. Imperative Sentences:
   - "[You (implied)] Please submit the code before 5 PM."
3. Dummy Subject 'There':
   - "There are [five new workstations] in the laboratory."
   - The plural noun 'five new workstations' is the true grammatical subject!
