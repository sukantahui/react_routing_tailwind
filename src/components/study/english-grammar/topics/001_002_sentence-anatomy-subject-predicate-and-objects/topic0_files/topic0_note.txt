1. SENTENCE ARCHITECTURE: THE DUAL CORE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Every standard English sentence consists of two fundamental components:
  1. SUBJECT: The person, entity, or concept that performs the action or is
     described. (Who or What the sentence is about).
  2. PREDICATE: Everything that is affirmed, denied, or asserted about the
     subject. Must contain at least one Finite Verb.

+-----------------------+------------------------------------------------------+
| Sentence Component    | Structure / Scope                                    |
+-----------------------+------------------------------------------------------+
| Simple Subject        | The core noun/pronoun head alone (e.g., 'tree').     |
| Complete Subject      | Simple subject + all determiners, adjectives,        |
|                       | prepositional modifiers.                             |
+-----------------------+------------------------------------------------------+
| Simple Predicate      | The finite verb group alone (e.g., 'has collapsed'). |
| Complete Predicate    | Finite verb + objects + complements + adverbials.    |
+-----------------------+------------------------------------------------------+

2. THE SEVEN FUNDAMENTAL SENTENCE PATTERNS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. SV   (Subject + Intransitive Verb)
   - Example: "The express train arrived."
2. SVO  (Subject + Transitive Verb + Direct Object)
   - Example: "Swadeep developed a web application."
3. SVOO (Subject + Ditransitive Verb + Indirect Object + Direct Object)
   - Example: "Sukanta Sir taught Tuhina English Grammar."
   - Prepositional Variant: "Sukanta Sir taught English Grammar to Tuhina."
4. SVC  (Subject + Linking/Copular Verb + Subject Complement)
   - Example: "Abhronila is an exceptional researcher." (Noun Complement)
   - Example: "The mangoes taste sweet." (Adjective Complement)
5. SVOC (Subject + Complex-Transitive Verb + Direct Object + Object Complement)
   - Example: "The committee elected Debangshu President."
   - Example: "The jury found the defendant innocent."
6. SVA  (Subject + Intransitive Verb + Obligatory Adverbial)
   - Example: "The coaching center is located in Barrackpore."
7. SVOA (Subject + Transitive Verb + Direct Object + Obligatory Adverbial)
   - Example: "He placed the reference books on the study table."

3. DIRECT OBJECT VS SUBJECT COMPLEMENT: THE EQUALITY TEST
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
+-------------------+--------------------+--------------------+----------------+
| Test Criterion    | Direct Object      | Subject Complement | Object Comp.   |
+-------------------+--------------------+--------------------+----------------+
| Verb Type         | Transitive Action  | Linking / Copula   | Complex-Trans. |
| Entity Relation   | Subject != Object  | Subject == Compl.  | Object == Comp.|
| Example           | "He kicked the     | "He is a doctor."  | "They named him|
|                   | ball." (He != Ball)| (He == Doctor)     | John." (Him==J)|
| Passive Voice     | Possible (Ball was | Impossible (No     | Possible       |
|                   | kicked by him)     | passive for SVC)   |                |
+-------------------+--------------------+--------------------+----------------+

4. BENGALI LEARNER PEDAGOGICAL BRIDGES (বাংলা মাধ্যম শিক্ষার্থীদের জন্য)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
* Trap 1: Subject Complement Confused with Adverb
  - Incorrect: "The flower smells sweetly" ❌
  - Correct:   "The flower smells sweet" ✔️
  - Rule: Sensory Linking Verbs (smell, taste, look, feel, sound) take Subject
    Complement Adjectives, never adverbs of manner!

* Trap 2: Dummy Subject 'There' Concord
  - "There IS five students in the room" ❌ (Wrong!)
  - "There ARE five students in the room" ✔️ (Correct! Subject is 'five students').

* Trap 3: Bengali Ditransitive Word Order
  - Bengali: "আমি তাকে বইটি দিলাম" (IO + DO)
  - English SVOO: "I gave him (IO) the book (DO)."
  - English SVO + Prep: "I gave the book (DO) to him (Prep Object)."
