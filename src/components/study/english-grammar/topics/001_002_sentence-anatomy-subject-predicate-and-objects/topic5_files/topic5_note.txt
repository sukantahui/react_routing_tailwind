1. THE 'TO' VS 'FOR' PREPOSITIONAL RULE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A. Verbs taking 'TO' (Transmission / Delivery / Communication):
   - give, send, lend, pass, pay, promise, show, teach, tell, write, read.
   - Example: "He lent [his laptop] TO [Swadeep]."

B. Verbs taking 'FOR' (Beneficiary / Creation / Acquisition):
   - buy, cook, bake, build, choose, find, fetch, order, prepare, make.
   - Example: "He bought [a laptop] FOR [his brother]."

2. THE 'EXPLAIN / SUGGEST' NON-DATIVE INVARIANT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- High-level Latinate communication verbs NEVER permit the SVOO pattern!
- Verbs: explain, suggest, describe, announce, confess, introduce, admit, repeat.
  * Wrong: "He explained me the question." ❌
  * Right: "He explained the question to me." ✔️
  * Wrong: "She suggested me a new plan." ❌
  * Right: "She suggested a new plan to me." ✔️

3. PRONOUN OBJECT ORDER RULE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
When both objects are pronouns, English mandates:
  [Verb] + [Direct Object Pronoun] + TO/FOR + [Indirect Object Pronoun]
  * "Give IT to ME." (Never "Give me it" in formal standard prose).
