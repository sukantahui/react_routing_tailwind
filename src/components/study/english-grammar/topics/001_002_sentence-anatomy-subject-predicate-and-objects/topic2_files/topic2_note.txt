1. SIMPLE SUBJECT (THE CORE NOMINAL HEAD)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Definition: The single noun or pronoun head stripped of all determiners, 
  adjectives, and modifying phrases.
- Example: "The old banyan [tree] near the river collapsed." -> Simple Subject: tree.

2. COMPOUND SUBJECT (CONJUNCTION-LINKED NOMINALS)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Definition: Two or more simple subjects joined by conjunctions sharing one verb.
- Rule 1: Joined by 'AND' -> Typically PLURAL.
  * "Swadeep and Debangshu [are] coding."
- Exception: Unitary Idea / Dish -> SINGULAR.
  * "Bread and butter [is] wholesome."
  * "Slow and steady [wins] the race."

3. THE RULE OF PROXIMITY (EITHER... OR / NEITHER... NOR)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Verb agrees with the subject CLOSEST to it:
  * "Neither the teacher nor the [students were] present." (Plural closest)
  * "Neither the students nor the [teacher was] present." (Singular closest)

4. INTERVENING PARENTHETICAL TRAPS (NOT COMPOUND SUBJECTS!)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- The following phrases DO NOT make a subject compound:
  * as well as, along with, together with, in addition to, accompanied by.
- Example: "The mentor, along with fifty students, [has] arrived." (Singular!)
