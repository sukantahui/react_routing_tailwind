1. WHAT IS A GERUND? (THE VERBAL NOUN)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A Gerund is a non-finite verb form ending in '-ing' that functions syntactically 
as a NOUN while retaining verbal characteristics (can take an object or adverbial).

+-------------------+----------------------------+-----------------------------+
| GERUNDIAL ROLE    | EXPLANATION                | CANONICAL EXAMPLES          |
+-------------------+----------------------------+-----------------------------+
| 1. Subject        | Acts as sentence subject   | - "SWIMMING is good exercise"|
|                   |                            | - "READING expands the mind"|
+-------------------+----------------------------+-----------------------------+
| 2. Direct Object  | Object of transitive verb  | - "She enjoys PAINTING."    |
|                   |                            | - "He avoided MEETING me."  |
+-------------------+----------------------------+-----------------------------+
| 3. Subj. Compl.   | Follows linking verb       | - "My passion is TEACHING." |
+-------------------+----------------------------+-----------------------------+
| 4. Prep. Object   | Follows a preposition      | - "Fond of SINGING."        |
|                   |                            | - "Prevented from ENTERING."|
+-------------------+----------------------------+-----------------------------+


2. THE POSSESSIVE CASE RULE BEFORE GERUNDS (CRITICAL EXAM LAW!)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Because a Gerund is a NOUN, any noun or pronoun modifying it MUST be in the 
POSSESSIVE (GENITIVE) CASE:

  * Pronouns: Use MY, YOUR, HIS, HER, ITS, OUR, THEIR (NOT: me, you, him, them)
  * Nouns:    Use Apostrophe + S (Rahul's, the teacher's)

  * INCORRECT: I insisted on HIM coming with us.
  * CORRECT:   I insisted on HIS coming with us.

  * INCORRECT: She disapproved of RAHUL smoking.
  * CORRECT:   She disapproved of RAHUL'S smoking.


3. GERUND VS PRESENT PARTICIPLE: THE DEFINITIVE DIAGNOSTIC TEST
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Both end in '-ing', but their grammatical identities are completely distinct:

  [A] GERUND = VERB + NOUN
      - Diagnostic: Can be replaced by a noun or the pronoun "IT".
      - "SWIMMING is refreshing." -> ("IT is refreshing" -> Valid! = Gerund)

  [B] PRESENT PARTICIPLE = VERB + ADJECTIVE
      - Diagnostic: Describes ongoing action or qualifies a noun.
      - "I saw a SWIMMING duck." -> ("a duck that was swimming" = Participle)


4. VERBS WITH DIVERGENT MEANINGS (GERUND VS INFINITIVE)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
+----------+---------------------------------+---------------------------------+
| VERB     | VERB + GERUND (-ING)            | VERB + TO-INFINITIVE            |
+----------+---------------------------------+---------------------------------+
| STOP     | Quit the habit / activity       | Pause in order to do something  |
|          | "He STOPPED SMOKING." (Gave up) | "He STOPPED TO SMOKE." (Paused) |
+----------+---------------------------------+---------------------------------+
| REMEMBER | Recall a past completed memory  | Remember a duty and perform it  |
|          | "I REMEMBER LOCKING the door."  | "REMEMBER TO LOCK the door."    |
+----------+---------------------------------+---------------------------------+
| FORGET   | Forget having done a past action| Forget to perform a future duty |
|          | "I FORGOT PAYING the bill."     | "I FORGOT TO PAY the bill."     |
+----------+---------------------------------+---------------------------------+
| REGRET   | Feel sorrow over a past action  | Apologize before bad news       |
|          | "I REGRET TELLING him."         | "I REGRET TO INFORM you..."     |
+----------+---------------------------------+---------------------------------+


5. PHRASAL PREPOSITIONS ENDING IN 'TO' (+ GERUND!)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
In the following idioms, 'to' is a PREPOSITION (not an infinitive marker) and 
MUST take a Gerund:
  - LOOK FORWARD TO + Gerund:    "Looking forward to MEETING you."
  - WITH A VIEW TO + Gerund:     "With a view to EXPANDING our business."
  - ACCUSTOMED / USED TO + Gerund: "Accustomed to WORKING late."
  - ADDICTED TO + Gerund:        "Addicted to GAMBLING."
  - AVERSE TO + Gerund:          "Averse to TAKING risks."


6. DANGLING (UNATTACHED) PARTICIPLES & HOW TO REPAIR THEM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A participial phrase at the start of a sentence must describe the SUBJECT of the 
main clause. If it attaches to the wrong subject, it creates a DANGLING ERROR:

  * THE CLASSIC DANGLING ERROR:
    ✗ "Walking in the garden, a snake bit him."
    (Implies that the snake was walking in the garden!)

  * METHOD 1 REPAIR (Convert to Adverbial Clause):
    ✓ "WHILE HE WAS WALKING in the garden, a snake bit him."

  * METHOD 2 REPAIR (Align Passive Main Subject):
    ✓ "Walking in the garden, HE WAS BITTEN by a snake."

  * THE WEATHER DANGLING ERROR:
    ✗ "Being a rainy day, I did not go to school." (Implies 'I was a rainy day'!)
    ✓ "IT BEING a rainy day, I did not go to school." (Nominative Absolute)


7. THE NOMINATIVE ABSOLUTE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
An independent noun/pronoun phrase coupled with a participle, grammatically 
free from the main predicate:
  - "The sun HAVING SET, the travelers halted for the night."
  - "God WILLING, we will triumph."
