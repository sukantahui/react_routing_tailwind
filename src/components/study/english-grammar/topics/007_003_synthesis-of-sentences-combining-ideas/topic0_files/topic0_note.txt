1. WHAT IS SYNTHESIS OF SENTENCES?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Synthesis is the grammatical art of combining two or more separate simple sentences 
into ONE single, coherent sentence without altering the original meaning.

Three Target Architectural Models:
  1. Synthesis into ONE SIMPLE SENTENCE (Strictly ONE Finite Verb)
  2. Synthesis into ONE COMPOUND SENTENCE (Two or more Co-ordinate Clauses)
  3. Synthesis into ONE COMPLEX SENTENCE (One Principal + Subordinate Clauses)

================================================================================
2. SYNTHESIS INTO ONE SIMPLE SENTENCE: THE 6 MASTER METHODS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
*** CARDINAL LAW: The resulting sentence must contain EXACTLY ONE FINITE VERB! ***

METHOD 1: USING PARTICIPLES (Present, Past, Perfect Participle)
  • Same Subject, Sequential Actions:
    - Input: "He heard the alarm. He jumped out of bed."
    - Output: "HEARING the alarm, he jumped out of bed." (Present Participle)
    - Input: "The surgeon finished the operation. He met the patient's family."
    - Output: "HAVING FINISHED the operation, the surgeon met the family." (Perfect Participle)

METHOD 2: USING NOUNS OR PHRASES IN APPOSITION
  • Renaming the subject with an explanatory nominal phrase:
    - Input: "Rabindranath Tagore was a Nobel laureate. He composed the national anthem."
    - Output: "Rabindranath Tagore, A NOBEL LAUREATE, composed the national anthem."

METHOD 3: USING PREPOSITION WITH NOUN OR GERUND
  • Converting clauses into prepositional modifiers:
    - Input: "He heard the catastrophic news. He fainted."
    - Output: "ON HEARING the catastrophic news, he fainted."
    - Input: "He is poor. He is remarkably honest."
    - Output: "IN SPITE OF his poverty, he is remarkably honest."

METHOD 4: USING THE NOMINATIVE ABSOLUTE
  • Used when the two sentences have DIFFERENT SUBJECTS and a cause-effect relationship:
    Formula: [Subject 1] + [Being / Having + V3] , [Subject 2 + Verb...]
    - Input: "The sun set. The villagers returned to their homes."
    - Output: "THE SUN HAVING SET, the villagers returned to their homes."
    - Input: "The weather was stormy. The ship did not sail."
    - Output: "THE WEATHER BEING STORMY, the ship did not sail."

METHOD 5: USING INFINITIVES (to + V1)
  • Compressing a purpose or outcome clause:
    - Input: "He works hard day and night. He wants to support his aged parents."
    - Output: "He works hard day and night TO SUPPORT his aged parents."
    - Input: "He is very proud. He will not apologize."
    - Output: "He is TOO PROUD TO APOLOGIZE."

METHOD 6: USING ADVERBS OR ADVERBIAL PHRASES
  • Condensing an entire secondary sentence into a single modifier:
    - Input: "The warrior died on the battlefield. His death was heroic."
    - Output: "The warrior died HEROICALLY on the battlefield."
    - Input: "He is an honest leader. There is no doubt about it."
    - Output: "He is UNDOUBTEDLY an honest leader."

3. SYNTHESIS INTO ONE COMPOUND SENTENCE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Joined by Coordinating Conjunctions:

1. Cumulative / Copulative (Addition): and, both...and, not only...but also
   - "He was an orator, AND he was a statesman."
2. Adversative (Contrast): but, yet, still, whereas, while
   - "He worked tirelessly, YET he failed to achieve his goal."
3. Disjunctive / Alternative (Choice): or, either...or, neither...nor, otherwise
   - "Work consistently, OR you will forfeit the opportunity."
4. Illative (Inference / Result): therefore, so, for, consequently
   - "He breached the contract; THEREFORE, he was penalized."

================================================================================
4. SYNTHESIS INTO ONE COMPLEX SENTENCE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Joined by Subordinating Connectives:

1. Using Noun Clauses (that, what, why, whether):
   - "I am certain THAT he will succeed."
2. Using Adjective (Relative) Clauses (who, which, that, where, whose):
   - "The student WHO scored first rank received a scholarship."
3. Using Adverbial Clauses (because, although, if, unless, as soon as, so that):
   - "ALTHOUGH he was fatigued, he continued the rescue mission."

================================================================================
5. COMMON TRAPS IN ICSE / ISC / COMPETITIVE EXAMS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Trap 1: "Join without using and, but, or so":
  - DO NOT create a compound sentence! Use Participles, Infinitives, Prepositions + Gerunds, or Nominative Absolute.

Trap 2: Dangling Modifier with Participles:
  - WRONG: "Walking in the garden, a snake bit him." (Means the snake was walking!)
  - RIGHT: "While he was walking in the garden, a snake bit him." OR "Walking in the garden, he was bitten by a snake."
