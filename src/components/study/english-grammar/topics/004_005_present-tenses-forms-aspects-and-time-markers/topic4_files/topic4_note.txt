1. FORMULA & SYNTACTIC ARCHITECTURE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
• Affirmative: Subject + have / has + Past Participle (V3)
  - I / We / You / They / Plural Subject + have + V3
    Example: They have published their findings in Nature.
  - He / She / It / Singular Subject + has + V3
    Example: Abhronila has calibrated the spectroscope.

• Negative: Subject + have / has + not + V3
  - Example: We have not received the official confirmation yet.

• Interrogative: Have / Has + Subject + V3 ...?
  - Example: Have you ever visited the historical museum at Barrackpore?

2. THE CENTRAL THEORETICAL PRINCIPLE: THE PRESENT-PAST BRIDGE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The Present Perfect is fundamentally a PRESENT tense with past reference. It connects an event that took place at an unspecified past time to the CURRENT MOMENT through one of four semantic bridges:

(A) Past Action with Direct Present Consequence (Resultative):
    - The action is finished, but its direct physical or legal outcome exists NOW.
    - Example: "I have cut my finger." -> Consequence: My finger is bleeding right now.
    - Example: "He has lost his key." -> Consequence: He cannot open the door right now.
    - (Contrast with Simple Past: "He lost his key yesterday, but found it this morning.")

(B) Life Experiences (Indefinite Past up to the Present):
    - What has (or has not) happened during someone's lifetime up to now.
    - Adverbs: ever, never, before, once, twice, several times.
    - Example: Have you ever seen a total solar eclipse?
    - Example: Tuhina has never failed a diagnostic test.

(C) Immediate Recent Past & Timely Completion:
    - Actions completed just moments ago or earlier than anticipated.
    - Adverbs: just, already, yet.
    - Placement Rules:
      • 'Just' and 'Already' sit between the auxiliary and V3: "She has ALREADY solved the problem."
      • 'Yet' sits at the end of negative sentences and questions: "Has he arrived YET?" / "No, he hasn't arrived YET."

(D) Unfinished Time Periods:
    - When the time period mentioned is NOT yet over at the time of speaking.
    - Markers: today, this morning (if still morning), this week, this semester, this year.
    - Example: "I have drunk three cups of tea today." (The day is still ongoing).

3. MANDATORY TIME MARKERS CATALOG
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
• JUST: A very short time ago (e.g. He has just stepped out).
• ALREADY: Sooner than expected (e.g. I have already submitted the report).
• YET: Expected event not happened until now (e.g. The results haven't been published yet).
• EVER: At any time in your life (questions: Have you ever...?).
• NEVER: Not at any time in your life (e.g. I have never broken a promise).
• SO FAR / UP TO NOW: Extent up to the present moment (e.g. We have reviewed 40 topics so far).
• LATELY / RECENTLY: In the recent past (e.g. Have you spoken to him recently?).

4. BENGALI PEDAGOGICAL INSIGHTS (বাংলায় বিশেষ ব্যাখ্যা)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
• বাংলায় 'পুরাঘটিত বর্তমান' (Present Perfect) এমন কাজ নির্দেশ করে যা অতীতকালে শেষ হয়েছে কিন্তু তার ফলাফল এখনো বিদ্যমান (যেমন: "আমি চাবি হারিয়ে ফেলেছি" = I have lost my key—এর মানে বর্তমানে আমার কাছে চাবি নেই)।
• কিন্তু যদি কোনো নির্দিষ্ট অতীতের সময় (যেমন: yesterday, two days ago, in 2020) উল্লেখ থাকে, তবে কোনোভাবেই Present Perfect হবে না; সেখানে Simple Past (V2) ব্যবহার করতেই হবে।
• 'Already' ইতিবাচক বাক্যে, 'Yet' প্রশ্ন ও নেতিবাচক বাক্যের শেষে বসে।
