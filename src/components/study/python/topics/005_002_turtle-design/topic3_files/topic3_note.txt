1. WHAT IS FILL MECHANICS IN TURTLE GRAPHICS?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
By default, drawing commands only produce hollow line strokes (wireframes).
To create solid colored geometric figures, Turtle uses a 3-step fill protocol:

1. `t.fillcolor(color)`:
   - Sets the active interior color (using color names, hex, or RGB tuples).

2. `t.begin_fill()`:
   - Records the start of a closed polygon path.
   - Turtle tracks all subsequent vertices.

3. `t.end_fill()`:
   - Automatically connects the current position back to the `begin_fill()` origin.
   - Fills the enclosed internal area with the active `fillcolor`.

2. THE CLOSURE RULE & UNCLOSED SHAPES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
If you call `end_fill()` on a non-closed path (such as a 3-sided open triangle),
Turtle automatically draws an invisible straight line connecting the end point
back to the start point and fills that triangular area.
Best practice: Always explicitly close your geometric paths before calling `end_fill()`.

3. FILLING SELF-INTERSECTING POLYGONS (WINDING RULE)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
When filling self-crossing stars (such as a 5-pointed star drawn with 144° turns),
Tkinter applies the Even-Odd parity rule:
- Outer star points are filled with color.
- The central pentagon may be left hollow or filled depending on coordinate parity.

4. COMMON PITFALLS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Forgetting `end_fill()`: The shape remains hollow and no color appears.
- Calling `begin_fill()` multiple times without matching `end_fill()`: Causes
  merged distorted fills across multiple disconnected shapes.
- Changing `fillcolor()` AFTER `end_fill()`: Only shapes drawn afterwards will
  use the new fill color.
