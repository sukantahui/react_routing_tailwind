TOPIC 12: WORKED EXAMPLE 6 – RAW-MATERIAL CONSTRAINT PROBLEM
================================================================================

1. PROBLEM STATEMENT
--------------------
A manufacturing company produces two products: Product P and Product Q.
The company uses three raw materials: Material X, Material Y, and Material Z.

Raw material requirements per unit:
              Product P   Product Q   Available
Material X       3 kg        2 kg       180 kg
Material Y       2 kg        4 kg       160 kg
Material Z       1 kg        3 kg       120 kg

Profit per unit: Product P = ₹120, Product Q = ₹100

The company wants to maximize total profit.

2. STEP-BY-STEP FORMULATION
---------------------------

STEP 1: Read and Understand
- Goal: Maximize profit
- Products: P and Q (2 products)
- Resources: 3 raw materials (X, Y, Z)
- No demand constraints

STEP 2: Identify Decision Variables
- x₁ = number of units of Product P produced
- x₂ = number of units of Product Q produced

STEP 3: Identify the Objective
- Profit per unit: P = ₹120, Q = ₹100
- Maximize Z = 120x₁ + 100x₂

STEP 4: Identify Constraints
- Material X: 3x₁ + 2x₂ ≤ 180
- Material Y: 2x₁ + 4x₂ ≤ 160
- Material Z: 1x₁ + 3x₂ ≤ 120

STEP 5: Add Non-negativity
- x₁ ≥ 0, x₂ ≥ 0

STEP 6: Review and Validate
- Test x₁ = 0, x₂ = 40:
  Material X = 80 ≤ 180 ✓
  Material Y = 160 ≤ 160 ✓
  Material Z = 120 ≤ 120 ✓

- Test x₁ = 40, x₂ = 20:
  Material X = 120 + 40 = 160 ≤ 180 ✓
  Material Y = 80 + 80 = 160 ≤ 160 ✓
  Material Z = 40 + 60 = 100 ≤ 120 ✓

STEP 7: Document
- Final Model:
  Maximize Z = 120x₁ + 100x₂
  Subject to:
    Material X: 3x₁ + 2x₂ ≤ 180
    Material Y: 2x₁ + 4x₂ ≤ 160
    Material Z: x₁ + 3x₂ ≤ 120
    x₁ ≥ 0, x₂ ≥ 0

3. GRAPHICAL SOLUTION
---------------------
Step 1: Plot constraints on x₁-x₂ plane
- Material X: 3x₁ + 2x₂ = 180 → x₂ = 90 - 1.5x₁
- Material Y: 2x₁ + 4x₂ = 160 → x₂ = 40 - 0.5x₁
- Material Z: x₁ + 3x₂ = 120 → x₂ = 40 - (1/3)x₁

Step 2: Identify feasible region
Step 3: Find corner points:
  - (0, 0)
  - (0, 40)
  - (30, 30)  [Intersection of X and Y]
  - (40, 20)  [Intersection of Y and Z]
  - (60, 0)   [Intersection of X and x₂ axis]

Step 4: Evaluate objective at each corner:
  - (0, 0): Z = 0
  - (0, 40): Z = 120(0) + 100(40) = 4,000
  - (30, 30): Z = 120(30) + 100(30) = 3,600 + 3,000 = 6,600
  - (40, 20): Z = 120(40) + 100(20) = 4,800 + 2,000 = 6,800
  - (60, 0): Z = 120(60) + 100(0) = 7,200

Wait! Let me verify the corner points more carefully.

Actually, let me recalculate properly:

Constraints:
1. Material X: 3x₁ + 2x₂ = 180
2. Material Y: 2x₁ + 4x₂ = 160
3. Material Z: x₁ + 3x₂ = 120

Corner points:
- (0,0): origin
- Intersection of X and Y:
  3x₁ + 2x₂ = 180
  2x₁ + 4x₂ = 160 → divide by 2: x₁ + 2x₂ = 80
  Solve: From X: 3x₁ + 2x₂ = 180
  From Y: 2x₁ + 4x₂ = 160
  Multiply Y by 1.5: 3x₁ + 6x₂ = 240
  Subtract: 4x₂ = 60 → x₂ = 15
  Then: 3x₁ + 2(15) = 180 → 3x₁ + 30 = 180 → 3x₁ = 150 → x₁ = 50
  So (50, 15) - but wait, check Z constraint: x₁ + 3x₂ = 50 + 45 = 95 ≤ 120 ✓
  So (50, 15) is a corner.

- Intersection of Y and Z:
  2x₁ + 4x₂ = 160
  x₁ + 3x₂ = 120
  From Z: x₁ = 120 - 3x₂
  Substitute: 2(120 - 3x₂) + 4x₂ = 160
  240 - 6x₂ + 4x₂ = 160
  -2x₂ = -80 → x₂ = 40
  Then: x₁ = 120 - 3(40) = 120 - 120 = 0
  So (0, 40) - but this is the same as the Y-intercept.

- Intersection of X and Z:
  3x₁ + 2x₂ = 180
  x₁ + 3x₂ = 120
  From Z: x₁ = 120 - 3x₂
  Substitute: 3(120 - 3x₂) + 2x₂ = 180
  360 - 9x₂ + 2x₂ = 180
  -7x₂ = -180 → x₂ = 25.71
  Then: x₁ = 120 - 3(25.71) = 120 - 77.14 = 42.86
  So (42.86, 25.71) - check Y constraint: 2(42.86) + 4(25.71) = 85.71 + 102.86 = 188.57 ≤ 160 ✗
  So (42.86, 25.71) is infeasible!

- Intersection of X and x₂=0: 3x₁ = 180 → x₁ = 60 → (60,0)
- Intersection of Y and x₁=0: 4x₂ = 160 → x₂ = 40 → (0,40)

Now let me find the feasible region corners:
- (0,0)
- (0,40) [from Y with x₁=0]
- (50,15) [intersection of X and Y]
- (60,0) [from X with x₂=0]

Check all corners against Z constraint:
- (0,40): x₁ + 3x₂ = 0 + 120 = 120 ≤ 120 ✓
- (50,15): x₁ + 3x₂ = 50 + 45 = 95 ≤ 120 ✓
- (60,0): x₁ + 3x₂ = 60 + 0 = 60 ≤ 120 ✓

Now evaluate objective:
- (0,0): Z = 0
- (0,40): Z = 120(0) + 100(40) = 4,000
- (50,15): Z = 120(50) + 100(15) = 6,000 + 1,500 = 7,500
- (60,0): Z = 120(60) + 100(0) = 7,200

Optimal solution: (50, 15) with profit = ₹7,500

4. INTERPRETATION
-----------------
- Produce 50 units of Product P and 15 units of Product Q.
- Total profit = ₹7,500.
- Resource usage at optimum:
  - Material X: 3(50) + 2(15) = 150 + 30 = 180 kg (fully used)
  - Material Y: 2(50) + 4(15) = 100 + 60 = 160 kg (fully used)
  - Material Z: 1(50) + 3(15) = 50 + 45 = 95 kg (slack: 25 kg)

5. KEY INSIGHTS
---------------
- Materials X and Y are binding (fully used).
- Material Z has slack (25 kg unused).
- Product P is more profitable per kg of Material X (₹40/kg) vs Product Q (₹50/kg).
- Product P is more profitable per kg of Material Y (₹60/kg) vs Product Q (₹25/kg).
- The optimal solution balances the use of all three materials.

6. RAW-MATERIAL CONSTRAINTS IN PRODUCTION
------------------------------------------
- Raw materials are the physical inputs needed for production.
- They are often the most critical constraints in manufacturing.
- Different products require different material compositions.
- Material constraints often have slack or are binding depending on the product mix.

7. WHAT WE LEARN
----------------
- How to handle multiple raw-material constraints.
- How materials with different availability affect the product mix.
- The importance of checking material usage at the optimum.
- How to identify which materials are binding.

8. KEY TAKEAWAYS
-----------------
- Raw-material constraints are common in manufacturing.
- Multiple materials may be binding or have slack.
- The optimal product mix balances material usage across all materials.
- Material constraints often interact with each other.
