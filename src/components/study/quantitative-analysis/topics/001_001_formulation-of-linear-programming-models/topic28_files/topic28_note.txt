TOPIC 28: WORKED EXAMPLE 22 – MINIMIZATION PROBLEM FROM A SOCIAL CONTEXT
================================================================================

1. PROBLEM STATEMENT
--------------------
A government agency runs two meal programs to provide food to communities:
- Standard Meal: Provides 600 calories per person, costs ₹50 per person
- Premium Meal: Provides 1000 calories per person, costs ₹80 per person

The agency must meet the following requirements:
- Serve at least 500 people total
- Provide at least 400,000 total calories
- At least 200 people must receive Premium Meals (special dietary needs)
- At most 400 people can receive Standard Meals (due to kitchen capacity)

The agency wants to minimize total cost.

2. STEP-BY-STEP FORMULATION
---------------------------

STEP 1: Read and Understand
- Goal: Minimize total cost
- Meal types: Standard, Premium (2 types)
- Requirements: Total people ≥ 500, Calories ≥ 400,000, Premium ≥ 200, Standard ≤ 400
- This is a minimization problem with ≥ and ≤ constraints

STEP 2: Identify Decision Variables
- x₁ = number of people receiving Standard Meals
- x₂ = number of people receiving Premium Meals

STEP 3: Identify the Objective
- Cost: Standard = ₹50/person, Premium = ₹80/person
- Minimize Z = 50x₁ + 80x₂

STEP 4: Identify Constraints
- Total people: x₁ + x₂ ≥ 500
- Calories: 600x₁ + 1000x₂ ≥ 400,000
- Premium minimum: x₂ ≥ 200
- Standard maximum: x₁ ≤ 400
- Non-negativity: x₁ ≥ 0, x₂ ≥ 0

STEP 5: Add Non-negativity
- x₁ ≥ 0, x₂ ≥ 0 (already covered)

STEP 6: Review and Validate
- Test a feasible solution:
  x₁ = 300, x₂ = 200
  Total people = 500 ≥ 500 ✓
  Calories = 600(300)+1000(200) = 180,000+200,000 = 380,000 ≥ 400,000 ✗

- Test another: x₁ = 300, x₂ = 220
  Total people = 520 ≥ 500 ✓
  Calories = 180,000+220,000 = 400,000 ≥ 400,000 ✓
  Premium = 220 ≥ 200 ✓
  Standard = 300 ≤ 400 ✓
  Cost = 50(300)+80(220) = 15,000+17,600 = ₹32,600

STEP 7: Document
- Final Model:
  Minimize Z = 50x₁ + 80x₂
  Subject to:
    Total People: x₁ + x₂ ≥ 500
    Calories: 600x₁ + 1000x₂ ≥ 400,000
    Premium: x₂ ≥ 200
    Standard Max: x₁ ≤ 400
    x₁ ≥ 0, x₂ ≥ 0

3. GRAPHICAL SOLUTION
---------------------
Step 1: Plot constraints on x₁-x₂ plane
- Total People: x₁ + x₂ = 500 → x₂ = 500 - x₁
- Calories: 600x₁ + 1000x₂ = 400,000 → divide by 200: 3x₁ + 5x₂ = 2,000 → x₂ = 400 - 0.6x₁
- Premium: x₂ = 200
- Standard Max: x₁ = 400

Step 2: Identify feasible region (intersection of all constraints)
Step 3: Find corner points of feasible region:

The feasible region is bounded by:
- x₂ ≥ 200 (Premium minimum)
- x₁ ≤ 400 (Standard maximum)
- x₁ + x₂ ≥ 500 (Total people minimum)
- 600x₁ + 1000x₂ ≥ 400,000 (Calories minimum)

Let me find the intersection points:

1. At x₂ = 200 (Premium minimum):
   - Total people: x₁ + 200 ≥ 500 → x₁ ≥ 300
   - Calories: 600x₁ + 1000(200) ≥ 400,000 → 600x₁ + 200,000 ≥ 400,000 → 600x₁ ≥ 200,000 → x₁ ≥ 333.33
   - Standard Max: x₁ ≤ 400
   - So with x₂ = 200, x₁ ≥ 333.33 (Calories is binding)
   - Point: (333.33, 200)

2. At x₁ = 400 (Standard maximum):
   - Total people: 400 + x₂ ≥ 500 → x₂ ≥ 100
   - Calories: 600(400) + 1000x₂ ≥ 400,000 → 240,000 + 1000x₂ ≥ 400,000 → 1000x₂ ≥ 160,000 → x₂ ≥ 160
   - Premium: x₂ ≥ 200
   - So with x₁ = 400, x₂ ≥ 200 (Premium is binding)
   - Point: (400, 200)

3. Intersection of Total People and Calories:
   x₁ + x₂ = 500
   600x₁ + 1000x₂ = 400,000
   From Total: x₂ = 500 - x₁
   Substitute: 600x₁ + 1000(500 - x₁) = 400,000
   600x₁ + 500,000 - 1000x₁ = 400,000
   -400x₁ = -100,000 → x₁ = 250
   Then x₂ = 500 - 250 = 250
   (250, 250) → check Premium: 250 ≥ 200 ✓, Standard Max: 250 ≤ 400 ✓
   Point: (250, 250)

4. Intersection of Total People and Premium:
   x₁ + x₂ = 500, x₂ = 200 → x₁ = 300
   Check Calories: 600(300)+1000(200) = 180,000+200,000 = 380,000 ≥ 400,000 ✗
   So (300, 200) is infeasible.

5. Intersection of Calories and Premium:
   600x₁ + 1000(200) = 400,000 → 600x₁ = 200,000 → x₁ = 333.33
   Check Total: 333.33 + 200 = 533.33 ≥ 500 ✓
   Check Standard Max: 333.33 ≤ 400 ✓
   Point: (333.33, 200)

6. Intersection of Calories and Standard Max:
   600(400) + 1000x₂ = 400,000 → 240,000 + 1000x₂ = 400,000 → 1000x₂ = 160,000 → x₂ = 160
   Check Total: 400 + 160 = 560 ≥ 500 ✓
   Check Premium: 160 ≥ 200 ✗
   So (400, 160) is infeasible.

7. Intersection of Total People and Standard Max:
   x₁ = 400, x₂ = 100
   Check Calories: 600(400)+1000(100) = 240,000+100,000 = 340,000 ≥ 400,000 ✗
   (400, 100) is infeasible.

8. Intersection of Total People, Calories, and Premium:
   (333.33, 200) is on Calories and Premium.

9. Intersection of Total People, Calories, and Standard Max:
   (400, 160) is on Calories and Standard Max but fails Premium.

So the feasible corner points are:
(250, 250), (333.33, 200), (400, 200)

Check each:
- (250, 250): Total=500 ✓, Calories=600(250)+1000(250)=150,000+250,000=400,000 ✓, Premium=250≥200 ✓, Standard=250≤400 ✓
- (333.33, 200): Total=533.33≥500 ✓, Calories=600(333.33)+200,000=200,000+200,000=400,000 ✓, Premium=200≥200 ✓, Standard=333.33≤400 ✓
- (400, 200): Total=600≥500 ✓, Calories=600(400)+1000(200)=240,000+200,000=440,000≥400,000 ✓, Premium=200≥200 ✓, Standard=400≤400 ✓

Step 4: Evaluate objective at each corner:
- (250, 250): Z = 50(250)+80(250) = 12,500+20,000 = ₹32,500
- (333.33, 200): Z = 50(333.33)+80(200) = 16,666.67+16,000 = ₹32,666.67
- (400, 200): Z = 50(400)+80(200) = 20,000+16,000 = ₹36,000

Step 5: Optimal solution: (250, 250) with cost = ₹32,500

4. INTERPRETATION
-----------------
- Provide 250 Standard Meals and 250 Premium Meals.
- Total cost = ₹32,500.
- Resource usage at optimum:
  - Total people: 250+250 = 500 (minimum met)
  - Calories: 600(250)+1000(250) = 150,000+250,000 = 400,000 (minimum met)
  - Premium: 250 ≥ 200 (satisfied, not binding)
  - Standard: 250 ≤ 400 (satisfied, not binding)
- Total People and Calories are binding; Premium and Standard have slack.

5. KEY INSIGHTS
---------------
- Social minimization problems aim to meet requirements at minimum cost.
- Both minimum requirements (≥) and maximum limits (≤) are present.
- The optimal solution balances cost with all requirements.
- The cheapest solution may not use the cheapest option exclusively.

6. REAL-WORLD APPLICATIONS
---------------------------
- Food aid and nutrition programs.
- Healthcare service delivery with budget constraints.
- Education resource allocation.
- Social welfare program optimization.

7. WHAT WE LEARN
----------------
- How to formulate social context minimization problems.
- How to handle multiple minimum and maximum constraints.
- How to balance cost with social requirements.
- The importance of meeting all requirements.

8. KEY TAKEAWAYS
-----------------
- Social minimization problems minimize cost while meeting requirements.
- Multiple constraints must be satisfied.
- The optimal solution may use a mix of options.
- Understanding efficiency helps identify the best allocation.
