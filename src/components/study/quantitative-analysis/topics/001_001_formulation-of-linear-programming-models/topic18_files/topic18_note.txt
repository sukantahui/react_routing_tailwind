================================================================================
TOPIC 18: WORKED EXAMPLE 12 – WORKFORCE ALLOCATION PROBLEM
================================================================================

1. PROBLEM STATEMENT
--------------------
A company needs to assign workers to three shifts (Morning, Evening, Night)
to meet minimum staffing requirements.

The company has two types of workers:
- Full-time workers: can work any shift, cost ₹1,200 per shift
- Part-time workers: can only work Morning and Evening shifts, cost ₹800 per shift

Minimum staff requirements per shift:
- Morning: 8 workers
- Evening: 6 workers
- Night: 4 workers

The company has available:
- Full-time workers: 10
- Part-time workers: 8

The company wants to minimize total labor cost.

2. STEP-BY-STEP FORMULATION
---------------------------

STEP 1: Read and Understand
- Goal: Minimize total labor cost
- Worker types: Full-time, Part-time
- Shifts: Morning, Evening, Night
- Requirements: Minimum staff per shift
- Availability: Max workers of each type

STEP 2: Identify Decision Variables
- x₁ = number of full-time workers assigned to Morning
- x₂ = number of full-time workers assigned to Evening
- x₃ = number of full-time workers assigned to Night
- x₄ = number of part-time workers assigned to Morning
- x₅ = number of part-time workers assigned to Evening

(Note: Part-time cannot work Night)

STEP 3: Identify the Objective
- Cost per shift: Full-time = ₹1,200, Part-time = ₹800
- Minimize Z = 1200(x₁ + x₂ + x₃) + 800(x₄ + x₅)

STEP 4: Identify Constraints
- Morning staffing: x₁ + x₄ ≥ 8
- Evening staffing: x₂ + x₅ ≥ 6
- Night staffing: x₃ ≥ 4
- Full-time availability: x₁ + x₂ + x₃ ≤ 10
- Part-time availability: x₄ + x₅ ≤ 8
- Non-negativity: all variables ≥ 0 (integer not required)

STEP 5: Add Non-negativity
- All xᵢ ≥ 0

STEP 6: Review and Validate
- Test a feasible solution:
  Full-time: Morning=4, Evening=2, Night=4 (total 10)
  Part-time: Morning=4, Evening=4 (total 8)
  Check: Morning=8 ✓, Evening=6 ✓, Night=4 ✓
  Cost = 1200(10) + 800(8) = 12,000 + 6,400 = 18,400

STEP 7: Document
- Final Model:
  Minimize Z = 1200(x₁ + x₂ + x₃) + 800(x₄ + x₅)
  Subject to:
    Morning: x₁ + x₄ ≥ 8
    Evening: x₂ + x₅ ≥ 6
    Night: x₃ ≥ 4
    Full-time: x₁ + x₂ + x₃ ≤ 10
    Part-time: x₄ + x₅ ≤ 8
    x₁, x₂, x₃, x₄, x₅ ≥ 0

3. SOLUTION APPROACH
---------------------
This is a 5-variable LP. We can solve using simplex or by reasoning.

Since full-time workers are more expensive (₹1,200 vs ₹800), we want to use
as many part-time workers as possible, but they can only work Morning and Evening.

Part-time availability is 8, and part-time can cover Morning and Evening.

Morning + Evening demand = 8 + 6 = 14.
If we use all 8 part-time workers, they can cover at most 8 of those 14 shifts,
leaving 6 shifts to be covered by full-time.

Night shift (4) must be covered by full-time workers (since part-time can't work Night).

So full-time needed = 6 (for Morning/Evening) + 4 (for Night) = 10 full-time workers.

This uses all 10 full-time workers and all 8 part-time workers.

Cost = 10 × 1,200 + 8 × 800 = 12,000 + 6,400 = 18,400.

Optimal allocation:
- Full-time: Morning=0, Evening=6, Night=4 (total 10)
- Part-time: Morning=8, Evening=0 (total 8)

Check: Morning = 0+8=8 ✓, Evening = 6+0=6 ✓, Night=4 ✓.
Cost = 1200(0+6+4) + 800(8+0) = 1200(10) + 800(8) = 18,400.

But we can also have multiple optimal solutions. For example:
- Full-time: Morning=2, Evening=4, Night=4 (total 10)
- Part-time: Morning=6, Evening=2 (total 8)
Morning=8, Evening=6, Night=4 ✓, cost same.

So there are multiple optimal solutions.

4. INTERPRETATION
-----------------
- The company should use all available workers (10 full-time, 8 part-time).
- Total cost = ₹18,400.
- Part-time workers should cover as much of the Morning/Evening demand as possible.
- Full-time workers cover the Night shift and the remaining Morning/Evening shifts.
- Multiple optimal allocations exist.

5. KEY INSIGHTS
---------------
- Workforce allocation problems involve assigning workers to shifts or tasks.
- Different worker types have different costs and availability.
- Minimum requirements must be met.
- The optimal solution balances cost and availability.

6. REAL-WORLD APPLICATIONS
---------------------------
- Hospital staff scheduling.
- Customer service center shift planning.
- Manufacturing shift assignment.
- Retail store staffing.

7. WHAT WE LEARN
----------------
- How to formulate workforce allocation problems.
- How to handle different worker types with different costs and constraints.
- How to meet minimum staffing requirements.
- How to identify multiple optimal solutions.

8. KEY TAKEAWAYS
-----------------
- Workforce allocation is a classic LP application.
- Different worker types have different costs and availability.
- Minimum requirements must be met.
- The optimal solution often uses all available workers.
