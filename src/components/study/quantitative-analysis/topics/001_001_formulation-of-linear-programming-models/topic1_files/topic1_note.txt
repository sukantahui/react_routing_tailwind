MEANING AND PURPOSE OF LINEAR PROGRAMMING
=========================================

WHAT DOES "LINEAR PROGRAMMING" MEAN?
-------------------------------------
- Linear: all relationships are straight‑line (no x², xy, etc.)
  → Proportionality: double input → double output.
  → Additivity: total = sum of parts (no cross‑effects).
- Programming: planning or scheduling, NOT computer programming.
  → Originated from military logistics planning in WWII.

→ Together: a method for optimal planning when all relations are linear.

THE CORE PURPOSE OF LP
----------------------
To make the best possible decision under scarcity.
- We have limited resources (money, time, materials, labour).
- We have competing activities (products, projects, investments).
- We want to achieve a single objective (max profit, min cost, etc.).
- LP gives the optimal allocation of resources to meet that objective.

WHY IS LP SO IMPORTANT?
-----------------------
1. It provides a systematic, quantitative approach to decision‑making.
2. It handles complex trade‑offs and reveals which resources are most valuable.
3. It has been proven effective for over 75 years across industries.
4. It can be implemented with readily available software.
5. It often leads to significant cost savings and profit improvements.

KEY ASSUMPTIONS OF LP
---------------------
- Linearity: objective and constraints are linear.
- Divisibility: variables can take fractional values.
- Certainty: all coefficients are known constants.
- Proportionality / Additivity: no interaction between variables.

Without these assumptions, LP may not apply; other OR techniques (nonlinear, integer, stochastic) are used.

REAL‑WORLD EXAMPLES
-------------------
- Manufacturing: production planning, product‑mix optimisation.
- Logistics: transportation, route planning, warehouse location.
- Finance: portfolio selection, risk management, budget allocation.
- Agriculture: crop planning, land use, irrigation scheduling.
- Healthcare: staff scheduling, resource allocation, patient flow.
- Education: time allocation, assignment of teachers to classes.

COMMON PITFALLS TO AVOID
------------------------
- Confusing "programming" with computer programming.
- Assuming LP can solve any optimisation problem (it's only for linear ones).
- Ignoring the assumptions of certainty and divisibility.
- Using LP when variables must be integers (use Integer Programming).
- Forgetting that the optimal solution is only as good as the model.

BEST PRACTICES FOR UNDERSTANDING LP
-----------------------------------
✓ Always define the objective first.
✓ List all constraints before solving.
✓ Use clear variable names and document them.
✓ Check for linearity – ensure no x² or xy terms.
✓ Include non‑negativity constraints.
✓ Test the model with simple numbers.
✓ Use sensitivity analysis to understand trade‑offs.
