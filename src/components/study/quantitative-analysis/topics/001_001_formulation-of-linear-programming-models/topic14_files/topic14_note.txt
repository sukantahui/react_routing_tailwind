TOPIC 14: WORKED EXAMPLE 8 – DIET AND NUTRITION PROBLEM
================================================================================

1. PROBLEM STATEMENT
--------------------
A nutritionist is planning a diet using two foods: Food A and Food B.
The diet must meet minimum daily requirements for three nutrients:
- Carbohydrates: at least 16 units
- Protein: at least 12 units
- Fat: at least 10 units

Nutrient content per unit of food:
              Food A   Food B   Minimum Required
Carbohydrates    2        4          16
Protein          3        1          12
Fat              1        3          10

Cost per unit: Food A = ₹20, Food B = ₹30

The nutritionist wants to minimize the total daily cost of the diet.

2. STEP-BY-STEP FORMULATION
---------------------------

STEP 1: Read and Understand
- Goal: Minimize total cost
- Foods: A and B (2 foods)
- Nutrients: Carbohydrates, Protein, Fat (3 nutrients with minimum requirements)
- This is a cost minimization problem with ≥ constraints

STEP 2: Identify Decision Variables
- x₁ = number of units of Food A in the diet
- x₂ = number of units of Food B in the diet

STEP 3: Identify the Objective
- Cost per unit: A = ₹20, B = ₹30
- Minimize Z = 20x₁ + 30x₂

STEP 4: Identify Constraints
- Carbohydrates: 2x₁ + 4x₂ ≥ 16
- Protein: 3x₁ + 1x₂ ≥ 12
- Fat: 1x₁ + 3x₂ ≥ 10

STEP 5: Add Non-negativity
- x₁ ≥ 0, x₂ ≥ 0

STEP 6: Review and Validate
- Test x₁ = 0, x₂ = 5:
  Carbohydrates = 20 ≥ 16 ✓
  Protein = 5 ≥ 12 ✗

- Test x₁ = 4, x₂ = 2:
  Carbohydrates = 8 + 8 = 16 ≥ 16 ✓
  Protein = 12 + 2 = 14 ≥ 12 ✓
  Fat = 4 + 6 = 10 ≥ 10 ✓
  Cost = 20(4) + 30(2) = 80 + 60 = ₹140

STEP 7: Document
- Final Model:
  Minimize Z = 20x₁ + 30x₂
  Subject to:
    Carbohydrates: 2x₁ + 4x₂ ≥ 16
    Protein: 3x₁ + 1x₂ ≥ 12
    Fat: x₁ + 3x₂ ≥ 10
    x₁ ≥ 0, x₂ ≥ 0

3. GRAPHICAL SOLUTION
---------------------
Step 1: Plot constraints on x₁-x₂ plane
- Carbohydrates: 2x₁ + 4x₂ = 16 → x₂ = 4 - 0.5x₁
- Protein: 3x₁ + 1x₂ = 12 → x₂ = 12 - 3x₁
- Fat: x₁ + 3x₂ = 10 → x₂ = (10 - x₁)/3

Step 2: Identify feasible region (above all three constraints)
Step 3: Find corner points:
  - Intersection of Protein and Fat:
    3x₁ + x₂ = 12
    x₁ + 3x₂ = 10
    Solve: From Protein: x₂ = 12 - 3x₁
    Substitute: x₁ + 3(12 - 3x₁) = 10
    x₁ + 36 - 9x₁ = 10
    -8x₁ = -26 → x₁ = 3.25
    Then: x₂ = 12 - 3(3.25) = 12 - 9.75 = 2.25
    So (3.25, 2.25)

  - Intersection of Carbohydrates and Protein:
    2x₁ + 4x₂ = 16 → divide by 2: x₁ + 2x₂ = 8
    3x₁ + x₂ = 12
    Solve: From first: x₁ = 8 - 2x₂
    Substitute: 3(8 - 2x₂) + x₂ = 12
    24 - 6x₂ + x₂ = 12
    -5x₂ = -12 → x₂ = 2.4
    Then: x₁ = 8 - 2(2.4) = 8 - 4.8 = 3.2
    So (3.2, 2.4)

  - Intersection of Carbohydrates and Fat:
    2x₁ + 4x₂ = 16
    x₁ + 3x₂ = 10
    From second: x₁ = 10 - 3x₂
    Substitute: 2(10 - 3x₂) + 4x₂ = 16
    20 - 6x₂ + 4x₂ = 16
    -2x₂ = -4 → x₂ = 2
    Then: x₁ = 10 - 3(2) = 10 - 6 = 4
    So (4, 2)

  Also check intersections with axes:
  - Carbohydrates with x₁-axis: 2x₁ = 16 → x₁ = 8 → (8,0)
  - Protein with x₂-axis: x₂ = 12 → (0,12)
  - Fat with x₂-axis: 3x₂ = 10 → x₂ = 3.33 → (0, 3.33)

  Corner points: (4, 2), (3.2, 2.4), (3.25, 2.25)

Step 4: Evaluate objective at each corner:
  - (4, 2): Z = 20(4) + 30(2) = 80 + 60 = 140
  - (3.2, 2.4): Z = 20(3.2) + 30(2.4) = 64 + 72 = 136
  - (3.25, 2.25): Z = 20(3.25) + 30(2.25) = 65 + 67.5 = 132.5

Step 5: Optimal solution: (3.25, 2.25) with cost = ₹132.5

4. INTERPRETATION
-----------------
- Include 3.25 units of Food A and 2.25 units of Food B in the diet.
- Total cost = ₹132.5.
- Nutrient usage at optimum:
  - Carbohydrates: 2(3.25) + 4(2.25) = 6.5 + 9 = 15.5 ≥ 16 ✗ Wait, 15.5 < 16!

Let me reconsider: At (3.25, 2.25):
- Carbohydrates: 2(3.25) + 4(2.25) = 6.5 + 9 = 15.5 (this is < 16, so it's infeasible!)

I need to check this point against Carbohydrates constraint.

Actually, (3.25, 2.25) is the intersection of Protein and Fat. I need to verify it satisfies Carbohydrates:
2(3.25) + 4(2.25) = 6.5 + 9 = 15.5 < 16 → INFEASIBLE!

So I need to find the correct optimal corner. Let me re-evaluate.

The feasible region is above all three constraints. The corner points are where constraints intersect:

1. Intersection of Carbohydrates and Protein: (3.2, 2.4)
   Check Fat: 3.2 + 3(2.4) = 3.2 + 7.2 = 10.4 ≥ 10 ✓
   Cost: 20(3.2) + 30(2.4) = 64 + 72 = 136

2. Intersection of Carbohydrates and Fat: (4, 2)
   Check Protein: 3(4) + 2 = 14 ≥ 12 ✓
   Cost: 20(4) + 30(2) = 80 + 60 = 140

3. Intersection of Protein and Fat: (3.25, 2.25)
   Check Carbohydrates: 2(3.25) + 4(2.25) = 6.5 + 9 = 15.5 < 16 ✗
   This point is NOT feasible because it violates Carbohydrates.

4. Carbohydrates with x₂-axis: x₂ = 4 → (0, 4)
   Check Protein: 0 + 4 = 4 ≥ 12 ✗ (infeasible)

5. Protein with x₁-axis: x₁ = 4 → (4, 0)
   Check Carbohydrates: 8 ≥ 16 ✗ (infeasible)
   Check Fat: 4 ≥ 10 ✗ (infeasible)

So the feasible corner points are:
- (3.2, 2.4) with cost = 136
- (4, 2) with cost = 140

The optimal solution is (3.2, 2.4) with cost = 136.

Wait, let me re-verify (3.2, 2.4):
- Carbohydrates: 2(3.2) + 4(2.4) = 6.4 + 9.6 = 16 ≥ 16 ✓
- Protein: 3(3.2) + 2.4 = 9.6 + 2.4 = 12 ≥ 12 ✓
- Fat: 3.2 + 3(2.4) = 3.2 + 7.2 = 10.4 ≥ 10 ✓

Yes, this is feasible. So the optimal solution is (3.2, 2.4) with cost = 136.

5. REVISED CORNER POINT ANALYSIS
--------------------------------
Corner points of feasible region:
- (3.2, 2.4): Z = 136
- (4, 2): Z = 140
- Also check (0, 4) with Carbohydrates: 16 ≥ 16 ✓, Protein: 4 ≥ 12 ✗ (infeasible)
- (4, 0): Protein: 12 ≥ 12 ✓, Carbohydrates: 8 ≥ 16 ✗ (infeasible)
- (0, 12): Carbohydrates: 48 ≥ 16 ✓, Fat: 36 ≥ 10 ✓ → (0, 12) cost = 360
- (8, 0): Protein: 24 ≥ 12 ✓, Fat: 8 ≥ 10 ✗ (infeasible)

So the feasible corner points are (3.2, 2.4), (4, 2), and (0, 12).

Costs:
- (3.2, 2.4): 136

Optimal solution: (3.2, 2.4) with cost = ₹136

6. INTERPRETATION
-----------------
- Include 3.2 units of Food A and 2.4 units of Food B in the diet.
- Total cost = ₹136.
- Nutrient usage at optimum:
  - Carbohydrates: 2(3.2) + 4(2.4) = 6.4 + 9.6 = 16 (fully used)
  - Protein: 3(3.2) + 2.4 = 9.6 + 2.4 = 12 (fully used)
  - Fat: 3.2 + 3(2.4) = 3.2 + 7.2 = 10.4 (slack: 0.4)

7. DIET PROBLEM INSIGHTS
-------------------------
- The diet problem is a classic LP application.
- It ensures nutritional requirements are met at minimum cost.
- Multiple nutrients must be considered simultaneously.
- Some nutrients may have slack at the optimum.

8. KEY TAKEAWAYS
-----------------
- Diet problems use ≥ constraints for nutritional requirements.
- The objective is to minimize cost.
- Multiple nutrients constrain the solution.
- Some nutrients may be exceeded (slack) at the optimum.
- This is a practical application in healthcare and nutrition.
