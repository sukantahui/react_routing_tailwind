1. THE MATHEMATICAL UPDATE EQUATIONS
================================================================================

Definition:
Immediately after committing an allocation quantity x_kl to cell (k, l), the 
transportation model updates its state vectors:

   S_k(new) = S_k(old) - x_kl   (Origin Supply Deduction)
   D_l(new) = D_l(old) - x_kl   (Destination Demand Deduction)

Fundamental Flow Conservation Invariant:
At every single intermediate iteration t of the Matrix Minima Method:
   ∑_{i ∈ ActiveRows} S_i(t) = ∑_{j ∈ ActiveCols} D_j(t)

Because x_kl is subtracted equally from both total supply and total demand, the 
residual system remains perfectly balanced at every micro-step.


2. LINE ELIMINATION CRITERIA & RULES
================================================================================

Rule 1: Origin Supply Exhaustion (S_k(new) == 0)
---------------------------------------------------------
- Physical Meaning: Origin k has shipped all available warehouse stock.
- Tableau Action: Cross out Row k with a neat horizontal line.
- Operational Effect: Origin k is permanently excluded from all future cost searches.

Rule 2: Destination Demand Satisfaction (D_l(new) == 0)
---------------------------------------------------------
- Physical Meaning: Destination l has received 100% of its required goods.
- Tableau Action: Cross out Column l with a neat vertical line.
- Operational Effect: Destination l is permanently excluded from all future cost searches.

Rule 3: Simultaneous Exhaustion / Degeneracy Protocol (S_k == 0 AND D_l == 0)
---------------------------------------------------------
- Physical Meaning: The allocation exactly satisfies both the origin and destination.
- Strict Rule: Cross out ONLY ONE line (e.g. Row k).
- Degeneracy Resolution: Mark the remaining demand in Column l as 0. Assign an 
  infinitesimal basic zero (ε) to an unassigned cell in Column l before crossing 
  it out. This ensures exactly m + n - 1 basic variables are created.

Case Study 1: Fasteners Plant Logistics (Debangshu)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Origins: Barrackpore (S1 = 50 tons), Ichapur (S2 = 70 tons)
Destinations: Kolkata (D1 = 40), Jadavpur (D2 = 50), Salt Lake (D3 = 30)
Cost Matrix (₹/ton):
                 D1 (Kolkata)   D2 (Jadavpur)   D3 (Salt Lake)   Supply
  S1 (Barrack.)       ₹4             ₹8              ₹6            50
  S2 (Ichapur)        ₹7             ₹3              ₹5            70
  Demand              40             50              30           120

State Evolution:
  - Step 1: Allocate x_22 = 50 tons @ ₹3.
    Updates: S2 = 70 - 50 = 20 tons; D2 = 50 - 50 = 0 (Col D2 Closed). Active Flow = 70.
  - Step 2: Allocate x_11 = 40 tons @ ₹4.
    Updates: S1 = 50 - 40 = 10 tons; D1 = 40 - 40 = 0 (Col D1 Closed). Active Flow = 30.
  - Step 3: Allocate x_23 = 20 tons @ ₹5.
    Updates: S2 = 20 - 20 = 0 (Row S2 Closed); D3 = 30 - 20 = 10 tons. Active Flow = 10.
  - Step 4: Allocate x_13 = 10 tons @ ₹6.
    Updates: S1 = 10 - 10 = 0; D3 = 10 - 10 = 0 (Both Closed). Active Flow = 0.
Total Freight Cost Z = (50 × ₹3) + (40 × ₹4) + (20 × ₹5) + (10 × ₹6) = ₹470.

4. COMMON PITFALLS & BEST PRACTICES
================================================================================

Common Mistakes:
1. One-Sided Subtraction: Subtracting from supply but forgetting demand.
2. Reusing Old Balances: Using initial capacity instead of decremented balance.
3. Premature Termination: Stopping before all lines reach zero balance.

Best Practices:
1. Neatly slash old balances and write new figures adjacent.
2. Maintain active boolean flags (`rowActive`, `colActive`) in code.
3. Validate marginal sums: ∑_j x_ij = S_i and ∑_i x_ij = D_j.
