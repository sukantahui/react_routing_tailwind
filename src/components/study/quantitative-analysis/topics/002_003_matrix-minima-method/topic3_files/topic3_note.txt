1. THE FUNDAMENTAL ALLOCATION RULE
================================================================================

Definition:
Once the candidate cell (k, l) with the lowest unit transportation cost c_kl is 
selected, the allocation procedure commits the maximum permissible shipment 
quantity x_kl to that route:

   x_kl = min(S_k, D_l)

Where:
- S_k is the remaining active supply capacity at Origin k.
- D_l is the remaining unsatisfied demand requirement at Destination l.

Why the "Minimum" Operator is Mandatory:
1. Upper Bound by Supply (x_kl ≤ S_k):
   A factory cannot ship more goods than it currently holds in stock.
2. Upper Bound by Demand (x_kl ≤ D_l):
   A retail depot cannot receive more inventory than its storage capacity.
3. Non-Negativity (x_kl ≥ 0):
   Guarantees all physical flows are strictly non-negative.


2. THREE CAPACITY SCENARIOS UPON ALLOCATION
================================================================================

Scenario 1: S_k < D_l (Supply Exhaustion)
---------------------------------------------------------
- Assigned Quantity: x_kl = S_k
- Updated Origin Balance: S_k(new) = S_k - S_k = 0 (Row k is exhausted & eliminated).
- Updated Destination Balance: D_l(new) = D_l - S_k > 0 (Column l remains active).
- Physical Meaning: Factory k has emptied its entire warehouse into Destination l, 
  but Destination l still requires more goods from other factories in later steps.

Scenario 2: S_k > D_l (Demand Satisfaction)
---------------------------------------------------------
- Assigned Quantity: x_kl = D_l
- Updated Origin Balance: S_k(new) = S_k - D_l > 0 (Row k remains active).
- Updated Destination Balance: D_l(new) = D_l - D_l = 0 (Column l is satisfied & eliminated).
- Physical Meaning: Destination l is 100% fulfilled. Factory k retains surplus 
  stock to supply other destinations in subsequent steps.

Scenario 3: S_k == D_l (Simultaneous Exhaustion / Degeneracy Risk)
---------------------------------------------------------
- Assigned Quantity: x_kl = S_k = D_l
- Both balances reach zero simultaneously.
- Correction Rule: Cross out ONLY Row k (or Column l). Assign an infinitesimal 
  zero allocation (ε) to an independent unallocated cell in the remaining line 
  before crossing it out. This preserves exactly m + n - 1 basic variables.


3. FOUR REAL-WORLD ALLOCATION CASE STUDIES (WITH ₹ CURRENCY)
================================================================================

Case Study 1: Fasteners Plant Logistics (Debangshu)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Origins: Barrackpore (S1 = 50 tons), Ichapur (S2 = 70 tons)
Destinations: Kolkata (D1 = 40), Jadavpur (D2 = 50), Salt Lake (D3 = 30)
Cost Matrix (₹/ton):
                 D1 (Kolkata)   D2 (Jadavpur)   D3 (Salt Lake)   Supply
  S1 (Barrack.)       ₹4             ₹8              ₹6            50
  S2 (Ichapur)        ₹7             ₹3              ₹5            70
  Demand              40             50              30           120

Allocation Steps:
  - Step 1: Cell (S2, D2) selected @ ₹3.
    Allocation: x_22 = min(70, 50) = 50 tons.
    Deduction: S2 = 70 - 50 = 20 tons; D2 = 50 - 50 = 0 (D2 Eliminated). Cost = 50 × ₹3 = ₹150.
  - Step 2: Cell (S1, D1) selected @ ₹4.
    Allocation: x_11 = min(50, 40) = 40 tons.
    Deduction: S1 = 50 - 40 = 10 tons; D1 = 40 - 40 = 0 (D1 Eliminated). Cost = 40 × ₹4 = ₹160.
  - Step 3: Cell (S2, D3) selected @ ₹5.
    Allocation: x_23 = min(20, 30) = 20 tons.
    Deduction: S2 = 20 - 20 = 0 (S2 Eliminated); D3 = 30 - 20 = 10 tons. Cost = 20 × ₹5 = ₹100.
  - Step 4: Final Cell (S1, D3) @ ₹6.
    Allocation: x_13 = min(10, 10) = 10 tons.
    Deduction: S1 = 0; D3 = 0 (Both Eliminated). Cost = 10 × ₹6 = ₹60.
Total Freight Cost Z = ₹150 + ₹160 + ₹100 + ₹60 = ₹470.

4. COMMON PITFALLS & BEST PRACTICES
================================================================================

Common Mistakes:
1. Over-Allocation: Assigning max(S_k, D_l) instead of min(S_k, D_l). This violates 
   feasibility and creates negative inventories.
2. Under-Allocation: Leaving unused slack at both origin and destination in a single 
   step, preventing line elimination and producing an invalid basis.
3. Forgetting Balance Deductions: Using original capacity numbers in later steps.

Best Practices:
1. Visual Circling: Draw a clean circle around x_kl in the center of the cell.
2. Immediate Balance Scratch-Off: Strike out the previous supply/demand and write the 
   new balance in bold beside it immediately.
3. Three-Point Check at Termination:
   - All row allocations sum to S_i.
   - All column allocations sum to D_j.
   - Total basic allocated cells = m + n - 1.
