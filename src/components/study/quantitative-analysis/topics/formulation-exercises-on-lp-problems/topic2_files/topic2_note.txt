TOPIC 2: PROFIT MAXIMIZATION PROBLEMS
=====================================

INTRODUCTION
------------
Profit maximization is the most common application of linear programming 
in business. The goal is to find the optimal product mix that generates 
maximum profit subject to resource constraints.

WHAT IS PROFIT MAXIMIZATION?
---------------------------
Profit maximization determines the quantities of products to produce to 
achieve the highest possible profit, given limited resources.

KEY FORMULA
-----------
Profit = Revenue - Total Costs
Total Profit = Σ(Price per unit - Variable Cost per unit) × Quantity - Fixed Costs

Since fixed costs are constant, maximizing profit is equivalent to 
maximizing total contribution margin:
Maximize Z = Σ(Price - Variable Cost) × Quantity

GENERAL FORM
------------
Maximize Z = p₁x₁ + p₂x₂ + ... + pₙxₙ

Subject to:
a₁₁x₁ + a₁₂x₂ + ... + a₁ₙxₙ ≤ b₁ (Resource 1)
a₂₁x₁ + a₂₂x₂ + ... + a₂ₙxₙ ≤ b₂ (Resource 2)
...
x₁, x₂, ..., xₙ ≥ 0

Where pᵢ = profit per unit of product i

KEY DIFFERENCES FROM COST MINIMIZATION
--------------------------------------
Profit Maximization:
- Objective: Max Z
- pᵢ = profit per unit
- Uses ≤ constraints
- Focus: maximize profit

Cost Minimization:
- Objective: Min Z
- cᵢ = cost per unit
- Uses ≥ constraints
- Focus: minimize cost

STEPS TO SOLVE
--------------
1. Identify products and variables
2. Calculate profit per unit
3. Identify resource constraints
4. Formulate the LP problem
5. Solve graphically
6. Interpret results

EXAMPLE
-------
Problem: A factory produces chairs (x) and tables (y).
Chair: ₹40 profit, 2 labor hours, 3 wood units
Table: ₹50 profit, 3 labor hours, 2 wood units
Available: 120 labor hours, 90 wood units

Formulation:
Maximize Z = 40x + 50y
Subject to:
2x + 3y ≤ 120 (Labor)
3x + 2y ≤ 90 (Wood)
x, y ≥ 0

Solution:
Corner points: (0,0), (30,0), (18,24), (0,40)
Optimal: (0,40) with Z = ₹2,000
Produce 0 chairs and 40 tables.

INTERPRETATION
--------------
- Optimal production mix: 0 chairs, 40 tables
- Maximum profit: ₹2,000
- Labor: fully utilized
- Wood: 10 units slack

KEY TERMS
---------
• Contribution Margin: Price - Variable Cost
• Bottleneck: Resource with zero slack
• Shadow Price: Value of additional resources
• Fixed Costs: Constant regardless of production

COMMON MISTAKES
---------------
1. Confusing revenue with profit
2. Forgetting variable costs
3. Ignoring fixed costs in profit calculation
4. Missing minimum production constraints
5. Not including all resource constraints

BEST PRACTICES
--------------
1. Calculate contribution margin correctly
2. Include all relevant costs
3. Consider minimum production requirements
4. Identify all resource constraints
5. Verify and interpret the solution

TEACHER'S NOTE
--------------
Profit maximization is the most intuitive LP application. Students 
understand the goal of making more profit. The key is helping them 
distinguish between revenue and profit, and understand that profit 
requires considering both income and costs. Encourage students to 
think about real businesses and how they might use LP to make better 
decisions."
