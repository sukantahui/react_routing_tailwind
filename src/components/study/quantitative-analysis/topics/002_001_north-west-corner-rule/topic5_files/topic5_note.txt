================================================================================
                    DEGENERACY IN TRANSPORTATION PROBLEMS
                               Detailed Notes
================================================================================

1. INTRODUCTION
================================================================================

Definition:
Degeneracy is a special situation in transportation problems where the number 
of positive allocations (basic variables) is less than the required number 
(m + n - 1).

Key Insight:
Degeneracy is like having fewer puzzle pieces than needed to complete the 
picture. You have to add a tiny piece (epsilon) to make everything fit and 
continue the solution process.

2. WHAT CAUSES DEGENERACY?
================================================================================

Primary Cause:
---------------------------------------------------------
- Simultaneous exhaustion of both supply and demand
- When an allocation exactly equals both remaining supply and demand
- Sᵢ = 0 and Dⱼ = 0 at the same time

Other Causes:
---------------------------------------------------------
1. NW Corner Method: Most likely to cause degeneracy
2. Equal Costs: When there are ties in the cost matrix
3. Equal Penalties: In VAM when penalties are equal
4. Dummy Variables: Adding dummies can create degeneracy
5. Exact Matches: When supplies and demands match exactly

3. HOW TO IDENTIFY DEGENERACY
================================================================================

Detection Steps:
---------------------------------------------------------
1. Count positive allocations (xᵢⱼ > 0)
2. Calculate required count: m + n - 1
3. Compare: If count < required → degeneracy
4. Verify: Double-check the count

Warning Signs:
---------------------------------------------------------
- Simultaneous exhaustion occurs
- Fewer cells allocated than expected
- Some rows or columns have no positive cells
- Unusual allocation patterns
- Algorithm behaves erratically

4. HANDLING DEGENERACY
================================================================================

The Epsilon (ε) Method:
---------------------------------------------------------
1. Identify the degenerate solution
2. Choose a zero cell (xᵢⱼ = 0)
3. Add epsilon (ε) to that cell
4. The cell becomes a basic variable
5. Now allocations = m + n - 1
6. Continue with the solution method

Choosing the Right Zero Cell:
---------------------------------------------------------
- Don't create a cycle
- Prefer cells with low costs
- Avoid duplicate rows or columns
- Maintain the basic structure
- Document your choice

5. DEGENERACY BY METHOD
================================================================================

Method | Likelihood of Degeneracy | Why?
---------------------------------------------------------
NW Corner | Very High | Ignores costs, systematic movement
Least Cost | Moderate | Considers costs, still possible
VAM | Low | Uses penalties, minimizes risk

6. IMPACT OF DEGENERACY
================================================================================

On MODI Method:
---------------------------------------------------------
- Requires exactly m+n-1 basic variables
- Degeneracy means fewer variables
- System of equations is underdetermined
- Dual variables can't be calculated
- Optimality check fails

On Simplex Method:
---------------------------------------------------------
- Can cause cycling (infinite loop)
- May not converge to optimal
- Requires special handling
- Slows down convergence

7. COMMON MISTAKES
================================================================================

❌ Ignoring Degeneracy:
   Proceeding without handling degeneracy.

❌ Wrong Epsilon Placement:
   Creating cycles or invalid structure.

❌ Too Many Epsilons:
   Adding more than needed.

❌ Not Verifying:
   Not checking count after adding epsilon.

❌ Not Documenting:
   Not recording epsilon placements.

❌ Confusing with Infeasibility:
   Degeneracy is feasible, just incomplete.

8. BEST PRACTICES
================================================================================

1. Early Detection:
   - Count allocations immediately after IBFS
   - Compare with m+n-1
   - Identify if degeneracy exists
   - Don't wait to handle it

2. Careful Epsilon Placement:
   - Choose a zero cell carefully
   - Avoid creating cycles
   - Prefer cells with low costs
   - Document your choice

3. Documentation:
   - Record all epsilon placements
   - Note the reason for each choice
   - Keep for future reference
   - Share with team members

4. Verification:
   - Verify allocation count after epsilon
   - Check that it equals m+n-1
   - Verify the solution is still feasible
   - Proceed with optimization

9. PROFESSIONAL TIPS
================================================================================

1. Expect Degeneracy:
   - Don't be surprised by it
   - It's common, especially with NW Corner
   - Be prepared to handle it

2. Systematic Approach:
   - Use a consistent method for epsilon
   - Document everything
   - Verify after each addition

3. Use Software:
   - Modern solvers handle degeneracy
   - But understand what they're doing
   - Review epsilon placements

4. Practice:
   - Work through degenerate examples
   - Learn to identify quickly
   - Build confidence

5. Document Everything:
   - Record epsilon placements
   - Note the reasoning
   - Keep for verification
