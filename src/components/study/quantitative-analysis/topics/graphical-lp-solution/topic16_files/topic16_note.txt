INTERSECTION OF TWO CONSTRAINT LINES
===============================================================================

TOPIC 16 – FINDING CORNER POINTS

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. WHAT IS THE INTERSECTION OF TWO LINES?
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The intersection of two constraint lines is the point where they cross.
This point is a corner point of the feasible region.

- The point (x, y) that satisfies both equations
- Every corner point is the intersection of two constraints
- Found by solving two linear equations simultaneously

Key idea: Intersections become corner points in LP.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. TYPES OF INTERSECTIONS
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Unique Intersection:
- Lines cross at exactly one point
- One unique solution
- Example: x+y=10 and 2x+y=14 → (4,6)

Parallel Lines:
- Lines never cross
- No solution
- Example: x+y=10 and x+y=5 → no intersection

Coincident Lines:
- Lines are identical
- Infinite solutions
- Example: x+y=10 and 2x+2y=20 → same line

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. METHODS FOR FINDING INTERSECTIONS
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Substitution Method:
1. Solve one equation for a variable.
2. Substitute into the other equation.
3. Solve for the remaining variable.
4. Substitute back to find the other variable.

Elimination Method:
1. Align equations (same order).
2. Add or subtract to eliminate a variable.
3. Solve for the remaining variable.
4. Substitute back to find the other variable.

Graphical Method:
1. Plot both lines.
2. Read the intersection point.
3. Use for visualization and verification.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. SPECIAL CASES
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Vertical Line (x = k):
- Substitute x = k into the other equation.
- Solve for y.
- Example: x=3 and 2x+3y=12 → (3,2)

Horizontal Line (y = k):
- Substitute y = k into the other equation.
- Solve for x.
- Example: y=3 and 3x+4y=24 → (4,3)

Line Through Origin (c = 0):
- Both equations have c = 0.
- Intersection at (0,0) if not parallel.
- Example: 2x-y=0 and x+2y=0 → (0,0)

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. COMMON MISTAKES
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
✗ Not checking if the intersection is feasible.
✗ Confusing substitution and elimination methods.
✗ Missing parallel lines (no solution).
✗ Missing coincident lines (infinite solutions).
✗ Arithmetic errors in solving systems.
✗ Forgetting to check if the point satisfies all constraints.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. PROFESSIONAL TIPS
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
✓ Choose elimination when coefficients align nicely.
✓ Choose substitution when one equation is easy to solve.
✓ Always check if the intersection satisfies all constraints.
✓ Use graphical methods to verify algebraic solutions.
✓ Watch for parallel and coincident lines.
✓ Write equations in standard form before solving.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. REAL-WORLD EXAMPLES
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Production: intersection of machine and labor constraints gives production mix
- Resource allocation: intersection of budget and time constraints
- Diet planning: intersection of calorie and protein constraints
- Investment: intersection of risk and return constraints
