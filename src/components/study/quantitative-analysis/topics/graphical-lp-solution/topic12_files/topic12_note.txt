===============================================================================
BOUNDED AND UNBOUNDED FEASIBLE REGIONS
===============================================================================

TOPIC 12 – UNDERSTANDING REGION TYPES

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. DEFINITIONS
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Bounded Region:
- Enclosed on all sides
- Finite area
- Has a maximum and minimum in all directions
- Always has a finite number of corner points
- Example: x≥0, y≥0, x+y≤10

Unbounded Region:
- Extends infinitely in some direction
- Infinite area
- Has no bound in at least one direction
- May have finite or infinite corner points
- Example: x≥0, y≥0, x+y≥4

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. HOW TO IDENTIFY
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Bounded Region Signs:
✓ There are ≤ constraints in both x and y directions
✓ The region does not extend to infinity
✓ All variables have upper bounds
✓ The region forms a closed polygon
✓ You can draw a circle around it

Unbounded Region Signs:
∞ Only ≥ constraints (minimum requirements)
∞ Missing upper bounds in some direction
∞ Variables can grow without limit
∞ Region extends to infinity
∞ Goes off the graph in some direction

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. WHY BOUNDEDNESS MATTERS
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
For Bounded Regions:
- Optimal solution ALWAYS exists
- Found at a corner point
- Maximization and minimization both work
- The simplex method terminates

For Unbounded Regions:
- Optimal solution MAY NOT exist
- Maximization can be unbounded (∞)
- Minimization often still works
- The simplex method may fail

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. KEY INSIGHTS
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Bounded regions are formed by ≤ constraints (upper bounds).
- Unbounded regions often come from ≥ constraints (minimum requirements).
- Non-negativity alone creates an unbounded region.
- Boundedness guarantees an optimal solution exists.
- For maximization, unbounded regions may have no finite optimum.
- For minimization, unbounded regions often still have an optimum.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. COMMON MISTAKES
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
✗ Assuming all LP regions are bounded.
✗ Not recognizing unbounded regions in maximization problems.
✗ Missing that non-negativity alone creates unbounded regions.
✗ Forgetting that unbounded regions can still have optimal values (minimization).
✗ Not checking if the objective can grow indefinitely.
✗ Thinking bounded means the same as always having a solution.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. PROFESSIONAL TIPS
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
✓ Look for ≤ constraints — they usually bound the region.
✓ If the region goes off the graph, it's likely unbounded.
✓ For bounded regions, optimal solutions always exist.
✓ For unbounded maximization, check if the objective goes to infinity.
✓ Include a boundedness check in your LP analysis.
✓ Draw the region first, then analyze its properties.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. REAL-WORLD EXAMPLES
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Bounded Examples:
- Production planning with limited resources (machine hours, labor, materials)
- Diet planning with calorie limits
- Budget allocation with fixed total

Unbounded Examples:
- Minimum requirements (at least 4 hours of study)
- Investment returns with no upper limit
- Resource allocation with no
