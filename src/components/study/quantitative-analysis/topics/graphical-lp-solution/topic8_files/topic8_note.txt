TOPIC 8 – RESTRICTING TO THE FIRST QUADRANT

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. WHAT ARE NON-NEGATIVITY RESTRICTIONS?
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Non-negativity restrictions are constraints that require decision variables
to be greater than or equal to zero: x ≥ 0 and y ≥ 0.

Why are they needed?
- Most real-world quantities can't be negative
- You can't produce negative items
- You can't spend negative money
- You can't have negative time

Key idea: Non-negativity ensures mathematical solutions are practically meaningful.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. GRAPHING NON-NEGATIVITY
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
x ≥ 0:
  - Vertical line at x = 0 (the y-axis)
  - Solid line (includes equality)
  - Shade to the RIGHT

y ≥ 0:
  - Horizontal line at y = 0 (the x-axis)
  - Solid line (includes equality)
  - Shade ABOVE

The intersection is the FIRST QUADRANT (QI).

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. CORNER POINTS WITH NON-NEGATIVITY
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Non-negativity creates important corner points:
- Origin: (0,0) — intersection of x=0 and y=0
- x-axis intercepts: (x, 0) — where constraints meet x-axis
- y-axis intercepts: (0, y) — where constraints meet y-axis

These are always candidates for optimal solutions.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. EFFECT ON FEASIBLE REGION
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Feasible region is restricted to the first quadrant
- All negative coordinates are eliminated
- The origin is always in the feasible region (if other constraints allow)
- Axis intercepts become important boundary points

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. COMMON MISTAKES
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
✗ Forgetting to include non-negativity constraints.
✗ Graphing in all four quadrants unnecessarily.
✗ Not recognizing origin as a potential optimum.
✗ Ignoring axis intercepts as corner points.
✗ Using dashed lines for x≥0 or y≥0 (should be solid).
✗ Assuming all variables are automatically non-negative.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. PROFESSIONAL TIPS
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
✓ Always include non-negativity in LP formulations.
✓ Graph only the first quadrant for LP problems.
✓ Origin is almost always a feasible corner point.
✓ Axis intercepts are key corner points.
✓ Use the first quadrant as your "playing field."
✓ Check if variables truly can't be negative.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. REAL-WORLD EXAMPLES
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Production planning: quantities can't be negative
- Budget allocation: spending can't be negative
- Time management: time can't be negative
- Inventory management: stock levels can't be negative
- Resource allocation: resources can't be negative
