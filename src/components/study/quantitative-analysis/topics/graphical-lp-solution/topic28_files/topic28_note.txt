===============================================================================
WORKED EXAMPLE 1: SIMPLE TWO-VARIABLE MAXIMIZATION
===============================================================================

TOPIC 28 – A COMPLETE LP SOLUTION

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. PROBLEM STATEMENT
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A small business produces product X and product Y.

- Product X: 2 hours labor, 3 units material, ₹3 profit
- Product Y: 3 hours labor, 2 units material, ₹4 profit
- Available: 12 hours labor, 12 units material

Question: How many units of each should be produced to maximize profit?

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. LP FORMULATION
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Decision Variables:
  x = units of product X
  y = units of product Y

Objective Function:
  Maximize Z = 3x + 4y

Constraints:
  2x + 3y ≤ 12  (labor)
  3x + 2y ≤ 12  (material)
  x ≥ 0, y ≥ 0  (non-negativity)

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. GRAPHICAL SOLUTION
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Corner Points:
  O (0,0)     → Z = 0
  A (4,0)     → Z = 12
  B (2.4,2.4) → Z = 16.8  ← OPTIMAL
  C (0,4)     → Z = 16

Optimal Solution:
  x = 2.4, y = 2.4, Z = 16.8

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. INTERPRETATION
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Produce 2.4 units of each product
- Maximum profit = ₹16.8
- Both constraints are binding (fully utilized)
- Labor: 2(2.4) + 3(2.4) = 12 hours ✓
- Material: 3(2.4) + 2(2.4) = 12 units ✓

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. KEY LESSONS
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
✓ Always define decision variables clearly first
✓ The optimal solution is at a corner point
✓ Both constraints are binding at the optimum
✓ Fractional solutions are allowed in LP
✓ Verify that the solution satisfies all constraints

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. COMMON MISTAKES
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
✗ Forgetting non-negativity constraints
✗ Misreading constraints from the problem
✗ Not checking all corner points
✗ Ignoring fractional solutions
✗ Not verifying the optimal solution

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. REAL-WORLD APPLICATION
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
This example represents a production planning problem where a business
must allocate limited resources (labor, materials) between two products
to maximize profit.

The same approach applies to:
- Manufacturing: allocate machine time, labor, materials
- Agriculture: allocate land, water, fertilizer
- Service: allocate time, staff, budget
