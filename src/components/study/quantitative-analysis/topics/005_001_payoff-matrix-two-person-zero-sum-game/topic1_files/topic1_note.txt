--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. DEFINITION OF A PLAYER IN GAME THEORY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A PLAYER is an autonomous, rational entity capable of making decisions among a set
of alternatives to maximize their expected monetary return or utility in Indian Rupees (₹).

ROLES IN A TWO-PERSON ZERO-SUM GAME:
1. PLAYER A (ROW PLAYER):
   • The MAXIMIZER.
   • Aims to maximize the minimum gain (Maximin Criterion).
   • Chooses from rows A_1, A_2, ..., A_m.

2. PLAYER B (COLUMN PLAYER):
   • The MINIMIZER.
   • Aims to minimize the maximum loss / payout to Player A (Minimax Criterion).
   • Chooses from columns B_1, B_2, ..., B_n.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. DEFINITION & ANATOMY OF A STRATEGY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A STRATEGY is a complete, pre-formulated contingency plan specifying the exact
action a player will take in every conceivable situation throughout the game.

TYPES OF STRATEGIES:

1. PURE STRATEGY:
   • A deterministic decision rule where a player chooses ONE specific course of
     action with 100% certainty (Probability p = 1.0).
   • Example: Player A always chooses Strategy A_1.

2. MIXED STRATEGY:
   • A decision rule where a player randomizes over two or more pure strategies
     according to a fixed probability distribution:
         p = (p_1, p_2, ..., p_m)
     subject to:
         p_i >= 0  for all i,   and   sum_{i=1}^m p_i = 1.0
   • Purpose: Eliminates predictability, preventing an intelligent rival from
     anticipating and countering deterministic play.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. DOMINANT AND DOMINATED STRATEGIES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. STRICTLY DOMINANT STRATEGY:
   • A strategy that yields a strictly higher payoff than all alternative strategies,
     regardless of what action the opponent chooses.

2. DOMINATED STRATEGY:
   • A strategy that yields an equal or worse payoff than another strategy for every
     possible opponent action.
   • Rational players NEVER play dominated strategies (they can be eliminated from the matrix).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. STRATEGY SPACES & NOTATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
For an m x n Game:
• Strategy Space of Player A: S_A = {A_1, A_2, ..., A_m} with probability vector p = (p_1, ..., p_m).
• Strategy Space of Player B: S_B = {B_1, B_2, ..., B_n} with probability vector q = (q_1, ..., q_n).
• Total Strategic Combinations: m * n outcomes in the payoff matrix.

5. FOUR REAL-WORLD BENGAL CASE STUDIES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

CASE STUDY 1: Precision Foundry Tender Bidding Strategy (Debangshu)
Debangshu in Barrackpore evaluated a pure strategy (fixed discount bidding) vs a mixed
strategy (randomizing discounts between ₹20,000 and ₹50,000), preventing competitors from guessing bids.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CASE STUDY 2: Cold-Chain Vaccine Facility Supplier Strategy (Mamata & Mahima)
Mamata and Mahima in Kolkata modeled backup refrigeration vendor options as a 2x2
strategic game, identifying a dominant strategy in backup solar generators over diesel engines.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CASE STUDY 3: Supermarket FMCG Retail Marketing Mix Strategy (Susmita)
Susmita in Ichapur analyzed promotional discount schedules between two competing
supermarkets, proving that adopting a mixed advertising strategy (60% weekend vs 40% weekday)
stabilized footfall revenue.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CASE STUDY 4: Educational High-Tech Lab Research Grant Consortium (Abhronila)
Abhronila analyzed strategy sets for university patent licensing in Jadavpur, identifying
that cross-licensing strictly dominated litigation in a ₹55 Lakh nanotechnology dispute.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. COMMON PITFALLS & PRACTICAL ADVICE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Never assume an opponent will repeat a predictable pure strategy if no saddle point exists.
- In mixed strategies, ensure probabilities sum to EXACTLY 1.0 (sum p_i = 1.0).
- Row players seek to MAXIMIZE payoffs; Column players seek to MINIMIZE payouts.
- Always state strategic payoffs in Indian Rupees (₹).
