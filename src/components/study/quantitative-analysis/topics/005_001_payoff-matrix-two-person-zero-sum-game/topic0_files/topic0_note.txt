1. DEFINITION & PURPOSE OF GAME THEORY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
GAME THEORY is the formal mathematical study of STRATEGIC INTERACTION among rational,
self-interested decision-makers (called PLAYERS).

KEY CHARACTERISTIC:
• In standard optimization (like Linear Programming), a single decision-maker controls
  their outcome against a passive environment.
• In GAME THEORY, the payoff (utility or profit in ₹) of each player depends NOT ONLY
  on their own chosen strategy, but also on the STRATEGIC CHOICES OF RIVAL PLAYERS!

HISTORICAL FOUNDATIONS:
• 1944: John von Neumann and Oskar Morgenstern published "Theory of Games and Economic Behavior".
• 1950: John Nash introduced the concept of Nash Equilibrium for non-cooperative games.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. THE 5 STRUCTURAL ELEMENTS OF A GAME
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A strategic game is formally defined as G = (N, {S_i}, {u_i}):

1. PLAYERS (N = {1, 2, ..., n}):
   • The rational, decision-making entities (firms, litigants, bidding contractors).

2. STRATEGY SETS (S_i for each player i):
   • The complete set of executable actions or contingency plans available to player i.

3. INFORMATION STRUCTURE:
   • The degree of knowledge players have regarding opponents' strategies, past moves,
     and payoff functions (Complete vs Incomplete, Perfect vs Imperfect).

4. PAYOFF FUNCTIONS (u_i(s_1, s_2, ..., s_n)):
   • Numerical utility, monetary profit, or loss measured in Indian Rupees (₹) resulting
     from a specific combination of chosen strategies.

5. RATIONALITY AXIOM:
   • Every player aims to maximize their own expected payoff and understands that all
     opponents are simultaneously doing the exact same thing.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. THE 5 MAJOR GAME CLASSIFICATIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. ZERO-SUM VS. NON-ZERO-SUM GAMES:
   • ZERO-SUM: Total sum of payoffs across all players equals ZERO for every outcome.
     One player's gain is strictly another player's loss (Pure Conflict).
   • NON-ZERO-SUM: Total payoff can expand (Win-Win / Cooperation) or contract (Lose-Lose / Price War).

2. COOPERATIVE VS. NON-COOPERATIVE GAMES:
   • COOPERATIVE: Players can form binding coalitions, sign legal contracts, and share payoffs.
   • NON-COOPERATIVE: Players make independent choices without binding enforcement mechanisms.

3. SIMULTANEOUS VS. SEQUENTIAL (DYNAMIC) GAMES:
   • SIMULTANEOUS: Players select strategies concurrently without knowing rivals' choices (Matrix Form).
   • SEQUENTIAL: Players move in turns, observing preceding actions (Game Tree / Extensive Form).

4. SYMMETRIC VS. ASYMMETRIC GAMES:
   • SYMMETRIC: Players have identical strategy sets and identical payoff structures.
   • ASYMMETRIC: Strategy options and payoff rewards differ across players.

5. FINITE VS. INFINITE GAMES:
   • FINITE: Finite number of players and finite number of available strategies.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. NORMAL FORM (PAYOFF MATRIX) REPRESENTATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
For a Two-Person Zero-Sum Game between Player A (Row Player) and Player B (Column Player):
• Player A chooses Row i (from m strategies: A_1, A_2, ..., A_m).
• Player B chooses Column j (from n strategies: B_1, B_2, ..., B_n).
• Matrix entry a_ij represents the PAYOFF TO PLAYER A (in ₹).
• Since the game is zero-sum, the PAYOFF TO PLAYER B is strictly -a_ij.

PAYOFF CONVENTION:
• Positive Entry (+a_ij): Payment FROM Player B TO Player A (Player A gains ₹a_ij).
• Negative Entry (-a_ij): Payment FROM Player A TO Player B (Player A loses ₹a_ij).

6. COMMON PITFALLS & PRACTICAL ADVICE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Never assume rivals will make irrational or foolish moves; always assume opponents are rational.
- In zero-sum games, matrix entries a_ij ALWAYS represent payoffs to the Row Player (Player A).
- Do not confuse Zero-Sum games (pure conflict) with Non-Zero-Sum games (potential cooperation).
- Always state financial payoffs and game values in Indian Rupees (₹).
