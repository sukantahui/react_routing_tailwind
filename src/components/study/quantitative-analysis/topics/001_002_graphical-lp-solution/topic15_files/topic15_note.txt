FINDING CORNER POINTS ALGEBRAICALLY
===============================================================================

TOPIC 15 – EXACT SOLUTIONS FOR LP PROBLEMS

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. WHY ALGEBRAIC METHODS?
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
While graphical methods give a visual understanding, algebraic methods
provide exact coordinates for corner points.

- Precision: Fractions and decimals are handled exactly
- Large Problems: Works even when graphs are too large
- Verification: Confirm graphical solutions with exact values

Key idea: Every corner point is the intersection of two constraint lines.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. ALGEBRAIC METHODS
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Substitution Method:
1. Solve one equation for a variable.
2. Substitute into the other equation.
3. Solve for the remaining variable.
4. Substitute back to find the other variable.

Elimination Method:
1. Align equations (same order of variables).
2. Add or subtract equations to eliminate a variable.
3. Solve for the remaining variable.
4. Substitute back to find the other variable.

Direct Substitution (for axes):
1. If x=0 or y=0, substitute directly.
2. Solve the other equation for the remaining variable.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. STEP-BY-STEP PROCESS
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEP 1: List all constraints, including non-negativity.
STEP 2: Identify pairs of constraints that could intersect.
STEP 3: Solve each pair of equations.
STEP 4: Check if the solution satisfies all constraints.
STEP 5: If feasible, it's a corner point.
STEP 6: List all corner points.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. EXAMPLES
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Example 1: Substitution
  x + y = 10  ... (1)
  2x + y = 14 ... (2)
  From (1): y = 10 - x
  Substitute into (2): 2x + (10 - x) = 14
  x = 4
  y = 6
  Solution: (4, 6)

Example 2: Elimination
  x + y = 10  ... (1)
  2x + y = 14 ... (2)
  Subtract (1) from (2): x = 4
  Substitute into (1): y = 6
  Solution: (4, 6)

Example 3: Direct Substitution
  2x + 3y = 12 ... (1)
  x = 0        ... (2)
  Substitute x=0 into (1): 3y = 12 → y = 4
  Solution: (0, 4)

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. COMMON MISTAKES
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
✗ Not verifying that the point satisfies all constraints.
✗ Making arithmetic errors in substitution.
✗ Choosing the wrong pair of constraints.
✗ Forgetting to include non-negativity constraints.
✗ Assuming all intersections are corner points.
✗ Not checking if the system has a solution.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. PROFESSIONAL TIPS
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
✓ Choose the method that makes the problem easiest.
✓ For x=0 or y=0 constraints, use direct substitution.
✓ When coefficients align nicely, use elimination.
✓ Always verify that the point satisfies all constraints.
✓ Double-check your arithmetic.
✓ Use a systematic table for multiple pairs.
