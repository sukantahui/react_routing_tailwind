TOPIC 3: COST MINIMIZATION PROBLEMS
===================================

INTRODUCTION
------------
Cost minimization problems are a fundamental application of linear 
programming where the objective is to meet requirements at the lowest 
possible cost. These problems are common in diet planning, blending, 
manufacturing, and logistics.

WHAT IS COST MINIMIZATION?
-------------------------
Cost minimization finds the optimal way to meet requirements while 
minimizing total cost. Unlike profit maximization, which focuses on 
resource constraints (≤), cost minimization focuses on requirement 
constraints (≥).

KEY CHARACTERISTICS
-------------------
1. Objective: Minimize total cost
2. Variables: Quantities of options/inputs
3. Requirements: ≥ constraints (minimum standards)
4. Availability: ≤ constraints (limits)
5. Non-negativity: x ≥ 0, y ≥ 0

GENERAL FORM
------------
Minimize Z = c₁x₁ + c₂x₂ + ... + cₙxₙ

Subject to (Requirements):
a₁₁x₁ + a₁₂x₂ + ... + a₁ₙxₙ ≥ b₁ (Requirement 1)
a₂₁x₁ + a₂₂x₂ + ... + a₂ₙxₙ ≥ b₂ (Requirement 2)
...

Subject to (Availability):
x₁ ≤ max₁, x₂ ≤ max₂, ...
x₁, x₂, ..., xₙ ≥ 0

COMMON APPLICATIONS
-------------------
1. Diet Planning:
   - Meet nutritional requirements
   - Minimize food cost
   - Example: Protein and carb requirements

2. Blending Problems:
   - Mix materials to meet specifications
   - Minimize material cost
   - Example: Chemical mixtures

3. Production Planning:
   - Meet production targets
   - Minimize production cost
   - Example: Machine allocation

4. Transportation:
   - Meet shipping requirements
   - Minimize shipping cost
   - Example: Warehouse distribution

KEY DIFFERENCES FROM PROFIT MAXIMIZATION
----------------------------------------
Cost Minimization:
- Min Z = c₁x₁ + c₂x₂
- Uses ≥ constraints
- Focus: Meet requirements at min cost

Profit Maximization:
- Max Z = p₁x₁ + p₂x₂
- Uses ≤ constraints
- Focus: Maximize profit with resources

EXAMPLE: DIET PLANNING
----------------------
Problem: Meet protein and carbohydrate requirements at minimum cost.

Food A: ₹20/serving, 4 protein, 3 carbs
Food B: ₹30/serving, 3 protein, 5 carbs
Requirements: 24 protein, 18 carbs

Formulation:
Minimize Z = 20x + 30y
Subject to:
4x + 3y ≥ 24 (Protein)
3x + 5y ≥ 18 (Carbs)
x, y ≥ 0

Solution:
Corner points: (0,8), (6,2), (6,0)
Optimal: (6,0) with Z = ₹120

INTERPRETATION
--------------
- Use 6 servings of Food A
- Use 0 servings of Food B
- Minimum cost: ₹120
- Protein: exactly met (24 units)
- Carbs: exactly met (18 units)

KEY TERMS
---------
• Surplus: Amount by which requirement is exceeded
• Binding Constraint: Requirement exactly met (surplus = 0)
• Shadow Price: Cost increase if requirement increases
• Feasible Region: All solutions meeting requirements

COMMON MISTAKES
---------------
1. Using ≤ instead of ≥ for requirements
2. Forgetting availability constraints
3. Ignoring surplus
4. Misinterpreting the feasible region

BEST PRACTICES
--------------
1. Identify all requirements carefully
2. Check for availability limits
3. Calculate surplus at optimal
4. Verify all constraints are satisfied
5. Interpret results in context

TEACHER'S NOTE
--------------
Cost minimization problems help students understand the concept of 
"meeting requirements at minimum cost." The key insight is that 
requirements are typically minimums (≥), not maximums (≤). I 
encourage students to think about their own decisions - buying 
groceries, planning meals, or choosing transportation - as 
cost minimization problems."
