1. THE BREAKDOWN OF PURE STRATEGIES (NON-SADDLE GAMES)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
In a Two-Person Zero-Sum Game:
• If Maximin (alpha) == Minimax (beta), a pure saddle point exists. Both players play pure strategies.
• If Maximin (alpha) < Minimax (beta), NO SADDLE POINT EXISTS.

WHAT HAPPENS IF PLAYERS USE PURE STRATEGIES WHEN ALPHA < BETA?
• Predictability Traps: If Player A plays a deterministic pure strategy, Player B can anticipate it
  and pick the column that minimizes Player A's payoff.
• Instability Cycles: If Player B counters Player A, Player A will switch rows; Player B will
  then switch columns. This creates an endless cycle of regret and unilateral deviations!

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. THE PHILOSOPHY OF RANDOMIZED PLAY (VON NEUMANN & MORGENSTERN)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
• John von Neumann proved the Minimax Theorem in 1928:
  "Every finite two-person zero-sum game has at least one equilibrium when players are allowed
   to randomize their strategy choices."

DEFINITION OF MIXED STRATEGY:
• Player A assigns a probability vector p = (p_1, p_2, ..., p_m) over m pure rows, where:
      sum_{i=1}^m p_i = 1.0    and    p_i >= 0  for all i
• Player B assigns a probability vector q = (q_1, q_2, ..., q_n) over n pure columns, where:
      sum_{j=1}^n q_j = 1.0    and    q_j >= 0  for all j

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. INFORMATIONAL IMMUNITY & EXPECTED PAYOFF
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
• By using a randomizing device (e.g. coin flip, dice, computer RNG) to select their move
  before each round, neither player can be predicted by the opponent.
• Even if the opponent knows the player's PROBABILITY DISTRIBUTION p*, the opponent CANNOT
  exploit it because the expected payoff is mathematically invariant across all opponent moves!

4. FOUR REAL-WORLD BENGAL CASE STUDIES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

CASE STUDY 1: Metal Foundry Night Patrol & Security Scheduling (Debangshu)
Debangshu in Barrackpore had 2 security patrol routes. Deterministic patrols allowed metal thieves
to steal alloy scrap. Randomizing patrols (50% Route 1, 50% Route 2) eliminated scrap theft completely,
saving ₹40,000 monthly.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CASE STUDY 2: Cold-Chain Vaccine Temperature Auditing (Mamata & Mahima)
Mamata and Mahima in Kolkata randomized hospital refrigerator audits (60% Morning, 40% Evening).
Hospital staff could no longer anticipate inspection hours, keeping compliance at 100% and preserving ₹2 Lakh vaccines.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CASE STUDY 3: Supermarket FMCG Surprise Weekend Discounts (Susmita)
Susmita in Ichapur faced a rival retail chain. Deterministic weekend sales were matched and undercut.
Randomizing surprise flash discounts (40% Friday, 60% Sunday) kept the competitor off-balance and boosted revenue by ₹25,000.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CASE STUDY 4: Educational High-Tech Lab Cross-Examination Bidding (Abhronila)
Abhronila randomized patent claim defense postures (70% Technical, 30% Commercial) in Jadavpur,
preventing the opposing legal team from anticipating litigation tactics and settling at ₹15 Lakh.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. COMMON PITFALLS & PRACTICAL ADVICE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Never use pure strategies when Maximin < Minimax; you will get trapped by a smart opponent.
- A mixed strategy is NOT erratic guessing; it is playing precisely computed mathematical probabilities!
- The probabilities in p and q must strictly sum to 1.0 and be non-negative.
- Express all expected payoffs and game values in Indian Rupees (₹).
