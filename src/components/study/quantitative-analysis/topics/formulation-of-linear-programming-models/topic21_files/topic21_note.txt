================================================================================
TOPIC 21: WORKED EXAMPLE 15 – BLENDING PROBLEM
================================================================================

1. PROBLEM STATEMENT
--------------------
A company produces a blended product by mixing two raw materials: Material X and Material Y.
The blend must meet specific quality requirements:

Quality requirements per unit of blend:
- Protein content: at least 20%
- Fat content: at most 15%
- Moisture content: at most 10%

Material compositions:
              Material X   Material Y
Protein (%)       30%         10%
Fat (%)           5%          20%
Moisture (%)      8%          12%

Cost per kg: Material X = ₹25, Material Y = ₹20

The company wants to produce 1,000 kg of the blend at minimum cost.

2. STEP-BY-STEP FORMULATION
---------------------------

STEP 1: Read and Understand
- Goal: Minimize cost for 1,000 kg blend
- Materials: X and Y (2 materials)
- Requirements: Protein ≥ 20%, Fat ≤ 15%, Moisture ≤ 10%
- Production: Exactly 1,000 kg of blend

STEP 2: Identify Decision Variables
- x₁ = kg of Material X used
- x₂ = kg of Material Y used

STEP 3: Identify the Objective
- Cost: Material X = ₹25/kg, Material Y = ₹20/kg
- Minimize Z = 25x₁ + 20x₂

STEP 4: Identify Constraints
- Total blend: x₁ + x₂ = 1,000
- Protein: 0.30x₁ + 0.10x₂ ≥ 0.20(1,000) = 200
  → 0.30x₁ + 0.10x₂ ≥ 200
  → 3x₁ + x₂ ≥ 2,000 (multiply by 10)
- Fat: 0.05x₁ + 0.20x₂ ≤ 0.15(1,000) = 150
  → 0.05x₁ + 0.20x₂ ≤ 150
  → 5x₁ + 20x₂ ≤ 15,000 (multiply by 100)
  → x₁ + 4x₂ ≤ 3,000 (divide by 5)
- Moisture: 0.08x₁ + 0.12x₂ ≤ 0.10(1,000) = 100
  → 0.08x₁ + 0.12x₂ ≤ 100
  → 8x₁ + 12x₂ ≤ 10,000 (multiply by 100)
  → 2x₁ + 3x₂ ≤ 2,500 (divide by 4)

STEP 5: Add Non-negativity
- x₁ ≥ 0, x₂ ≥ 0

STEP 6: Review and Validate
- Test x₁ = 500, x₂ = 500:
  Total = 1,000 ✓
  Protein = 0.30(500)+0.10(500) = 150+50 = 200 ≥ 200 ✓
  Fat = 0.05(500)+0.20(500) = 25+100 = 125 ≤ 150 ✓
  Moisture = 0.08(500)+0.12(500) = 40+60 = 100 ≤ 100 ✓
  Cost = 25(500)+20(500) = 12,500+10,000 = 22,500

STEP 7: Document
- Final Model:
  Minimize Z = 25x₁ + 20x₂
  Subject to:
    Blend: x₁ + x₂ = 1,000
    Protein: 3x₁ + x₂ ≥ 2,000
    Fat: x₁ + 4x₂ ≤ 3,000
    Moisture: 2x₁ + 3x₂ ≤ 2,500
    x₁ ≥ 0, x₂ ≥ 0

3. SOLUTION APPROACH
---------------------
This is a 2-variable LP with equality and inequality constraints.

From blend constraint: x₂ = 1,000 - x₁

Substitute into constraints:
Protein: 3x₁ + (1,000 - x₁) ≥ 2,000 → 2x₁ + 1,000 ≥ 2,000 → 2x₁ ≥ 1,000 → x₁ ≥ 500

Fat: x₁ + 4(1,000 - x₁) ≤ 3,000 → x₁ + 4,000 - 4x₁ ≤ 3,000 → -3x₁ ≤ -1,000 → x₁ ≥ 333.33

Moisture: 2x₁ + 3(1,000 - x₁) ≤ 2,500 → 2x₁ + 3,000 - 3x₁ ≤ 2,500 → -x₁ ≤ -500 → x₁ ≥ 500

So x₁ ≥ 500 (from protein and moisture) and x₁ ≥ 333.33 (from fat).

Since x₁ + x₂ = 1,000 and x₁ ≥ 500, x₂ ≤ 500.

The objective: Z = 25x₁ + 20(1,000 - x₁) = 25x₁ + 20,000 - 20x₁ = 20,000 + 5x₁

To minimize Z, we want the smallest x₁, which is x₁ = 500.

Then x₂ = 1,000 - 500 = 500.

So optimal: x₁ = 500, x₂ = 500, cost = 25(500) + 20(500) = 12,500 + 10,000 = ₹22,500

4. INTERPRETATION
-----------------
- Use 500 kg of Material X and 500 kg of Material Y.
- Total cost = ₹22,500.
- Quality check at optimum:
  - Protein: 0.30(500)+0.10(500) = 150+50 = 200 (20%) ✓
  - Fat: 0.05(500)+0.20(500) = 25+100 = 125 (12.5%) ≤ 15% ✓
  - Moisture: 0.08(500)+0.12(500) = 40+60 = 100 (10%) ✓
- Protein and moisture are binding; fat has slack.

5. KEY INSIGHTS
---------------
- Blending problems involve mixing ingredients to meet quality specifications.
- The objective is to minimize cost while meeting all requirements.
- Some constraints may be binding, others may have slack.
- The blend total is typically fixed (exactly 1,000 kg).

6. REAL-WORLD APPLICATIONS
---------------------------
- Petroleum refining (gasoline blending).
- Animal feed formulation.
- Food product blending (juice, flour, etc.).
- Chemical manufacturing.

7. WHAT WE LEARN
----------------
- How to formulate blending problems.
- How to handle equality constraints (fixed blend amount).
- How to handle percentage constraints.
- The importance of quality specifications.

8. KEY TAKEAWAYS
-----------------
- Blending problems are common in manufacturing.
- Fixed blend amount creates an equality constraint.
- Percentage constraints require careful conversion.
- The optimal solution meets all quality requirements at minimum cost.
