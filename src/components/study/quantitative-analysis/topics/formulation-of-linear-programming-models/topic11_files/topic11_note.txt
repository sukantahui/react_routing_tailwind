================================================================================
TOPIC 11: WORKED EXAMPLE 5 – LABOUR AND MACHINE-HOUR CONSTRAINTS
================================================================================

1. PROBLEM STATEMENT
--------------------
A manufacturing company produces two products: Product A and Product B.
The company has two key resources that constrain production:
- Labour hours (total: 100 hours per week)
- Machine hours (total: 80 hours per week)

Resource requirements per unit:
              Product A   Product B   Available
Labour (hrs)       2           4         100
Machine (hrs)      3           2          80

Profit per unit: Product A = ₹50, Product B = ₹60

Additionally, market demand requires at least 10 units of Product A to be produced.

The company wants to maximize total profit.

2. STEP-BY-STEP FORMULATION
---------------------------

STEP 1: Read and Understand
- Goal: Maximize profit
- Products: A and B (2 products)
- Resources: Labour (100 hrs), Machine (80 hrs)
- Requirement: At least 10 units of Product A

STEP 2: Identify Decision Variables
- x₁ = number of units of Product A produced
- x₂ = number of units of Product B produced

STEP 3: Identify the Objective
- Profit per unit: A = ₹50, B = ₹60
- Maximize Z = 50x₁ + 60x₂

STEP 4: Identify Constraints
- Labour: 2x₁ + 4x₂ ≤ 100
- Machine: 3x₁ + 2x₂ ≤ 80
- Demand: x₁ ≥ 10

STEP 5: Add Non-negativity
- x₁ ≥ 0, x₂ ≥ 0

STEP 6: Review and Validate
- Test x₁ = 10, x₂ = 0:
  Labour = 20 ≤ 100 ✓
  Machine = 30 ≤ 80 ✓
  Demand = 10 ≥ 10 ✓

- Test x₁ = 20, x₂ = 10:
  Labour = 40 + 40 = 80 ≤ 100 ✓
  Machine = 60 + 20 = 80 ≤ 80 ✓
  Demand = 20 ≥ 10 ✓

STEP 7: Document
- Final Model:
  Maximize Z = 50x₁ + 60x₂
  Subject to:
    Labour: 2x₁ + 4x₂ ≤ 100
    Machine: 3x₁ + 2x₂ ≤ 80
    Demand: x₁ ≥ 10
    x₁ ≥ 0, x₂ ≥ 0

3. GRAPHICAL SOLUTION
---------------------
Step 1: Plot constraints on x₁-x₂ plane
- Labour: 2x₁ + 4x₂ = 100 → x₂ = 25 - 0.5x₁
- Machine: 3x₁ + 2x₂ = 80 → x₂ = 40 - 1.5x₁
- Demand: x₁ ≥ 10 (vertical line)

Step 2: Identify feasible region
Step 3: Find corner points:
  - (10, 0)
  - (10, 20)
  - (20, 10)
  - (26.67, 0)

Step 4: Evaluate objective at each corner:
  - (10, 0): Z = 50(10) + 60(0) = 500
  - (10, 20): Z = 50(10) + 60(20) = 500 + 1,200 = 1,700
  - (20, 10): Z = 50(20) + 60(10) = 1,000 + 600 = 1,600
  - (26.67, 0): Z = 50(26.67) + 60(0) = 1,333.5

Step 5: Optimal solution: (10, 20) with profit = ₹1,700

4. INTERPRETATION
-----------------
- Produce 10 units of Product A and 20 units of Product B.
- Total profit = ₹1,700.
- Resource usage at optimum:
  - Labour: 2(10) + 4(20) = 20 + 80 = 100 hrs (fully used)
  - Machine: 3(10) + 2(20) = 30 + 40 = 70 hrs (slack: 10 hrs)
  - Demand: 10 ≥ 10 (just met)

5. KEY INSIGHTS
---------------
- Labour is the binding constraint (fully used).
- Machine has 10 hours of slack (unused capacity).
- The demand constraint is binding at the minimum (x₁ = 10).
- The optimal solution produces exactly the minimum required units of A.
- Product B is more profitable per machine hour but uses more labour.

6. LABOUR VS MACHINE-HOUR CONSTRAINTS
--------------------------------------
- Labour constraints represent limits on available workforce hours.
- Machine-hour constraints represent limits on available equipment time.
- These are the two most common resource constraints in manufacturing.
- They often compete: more labour vs more machine time.

7. WHAT WE LEARN
----------------
- How to handle two resource constraints (Labour and Machine).
- How to include demand constraints alongside resource constraints.
- How to identify binding vs non-binding resources.
- The importance of checking resource utilization.

8. KEY TAKEAWAYS
-----------------
- Labour and machine-hour constraints are common in production planning.
- Multiple constraints can be binding or have slack.
- Demand constraints add minimum production requirements.
- The optimal solution balances resource usage and demand.
