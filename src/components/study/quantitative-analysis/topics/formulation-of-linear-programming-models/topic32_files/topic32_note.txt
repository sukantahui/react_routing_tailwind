TOPIC 32: TRANSLATING VERBAL CONDITIONS INTO MATHEMATICAL INEQUALITIES
================================================================================

1. INTRODUCTION
---------------
One of the most challenging aspects of LP formulation is translating verbal
conditions into mathematical inequalities. This topic provides a systematic
approach to identifying the correct inequality direction and writing constraints
from word problems.

2. KEYWORDS AND THEIR MEANINGS
-------------------------------
Keyword                Meaning              Inequality   Example
"at most"              ≤                    ≤           x ≤ 10
"no more than"         ≤                    ≤           x ≤ 10
"cannot exceed"        ≤                    ≤           x ≤ 10
"maximum"              ≤                    ≤           x ≤ 10
"limited to"           ≤                    ≤           x ≤ 10
"not to exceed"        ≤                    ≤           x ≤ 10

"at least"             ≥                    ≥           x ≥ 10
"no less than"         ≥                    ≥           x ≥ 10
"minimum"              ≥                    ≥           x ≥ 10
"must be at least"     ≥                    ≥           x ≥ 10
"should not be less"   ≥                    ≥           x ≥ 10

"exactly"              =                    =           x = 10
"equal to"             =                    =           x = 10
"is"                   =                    =           x = 10
"must be"              =                    =           x = 10

3. STEP-BY-STEP TRANSLATION PROCESS
-----------------------------------
Step 1: Identify the quantity being described.
Step 2: Identify the inequality keyword.
Step 3: Determine the correct inequality direction.
Step 4: Write the mathematical expression.
Step 5: Check the direction with a simple example.

4. COMMON PHRASES AND THEIR TRANSLATIONS
-----------------------------------------
Phrase                    Translation
"x is at most 5"          x ≤ 5
"x is no more than 5"     x ≤ 5
"x cannot exceed 5"       x ≤ 5
"x is at least 5"         x ≥ 5
"x is no less than 5"     x ≥ 5
"x must be at least 5"    x ≥ 5
"x is exactly 5"          x = 5
"x is equal to 5"         x = 5

5. COMPLEX PHRASE TRANSLATIONS
-------------------------------
Phrase                    Translation
"The sum of x and y is at most 10"     x + y ≤ 10
"The total of x and y is at least 20"  x + y ≥ 20
"The difference of x and y is exactly 5"  x - y = 5
"x is twice y"            x = 2y
"x is 3 more than y"      x = y + 3
"x is at least twice y"   x ≥ 2y
"The ratio of x to y is 2"  x/y = 2 → x = 2y

6. COMMON MISTAKES
-------------------
- Using the wrong inequality direction (≤ vs ≥).
- Confusing "at most" with "at least".
- Forgetting to include all terms in the expression.
- Not checking units for consistency.
- Translating verbal conditions too literally without considering the algebra.
- Mixing up variables and constants.

7. BEST PRACTICES
------------------
- Read the problem carefully and underline keywords.
- Write the inequality direction clearly.
- Use variables consistently throughout the problem.
- Check the translation with a simple numerical example.
- Convert all terms to the same units.
- Verify that the inequality makes logical sense.

8. TIPS & TRICKS
-----------------
- "At most" → ≤ (think: "at the maximum")
- "At least" → ≥ (think: "at the minimum")
- "Exactly" → = (think: "precisely")
- When in doubt, test with a number.
- Draw a number line to visualize the inequality.
- Practice with a variety of phrases.

9. SUMMARY
-----------
Translating verbal conditions into mathematical inequalities is a critical skill
in LP formulation. With practice, you'll develop an intuition for the correct
inequality direction. Remember: "at most" means ≤, "at least" means ≥, and
"exactly" means =.

"Think about: What does the condition mean in words? What would a number
that violates the condition look like?"
