================================================================================
TOPIC 13: WORKED EXAMPLE 7 – COST MINIMIZATION PROBLEM
================================================================================

1. PROBLEM STATEMENT
--------------------
A company needs to produce two products: Product X and Product Y.
The company must meet minimum requirements for two resources:
- Resource A: at least 24 units
- Resource B: at least 20 units

Resource requirements per unit:
              Product X   Product Y   Minimum Required
Resource A       2           3           24
Resource B       4           2           20

Cost per unit: Product X = ₹40, Product Y = ₹60

The company wants to minimize total production cost.

2. STEP-BY-STEP FORMULATION
---------------------------

STEP 1: Read and Understand
- Goal: Minimize total cost
- Products: X and Y (2 products)
- Resources: A and B (2 resources with minimum requirements)
- This is a minimization problem (unlike previous maximization examples)

STEP 2: Identify Decision Variables
- x₁ = number of units of Product X produced
- x₂ = number of units of Product Y produced

STEP 3: Identify the Objective
- Cost per unit: X = ₹40, Y = ₹60
- Minimize Z = 40x₁ + 60x₂

STEP 4: Identify Constraints
- Resource A: 2x₁ + 3x₂ ≥ 24
- Resource B: 4x₁ + 2x₂ ≥ 20

STEP 5: Add Non-negativity
- x₁ ≥ 0, x₂ ≥ 0

STEP 6: Review and Validate
- Test x₁ = 0, x₂ = 10:
  Resource A = 30 ≥ 24 ✓
  Resource B = 20 ≥ 20 ✓
  Cost = 60(10) = ₹600

- Test x₁ = 6, x₂ = 4:
  Resource A = 12 + 12 = 24 ≥ 24 ✓
  Resource B = 24 + 8 = 32 ≥ 20 ✓
  Cost = 40(6) + 60(4) = 240 + 240 = ₹480

STEP 7: Document
- Final Model:
  Minimize Z = 40x₁ + 60x₂
  Subject to:
    Resource A: 2x₁ + 3x₂ ≥ 24
    Resource B: 4x₁ + 2x₂ ≥ 20
    x₁ ≥ 0, x₂ ≥ 0

3. GRAPHICAL SOLUTION
---------------------
Step 1: Plot constraints on x₁-x₂ plane
- Resource A: 2x₁ + 3x₂ = 24 → x₂ = 8 - (2/3)x₁
- Resource B: 4x₁ + 2x₂ = 20 → x₂ = 10 - 2x₁

Step 2: Identify feasible region (above both constraints)
Step 3: Find corner points:
  - Intersection of A and B:
    2x₁ + 3x₂ = 24
    4x₁ + 2x₂ = 20
    Solve: From B: 2x₁ + x₂ = 10 → x₂ = 10 - 2x₁
    Substitute: 2x₁ + 3(10 - 2x₁) = 24
    2x₁ + 30 - 6x₁ = 24
    -4x₁ = -6 → x₁ = 1.5
    Then: x₂ = 10 - 2(1.5) = 10 - 3 = 7
    So (1.5, 7)

  - Intersection of A with x₂-axis: 2x₁ = 24 → x₁ = 12 → (12, 0)
  - Intersection of B with x₁-axis: 4x₁ = 20 → x₁ = 5 → (5, 0)
  - Intersection of A with x₁-axis: 2x₁ = 24 → x₁ = 12 → (12, 0)
  - Intersection of B with x₂-axis: 2x₂ = 20 → x₂ = 10 → (0, 10)

  Corner points: (0, 10), (1.5, 7), (12, 0)

Step 4: Evaluate objective at each corner:
  - (0, 10): Z = 40(0) + 60(10) = 600
  - (1.5, 7): Z = 40(1.5) + 60(7) = 60 + 420 = 480
  - (12, 0): Z = 40(12) + 60(0) = 480

Step 5: Optimal solution: (1.5, 7) with cost = ₹480

Note: There are multiple optimal solutions! (1.5, 7) and (12, 0) both give ₹480.

4. INTERPRETATION
-----------------
- Produce 1.5 units of Product X and 7 units of Product Y (or 12 units of X and 0 units of Y).
- Total cost = ₹480.
- Resource usage at optimum:
  - Resource A: 2(1.5) + 3(7) = 3 + 21 = 24 (fully used)
  - Resource B: 4(1.5) + 2(7) = 6 + 14 = 20 (fully used)

5. KEY INSIGHTS
---------------
- Both resources are fully used at the optimum (binding).
- There are multiple optimal solutions (alternative optima).
- Cost minimization problems use ≥ constraints (minimum requirements).
- The feasible region is above the constraints (unlike ≤ constraints where it's below).

6. COST MINIMIZATION VS PROFIT MAXIMIZATION
--------------------------------------------
Feature           Cost Minimization        Profit Maximization
Objective         Minimize cost            Maximize profit
Constraint type   ≥ (minimum requirements) ≤ (maximum capacities)
Feasible region   Above constraints        Below constraints
Interpretation    Meet requirements        Use resources efficiently

7. WHAT WE LEARN
----------------
- How to formulate a cost minimization problem.
- The difference between minimization and maximization.
- How to handle ≥ constraints (minimum requirements).
- Alternative optimal solutions.
- The importance of checking all corner points.

8. KEY TAKEAWAYS
-----------------
- Cost minimization is the dual of profit maximization.
- Minimum requirements use ≥ constraints.
- The feasible region extends away from the origin.
- Alternative optimal solutions exist when the objective is parallel to a constraint.
- Always evaluate all corner points.
