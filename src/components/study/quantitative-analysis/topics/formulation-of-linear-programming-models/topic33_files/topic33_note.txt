================================================================================
TOPIC 33: DISTINGUISHING OBJECTIVE FUNCTIONS FROM CONSTRAINTS
================================================================================

1. INTRODUCTION
---------------
One of the most fundamental skills in LP formulation is distinguishing between
the objective function and constraints. This topic provides a clear framework
for identifying which parts of a word problem belong to the objective and which
belong to the constraints.

2. OBJECTIVE FUNCTION VS CONSTRAINTS
------------------------------------
Objective Function:
- What you want to optimize (maximize or minimize)
- A single linear expression
- Contains coefficients that represent contributions (profit, cost, etc.)
- Answer: "What are we trying to achieve?"

Constraints:
- What limits your decisions
- Multiple inequalities or equalities
- Contain coefficients that represent resource usage, requirements, etc.
- Answer: "What restrictions must we respect?"

3. KEY CHARACTERISTICS
----------------------
Objective Function:
- Always has a maximize or minimize statement
- Usually appears at the beginning of the model
- Has no inequality sign (just expression)
- Variables appear with coefficients representing contribution per unit

Constraints:
- Always have inequality signs (≤, ≥, =) or equality signs
- Usually appear after the objective
- Have a Right Hand Side (RHS) value
- Variables appear with coefficients representing resource usage

4. HOW TO IDENTIFY THE OBJECTIVE
--------------------------------
Look for keywords:
- "Maximize", "Max", "Increase", "Highest", "Best" → Maximization
- "Minimize", "Min", "Decrease", "Lowest", "Cheapest" → Minimization
- "Profit", "Revenue", "Cost", "Time", "Distance" → What is being optimized

Ask: "What is the decision-maker trying to achieve?"
The answer is the objective.

5. HOW TO IDENTIFY CONSTRAINTS
------------------------------
Look for keywords:
- "At most", "No more than", "Cannot exceed" → ≤ constraints
- "At least", "No less than", "Minimum" → ≥ constraints
- "Exactly", "Equal to" → = constraints
- "Limited to", "Available", "Capacity" → Resource constraints

Ask: "What limits the decision-maker's choices?"
The answers are the constraints.

6. COMMON MISTAKES
-------------------
- Confusing the objective with constraints
- Including the objective in the constraints section
- Forgetting the objective entirely
- Using inequality signs in the objective
- Not having a clear objective direction (max or min)
- Including constraints that are actually part of the objective

7. BEST PRACTICES
------------------
- Write the objective first, then the constraints
- Clearly state "Maximize" or "Minimize" before the objective
- Label constraints clearly
- Check that the objective has no inequality sign
- Check that constraints have inequality or equality signs
- Test with a simple example

8. TIPS & TRICKS
-----------------
- The objective answers "What do we want?"
- Constraints answer "What limits us?"
- The objective is a single expression; constraints are multiple
- The objective has coefficients representing value; constraints have coefficients representing usage
- If it has "≤", "≥", or "=", it's a constraint

9. SUMMARY
-----------
Distinguishing between the objective function and constraints is essential for
correct LP formulation. Remember: the objective is what you want to optimize;
constraints are the limits you must respect.

"Think about: What are we trying to achieve? That's the objective.
What limits us? Those are the constraints."
