--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. DEFINITION & PHILOSOPHY OF THE PRINCIPLE OF DOMINANCE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The PRINCIPLE OF DOMINANCE is a mathematical rule of game theory used to simplify
large m x n payoff matrices by eliminating INFERIOR (DOMINATED) strategies.

RATIONALITY AXIOM:
• A rational player will NEVER play a strategy that yields a worse or equal payoff
  in all possible opponent states compared to an available alternative strategy.
• Deleting dominated strategies leaves the optimal Value of the Game (v* in ₹)
  and the equilibrium probabilities of the remaining strategies 100% UNCHANGED.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. ASYMMETRIC DOMINANCE CRITERIA
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

• FOR PLAYER A (ROW MAXIMIZER):
  - Row i dominates Row j if:
        a_ik >= a_jk    for all columns k = 1, ..., n
  - ACTION: ELIMINATE THE SMALLER ROW (Row j).

• FOR PLAYER B (COLUMN MINIMIZER):
  - Column r dominates Column s if:
        a_kr <= a_ks    for all rows k = 1, ..., m
  - ACTION: ELIMINATE THE LARGER COLUMN (Column s).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. STRICT VS WEAK DOMINANCE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. STRICT DOMINANCE:
   • Every element in the dominant strategy is STRICTLY superior (a_ik > a_jk for all k).
   • Deleting strictly dominated strategies preserves ALL Nash equilibria.

2. WEAK DOMINANCE:
   • Elements are superior or equal (a_ik >= a_jk for all k, with at least one strict >).
   • Deleting weakly dominated strategies preserves AT LEAST ONE Nash equilibrium.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. ITERATED ELIMINATION OF DOMINATED STRATEGIES (IEDS)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
• Elimination proceeds sequentially in rounds:
  1. Delete dominated rows.
  2. Inspect the newly reduced matrix; deleting rows may cause previously non-dominated
     columns to become dominated!
  3. Delete newly dominated columns.
  4. Repeat until no further rows or columns can be eliminated.
• Target: Reduce large m x n matrices down to a 2x2 matrix (solvable via algebraic formulas)
  or a 1x1 cell (pure saddle point equilibrium!).

5. FOUR REAL-WORLD BENGAL CASE STUDIES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

CASE STUDY 1: Precision Foundry Supplier Reduction via Dominance (Debangshu)
Debangshu in Barrackpore evaluated a 4x4 metal alloy bidding game. Iterative dominance
eliminated 3 inferior shifts, reducing the matrix to a single 1x1 saddle point of ₹40,000 profit.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CASE STUDY 2: Cold-Chain Hospital Logistics Route Pruning (Mamata & Mahima)
Mamata and Mahima in Kolkata pruned 2 expensive delivery routes that were strictly dominated
by express highway couriers, reducing a 3x3 logistics game to a solvable 2x2 matrix.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CASE STUDY 3: Supermarket FMCG Retail Promotional Pruning (Susmita)
Susmita in Ichapur eliminated a loss-making weekend coupon scheme that was weakly dominated
by bundle discounts, protecting retail margins by ₹18,000.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CASE STUDY 4: Educational High-Tech Lab Patent Licensing Strategy Pruning (Abhronila)
Abhronila used dominance reduction in Jadavpur to eliminate 2 high-risk litigation rows,
streamlining a 4x3 university royalty negotiation down to an equitable 2x2 matrix.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. COMMON PITFALLS & PRACTICAL ADVICE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Never eliminate the larger row; Player A wants to MAXIMIZE, so eliminate the SMALLER row!
- Never eliminate the smaller column; Player B wants to MINIMIZE, so eliminate the LARGER column!
- Check EVERY element across all cells; partial dominance across only some cells is NOT valid!
- State all reduced matrix payoffs and game values in Indian Rupees (₹).
