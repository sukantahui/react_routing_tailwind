--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. THE PRINCIPLE OF DYNAMIC PENALTY RECALCULATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Vogel's Approximation Method (VAM) is an ADAPTIVE iterative heuristic.
After each allocation step, an exhausted row or column is struck out.
This line elimination permanently removes a set of candidate shipping routes
from all intersecting rows and columns.

WHY PENALTIES MUST BE RECOMPUTED:
1. Candidate Pool Shift: An intersecting line may lose its cheapest route c_(1)
   or its second-cheapest route c_(2).
2. Risk Escalation: If a line loses its second-cheapest backup route, its penalty
   can jump dramatically (e.g. from ₹2 to ₹18), signaling an urgent new risk.
3. Sub-matrix Contraction: The problem dimensions reduce from m x n to
   (m-1) x n or m x (n-1), requiring fresh opportunity cost evaluation.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. MATHEMATICAL EFFECT ON REMAINING LINES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Case A: Crossed-out cell was the LOWEST cost c_(1)
  - Old c_(2) becomes the NEW lowest cost c_(1)'.
  - Old c_(3) becomes the NEW second-lowest cost c_(2)'.
  - New penalty: P' = c_(3) - c_(2).

Case B: Crossed-out cell was the SECOND-LOWEST cost c_(2)
  - Lowest cost c_(1) remains unchanged.
  - Old c_(3) becomes the NEW second-lowest cost c_(2)'.
  - New penalty: P' = c_(3) - c_(1) (PENALTY WIDENS!).

Case C: Crossed-out cell was HIGHER than second-lowest (c >= c_(3))
  - Top two lowest costs are undisturbed.
  - New penalty remains identical: P' = P.

Case D: Only ONE cell remains in the line
  - No second choice exists; mark penalty as '—'.
  - Mandatory direct allocation is performed in the final pass.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. STEP-BY-STEP RECALCULATION WORKFLOW
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Step 1: Execute allocation x_kl = min(S_k, D_l) in Pass p.
Step 2: Strike out the satisfied line (Row k or Column l).
Step 3: In the margin, label the next iteration column P_R(p+1) on the right
        and the next iteration row P_C(p+1) at the bottom.
Step 4: For all crossed-out lines, write '—' in the new margin.
Step 5: For all surviving rows i, identify the two lowest active cells and write
        P_i(p+1) = c_(i,2)' - c_(i,1)'.
Step 6: For all surviving columns j, identify the two lowest active cells and write
        P_j(p+1) = c_(2,j)' - c_(1,j)'.
Step 7: Locate the new maximum penalty Max_P(p+1) and repeat until the tableau
        is completely solved.

5. COMMON PITFALLS & PRACTICAL ADVICE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Never reuse old penalties; crossed-out lines make old differences invalid.
- Always write '—' for eliminated lines to prevent accidental inclusion.
- Maintain multi-column margin headers P_1, P_2, P_3 for clean documentation.
