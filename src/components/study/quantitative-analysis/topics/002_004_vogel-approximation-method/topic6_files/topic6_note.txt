--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. BALANCED VS. UNBALANCED TRANSPORTATION MODELS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A standard transportation linear programming problem requires total supply
to equal total demand (Conservation of Flow).

CASE 1: BALANCED TRANSPORTATION PROBLEM
- Condition: Sum_{i=1}^m S_i == Sum_{j=1}^n D_j
- No artificial dummy lines are needed. VAM begins directly on the original tableau.

CASE 2: UNBALANCED PROBLEM WITH SURPLUS SUPPLY (Sum S_i > Sum D_j)
- Total production exceeds market requirements.
- Solution: Add a fictitious DUMMY DESTINATION (Column D_{n+1}).
- Demand requirement: D_{dummy} = Sum S_i - Sum D_j.
- Unit transportation costs: c_{i, dummy} = ₹0 for all rows i.
- Managerial meaning: Allocations to D_{dummy} represent unsold surplus inventory
  retained in origin warehouses.

CASE 3: UNBALANCED PROBLEM WITH SURPLUS DEMAND (Sum D_j > Sum S_i)
- Total customer demand exceeds factory production.
- Solution: Add a fictitious DUMMY ORIGIN (Row S_{m+1}).
- Supply capacity: S_{dummy} = Sum D_j - Sum S_i.
- Unit transportation costs: c_{dummy, j} = ₹0 for all columns j.
- Managerial meaning: Allocations from S_{dummy} represent unmet market shortages
  (delayed orders / lost sales).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. IMPACT OF ₹0 DUMMY CELLS ON PENALTY CALCULATIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
When a ₹0 dummy column is added:
- In every row i, ₹0 becomes the smallest unit cost c_(i,1) = ₹0.
- The previous lowest cost c_(old min) becomes the second-lowest cost c_(i,2).
- The updated row penalty becomes:
    P_i = c_(old min) - ₹0 = c_(old min)
- The dummy column's own penalty is P_{dummy} = ₹0 - ₹0 = ₹0.

When a ₹0 dummy row is added:
- In every column j, ₹0 becomes the smallest unit cost c_(1,j) = ₹0.
- The updated column penalty becomes:
    P_j = c_(old min) - ₹0 = c_(old min)
- The dummy row's own penalty is P_{dummy} = ₹0 - ₹0 = ₹0.

3. FULL WORKED NUMERICAL EXAMPLES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

EXAMPLE A: Excess Supply (Debangshu's Barrackpore Foundry)
Origins: S1 (Barrackpore, 50 tons), S2 (Ichapur, 70 tons), S3 (Kolkata, 80 tons).
Total Supply = 200 tons.
Destinations: D1 (Jadavpur, 60 tons), D2 (Salt Lake, 50 tons), D3 (Howrah, 60 tons).
Total Demand = 170 tons.
Difference = 200 - 170 = 30 tons Excess Supply -> Add Dummy Column D4 (Demand = 30 tons, rate = ₹0).

Cost Matrix with Dummy Column (₹/ton):
       D1    D2    D3    D_Dummy  Supply  P_R(1)
  S1  ₹8    ₹14   ₹12     ₹0       50      ₹8 - ₹0 = ₹8
  S2  ₹5    ₹19   ₹10     ₹0       70      ₹5 - ₹0 = ₹5
  S3  ₹11   ₹13   ₹7      ₹0       80      ₹7 - ₹0 = ₹7
Demand 60    50    60      30      200
P_C(1) ₹3    ₹1    ₹3      ₹0

VAM Execution:
- Pass 1: Max Penalty = ₹8 at Row 1. Lowest cost in Row 1 is cell (S1, D_Dummy) @ ₹0!
  Allocate x_14 = min(50, 30) = 30 tons to (S1, D_Dummy).
  Dummy Column D4 is satisfied and crossed out!
- Remaining 3x3 matrix is now balanced with 170 tons.
- Final allocations: x_14 = 30 @ ₹0, x_11 = 20 @ ₹8, x_21 = 40 @ ₹5, x_22 = 30 @ ₹19...
- Total Cost Z excludes the dummy shipment (30 × ₹0 = ₹0).

EXAMPLE B: Excess Demand (Mamata & Mahima's Healthcare Network)
Origins: S1 (Kolkata Central, 60 boxes), S2 (Barrackpore Depot, 40 boxes).
Total Supply = 100 boxes.
Destinations: D1 (Howrah, 50 boxes), D2 (Barasat, 40 boxes), D3 (Jadavpur, 30 boxes).
Total Demand = 120 boxes.
Difference = 120 - 100 = 20 boxes Shortage -> Add Dummy Row S3 (Supply = 20 boxes, rate = ₹0).

Cost Matrix with Dummy Row (₹/box):
       D1    D2    D3   Supply  P_R(1)
  S1  ₹6    ₹18   ₹9     60      ₹9 - ₹6 = ₹3
  S2  ₹4    ₹25   ₹15    40      ₹15 - ₹4 = ₹11 (MAX!)
  S_D ₹0    ₹0    ₹0     20      ₹0 - ₹0 = ₹0
Demand 50   40    30    120
P_C(1) ₹4   ₹18   ₹9

VAM Execution:
- Pass 1: Max Penalty is Row 2 (Barrackpore) with P = ₹11. Lowest cell is (S2, D1) @ ₹4.
  Allocate x_21 = min(40, 50) = 40 boxes. Row 2 is crossed out!
- Terminal Pass: 20 boxes shortage assigned to cell (S_Dummy, D2) @ ₹0, meaning Barasat
  absorbs the 20-box shortage.

5. COMMON PITFALLS & PRACTICAL ADVICE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Never omit the balance check; executing VAM without balancing invalidates the solution.
- In dummy lines, always assign unit cost ₹0 (unless specific holding/penalty costs are given).
- Remember that dummy allocations contribute ₹0 to total cost Z = Sum (c_ij * x_ij).
- Basis count must include the dummy dimension: (m + n + 1) - 1 = m + n basic cells.
