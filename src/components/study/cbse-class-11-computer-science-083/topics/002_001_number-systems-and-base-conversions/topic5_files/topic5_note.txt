1. WHY USE BINARY AS AN INTERMEDIARY?
Direct conversion between Octal (Base 8) and Hexadecimal (Base 16) is computationally heavy if performed through Decimal (requires long multi-step multiplication and division).
Since both 8 and 16 are direct powers of 2 (8 = 2³, 16 = 2⁴), Binary serves as a lightning-fast intermediary bridge:

Octal (Base 8) <---> Binary (Base 2) <---> Hexadecimal (Base 16)

2. OCTAL TO HEXADECIMAL CONVERSION ALGORITHM:
- Step 1 (Octal -> Binary): Expand each octal digit into its 3-bit binary equivalent.
- Step 2 (Binary -> Hexadecimal): Regroup the combined binary bitstream into 4-bit nibbles from right to left (pad leftmost with 0s if needed).
- Step 3: Replace each 4-bit nibble with the corresponding Hex character.

Example 1: Convert (756)₈ to Hexadecimal
Step 1: Expand octal digits to 3-bit binary:
  7 -> 111
  5 -> 101
  6 -> 110
  Combined Binary: 111101110

Step 2: Regroup into 4-bit nibbles from right:
  (1) (1110) (1110)
  Pad with 3 leading zeros: (0001) (1110) (1110)

Step 3: Convert nibbles to Hex:
  0001 -> 1
  1110 -> E (14)
  1110 -> E (14)
Result: (756)₈ = (1EE)₁₆

3. HEXADECIMAL TO OCTAL CONVERSION ALGORITHM:
- Step 1 (Hexadecimal -> Binary): Expand each hexadecimal character into its 4-bit binary equivalent.
- Step 2 (Binary -> Octal): Regroup the combined binary bitstream into 3-bit triplets from right to left (pad leftmost with 0s if needed).
- Step 3: Replace each 3-bit triplet with the corresponding Octal digit (0-7).

Example 2: Convert (1EE)₁₆ to Octal
Step 1: Expand hex characters to 4-bit binary:
  1 -> 0001
  E -> 1110
  E -> 1110
  Combined Binary: 000111101110

Step 2: Regroup into 3-bit triplets from right:
  (000) (111) (101) (110)

Step 3: Convert triplets to Octal:
  000 -> 0 (leading zero ignored)
  111 -> 7
  101 -> 5
  110 -> 6
Result: (1EE)₁₆ = (756)₈

4. CBSE EXAMINATION TIPS:
- Always show both the intermediate binary expansion step and the re-grouping step.
- Never convert through decimal during speed exams; the binary bridge is 5x faster and eliminates arithmetic calculation errors.
