1. WHAT IS COMPUTATIONAL THINKING (CT)?
   - Computational Thinking is a fundamental thought process involved in formulating
     problems and their solutions in such a way that the solutions can be effectively
     carried out by an information-processing agent (such as a computer).
   - Term pioneered by Jeannette Wing (2006).

2. THE FOUR PILLARS OF COMPUTATIONAL THINKING:

   PILLAR 1: DECOMPOSITION (Breaking Down)
      - Definition: Breaking down a complex problem, system, or dataset into smaller,
        more manageable, and independently solvable parts (sub-problems).
      - Why it Matters: Large monolithic tasks are overwhelming; solving smaller sub-tasks
        one by one reduces cognitive load and allows modular team collaboration.
      - Example: Building a School Management System is decomposed into:
        * Student Admission Module
        * Fee Collection Module
        * Examination & Grading Module
        * Library Management Module

   PILLAR 2: PATTERN RECOGNITION (Finding Similarities)
      - Definition: Looking for recurring patterns, similarities, trends, and shared
        characteristics among decomposed problems or past solved problems.
      - Why it Matters: Allows reusing proven existing algorithms and formulas rather
        than reinventing the wheel.
      - Example: Recognizing that calculating the area of a circle, sphere surface area,
        and cylinder volume all require multiplication by Pi (π * r^2).

   PILLAR 3: ABSTRACTION (Filtering Irrelevant Details)
      - Definition: Focusing exclusively on the essential, high-level features necessary
        for solving the problem while filtering out and ignoring unnecessary, distracting details.
      - Why it Matters: Reduces complexity by creating clean models.
      - Example: A Google Maps navigation app shows only roads, turns, and real-time traffic;
        it deliberately hides roadside trees, building interior layouts, and street lamp locations.

   PILLAR 4: ALGORITHM DESIGN (Step-by-Step Instructions)
      - Definition: Developing a step-by-step, ordered set of precise rules or instructions
        to solve the problem or achieve the desired outcome every single time.
      - Why it Matters: Provides a deterministic, repeatable recipe that can be automated
        in Python or any other programming language.
      - Example: The exact sequence of steps to compute Prime Numbers or Sort an Array.

3. SUMMARY TABLE FOR BOARD EXAMINATIONS:
   -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   Pillar                 Core Action                      Key Benefit
   -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   1. Decomposition       Divide complex into simple parts Simplifies management & modularity
   2. Pattern Recognition Identify recurring similarities  Enables reuse of existing solutions
   3. Abstraction         Filter out irrelevant noise      Focuses on essential characteristics
   4. Algorithm Design    Create step-by-step recipe       Enables automated computer execution
   -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
