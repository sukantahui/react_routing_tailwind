1. WHAT IS THE INTERNET OF THINGS (IoT)?
   - The Internet of Things (IoT) refers to a vast interconnected network of physical
     objects ("things") embedded with sensors, software, actuators, and network
     connectivity that enable them to collect, exchange, and act upon data over the internet
     without requiring direct human-to-human or human-to-computer intervention.
   - Term popularized by Kevin Ashton in 1999 (initially linked with RFID).

2. THE 3-TIER / 4-TIER ARCHITECTURE OF IoT:
   Tier 1: Perception (Sensing) Layer:
      - Physical hardware collecting data: Sensors (Temperature, Humidity, Light LDR,
        Gas/Smoke, Pressure), Actuators, and RFID tags.
   Tier 2: Network / Transport Layer:
      - Transmits captured sensory data to cloud gateways securely.
      - Protocols: Wi-Fi, Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE), Zigbee, LoRaWAN, Cellular (4G/5G), MQTT, CoAP.
   Tier 3: Middleware / Processing Layer:
      - Cloud servers, edge gateways, stream processors, and databases storing and analyzing telemetry.
   Tier 4: Application Layer:
      - User interfaces, smart home mobile dashboards, automated alerts, and analytics dashboards.

3. SENSORS VS. ACTUATORS IN IoT:
   - SENSORS (Data In): Devices that detect environmental physical changes (heat, light, moisture)
     and convert them into electrical analog/digital signals.
   - ACTUATORS (Physical Action Out): Devices that receive electrical control signals and perform
     mechanical actions (e.g., turning on an AC motor, opening an electronic door lock, turning on a water valve).

4. RADIO FREQUENCY IDENTIFICATION (RFID):
   - Definition: A wireless contactless communication technology that uses radio frequency
     electromagnetic waves to automatically identify and track tags attached to objects.
   - RFID Components:
     1. RFID Tag: Contains a microchip (stores unique ID/data) and an antenna.
        - Passive RFID Tag: Has no internal battery; powered by the electromagnetic field of the reader.
        - Active RFID Tag: Contains an onboard battery for long-range transmission (up to 100 meters).
     2. RFID Reader: Emits radio signals to power passive tags and reads the reflected data.
   - Prominent Use Case in India: FASTag for electronic toll collection across national highways.

5. MAJOR REAL-WORLD DOMAINS OF IoT:
   A. Smart Homes:
      - Automated lighting (turns on when motion is detected), smart thermostats (adjusts AC based on room occupancy),
        voice-activated appliances, smart door locks with biometric security.
   B. Smart Agriculture (Precision Farming):
      - Soil moisture sensors automatically trigger drip irrigation pumps, saving water.
      - Drone crop monitoring and weather prediction sensors.
   C. Smart Cities:
      - Smart Street Lighting: Streetlights dim during low traffic and brighten when cars approach.
      - Smart Waste Management: Trash bins equipped with ultrasonic level sensors alert municipal trucks when full.
      - Intelligent Traffic Management: Traffic signals adjust green light duration dynamically based on live vehicle density.
   D. Smart Healthcare (Internet of Medical Things - IoMT):
      - Wearable fitness trackers (smartwatches monitoring heart rate, SpO2, sleep).
      - Remote patient monitoring devices transmitting vitals directly to hospital doctors.

6. CHALLENGES IN IoT:
   - Security & Privacy: Vulnerability to hacking and unauthorized surveillance.
   - Interoperability & Standards: Multi-vendor devices using differing protocols.
   - Power & Bandwidth: Billions of devices require low-power long-range communication.
