1. WHAT IS A ROBOT & ROBOTICS?
   - Robotics is an interdisciplinary branch of engineering and computer science
     involving the design, construction, operation, and programming of robots.
   - A Robot is an electro-mechanical machine capable of carrying out a complex
     series of actions automatically, guided by computer programs and sensor feedback.

2. THE "SENSE - PLAN - ACT" AUTONOMOUS CONTROL LOOP:
   +-------------------------------------------------------------------+
   |  1. SENSE: Gather environmental data via sensors (vision, distance) |
   |  2. PLAN: Microcontroller / CPU processes data & decides action     |
   |  3. ACT: Actuators (motors, servos, grippers) execute physical move |
   +-------------------------------------------------------------------+

3. PRIMARY STRUCTURAL & FUNCTIONAL COMPONENTS OF A ROBOT:
   A. The Controller (The "Brain"):
      - The central microprocessor / microcontroller (e.g., Arduino, Raspberry Pi, ARM)
        that runs the control algorithms, reads sensor inputs, and issues actuation commands.
   B. Sensors (The "Senses"):
      - Convert physical environmental phenomena into electrical signals.
      - Types of Robot Sensors:
        * Ultrasonic / LiDAR Sensors: Measure distance by emitting sound / laser pulses.
        * Infrared (IR) Proximity Sensors: Obstacle detection and line following.
        * Cameras / Vision Sensors: Object classification, facial recognition, SLAM.
        * Gyroscope & Accelerometer (IMU): Measure orientation, tilt, and balance.
        * Tactile / Touch Sensors: Detect physical contact and grip pressure.
   C. Actuators (The "Muscles"):
      - Convert electrical, hydraulic, or pneumatic energy into mechanical motion.
      - Types of Actuators:
        * DC Motors & Stepper Motors: Drive wheels and rotational joints with precision.
        * Servo Motors: Provide angular position control for robot arms and steering.
        * Hydraulic Actuators: Use pressurized liquid for heavy-duty industrial lifting.
        * Pneumatic Actuators: Use compressed air for rapid, clean linear movements.
   D. End Effectors (The "Hands / Tools"):
      - The device attached to the wrist/tip of a robotic arm to interact with objects.
      - Examples: Mechanical grippers, vacuum suction cups, welding torches, laser cutters.
   E. Power Supply:
      - Rechargeable Lithium-ion batteries, solar panels, or tethered AC power.

4. MAJOR CLASSIFICATIONS & APPLICATIONS OF ROBOTS:
   A. Industrial Automation & Manufacturing:
      - Automotive assembly lines: High-speed spot welding, spray painting, precision milling.
      - Pick-and-place robots in automated e-commerce sorting warehouses (e.g., Amazon Kiva).
   B. Healthcare & Medical Robotics:
      - Robotic Surgery (e.g., da Vinci Surgical System): Provides sub-millimeter precision,
        smaller incisions, minimal blood loss, and faster patient recovery.
      - Robotic prosthetics and rehabilitation exoskeletons.
   C. Hazardous & Extreme Environments:
      - Space Exploration: NASA Mars Rovers (Curiosity, Perseverance) and ISRO Moon Rovers (Pragyan).
      - Bomb disposal (EOD) and deep-sea underwater exploration (ROVs / AUVs).
   D. Domestic & Service Robots:
      - Autonomous robotic vacuum cleaners (Roomba), lawn mowers, delivery drones.
   E. Agricultural Robotics:
      - Autonomous crop harvesting, drone pesticide spraying, soil quality inspection.

5. ADVANTAGES & LIMITATIONS IN SOCIETY:
   - Advantages: High repeatability, 24x7 continuous operation, precision, human safety in hazardous zones.
   - Limitations: High initial capital cost, lack of true creative intuition, displacement of repetitive manual jobs.
