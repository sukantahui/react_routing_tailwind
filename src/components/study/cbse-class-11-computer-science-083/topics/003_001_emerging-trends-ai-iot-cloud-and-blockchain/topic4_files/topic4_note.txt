1. WHAT ARE IMMERSIVE EXPERIENCES?
   - Immersive technologies create synthetic, multi-sensory digital simulations
     that alter or replace a user's perception of the physical environment.
   - They leverage 3D computer graphics, spatial audio, haptic feedback,
     accelerometers, gyroscopes, and depth sensors to give a sense of presence.

2. THE REALITY-VIRTUALITY CONTINUUM (Milgram's Model):
   Real Environment <---- Augmented Reality (AR) ---- Mixed Reality (MR) ---- Augmented Virtuality ----> Virtual Reality (VR)

3. AUGMENTED REALITY (AR):
   - Definition: A technology that overlays computer-generated digital visual/audio elements
     (such as 3D graphics, text, animation) onto the real-world view in real time.
   - Core Features:
     * Real-world environment remains directly visible.
     * Digital content is super-imposed onto the physical world.
     * Accessible via standard smartphones, tablets, HUDs, or AR smart glasses.
   - Examples & Use Cases:
     * Pokémon GO (placing creatures on real roads/parks via camera).
     * IKEA Place (previewing 3D furniture models inside living rooms before purchase).
     * Google Lens (translating physical signboards in real time).

4. VIRTUAL REALITY (VR):
   - Definition: A completely synthetic, computer-generated 3D artificial environment
     that totally shuts out the physical world and immerses the user inside a simulation.
   - Core Features:
     * User wears an opaque Head-Mounted Display (HMD) with dual high-refresh-rate displays.
     * Total visual and auditory isolation from physical surroundings.
     * Senses manipulated through spatial audio, motion controllers, and haptic gloves.
   - Examples & Use Cases:
     * Flight simulation and combat training for pilots and defense forces.
     * Complex surgical rehearsals for medical doctors.
     * Immersive 3D video gaming (Meta Quest, PlayStation VR, HTC Vive).

5. MIXED REALITY (MR / EXTENDED REALITY - XR):
   - Definition: An advanced hybrid state where physical and digital elements co-exist
     and interact with each other in real time.
   - Core Features:
     * Digital objects are anchored to physical geometry (tables, walls, floors).
     * Real objects can occlude (hide) virtual objects.
     * Interactive physics: A virtual ball bounces realistically off an actual physical table.
   - Examples: Microsoft HoloLens 2, Apple Vision Pro, Magic Leap.

6. TRACKING CONCEPTS IN IMMERSIVE HARDWARE:
   A. 3-DoF (Three Degrees of Freedom):
      - Tracks rotational movements only: Pitch (up/down), Yaw (left/right), Roll (tilt).
      - User can look around from a fixed stationary point, but cannot walk.
   B. 6-DoF (Six Degrees of Freedom):
      - Tracks 3 Rotational axes + 3 Translational axes (X: left/right, Y: up/down, Z: forward/backward).
      - Allows true 'room-scale' movement where walking in reality translates into walking in 3D space.
