1. GENDER DISPARITY & ISSUES IN COMPUTING & TECH
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A. THE DIGITAL GENDER DIVIDE:
   - Disparity in access to computing hardware, high-speed internet, digital literacy,
     and technical career pathways between genders.
   - Socio-cultural barriers often result in lower female enrollment in Computer Science
     degrees despite high aptitude.

B. HISTORIC & CONTEMPORARY FEMALE ROLE MODELS IN COMPUTING:
   - Ada Lovelace (1815–1852): The world's first computer programmer; designed the first
     algorithm for Charles Babbage's Analytical Engine.
   - Rear Admiral Grace Hopper (1906–1992): Developed the first compiler (A-0), pioneered
     high-level programming languages (COBOL), and popularized the term "debugging".
   - Katherine Johnson & NASA Human Computers: Calculated orbital trajectories for Mercury & Apollo.
   - Radia Perlman: Mother of the Internet; invented the Spanning Tree Protocol (STP).

C. PROMOTING GENDER INCLUSION IN IT:
   - Equal access to coding labs, robotics kits, and hardware resources.
   - Elimination of unconscious gender bias in curriculum materials and hiring pipelines.
   - Mentorship networks, girls-who-code clubs, and inclusive workplace policies.

2. DISABILITY BARRIERS & ASSISTIVE TECHNOLOGIES (DIVYANGJAN)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Computing interfaces must accommodate users across four primary disability categories:

1. VISUAL IMPAIRMENTS (Blindness, Low Vision, Color Blindness):
   - Assistive Hardware & Software:
     * Screen Readers (NVDA, JAWS, VoiceOver, Orca): Converts text and semantic HTML
       into synthesized speech.
     * Refreshable Braille Displays: Electro-mechanical pins raised dynamically for tactile reading.
     * Screen Magnifiers: Zooms portions of the screen (e.g. Windows Magnifier).
     * High Contrast Themes: Minimum WCAG contrast ratio (4.5:1 for normal text).
     * Alternative Text (`alt` attribute): Mandatory descriptions for all informative images.

2. HEARING IMPAIRMENTS (Deafness, Hard of Hearing):
   - Assistive Solutions:
     * Closed Captioning (CC) & Subtitles: Real-time text transcription of audio & sound effects.
     * Visual & Vibrational Alerts: Screen flashes or haptic buzzes instead of audio beeps.
     * Speech-to-Text Transcribers: Real-time classroom lecture transcription engines.

3. MOTOR & PHYSICAL IMPAIRMENTS (Paralysis, Tremors, Quadriplegia, Cerebral Palsy):
   - Assistive Solutions:
     * Eye-Tracking Cameras: Gaze-directed mouse pointer control and dwell-clicking.
     * Adaptive Switches & Sip-and-Puff Devices: Operates computers using air breath pressure.
     * Voice Recognition & Speech Dictation: Complete hands-free keyboard typing.
     * 100% Keyboard Operability: Full navigation via `Tab`, `Shift+Tab`, `Enter`, and Arrow keys.

4. COGNITIVE & LEARNING IMPAIRMENTS (Dyslexia, ADHD, Autism):
   - Assistive Solutions:
     * Dyslexia-Friendly Fonts (OpenDyslexic): Weighted bottom glyphs to prevent letter flipping.
     * Reader Mode / Distraction-Free Views: Removes sidebars, ads, and auto-playing videos.
     * Text-to-Speech Highlighting: Highlights active words synchronously while reading aloud.

3. WEB CONTENT ACCESSIBILITY GUIDELINES (WCAG) - THE 'POUR' PRINCIPLES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Perceivable: Information and UI components must be presentable to users in ways they can perceive (Alt text, Captions, High contrast).
2. Operable: UI components and navigation must be operable via keyboard without mouse dependency (No keyboard traps).
3. Understandable: Information and operation of user interface must be clear, predictable, and readable.
4. Robust: Content must be robust enough to be interpreted reliably by a wide variety of user agents, including assistive tools.

4. LEGAL & NATIONAL ACCESSIBILITY FRAMEWORKS IN INDIA
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Sugamya Bharat Abhiyan (Accessible India Campaign, 2015):
  Flagship nationwide campaign to ensure universal accessibility across built environments,
  public transport systems, and government ICT portals (mandatory GIGW compliance).
- Rights of Persons with Disabilities (RPwD) Act, 2016:
  Mandates non-discrimination, equal educational opportunities, and statutory digital accessibility.
