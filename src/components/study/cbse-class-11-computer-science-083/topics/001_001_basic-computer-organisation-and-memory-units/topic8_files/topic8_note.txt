1. INPUT DEVICES OVERVIEW
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Input devices transduce real-world physical, optical, mechanical, or acoustic
signals into binary digital bitstreams consumable by the CPU and primary memory.

Key Specialized Input Devices for CBSE Examinations:
1. Keyboard & Mouse:
   - Keyboard: Key matrix sends scan codes (e.g. ASCII / Unicode) upon keypress.
   - Mouse: Optical sensor tracks surface displacement (X, Y coordinate deltas).

2. Optical Mark Reader (OMR):
   - Principle: Detects presence or absence of dark pencil/pen marks on pre-printed forms
     by measuring reflected light intensity.
   - Application: Evaluates competitive examinations and CBSE objective answer sheets.

3. Optical Character Recognition (OCR):
   - Principle: Scans physical printed/handwritten text documents and uses pattern
     recognition algorithms to convert pixel images into editable digital text strings.
   - Application: Digitizing historical books, automatic passport readers at airports.

4. Magnetic Ink Character Recognition (MICR):
   - Principle: Reads characters printed with magnetized iron oxide ink (E-13B font)
     using magnetic read heads.
   - Key Feature: Highly secure against fraud; characters readable even if stamped or smudged.
   - Application: Processing banking cheques (Cheque number, 9-digit MICR code).

5. Barcode & QR Code Scanners:
   - Reads 1D linear barcodes (UPC/EAN) or 2D matrix QR codes for instant inventory lookup
     and UPI payments.


2. OUTPUT DEVICES: SOFT COPY VS HARD COPY
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
* Soft Copy Devices: Output is temporary, electronic, and vanishes upon power off.
  - Examples: Visual Display Units (VDU / Monitors: LCD, LED, OLED), Projectors, Audio Speakers.
* Hard Copy Devices: Output is permanent, tangible, and printed on paper or physical sheets.
  - Examples: Printers, Plotters, 3D Printers.


3. PRINTER TAXONOMY: IMPACT VS NON-IMPACT
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A. IMPACT PRINTERS:
   - Working Principle: Forms characters by physically striking an inked ribbon against paper.
   - Characteristics: Noisy, slow, low resolution (draft quality), BUT CAN produce carbon copies.
   - Examples: Dot Matrix Printers (DMP), Daisy Wheel Printers, Line Printers.
   - Typical Use: Railway reservation tickets, GST duplicate invoices, bank passbooks.

B. NON-IMPACT PRINTERS:
   - Working Principle: Forms images without mechanical impact using heat, laser light, or ink sprays.
   - Characteristics: Silent, fast, ultra-high resolution (1200+ DPI), CANNOT produce carbon copies.
   - Examples:
     * Laser Printers: Uses a rotating electrostatic drum, laser beam, and dry toner powder.
     * Inkjet Printers: Sprays microscopic droplets of liquid ink through thermal/piezo nozzles.
     * Thermal Printers: Uses heat-sensitive paper (ATM receipts, POS billing machines).


4. PLOTTERS (VECTOR GRAPHICS OUTPUT)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
* Principle: Uses mechanically driven colored ink pens to draw continuous smooth vector
  lines directly onto large paper rolls.
* Application: Architectural blueprints, civil engineering maps, CAD drawings, large vinyl banners.
* Types: Drum Plotters (paper moves on a cylinder) and Flatbed Plotters (paper is stationary).
